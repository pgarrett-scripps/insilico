# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
This structural biology and immunology study presents cryo-EM structures of EtpA complexed with monoclonal antibodies, mass-spectrometry glycosylation mapping, and polyclonal epitope analysis. The quantitative claims are generally well-supported by appropriate methods, though several statistical presentations lack rigor in reporting. The work is sound and the data support the main conclusions about repeat-domain function and glycosylation's role in antigenicity, but incomplete reporting of test details and effect sizes limits reproducibility and interpretation in places.

## Strengths
- Glycosylation occupancy is comprehensively mapped across 166 asparagine sites with explicit occupancy thresholds (≥25%, ≥50%, ≥75%) and peptide counts reported, enabling readers to assess confidence in each site.
- Functional assays (erythrocyte pull-down, ELISA, bacterial adhesion) include appropriate controls and report geometric means with statistical tests named, though confidence intervals are absent.
- Cryo-EM reconstructions are validated with FSC curves, resolution estimates, and model-to-map fit assessment, meeting structural biology standards.

## Weaknesses
- The central claim that 'C-terminal repeat regions direct blood group A binding' rests on comparing full-length EtpA to a truncated variant (EtpA1-1086) lacking repeats. Figure 1B–D show the truncation binds poorly to blood group A, but the manuscript does not exclude the alternative that truncation causes misfolding or instability rather than loss of a specific binding function. The immunoblot (Fig. 1A inset) confirms secretion but does not assess whether the truncated protein is properly folded in solution. Circular dichroism, size-exclusion chromatography, or thermal stability data would distinguish a folding defect from loss of a functional domain.
- Monoclonal antibody inhibition experiments (Fig. 1E–I) show that mAb 1G05 (CTR-binding) inhibits blood group A interactions while mAb 1C08 (NTS-binding) does not, supporting the repeat-domain hypothesis. However, the manuscript does not report the affinity (KD) of these mAbs for EtpA or the concentration used in inhibition assays. Without this, it is unclear whether 1C08's lack of inhibition reflects weak binding, steric inaccessibility, or genuine functional irrelevance. Supplemental Figure 1A shows Octet kinetics (KD values stated as 'high affinity' without numbers), but the main text does not cite these values or explain how antibody concentration was chosen for inhibition studies.
- The polyclonal epitope mapping (Fig. 5, EMPEM) identifies '5–6 and 3–4 unique epitopes' on NTS and CTR domains respectively, but the method for counting epitopes from 2D class averages is not quantified. No inter-rater reliability, no definition of what constitutes a 'unique' epitope (spatial separation threshold?), and no statement of how many particles or micrographs were analyzed. The claim that this represents 'a large immunogenic surface with multiple potential epitopes' is qualitative and cannot be verified from the methods provided.

## Questions
- Figure 1B reports geometric mean fluorescence intensity (GMFI) with p-values from Kruskal-Wallis testing but no n per group, no error bars, and no statement of how many independent experiments were performed—was this n=2 replicates or n=20? The figure legend says 'n=20 replicate fields from two independent experiments,' which suggests pseudo-replication (20 fields from 2 biological replicates); please clarify the independent unit.
- Supplemental Figure 1A (Octet kinetics) shows binding curves but the main text never cites KD values—what are the actual affinities of 1C08 and 1G05 for rEtpA, and were these used to normalize antibody concentrations in inhibition assays (Fig. 1E–I)?
- In the glycosylation analysis, 30 of 196 asparagine residues yielded no detected peptides (line 243). Were these sites excluded from the occupancy calculation, or does the 61 glycans-per-molecule average (line 244) assume 0% occupancy for undetected sites? If the latter, this underestimates true occupancy at detectable sites.
- Figure 3E–F claim 'glycan-glycan hydrogen bonds' and 'glycan-amino acid hydrogen bonds' but provide no distance cutoffs, no validation that these are energetically significant (e.g., bond length, angle), and no comparison to a null model of random glycan placement—how were these contacts defined and are they enriched beyond chance?
- The N-to-A mutations (Supplemental Fig. 9A) show 1G05 avidity decreases significantly (p=0.0003) while 1C08 binding is unimpaired, but avidity is measured by kinetic ELISA with 8 M urea as a chaotropic agent. Was this assay validated for measuring avidity in this context, and does the urea concentration and incubation time match published protocols?
