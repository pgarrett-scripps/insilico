# Meta-Review

**Draft recommendation:** major

## Synthesis
Seven of eight specialists converge on a consistent picture: the cryo-EM structures (3.3–4.0 Å, validated by FSC/MolProbity/EMRinger) and the site-specific glycosylation map (133/166 Asn sites, cross-validated against native H10407 protein) are technically solid and represent a genuine contribution to ETEC adhesin biology (clarity, methodology, reproducibility, novelty, literature, data_analysis, rigor all score 4/5 at confidence 4–5). The debate largely upheld the strongest empirical claim — that CTR-binding mAbs (1G05, human 1F09) inhibit blood-group-A binding and adhesion while NTS-binding mAbs (1C08) do not — as genuinely convergent, independent-line evidence (advocate's point, conceded by skeptic). Where the panel is unanimous in caution is that several downstream interpretive claims outrun the data as presented: (1) the truncation construct EtpA1-1086 has no fold-verification (CD/SEC/thermal shift), so loss of bgA binding cannot yet be distinguished from global misfolding (clarity, data_analysis, novelty, skeptic all raise this independently); (2) the 'immune evasion via glycan heterogeneity' narrative in the Discussion is asserted beyond what the data show, which is equally consistent with affinity maturation toward stable glycosylated epitopes (rigor, literature, novelty, and the skeptic's unrebutted point); (3) EMPEM epitope counts and the 'neutralizing vs. decoy' epitope framing rest on subjective visual clustering of negative-stain 2D classes without quantitative criteria (methodology, reproducibility, data_analysis, rigor). None of these are treated by the panel as invalidating the core structural/glycosylation findings — they are hedging and follow-up-experiment issues. The clear outlier is the ethics reviewer (2/5, confidence 5), who identifies that the primary human ETEC challenge study supplying the plasmablasts for several mAbs (including protective 1F09) has no IRB protocol number, approval statement, or consent disclosure in the manuscript itself — only a secondary-use IRB citation for archived specimens is given — alongside a missing data-deposition confirmation for promised EMDB/PDB accessions and an incompletely stated competing-interest disclosure (a directly relevant patent held by a co-author). In debate, the skeptic treated this as a hard, unresolved compliance gap that the advocate did not actually rebut (the advocate's turn addressed only structural/functional merits and did not engage the ethics point at all).

## Decisive Factors
The confidence-weighted average across the eight reviewers (3.73/5, dominated by seven high-confidence 'minor' verdicts) would nominally support a minor-revision recommendation, and the scientific critiques raised by the panel (fold-verification of the truncation construct, overclaiming on immune evasion, unquantified EMPEM epitope counts) are all fixable via textual hedging or supplementary experiments, consistent with 'minor.' I diverge to 'major' specifically because of the ethics reviewer's high-confidence, specific finding — undisclosed IRB/consent status for the primary human challenge study, an unconfirmed data-deposition promise for a structural paper whose central evidence (cryo-EM maps/models) is otherwise uninspectable without those accessions, and an incompletely stated financial conflict of interest directly tied to the vaccine antigen under study. This is not a matter of specialist expertise or a stylistic quibble; it concerns whether readers can trust the ethical provenance and verifiability of the human-subject-derived materials and the structural data underpinning every other reviewer's positive assessment. The debate did not resolve this: the skeptic's objection stands unrebutted, and the advocate did not contest it. Because the compliance gaps are plausibly resolvable by the authors supplying a protocol number, confirming deposition, and amending the COI statement — rather than reflecting an actual absence of ethical oversight or a scientific flaw in the structural work — reject is not warranted and would go beyond what any reviewer concluded. But a recommendation weaker than 'major' would mean the venue treats an unverified human-subjects disclosure and an unconfirmed data-availability promise as cosmetic, which contradicts the ethics reviewer's explicit 'HARD deficiency' framing and the journal's own emphasis on verifiability of central evidence and honest reporting of competing interests. Major revision — addressing IRB/consent disclosure for the primary challenge study, confirming EMDB/PDB deposition, correcting the COI statement, and hedging the immune-evasion and truncation-based claims per the specialist reports — is the calibrated outcome.
