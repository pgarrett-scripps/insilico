## Concessions
- **ethics** — The primary ETEC H10407 human challenge study supplying plasmablasts for several mAbs (including protective 1F09) has no IRB protocol number, approval statement, or consent disclosure in the manuscript itself—only the secondary-use IRB (Protocol 201110126) for archived specimens is cited.
  - Proposed change: Add an explicit statement in Methods (Plasmablast isolation from volunteers section) citing the original challenge-study IRB protocol number and approval body (Naval Medical Research Command IRB, as referenced in Guerrez et al. 2024, ref. 88), including consent language, so the primary manuscript is self-contained without requiring readers to chase an external citation.
- **ethics** — Cryo-EM maps and atomic models are promised for EMDB/PDB deposition but no accession numbers are provided, leaving the central structural evidence unverifiable at submission.
  - Proposed change: Provide EMDB and PDB accession numbers for both the 1C08- and 1G05-bound complexes in the Data Availability statement and Supplemental Table 3 prior to publication, or state explicitly that accessions are on hold pending review with reviewer-only access codes.
- **ethics** — The competing-interest disclosure states 'no financial competing interests related to the work' but then discloses a patent (US 8323668) held by J.M. Fleckenstein directly covering EtpA vaccine antigens.
  - Proposed change: Revise the Conflicts of Interest section to explicitly state that the patent covers use of EtpA-related antigens in vaccine development and therefore is directly related to the findings reported here, removing the initial blanket 'no financial competing interests' framing.
- **clarity** — The truncated construct rEtpA1-1086 is shown to be secreted but no biophysical data confirm it is properly folded, so loss of blood-group-A binding could reflect global misfolding rather than specific loss of the repeat-mediated binding function.
  - Proposed change: Add size-exclusion chromatography and/or thermal-shift (DSF) data comparing rEtpA1-1086 to full-length rEtpA in a new supplemental figure, and soften the claim in Results to note that efficient TPS-pathway secretion is consistent with, but not proof of, native folding.
- **rigor** — The Discussion asserts that glycosylation heterogeneity is 'exploited as a mechanism of immune evasion' when the data are equally consistent with affinity maturation toward stable, high-occupancy glycosylated epitopes.
  - Proposed change: Revise the Discussion paragraph (lines ~390-401) to present immune evasion and affinity-maturation-toward-conserved-glycans as two co-equal, untested hypotheses rather than asserting evasion as the conclusion, consistent with the hedged language already used in the Results section.
- **methodology** — EMPEM epitope counts (5-6 NTS, 3-4 CTR) are derived from visual inspection of 2D class averages without stated quantitative criteria for distinguishing unique epitopes.
  - Proposed change: Add a methods paragraph specifying the criteria used to call epitopes distinct (minimum angular/spatial separation of Fab density relative to the cryo-EM-defined domain boundaries, minimum particle count per 2D class) and revise the text from 'confidently identify' to 'estimate' throughout Results and Figure 5 legend.
- **literature** — Characterizing EtpC as a 'low-fidelity' glycosyltransferase is inconsistent with the manuscript's own MS data showing higher-fidelity, higher-occupancy modification at canonical N-X-S/T sequons versus non-canonical sites.
  - Proposed change: Revise terminology throughout to distinguish 'promiscuous' or 'relaxed sequence specificity' (referring to EtpC's tolerance of non-canonical sites and lack of strict discrimination among candidate sequons) from occupancy/fidelity at any given site, and add a clarifying sentence in Results acknowledging the canonical-sequon occupancy bias reported in Supplemental Figure 5.
- **literature** — The abstract and Results state that mAbs 'directly engage' glycan-containing epitopes, but the cryo-EM maps show clear density only for monohexose, not dihexose, and glycan contact is partly inferred from N-to-A mutagenesis rather than always directly visualized.
  - Proposed change: Soften abstract and Results wording to 'mAb epitopes encompass N-linked glycosylation sites, with monohexose density directly visualized in the cryo-EM maps and glycan contribution to affinity confirmed by N-to-A mutagenesis,' rather than asserting uniform direct visualization of glycan engagement.
- **data_analysis** — KD values for 1C08 and 1G05 (Supplemental Figure 1A) are never cited in the main text, so readers cannot assess whether antibody concentrations used in inhibition assays were appropriate relative to affinity.
  - Proposed change: Add the measured KD values for 1C08 and 1G05 to the Results text (Section 'Antibodies targeting EtpA repeats...') and to the Figure 1 legend, and state explicitly how antibody concentrations for inhibition assays were chosen relative to these KD values.
- **data_analysis** — Figure 1B legend states 'n=20 replicate fields from two independent experiments,' raising concern about pseudo-replication if fields rather than biological replicates are treated as the statistical unit.
  - Proposed change: Revise all functional-assay figure legends to explicitly separate biological replicate number (independent experiments) from technical replicate number (fields/wells per experiment), and re-run statistical tests treating biological replicate as the unit of analysis where applicable, reporting both in the legend.

## Disagreements
- **methodology** — The claim that the CTR domain is functionally required conflates epitope location (CTR vs NTS) with functional consequence (inhibition of adhesion), since only one mAb per domain per species was primarily tested.
  - Response: This pattern is not based on a single mAb pair but replicates independently across two antibody repertoires raised in different species by different immunization routes: two mouse mAbs (1G05 CTR-inhibitory, 1C08 NTS-non-inhibitory) and four human volunteer-derived mAbs (three NTS-binding, all non-inhibitory; one CTR-binding, 1F09, inhibitory). This cross-species, cross-repertoire convergence on the same domain-function relationship is precisely the kind of independent confirmation that argues against epitope location being a confound of a single clonal quirk.
  - Manuscript reference: Results, 'Antibodies targeting EtpA repeats interrupt bgA binding and ETEC adhesion': 'This pattern was also observed in monoclonals isolated from human volunteers challenged with ETEC H10407, with the three monoclonals that recognized the NTS domain (figure 1H) failing to inhibit EtpA-bgA interactions (figure 1I). Conversely the single monoclonal (1F09) that bound the CTR significantly inhibited EtpA interactions with BgA.'
- **rigor** — The title's claim that repeat modules and N-linked glycans 'define structure and antigenicity' is too strong, since the core β-helix fold is conserved across the TpsA family and not unique to EtpA.
  - Response: The title's claim is about EtpA's specific structure and antigenicity, not about inventing a novel fold class. We show the repeat modules specifically determine ligand engagement (bgA binding lost upon truncation) and antigenic outcome (CTR-binding mAbs neutralizing, NTS-binding mAbs not), and that glycans specifically define which epitopes are targeted and with what affinity (N849A/N1077A/N1305A mutation reduces 1G05 but not 1C08 binding). This is a defensible use of 'define' in the sense of determining functional and antigenic properties, distinct from a claim about novel secondary-structure architecture.
  - Manuscript reference: Results, 'Molecular Interactions with the mAbs1C08 and 1G05': 'A defining feature of both epitopes is the presence of a single centrally located N-linked hexose residue, N349 and N849... mutated EtpA asparagine residues at N849, N1077, and N1305 to alanine. Affinity of the 1G05 mAb for the mutant protein was significantly diminished while binding of 1C08 was unimpaired.'
- **novelty** — The framing that EMPEM reveals both 'neutralizing and decoy' epitopes is speculative because polyclonal sera were not functionally assayed for adhesion/bgA inhibition.
  - Response: We agree the polyclonal sera themselves were not functionally tested, but the manuscript already hedges this inference explicitly rather than asserting it as demonstrated fact, drawing the neutralizing/decoy distinction only by analogy to the domain-specific functional data from monoclonals (1G05/1F09 vs 1C08 and the three human NTS mAbs). No claim is made that specific EMPEM-identified epitopes have been individually validated as decoy or neutralizing.
  - Manuscript reference: Results, EMPEM section: 'suggesting that the polyclonal response to EtpA likely generates a variety of both neutralizing and decoy epitopes' and Abstract: 'suggesting that EtpA vaccination generates responses against neutralizing and decoy regions of the molecule.'

## Load-bearing critiques
- Absence of an explicit, verifiable IRB approval/protocol number and informed-consent disclosure in-text for the primary ETEC H10407 human challenge study that supplied plasmablasts for monoclonal antibody isolation
- Unconfirmed EMDB/PDB deposition and missing accession numbers for the cryo-EM maps and atomic models, which are the central verifiable evidence underlying all structural claims in the manuscript
