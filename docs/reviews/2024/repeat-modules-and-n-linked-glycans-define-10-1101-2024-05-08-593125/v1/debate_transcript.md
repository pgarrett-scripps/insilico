# Debate Transcript

## Advocate — round 1

EtpA is a priority ETEC vaccine antigen, and this manuscript delivers the first complete cryo-EM structure of the full-length adhesin (3.3–4.0 Å, validated by FSC, MolProbity, and EMRinger per the methodology and reproducibility reviewers), resolving its 29-nm β-helix and, critically, the structural basis for two functionally distinct mAbs. The functional logic is convergent and multi-method: truncation (EtpA1-1086) loses bgA binding across three independent assays (epithelial cells, ELISA, erythrocytes, Fig.1B-D), CTR-binding mAbs 1G05 and human 1F09 inhibit bgA binding and adhesion while NTS-binding mAbs do not (Fig.1E-I), and this pattern replicates across both mouse and human-derived antibodies—exactly the kind of independent-line convergence that makes a mechanistic claim credible even without every confound eliminated. The glycosylation analysis is a genuine, quantitatively rigorous contribution: 133 of 166 analyzable Asn sites mapped with explicit occupancy thresholds, cross-validated against native H10407 protein, revealing unprecedented density (~4% of residues) for a bacterial adhesin—novelty reviewer calls this

## Skeptic — round 1

The load-bearing objection isn't structural—it's compliance. The ethics reviewer identifies that the primary ETEC H10407 human challenge study (source of the plasmablasts underlying mAbs 1F09 and others, line 503) has no IRB protocol number, approval statement, or consent disclosure anywhere in this manuscript; only the secondary use of archived specimens (Protocol 201110126) is cited. Experimental infection of human volunteers with a toxin-producing pathogen is exactly the kind of high-risk work that cannot rely on a reader chasing an external citation (Guerrez et al. 2024) to confirm ethical oversight existed. This is flagged as a HARD deficiency, and I'd call it FIXABLE only if the authors can supply the actual protocol number now—if they cannot, it is fatal to publication regardless of structural quality.

On the advocate's convergence argument: I'll concede the mAb epitope-specific data (1G05/1F09 vs 1C08, independent of the truncation construct) is genuinely convergent evidence that CTR-binding antibodies inhibit function—that point is fairly won. But the truncation experiment itself, which the advocate leans on as one of three 'independent assays,' is not independent of a single confound: no CD, SEC, or thermal stability data confirm EtpA1-1086 is folded (clarity and data_analysis reviewers both raise this). That's FIXABLE, not fatal, but it means the truncation data shouldn't be counted as a fourth independent line—it's one construct interpreted two ways.

Finally, the rigor reviewer's point stands unaddressed by the advocate: 'immune evasion' is asserted in the Discussion as if demonstrated, when the data are equally consistent with affinity maturation toward stable glycan-bearing epitopes.

**Key points:**
- IRB/consent gap for the primary H10407 human challenge study is undocumented in-text — a compliance-blocking issue, not merely stylistic
- Fixable only if authors can supply the actual protocol number; as it stands this is a hard barrier to acceptance
- Truncation construct (EtpA1-1086) lacks fold-verification (CD/SEC/thermal), so it cannot be counted as an independent confirmatory assay alongside the mAb epitope data
- Concede: mAb-based epitope-function data (1G05/1F09 vs 1C08) is genuinely convergent and credible
- Immune evasion narrative remains asserted rather than demonstrated, per rigor reviewer — fixable via hedging, not yet fixed
