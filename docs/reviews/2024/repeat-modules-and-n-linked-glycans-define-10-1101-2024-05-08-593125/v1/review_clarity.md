# Clarity & Presentation Reviewer

## Summary
This is a well-presented structural and immunological study of EtpA, an ETEC adhesin vaccine candidate. The manuscript is clearly written, figures are informative and properly referenced, and the central claims about repeat-domain involvement in blood-group-A binding and glycosylation's role in antigenicity are well-supported by cryo-EM structures, mass spectrometry, and functional assays. Presentation is substantially above average for a structural biology paper of this scope and complexity. Minor friction points exist around terminology consistency and one figure legend that could be more explicit, but these do not impede understanding of the work or its significance.

## Strengths
- The cryo-EM structures are presented with appropriate resolution context (3.3–4 Å) and the atomic models are validated against map density with standard metrics (MolProbity, EMRinger), allowing readers to assess confidence in structural claims independently.
- Glycosylation data integrate three orthogonal approaches (MS occupancy mapping, cryo-EM density visualization, and functional mutation studies) with clear quantitative reporting (61 glycans per molecule, 94 of 133 sites ≥25% occupancy), making the heterogeneity claim concrete and verifiable.
- The manuscript maintains a coherent narrative arc from functional hypothesis (repeats mediate blood-group-A binding) through structural mechanism to immunological implications, with each result section clearly motivated by the preceding one.

## Weaknesses
- The central claim that 'the C-terminal repeat region of EtpA is essential to ETEC virulence' (line 177) rests on the observation that truncated EtpA (residues 1–1086) fails to bind blood-group-A glycans and that mAbs targeting the CTR inhibit this binding. However, the manuscript does not exclude the alternative that the truncation causes global misfolding rather than specifically removing a functional domain: no biophysical characterization (circular dichroism, thermal stability, size-exclusion chromatography) is provided to confirm that rEtpA1-1086 is folded. The immunoblot in Figure 1A shows the truncated protein is secreted, but secretion does not guarantee native fold. If the truncation is misfolded, the loss of binding reflects structural collapse, not loss of a discrete binding module. This would not invalidate the repeat region's importance, but it would leave the mechanism unresolved. Resolving this requires either (i) biophysical evidence that rEtpA1-1086 is folded, or (ii) a more granular truncation series to map the minimal functional unit.
- The claim that glycosylation 'contributes to overall EtpA stability' (line 287) is inferred from the observation of glycan–glycan and glycan–amino-acid interactions (556 Å buried surface area, 13 hydrogen bonds, 47 contacts) and the statement that 'EtpC is required for efficient secretion' (line 242). However, the manuscript provides no direct measurement of stability (melting temperature, aggregation kinetics, or proteolytic resistance) for wild-type versus glycosylation-deficient EtpA. The poor expression of EtpA without EtpC (line 242) could reflect impaired secretion, misfolding, or aggregation—these are not distinguished. The N→A mutations at positions 849, 1077, and 1305 (Supplemental Figure 9) reduce mAb 1G05 avidity but do not test global stability. A thermal shift assay or limited proteolysis comparing wild-type and glycosylation-null EtpA would directly test the stabilization claim.

## Questions
- Figure 3D states 'we were able to model hexose residues at 39' sites, but the text earlier claims 'confirmed N-linked glycosylation sites (NGS) on EtpA are asymmetrically distributed' (line 264)—is the asymmetry based on the 39 modeled sites or on the full MS occupancy map of 133 sites?
- The EMPEM analysis (Figure 5, lines 349–356) identifies '5–6 and 3–4 unique epitopes' on NTS and CTR domains respectively, but the confidence in this count and the method for distinguishing unique epitopes from overlapping Fab densities in 2D class averages is not explained—how were epitopes delineated?
- Supplemental Figure 7 shows native EtpA glycosylation from H10407 with 'much lower peptide counts' than rEtpA; does this lower coverage bias the comparison, and were the same occupancy thresholds (≥25%) applied to both datasets?
