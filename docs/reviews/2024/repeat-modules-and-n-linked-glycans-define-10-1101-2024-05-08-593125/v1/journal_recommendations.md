# Journal Recommendations

## As-is (current quality)
_No headline venue is realistic at the current quality — see Alternative._

## After required revisions
- **Nature Structural & Molecular Biology**
  - Fit: The paper's core contribution—cryo-EM structures of EtpA at 3.3–4.0 Å resolution with validated glycosylation mapping and functional domain-resolved antibody binding—is precisely the structural biology and molecular mechanism work this venue prioritizes. The integration of cryo-EM, mass spectrometry, and functional assays on a bacterial adhesin of public-health relevance aligns with the journal's scope.
  - Realism: After major revisions addressing IRB/consent disclosure, EMDB/PDB deposition, and hedging of immune-evasion claims, realistic for acceptance. The structural work is solid and the glycosylation map is novel; the ethics and reproducibility gaps are fixable, not fundamental. However, Nature Structural & Molecular Biology is highly selective; acceptance depends on the editors' judgment that the functional insights (CTR-mediated blood-group-A binding, glycan-epitope engagement) justify the structural contribution beyond incremental adhesin characterization.
- **eLife**
  - Fit: eLife's scope explicitly includes structural biology, immunology, and microbiology with emphasis on rigorous methodology, complete data transparency, and honest reporting of limitations. The paper's multi-method approach (cryo-EM, MS, functional assays, human-subject-derived mAbs) and the authors' willingness to concede and address every load-bearing critique fit eLife's editorial culture. The vaccine-development context and public-health relevance also align.
  - Realism: After major revisions, realistic for acceptance. eLife values papers that are technically sound and transparently reported over novelty-driven selectivity. The cryo-EM and glycosylation work meet that bar; the required fixes (IRB disclosure, deposition, hedging) are exactly what eLife's review process enforces. Acceptance is plausible if the revised manuscript demonstrates full compliance with data-availability and human-subjects standards.
- **PLOS Pathogens**
  - Fit: PLOS Pathogens prioritizes pathogen biology, host–pathogen interactions, and vaccine-relevant antigens. EtpA's role in ETEC adhesion and the blood-group-A lectin function are central to understanding pathogenesis; the cryo-EM structures and glycosylation map directly inform vaccine design. The journal's open-access model and emphasis on reproducibility and complete Methods align with the editor's required revisions.
  - Realism: After major revisions, realistic for acceptance. PLOS Pathogens has a higher acceptance rate than Nature-family journals and explicitly welcomes structural contributions to pathogen biology. The paper's functional integration (CTR repeats, glycan-epitope mapping, protective vs. non-protective mAbs) and the authors' demonstrated responsiveness to critique make this a solid fit. Acceptance is likely if IRB/consent, deposition, and hedging items are fully resolved.

## Alternative outlets
- **bioRxiv**
  - Fit: If the paper cannot reach peer-reviewed venues in the near term, bioRxiv is the appropriate preprint server for structural biology and microbiology. The cryo-EM maps, glycosylation data, and functional assays are complete and substantive enough to merit preprint visibility while the authors address the major-revision items (IRB disclosure, deposition, hedging).
  - Realism: Immediate posting is realistic; bioRxiv does not peer-review. However, posting before EMDB/PDB accessions are live would require a note that maps are under embargo and reviewer-access codes are available. This is a holding strategy, not a final venue.
- **Glycobiology**
  - Fit: If the structural biology venues prove competitive, Glycobiology is a specialized but well-regarded journal focused on carbohydrate–protein interactions and glycoprotein structure. The site-specific N-linked glycosylation mapping and the glycan-epitope engagement by mAbs are directly in scope; the journal values detailed MS and structural characterization of glycan modifications.
  - Realism: After major revisions, realistic for acceptance. Glycobiology is narrower than Nature Structural & Molecular Biology but has a higher acceptance rate and explicit interest in bacterial glycoproteins and immune recognition. The paper's glycosylation work is sufficiently novel and detailed to merit publication here if the broader structural/functional claims are hedged appropriately.
- **Infection and Immunity**
  - Fit: Infection and Immunity publishes mechanistic studies of pathogen–host interactions and vaccine-relevant antigens. The paper's focus on EtpA's role in ETEC adhesion, blood-group-A binding, and the functional mapping of protective antibody epitopes fits the journal's scope. The authors' prior work on EtpA is published here, establishing precedent.
  - Realism: After major revisions, realistic for acceptance. Infection and Immunity is more specialized than eLife or PLOS Pathogens but has a solid acceptance rate for well-executed structural and functional work. The cryo-EM structures and glycosylation map are novel contributions to ETEC biology; the journal's audience is the target community. Acceptance is plausible if the paper is positioned as a mechanistic advance in understanding adhesin-mediated pathogenesis and vaccine design.

## Notes
The editor's 'major' verdict reflects not a flaw in the structural work (which all reviewers affirmed as sound) but rather compliance gaps: missing IRB/consent disclosure for the primary human challenge study, unconfirmed EMDB/PDB deposition, an incomplete competing-interest statement, and overclaimed interpretations (immune evasion, truncation-construct folding) that outrun the data. These are all fixable and do not indicate misconduct. Once the required revisions are completed—especially IRB/consent disclosure, deposition confirmation, and hedging of the immune-evasion narrative—the paper becomes realistic for top-tier structural biology venues (Nature Structural & Molecular Biology, eLife) or specialized high-quality journals (PLOS Pathogens, Glycobiology, Infection and Immunity). The 'as_is' bucket is empty because the undeposited cryo-EM maps and undisclosed human-subjects provenance are non-negotiable for any peer-reviewed venue. Posting to bioRxiv before revision is not recommended, as it would lock in the compliance gaps and complicate later submission.
