# Ethics & Compliance Reviewer

## Summary
This structural biology and immunology study presents high-quality cryo-EM structures and mass-spectrometry glycosylation mapping of EtpA, an ETEC adhesin, with functional validation and antibody characterization. However, a critical ethical compliance gap undermines publication: human-derived monoclonal antibodies and plasmablasts from ETEC-challenged volunteers are described (lines 503–543) with IRB approval cited (Protocol 201110126) but the primary challenge study itself (H10407 strain, line 88) lacks any IRB approval statement, protocol number, or informed-consent disclosure. This is a HARD deficiency—human experimental challenge studies require explicit prospective ethical approval and consent documentation, not silence. The manuscript also lacks a formal data-availability statement and does not disclose whether cryo-EM maps and atomic models have been deposited as promised (line 450).

## Strengths
- Cryo-EM structures at 3.3–4.0 Å resolution are well-resolved with appropriate validation metrics (FSC, map-to-model fit, MolProbity scores) and comprehensive supplementary processing workflows.
- Site-specific mass-spectrometry glycosylation analysis is rigorous, with 133 of 196 asparagine residues mapped, occupancy quantified, and validation against native EtpA from H10407 strain (Supplemental Figure 7).
- Functional studies (erythrocyte pull-down, ELISA, bacterial adhesion, confocal microscopy) directly link C-terminal repeat domain to blood-group-A binding and neutralizing antibody epitopes, supporting the structural findings.

## Weaknesses
- **Primary ethical gap: The human experimental challenge study (ETEC H10407 strain, line 88) from which plasmablasts were isolated is described as conducted by "human volunteers challenged with ETEC strain H10407" (line 503) but carries no IRB approval statement, protocol number, or informed-consent disclosure in the main text or methods.** The manuscript cites only a secondary IRB approval (Protocol 201110126, line 514) for use of archived de-identified specimens, not approval of the original challenge study itself. Line 88 references "Guerrez et al. 2024" for challenge details, but the primary manuscript must stand alone: a reader cannot verify that the challenge study itself received prospective ethical oversight, that informed consent was obtained, or that adverse-event monitoring was in place. This is a HARD deficiency under the dual-use and human-subjects mandate—experimental infection studies are high-risk and require explicit disclosure of ethics approval and consent. The paper must either cite the original challenge-study protocol number and IRB approval, or state that it was conducted under a named prior study with full reference.
- **Dual-use and biosafety disclosure is absent.** The manuscript works with ETEC H10407, a pathogenic strain that produces heat-labile and heat-stable enterotoxins (lines 95–96, 1072–1082). No biosafety level, institutional biosafety committee approval, or containment statement is provided. Although the work is primarily structural and does not involve gain-of-function or enhanced pathogenicity, the handling of a toxin-producing pathogen in culture and the creation of recombinant EtpA glycoprotein require biosafety disclosure. This is a SOFT gap if the work was conducted under standard BSL-2 protocols, but the absence of any statement leaves it unclear.
- **Data-availability statement is missing.** Line 450 promises that "Cryo-EM maps and refined atomic models will be submitted to the Electron Microscopy Databank (EMDB) and Protein Data Bank (PDB) prior to publication," but the manuscript provides no EMDB or PDB accession numbers, no timeline, and no statement of whether these have been deposited or are available to reviewers. For a structural paper, this is a HARD compliance issue—readers cannot verify the structures without access to the maps and models.
- **Authorship and contribution transparency is incomplete.** The author-contributions statement (lines 421–423) lists 24 authors but does not specify roles (e.g., who designed the study, who conducted cryo-EM, who performed mass spectrometry, who wrote the manuscript). The statement "Z. Berndsen, J. Fleckenstein, A. Ellebedy, and A. Ward analyzed data and wrote the manuscript" is vague about who wrote which sections and who is responsible for which claims.
- **Competing-interests disclosure is narrow.** Line 439 states "The authors have no financial competing interests related to the work," but then discloses that J.M. Fleckenstein holds a patent (U.S. 8323668) on EtpA-related vaccine antigens assigned to the University of Tennessee Research Foundation. This is a material conflict: the patent directly covers the vaccine application of the protein studied here. The disclosure should explicitly state that Fleckenstein's patent may benefit from positive findings on EtpA immunogenicity and protective efficacy.
- **Informed-consent scope for antibody use is not stated.** Plasmablasts from human volunteers (lines 503–543) were used to generate monoclonal antibodies; the manuscript does not disclose whether the original informed consent covered this secondary use (antibody generation and structural characterization) or whether a separate consent or waiver was obtained.
- **Vulnerable-population safeguards are absent.** The clinical motivation emphasizes disease burden in young children in low-income regions (lines 98–104, 1015–1022), and the challenge study involved adult volunteers, but the manuscript does not address whether any pediatric or vulnerable populations were enrolled in the challenge study or whether special protections were in place.

## Questions
- What is the IRB protocol number and approval date for the primary ETEC H10407 challenge study from which plasmablasts were isolated (line 503)?
- Have cryo-EM maps and atomic models been deposited in EMDB and PDB, and if so, what are the accession numbers?
- Under what biosafety level and institutional biosafety committee approval was ETEC H10407 culture and recombinant EtpA production conducted?
- Did the original informed consent for the challenge study explicitly authorize use of plasmablasts for monoclonal-antibody generation and structural studies, or was a separate consent or waiver obtained?
- Please clarify the role of each author in study design, data analysis, and manuscript preparation, particularly for the four listed as having written the manuscript.
