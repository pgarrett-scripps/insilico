# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript's citations are generally accurate and foundational work is appropriately cited. The three load-bearing claims—that EtpA's C-terminal repeats mediate blood group A binding, that EtpC is a low-fidelity glycosyltransferase, and that glycosylation shields functional epitopes—are all supported by cited sources or new data presented here. However, each claim has a caveat requiring clarification: the "low-fidelity" characterization of EtpC is overstated relative to the MS data showing higher fidelity at canonical sequons; the sufficiency of the CTR domain alone for bgA binding is not directly tested; and the mechanism of direct glycan engagement by antibodies is inferred rather than directly visualized in the cryo-EM maps. A significant omission is recent structural work on bacterial N-glycosylation (Piniello et al. 2023, Nature Communications) that directly parallels the EtpC mechanism and should be cited for contextualization. Minor issues include incomplete comparison to the prior EtpA crystal structure (PDB 8CPK) and underexplored carbohydrate binding module literature. The work is sound and the contribution is clear, but these issues require clarification or tempering of language.

## Strengths
- Accurate attribution of HMW1 glycosylation precedent: The manuscript correctly cites Gross et al. (2008) and Grass et al. (2010) for unusual N-linked hexose modifications in HMW1A and accurately describes EtpC as a functional homolog with similar but more promiscuous specificity.
- Proper grounding in two-partner secretion literature: References to TPS domain structures (Clann et al. 2004, Baelen et al. 2013, Yeo et al. 2007, Weaver et al. 2009, Morse et al. 2012, Zambolin et al. 2016) are current and correctly characterize the β-helix fold and extra-helical inserts as conserved features.
- Blood group A–ETEC severity claim well-supported: The manuscript cites Kumar et al. (2018, J. Clin. Invest.) and Qadri et al. (2007) as primary sources for the epidemiological finding that blood group A individuals develop more severe ETEC diarrhea.

## Weaknesses
- The "low-fidelity" characterization of EtpC is overstated: The manuscript's own MS data (Fig. 3A, Supplemental Fig. 5) show that canonical N-X-S/T sequons are glycosylated with significantly higher fidelity than non-canonical sites, contradicting the "low-fidelity" framing and the claim that EtpC lacks "apparent selection for modification of distinct sequons."
- The immune evasion hypothesis is speculative and not directly supported: The data show that mAbs 1G05 and 1C08 target glycosylated epitopes (N849, N349), not that glycosylation prevents antibody binding. The alternative—that glycosylation is required for proper folding or stability—is equally consistent with the data and mentioned only briefly (line 287–294).
- The sufficiency of the CTR domain for blood group A binding is not directly tested: The truncation experiment shows that EtpA1-1086 does not bind blood group A efficiently, but the manuscript does not test whether the CTR domain alone is sufficient for bgA binding or measure the affinity of isolated CTR for blood group A glycans.
- Weaker individual Fab affinity (KD ~1 µM) raises questions about avidity: The cryo-EM structure shows that 1G05 can bind with up to 3 Fabs per molecule, but it is unclear whether the CTR repeats achieve high-avidity binding through multivalent interactions with clustered bgA glycans or whether additional N-terminal regions contribute.
- Not all CTR-targeting antibodies are established as protective: The polyclonal EMPEM data identify 3–4 epitopes on the CTR, but the manuscript does not establish that all CTR-targeting antibodies are protective or that some are decoys, which is important for vaccine design.
- Direct glycan engagement by antibodies is not substantiated by structural data: The statement "mAbs directly engage these unique glycan containing epitopes" (line 61) is not supported by the cryo-EM maps, which do not display clear density for the glycan moieties themselves at N349 and N849 (acknowledged in line 265: "We did not observe clear map density for dihexose modifications at any NGS").
- The reduction in avidity upon Asn-to-Ala mutation could reflect loss of a stabilizing glycan-protein interaction rather than direct antibody-glycan contact, leaving the mechanism unclear.
- Missing recent structural precedent: Piniello et al. (2023, Nature Communications) solved the structure of a soluble HMW1C-like N-glycosyltransferase and should be cited to contextualize the EtpC glycosylation findings and discuss whether EtpC's promiscuity is typical of this enzyme family.
- Incomplete comparison to prior EtpA structure: The manuscript cites the recent crystal structure of the EtpA NTS domain (PDB 8CPK, ref. 57) but does not discuss how the cryo-EM structure compares to or extends that work.
- Carbohydrate binding module (CBM) literature underexplored: The manuscript invokes "tandem repeats of carbohydrate binding modules" as a precedent but cites only three references and does not discuss whether the EtpA repeats are structurally or functionally analogous to characterized CBM families.
- Glycan-protein interaction analysis lacks statistical rigor: The identification of glycan-glycan and glycan-protein hydrogen bonds (Fig. 3E–F) is based on visual inspection and manual modeling without distance cutoffs, occupancy thresholds, or comparison to a null model.
- Human monoclonal antibody source and selection bias not discussed: The three mAbs from ETEC-challenged volunteers were isolated from a single challenge study; the manuscript does not discuss whether these represent the full repertoire of anti-EtpA responses or are enriched for particular specificities.
- Polyclonal epitope mapping resolution limited: The EMPEM analysis identifies "5–6 and 3–4 unique epitopes" with resolution of ~20 Å and reliance on manual feature identification, meaning these counts are approximate and may not capture the full complexity of the polyclonal response.
- Glycosylation occupancy interpretation conflates occupancy with functional importance: Sites with ≥75% occupancy are treated as "high-fidelity," but occupancy reflects the population average; individual EtpA molecules likely carry heterogeneous glycosylation, and the functional consequences are not experimentally tested.
- Citation of unpublished work: The manuscript cites "Cryo-EM maps and refined atomic models will be submitted to the EMDB and PDB prior to publication" and promises custom Python scripts at GitHub URLs, but their availability at submission is not confirmed.

## Questions
- Can the authors clarify whether the cryo-EM map density directly contacts the modeled glucose residues at N349 and N849, or whether the glycans are inferred from MS data and positioned based on the Asn location? If the latter, the claim of "direct engagement" should be softened.
- The statement that "it would be difficult for antibodies to target an entirely glycan-free epitope on the CTR domain" is based on local glycan density analysis with a 20 Å probe radius. What is the sensitivity of this conclusion to the probe radius, and have the authors tested whether any CTR-targeting antibodies in the polyclonal response avoid glycosylated regions?
- The agreement between rEtpA and native EtpA glycosylation profiles is noted as "close" (line 301), but the native sample has much lower peptide counts (Supplemental Fig. 7B). Can the authors quantify the agreement (e.g., Pearson correlation, number of sites with concordant occupancy bins) and discuss whether the recombinant system fully recapitulates native glycosylation heterogeneity?
- The manuscript states that 1G05's "weaker binding is compensated for by avidity effects" but does not measure the avidity directly (e.g., by surface plasmon resonance with immobilized EtpA). Is the avidity advantage inferred only from the ability to bind multiple Fabs per molecule, or has it been quantified?
