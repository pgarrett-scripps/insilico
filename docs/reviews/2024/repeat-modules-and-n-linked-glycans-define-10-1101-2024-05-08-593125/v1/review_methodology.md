# Methodology Reviewer

## Summary
The design robustly supports the main structural and antigenic claims through complementary cryo-EM, mass spectrometry, and functional assays. The work maps EtpA's architecture, glycosylation, and antibody epitopes with appropriate controls and replication. One load-bearing claim—that glycans directly mediate antibody recognition—rests on mutation data that is suggestive but limited in scope. Overall the design is sound for the conclusions stated, with minor gaps in experimental scope that do not undermine the core contribution.

## Strengths
- Cryo-EM structures of EtpA bound to two distinct mAbs at 3.3–4.0 Å resolution provide direct visual evidence for epitope location and glycan engagement, with independent complex preparations and particle stacks validating the key structural claims.
- Site-specific mass spectrometry with high occupancy thresholds (≥25% and ≥75%) and validation against native EtpA from H10407 establishes the glycosylation profile as a genuine feature of the protein, not an artifact of recombinant expression.
- Functional assays (erythrocyte pull-down, ELISA, bacterial adhesion, confocal microscopy) consistently show that C-terminal repeats are required for blood group A binding, with independent cell lines and multiple readouts supporting this claim.

## Weaknesses
- **Glycan-mediated antibody recognition (load-bearing claim).** The authors conclude that mAbs directly engage N-linked glycan epitopes (Abstract, Results, Discussion). Evidence: (1) cryo-EM maps show glycan density at N349 and N849 within the 1C08 and 1G05 epitopes; (2) mutation of N849, N1077, N1305 to alanine reduces 1G05 avidity (Supplemental Fig. 9A). However, the mutation experiment is limited: only three sites were mutated, all within one epitope region; the mutations reduce but do not abolish binding (avidity indices remain ~50–70% of wild-type); and critically, the authors do not test whether the glycans themselves are necessary by expressing EtpA without EtpC (the glycosyltransferase). The cryo-EM density is consistent with glycan contact but does not prove the glycan is the binding determinant rather than a structural scaffold that positions protein residues. An alternative explanation: the mutations disrupt local protein structure or electrostatics, and the glycans are passengers. To distinguish: express EtpA in the absence of functional EtpC, purify it, and test 1G05 binding by ELISA or surface plasmon resonance. If binding is abolished, glycans are necessary; if binding persists, they are not sufficient.
- **Protective epitope identification (load-bearing claim).** The authors claim that mAbs targeting the C-terminal repeat (CTR) domain are protective because they inhibit blood group A binding, while N-terminal secretion (NTS) domain mAbs are non-protective (Results, Discussion). Evidence: 1G05 (CTR-binding) inhibits EtpA–bgA interactions and ETEC adhesion; 1C08 (NTS-binding) does not. However, this design conflates two variables: epitope location (CTR vs. NTS) and functional consequence (inhibition of adhesion). The authors do not test whether other CTR-binding mAbs that do not inhibit adhesion exist, nor do they test whether NTS-binding mAbs could inhibit adhesion through a different mechanism (e.g., steric hindrance, conformational change). The negative stain EMPEM (Fig. 5) identifies 5–6 epitopes on the NTS and 3–4 on the CTR, but does not functionally characterize them. To strengthen: isolate and test all 7+ mAbs from vaccinated mice for both adhesion inhibition and epitope mapping, establishing whether CTR-binding is sufficient or merely correlated with protection.
- **Polyclonal epitope mapping by EMPEM (load-bearing claim).** The authors conclude that rEtpA vaccination generates responses against 'numerous distinct epitopes' on both NTS and CTR domains, with both 'neutralizing and decoy' epitopes (Abstract, Discussion). Evidence: negative-stain EM class averages showing Fabs bound at different positions (Fig. 5B). However, the method relies on visual identification of Fab positions in 2D class averages, which is inherently subjective and difficult to validate. The authors state they 'confidently identified' epitopes but provide no inter-rater reliability, no quantitative distance metrics between epitope clusters, and no independent corroboration (e.g., peptide scanning, alanine scanning, or competition assays). The claim that some epitopes are 'decoy' is speculative—no functional assay demonstrates that antibodies to those epitopes fail to protect. To validate: perform epitope binning by competition ELISA or surface plasmon resonance with the polyclonal sera, or use peptide-array or phage-display screening to map epitopes orthogonally.
- The truncation experiment (Fig. 1A–D) uses a single truncated construct (EtpA1-1086) to test repeat function; testing multiple truncation points would strengthen the claim that repeats are necessary and sufficient.
- Glycosylation occupancy is heterogeneous (ranging from 0% to >95% at different sites), but the functional consequence of this heterogeneity is not tested—e.g., do sites with low occupancy contribute less to antibody binding or immune evasion?
- The cryo-EM model includes 39 modeled glucose residues (through CTR2) but map resolution diminishes beyond that; the extent to which unmodeled glycans in CTR3–4 affect the conclusions is not discussed.
- Native EtpA glycosylation (Supplemental Fig. 7) is validated at only 48 of 166 potential sites due to low peptide detection, leaving ~70% of the glycosylation profile unconfirmed in the native protein.
- The study uses only one ETEC strain (H10407) for native EtpA validation; cross-strain glycosylation heterogeneity is not explored despite the authors' emphasis on EtpA conservation across geographically diverse ETEC isolates.

## Questions
- For the N849A/N1077A/N1305A triple mutant (Supplemental Fig. 9), were individual alanine substitutions tested to determine which glycosylation site(s) drive the avidity loss, or only the triple mutant?
- In the negative-stain EMPEM analysis, how were the 5–6 NTS and 3–4 CTR epitopes distinguished and counted—what distance threshold or visual criterion separated one epitope from another?
- Were the two mAbs (1C08, 1G05) tested for cross-competition to confirm they recognize truly distinct epitopes, or is non-competition assumed from the cryo-EM structures alone?
