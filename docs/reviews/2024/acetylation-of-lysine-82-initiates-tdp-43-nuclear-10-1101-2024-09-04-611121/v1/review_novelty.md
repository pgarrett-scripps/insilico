**Novelty & Contribution Review**

**Overall take.** The contribution is real but its headline novelty is overstated relative to work the manuscript itself cites. The central mechanistic claim — that K82 acetylation abolishes TDP-43–importin-α1 binding and nuclear import — was already reported by Ko et al. 2024 (ref 30), which the manuscript acknowledges only in passing ("consistent with a recent report"). The genuinely novel elements are the proteasome-activity trigger linking aging to TDP-43 mislocalization, and the antibody-based detection of endogenous K82 acetylation in sALS tissue with its soluble/insoluble distribution. Repositioned around those, this is a solid contribution; as framed, the abstract leads with a finding that is not first. My searches (find_related_work and search_preprints across ~10 query formulations) returned mostly noise and no preempting preprint, so I found no unacknowledged priority; the preemption I flag is from the manuscript's own reference list.

**Load-bearing claims.**

**1. "Acetylation at lysine 82 is sufficient to abolish TDP-43 binding to importin-α1 and subsequent nuclear import" (abstract).** This is the paper's central mechanistic claim, and it is not novel. The manuscript's own ref 30 (Ko et al. 2024, *J Mol Biol* 168751, "Single acetylation-mimetic mutation in TDP-43 Nuclear Localization Signal disrupts importin alpha1/beta signaling") already reported that the K82Q acetylation mimic disrupts importin-α1/β signaling and nuclear import. The manuscript's delta over Ko et al. — the proteasome-inhibition trigger, the identification of *endogenous* (not mimetic) acetylation by mass spec, and the disease-tissue antibody detection — is real and worth publishing, but the abstract and title present the K82-acetylation-disrupts-import finding as the discovery rather than as a confirmation with a new upstream trigger. The discussion should state the delta over Ko et al. explicitly.

**2. "Acetylation of lysine 82 within the TDP-43 cNLS is found in postmortem samples from sporadic ALS."** Partially preempted by Kametani et al. 2016 (ref 34), which detected K82 acetylation by mass spec in ALS postmortem brain (1 of 2 samples). The manuscript's delta is real — antibody-based detection in 6/6 sALS samples, and the novel observation that K82 acetylation appears in both soluble and insoluble fractions while S409/410 phosphorylation is insoluble-only, suggesting acetylation precedes phosphorylation. But the claim as worded ("is found in postmortem samples") is not first; it should be framed as an extension to a larger cohort with a new reagent and a new distribution finding.

**3. "Reduced proteasome activity initiates TDP-43 nuclear loss of function" via cNLS modification.** This is the genuinely novel element. I found no prior work linking age-related proteasome decline to TDP-43 mislocalization through NLS acetylation. This is the strongest novelty claim and should be the headline of the paper.

**Sweep.**

- The K82 + (K95 or K97) bipartite dissection (Fig 4G–H) is a useful refinement of the structural work of Doll et al. 2022 (ref 19), but incremental.
- The claim that TDP-43 is "the protein whose nuclear localization is most sensitive to reduced proteasome activity" (Fig 1E) is a striking quantitative claim whose novelty depends on the TMT mass-spec analysis — that is rigor's call, but it is the kind of claim that should be flagged for the data-analysis reviewer.

- The "first to" language is appropriately absent — the manuscript does not claim priority over Ko et al. — but the abstract's emphasis still inflates the novelty of the K82-import finding.

**Strengths.**
- The proteasome-trigger → NLS-acetylation → import-failure axis is a coherent, testable mechanism that plausibly connects aging to TDP-43 proteinopathy.
- The antibody-based detection of endogenous K82 acetylation in 6/6 sALS motor cortices, with the soluble/insoluble distribution, is a genuine extension over prior mass-spec detection.
- The bipartite NLS dissection identifying K82 plus K95/K97 as the importin-α1 determinants is a clean, systematic mutagenesis design.
