# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The citation record is largely sound: the directly-competing prior work (Ko et al. 2024, which already showed a single acetylation-mimetic mutation in the TDP-43 NLS disrupts importin α1/β signaling) is cited and correctly characterized as "consistent with a recent report," and the foundational importin-recognition work (Doll et al. 2022) is cited and verified. Two points need attention: one load-bearing attribution I could not verify from metadata, and one possible completeness gap I could not fully rule out because my related-work searches repeatedly errored. Neither is a demonstrated fabrication or misattribution, so this is minor revision rather than rejection.

## Strengths
- The directly-competing Ko et al. 2024 result is cited and accurately described rather than omitted to inflate novelty.
- Foundational citations (Doll 2022 for importin-α1/β recognition; Lee 2006 for PY-NLS/importin-β2; Winton 2008 for the cNLS) are the correct primary sources.
- Self-citation (Yu 2021, Polymenidou 2011, Melamed 2019, Klim 2019) is proportionate and each supports the claim it is attached to.

## Weaknesses
- The independent human-tissue corroboration of the central finding rests on an attribution I could not verify. The Discussion states: "Our observation is supported by a mass spectrometry analysis of TDP-43 post translational modifications which found K82 acetylation in one out of two ALS postmortem samples34" (Kametani et al., Sci Rep 2016, PMID 26980269). I confirmed the paper exists and is a mass-spec analysis of accumulated TDP-43 in ALS brains, but I could not confirm from its metadata that it specifically identified acetylation at K82 (rather than another lysine) in one of two samples. Because this is the only independent human-tissue corroboration of the paper's central claim, please confirm the specific residue and sample count, or soften the sentence to match what Kametani actually reported.
- The manuscript presents "TDP-43 mislocalization is triggered by reduced proteasome activity" as a new finding without, as far as I can see, citing prior reports of proteasome-inhibitor-induced TDP-43 cytoplasmic redistribution/aggregation. If any directly competing report already showed this, it should be cited and distinguished. I could not complete the verification search (tool errors), so I flag this as a question rather than a demonstrated omission.

## Questions

- Which prior reports of proteasome-inhibitor-induced TDP-43 mislocalization were considered, and why are they not cited or distinguished from the present finding?
