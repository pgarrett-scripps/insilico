# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The citation record is largely sound — attributions for the aging-proteasome-decline claim, the passive nuclear-export claim, and the ALS-mutation claim all resolve to appropriate primary sources, and the directly competing Ko et al. 2024 work is cited rather than suppressed. However, two directly relevant works on TDP-43 nuclear import are omitted, and the manuscript's novelty framing against Ko et al. is under-delineated. These are fixable but substantive citation defects, warranting major revision.

## Weaknesses
- The manuscript's central claim — that modification of a lysine within the TDP-43 cNLS controls its nuclear import — omits the directly competing "Genetically encoded lysine photocage for spatiotemporal control of TDP-43 nuclear import" (Shadish et al., *Biophysical Chemistry* 2024, PMID 38290242), which already demonstrated lysine-dependent control of TDP-43 nuclear import. This is directly competing work bearing on the same mechanism, and its absence inflates the apparent novelty of the K82-acetylation finding. (HARD)
- The manuscript's importin-α1 mechanism and its therapeutic framing omit "Nuclear import receptors are recruited by FG-nucleoporins to rescue hallmarks of TDP-43 proteinopathy" (2022), a directly competing line showing that nuclear-import-receptor engagement rescues TDP-43 proteinopathy — directly relevant to the claim that disrupted importin engagement drives the phenotype. (HARD)
- The manuscript states that acetylation at K82 "is sufficient to abolish TDP-43 binding to importin-α1 and subsequent nuclear import" as a finding, but Ko et al. 2024 (ref 30) already established the K82Q-mimic disruption of importin-α1/β signaling. The manuscript acknowledges this in one clause but never states what it adds beyond Ko et al. (the endogenous PTM detection in sALS tissue, the proteasome-inhibition trigger) — the novelty reviewer's verdict, but the citation record should make the incremental contribution explicit rather than leaving it implicit.
- Ref 34 (Kametani et al. 2016) is cited for "K82 acetylation in one out of two ALS postmortem samples"; I could not verify the residue and count from metadata alone — please confirm the exact finding.
- The reference list as parsed contains garbled entries (e.g., ref 24 trailing "39", ref 26 lowercase journal name), so I could not confirm every entry resolves; please confirm no fabricated or mis-typed entries survive the conversion.
- Citations are reasonably current (2022, 2024 present), so currency is not a concern.
- The aging-proteasome-decline and passive-export attributions are accurate and appropriately primary-sourced.

## Questions
- In Kametani et al. 2016, what exactly is reported — which residue(s) and how many of how many ALS samples — that supports the "one out of two" K82-acetylation claim?
- Were the Shadish et al. 2024 photocage paper and the 2022 FG-nucleoporin import-receptor rescue paper considered, and if so why are they not cited?
