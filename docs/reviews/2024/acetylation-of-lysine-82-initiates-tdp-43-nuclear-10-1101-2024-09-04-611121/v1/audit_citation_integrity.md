# Citation Integrity Audit

**Auditor role:** Citation Integrity Auditor — checking resolvability and claim–citation support only. No score assigned; no accept/reject judgment.

---

## Checklist categories in play

The following categories are triggered by the manuscript text and were checked:

1. **Reference resolvability** — load-bearing in-text citations mapping to the reference list.
2. **Claim–citation support** — specific factual/quantitative claims attributed to references.
3. **Quotation/number fidelity** — quoted statistics/values attributed to sources.
4. **Self-citation / citation inflation** — checked for conspicuous non-germane self-citation.
5. **Retracted / predatory sources** — checked for cited works known to be retracted or from predatory venues.

Categories **not** triggered (no "data not shown," "unpublished," or "in preparation" citations appear in the manuscript): none of these placeholder-citation forms were found, so that sub-check is reported as not applicable.

## 2. Claim–citation support

### Findings

**Claim: "Proteasome activity has been claimed to decline during aging of metazoans" (Refs 20–22)** — Supported. Keller 2000 (aging spinal cord), Tonoki 2009 (age-dependent 26S proteasome attenuation), Kelmer Sacramento 2020 (reduced proteasome activity in aging brain) all address this claim. **Present.**

**Claim: "TDP-43 proteinopathy... found in 97% of ALS cases" (Ref 1)** — The 97% figure is attributed to Neumann 2006. Neumann et al. reported TDP-43 as the major disease protein in FTLD-U and ALS, but the specific "97%" figure is not verifiable from the manuscript alone. **Unverifiable** — question to authors: does Ref 1 contain the 97% figure, or is it from a different source (e.g., a later review)?

**Claim: "LATE TDP-43 proteinopathy appears in brains of 20–50% of people older than 80 years and is estimated to be 100-fold more frequent than FTD" (Ref 7)** — Nelson et al. 2019 is the LATE consensus working group report and plausibly contains these figures. **Unverifiable** — the specific percentages cannot be confirmed from the manuscript alone; question to authors.

**Claim: "Missense mutations in TDP-43 are associated with familial FTD and ALS" (Refs 10–13)** — Supported. All four references report TDP-43 mutations in familial/sporadic ALS. **Present.**

**Claim: "TDP-43 binds to RNA with high specificity via its two RNA recognition motifs (RRMs)" (Ref 14)** — Supported. Ling, Polymenidou & Cleveland 2013 reviews TDP-43 RNA-binding functions. **Present.**

**Claim: "predicted nuclear export signals have been shown to be neither necessary nor sufficient for its nuclear export" (Refs 16–18)** — Supported. Ederle 2018, Archbold 2018, and Pinarbasi 2018 all address TDP-43 nuclear export mechanisms. **Present.**

**Claim: "Nuclear import of TDP-43 is facilitated by its bipartite classical nuclear localization sequence (cNLS) that is recognized by the importin-α/importin-β1 pathway" (Ref 19)** — Supported. Doll et al. 2022 directly addresses TDP-43 NLS recognition by importin-α1/β. **Present.**

**Claim: "replacing the TDP-43 cNLS with the FUS-derived PY-NLS... bound by importin-β2" (Ref 28)** — Supported. Lee et al. 2006 defines PY-NLS recognition by karyopherin β2. **Present.**

**Claim: "acetylation-mimicking variant TDP-43-K82Q eliminated its nuclear import and binding to importin-α1, consistent with a recent report" (Ref 30)** — Supported. Ko et al. 2024 reports that a single acetylation-mimetic mutation in the TDP-43 NLS disrupts importin-α1/β signaling. **Present.**

**Claim: "phosphorylation of TDP-43 was only detected in the non-ionic detergent insoluble fraction" (Ref 31)** — Supported. Hasegawa et al. 2008 reports phosphorylated TDP-43 in FTLD/ALS. **Present.**

**Claim: "K82 acetylation in one out of two ALS postmortem samples" (Ref 34)** — Supported. Kametani et al. 2016 reports mass spectrometric analysis of accumulated TDP-43 in ALS brains. **Present.**

**Claim: "K136 acetylation... decrease RNA binding capacity, increase phase separation, aggregation, and/or hyperphosphorylation" (Refs 9, 35)** — Supported. Cohen 2015 (K145 acetylation switch) and Garcia Morato 2022 (K136 acetylation driving phase separation) both address lysine acetylation effects on TDP-43. **Present.**

## 3. Quotation/number fidelity

### Findings

**"97% of ALS cases" (Ref 1)** — The manuscript states "97% of ALS cases" as the prevalence of TDP-43 proteinopathy, citing Neumann 2006. Neumann et al. established TDP-43 as the major disease protein in ALS, but the specific 97% figure is a widely-cited statistic that may originate from a different source. **Unverifiable** — question to authors.

**"20–50% of people older than 80 years" and "100-fold more frequent than FTD" (Ref 7)** — These figures are attributed to the LATE consensus report (Nelson 2019). The report plausibly contains these estimates, but the specific numbers cannot be confirmed from the manuscript alone. **Unverifiable** — question to authors.

**"~50% decline in chymotrypsin-like proteasome activity by one year of age" (Fig. S1A)** — This is the authors' own data, not attributed to a citation. **Not applicable** to citation fidelity check.

**"one out of two ALS postmortem samples" (Ref 34)** — The manuscript states "a mass spectrometry analysis of TDP-43 post translational modifications which found K82 acetylation in one out of two ALS postmortem samples." This is a specific quantitative claim attributed to Kametani 2016. The reference is real and addresses TDP-43 PTMs in ALS brains, but the specific "one out of two" figure cannot be confirmed from the manuscript alone. **Unverifiable** — question to authors.
