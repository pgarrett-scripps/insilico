# Journal Recommendations

## As-is (current quality)
- **Neurobiology of Disease**
  - Fit: The paper's core topic — a post-translational mechanism (K82 acetylation) driving TDP-43 nuclear loss-of-function and cytoplasmic accumulation in ALS — sits squarely in this journal's scope of molecular mechanisms of neurodegeneration, and the manuscript already contains a complete mechanistic arc (proteasome inhibition → cNLS modification → importin-α1 disruption → mislocalization → stathmin-2 loss).
  - Realism: Realistic at current quality: the journal tolerates strong single-mechanism studies, though reviewers will likely still press for the data-deposition and antibody-validation gaps before acceptance.
- **Journal of Biological Chemistry**
  - Fit: The mechanistic depth — quantitative PTM mass spectrometry, peptide–importin binding assays, and systematic lysine-to-arginine mutagenesis mapping of the bipartite cNLS — is a natural fit for a journal that values rigorous biochemical dissection of a protein–protein interaction.
  - Realism: Moderate-to-good odds as-is; JBC reviewers will accept the mechanistic core but will require deposition of the MS data and resolution of the acetylation-vs-ubiquitination ambiguity at K79/K82/K84.
- **Molecular Neurobiology**
  - Fit: The ALS/TDP-43 proteinopathy topic and the combination of cell-biology, proteomics, and patient-tissue evidence fit this journal's broad molecular-neuroscience scope.
  - Realism: Reasonable acceptance odds at current quality given the journal's tolerance for multi-modal descriptive-plus-mechanistic studies, though the sALS tissue claims would benefit from the requested controls.

## After required revisions
- **Molecular Neurodegeneration**
  - Fit: This is the leading specialty journal for ALS/FTD and TDP-43 biology, and the manuscript addresses a field-defining question (what initiates TDP-43 nuclear loss-of-function). Once the PTM/TMT data are deposited, the ac-K82 antibody is validated against K79/K84-acetylated peptides, and the causal language is scaled to the correlational evidence, the mechanistic depth and patient-tissue confirmation make it a strong fit.
  - Realism: Good odds after the required revisions; this is the most natural high-fit home for the work once rigor and reproducibility concerns are resolved.
- **Acta Neuropathologica**
  - Fit: The detection of K82 acetylation in sALS motor cortex, with soluble-vs-insoluble fractionation and correlation with phospho-TDP-43 load, is exactly the kind of neuropathological-plus-mechanistic evidence this journal prizes; the human-tissue component is a particular strength here.
  - Realism: Realistic after revision, contingent on the competing-peptide control and blinding for the ac-K82 tissue immunoblots and on depositing the supporting data.
- **Nature Communications**
  - Fit: The combination of a novel mechanistic claim (K82 acetylation as an initiator of TDP-43 nuclear loss), quantitative proteomics, and validation in human ALS tissue fits this journal's appetite for broad-impact, multi-technique molecular studies.
  - Realism: Moderate odds after full revision; the panel's concerns about dose consistency, antibody validation, and overclaimed causality would need to be fully resolved, and the title's 'initiates' claim toned down.

## Alternative outlets
- **bioRxiv**
  - Fit: As an open-access preprint server, bioRxiv lets the authors post the current version immediately, making the data and claims publicly inspectable and citable while the required revisions (deposition, antibody validation, ethics statement) are completed.
  - Realism: Immediate and unconditional; this is the natural fallback to establish priority and public availability regardless of journal outcome.
- **Neurobiology of Aging**
  - Fit: The age-related decline in proteasome activity framing and the LATE/aging context of TDP-43 proteinopathy fit this journal's focus on aging-related neurodegeneration, and it is more tolerant of the current correlational scope.
  - Realism: Moderate odds as a fallback; the aging/proteasome angle is a good fit even if the causal 'initiates' claim is not fully defensible.
- **Frontiers in Molecular Neuroscience**
  - Fit: A broad molecular-neuroscience outlet that accepts mechanistic cell-biology and proteomics studies of neurodegeneration with a lower bar on multi-modal rigor than the headline venues.
  - Realism: Reasonable fallback odds at current quality, though the same deposition and validation requests would likely be raised.

## Notes
The manuscript is a wet-lab molecular/cell-biology and neuropathology study, not a computational one, so the In Silico overlay format is a somewhat unusual fit; the paper would normally target a molecular-neuroscience journal. Fit confidence is high for the specialty venues (Molecular Neurodegeneration, Acta Neuropathologica) and moderate for the broader ones. The single most important revision for any venue is depositing the TMT nuclear-proteome and PTM mass-spectrometry data, since the panel flagged that the central quantitative evidence is currently uninspectable.
