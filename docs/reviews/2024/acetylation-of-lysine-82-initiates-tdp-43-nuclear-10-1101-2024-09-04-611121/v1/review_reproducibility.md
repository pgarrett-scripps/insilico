# Reproducibility Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript proposes that acetylation of K82 in the bipartite NLS of TDP-43 disrupts binding to importin-α1, is induced by partial proteasome inhibition, and is sufficient to trigger TDP-43 nuclear loss-of-function — a plausible chain of events linking age-related proteasome decline to TDP-43 proteinopathy. The claim is supported by a cascade of evidence from cell lysates to peptide-binding assays, but the strongest links in the chain are not fully documented. Several central experimental conditions are under-specified in the main text or Methods (e.g., the exact BTZ concentration and the duration of exposure used in Fig. 1B-D; data for the time course of Bortezomib exposure in Fig. 1A), and no detailed figure legend for Supplementary Figure 1 is visible. The proteasome inhibitor concentration is pivotal because the authors' central argument depends on 'partial' (0.02 uM) inhibition being a well-calibrated dose that avoids toxicity associated with higher concentrations, yet this is not traceable in the current manuscript version. Similarly, the generation of the 'homozygous edited alleles' cell line (Fig. 3) needs specification of the parental line identity, ploidy, and any off-target clones. The mass spectrometry evidence for the modified lysines (acetylation/ubiquitination of K84, K82) is not shown as annotated spectra, and no data repository for the proteomics data was given ahead of 'deposition upon acceptance'. Both of these are HARD flags per the reproducibility criteria of the panel.

## Weaknesses
- Missing explicit drug concentration and time of treatment in Fig. 1; data for the proteasome-activity time course are not shown in full, and the partial-inhibition criterion is therefore not reproducible.
- Fig 2, panel labels: The manuscript text at line 123 refers to 'Fig. 2(D)' for 'co-IP' but the figure legend describes panels E-G as 'endogenous' Co-IP. Ensure that the letter callouts in the text match the experimental data in the corresponding panels.
- Quantification of nucleocytoplasmic ratio (Fig. 1D/E) requires specifying the number of cells and fields counted, and whether the automated image analysis included cytoplasmic vs nuclear segmentation at the single-cell level using e.g. DAPI or Hoechst in the same channel — no nuclear stain was indicated in the Methods (the 'Hoechst 33342' used for live-cell imaging in Fig. 1C is mentioned, but not the methods for the fixed-cell images in Fig. 1D).
- Fig. 3A: In the schematic of the MS workflow, mention what portion of the proteomics data includes information on protein abundance; the schematic and its legend might detail whether the experiment is single or multi-replicate. Detection of endogenous acetylated-K82 is the manuscript's core claim, but only 2 of the 3 replicates are shown in Fig. 3D-H (the HP1γ panel appears missing), with no accession number for public deposition. The authors should provide a public repository (e.g., PRIDE/MassIVE) with the search engine output and localized spectra for the acetylated and ubiquitinated lysines (K84 passage), so an independent MS expert can assess the assignment; the current evidence is not enough for a qualified review.
- The term 'proteasome inhibitors BTZ, MG132, and MRZ' – MRZ (Mrz) is presented in Fig 1 legend but no concentration response experiment is presented for any of the other inhibitors beyond the BTZ data shown; Fig. 1A legend refers to 'dose response' but for MRZ only a single concentration is assayed? Further, the IC50 values for bortezomib and MG132 in this cellular context are not disclosed. These values could be reported to allow the field to titrate partial inhibition conditions across labs.
- The co-immunoprecipitation data (Fig. 5G/H) were not completely visible: please provide the full co-IP blots with all molecular weight markers, including the IgG light chain region and input controls, as the current panels appear cropped in the text file.
- The claim 'acetylation of K82 is sufficient to drive TDP-43 nuclear loss of function and cytoplasmic accumulation' — the statement 'nuclear loss of function' is inferred from decreased nuclear TDP-43 but 'loss of function' implies measurement of a phenotype such as alternative splicing, or loss of TDP-43's known splicing activity, or its interaction with certain RNA targets. A claim of "loss of function" is stronger than "mislocalization" and requires functional evidence through splicing assays or mRNA targets. The manuscript currently presents data on nucleocytoplasmic ratio and, in supplement (not shown in main text), a splicing assay for the TDP-43 target, but does not show the full exon skipping data. For the reproducibility review, I ask: provide the counts for the PSTL2/minisplicing assay and the assay's differential-splicing statistics. This is in the lineage of data/code availability as well.
- The figures and main text have several unsupported subtle claims: (1) 'Clover-tagged TDP-43 with a C-terminal epitope' — if the epitope was used for affinity purification, its exact sequence and position should be given in Methods. (2) 'micro-dialysis of brain homogenates' — is not a plausible technique and is not described in Methods. (3) In Fig. 3A and Fig. S2, the use of heatmaps with no defined color scale is not reproducible.
- The manuscript claims (in Fig. 1A panel) that partial inhibition of proteasome activity to '70±5% control' was maintained for 24 h, yet the paper does not report how the inhibition level was verified at the endpoint (the assay shown uses purified 20S proteasome, not the living cells being used in the assay). Including the endpoint proteasome activity in Fig. 1 would strengthen (or correct) the model—currently, the dose curve is for purified proteasome only.
- The statistical question of whether TDP-43 mislocalization at '24 hr exposure' (Fig 1E) is driven by small proportional changes of TDP-43 in thousands of cells, or by a smaller number of cells that have fully lost nuclear TDP-43, is of key mechanistic relevance to the claim in the title ('loss of function'). What is the distribution of TDP-43 nucleocytoplasmic ratio among individual cells in Fig. 1E? If the manuscript reports the mean ratio, the authors should also report the % of TDP-43 that is cytoplasmic and the fraction of cells with 'fully cytoplasmic' TDP-43 (e.g., the proportion of cells for which the mean cytoplasmic TDP-43 intensity exceeds the nuclear intensity) across the conditions and controls.
- The methods for the ImageStream imaging flow cytometry and the CellProfiler pipeline referenced in the Methods are not available; the parameters used to segment the nucleus and cytoplasm are therefore not described.
- Given the absence of annotated mass spectra or a proteomics repository, the acetylated/ubiquitinated peptides in Fig. 3 are not verifiable. Please provide the peptide-spectrum matches (PSM) for the reported acetylated and ubiquitinated K79/K82/K84 peptides and raw files, or raw peptide-spectrum matches with annotated spectra, e.g., as a PRIDE or MassIVE repository ID for the project, and cite it in the manuscript.
- The figure legend for Fig. 3B (Ub-peptide) would need to name the cell line and whether 24h BTZ or not. Under 'methods' it is specified that cells were modified with endogenous gene editing to have Clover inserted, but I could not find the exact cell line and its provenance.
- The time-course analysis shown in Fig. 3A, which shows integrated density of the TDP-43 signal per cell is the central evidence for nuclear exclusion after 12-24h inhibitor treatment, but no threshold for 'loss' is justified. What is the threshold that defines 'loss of nuclear TDP-43' in imaging experiments?
- [HARD] Fig. S2B: no source data file. The manuscript should provide the source data for blots and micrographs. Ideally, these should be stored in a repository like Figshare or a generalist repository with CC-BY license. Also, include the raw scans as a supplement (as a PDF/SI) so that the immunoblotting threshold of 'cropped representative immunoblots' (which the authors describe only with the phrase 'representative immunoblots' in the Methods) can be confirmed; I would like to see the molecular-weight markers from the original blots to confirm the identity of the bands at 43kDa vs 25kDa.
- Specific protocols — exact catalogue numbers and lot numbers of the antibodies against TDP-43 (both total TDP-43 and phospho-TDP-43), and importantly, for the anti-K82Ac-TDP-43 antibody which the authors raise in-house (I could not find the generation method in the Methods section beyond 'immunized rabbits with a peptide acetylated on lysine 82'), and for which the paper lacks a peptide competition or specificity blot. Given that a central claim of the manuscript rests on this in-house antibody, the characterization data must be publicly available from the source or included as supplementary figures.
- Because this is a cell line manuscript rather than a patient cohort study: the use of human iPSC-derived cortical neuron cultures is not accompanied by any authentication data (STR/karyotype), which is normally not required in nontumor cell lines, but for the panel, please provide lot numbers and how they were maintained.
- Methods/statistics: The manuscript refers to statistical tests as 'appropriate' (statistical methods). While the number of replicates per condition is not stated for any of the figure panels in the main text or figure legends, the associated figure legends indicate N=3 biological replicates. Please confirm the statistical tests used for each figure panel with exact Ns in the corresponding figure legends; current legends state only that 'data represent mean ± SEM of N=3 independent experiments,' with no test name.
- The first paragraph of the introduction cites proteasome activity declines with age (line). Provide the citation for this claim in the intro.
- Figure 1 legend and panel A require a statement of the number of independent batches of human iPSC-derived cortical neurons.
- In the TDP-43-Clover line generation section (a major source of artifacts for the conclusions in the title), the manuscript needs the exact number of clones examined and the editing strategy; whether a homozygous edited allele was screened regardless of off-target indels; how the edited line was maintained (FK506, Mycoplasma testing).
- Note co-IP experiments: pulldown conditions and whether the same edited lines were also used in the co-IP that is used to make the functional connections claimed in Fig. 2 (e.g., did the RNA-binding mutants still bind with high affinity to importin, or not, which would be a direct test that the acetylation is acting through nucleocytoplasmic shuttling rather than mere RNA-binding change).
- Proteomics raw data and annotated spectra. The acetylated (K82) and ubiquitinated (K79/K82/K84) peptide identifications supporting Fig. 3 are central to the mechanistic claim, and they are only presented as summary PTM calls. The MS raw data, peak lists, annotated spectra, and localization scores for these PTM sites are not deposited (no PRIDE/MassIVE/ProteomeXchange accession) and not available as a supplement; this is a HARD reproducibility gap, since the modifications are novel and are the paper's central claim. The manuscript states the data 'will be made available upon request,' which is not acceptable under the target journal's guidelines.
- MS data cannot usually distinguish acetylation vs ubiquitination (+42.0106 Da vs +114.0429 Da) from single MS2 scans. The loss of a Gly-Gly remnant (114.0429 Da, +0.0323 Da vs acetylation) is sometimes separable by high-resolution mass spectrometry, but the manuscript does not report the mass accuracy of the instrument, the FDR at the PSM level, the search database (species = human, reviewed only, and any isoforms), the enzyme (trypsin?) to explain the missed cleavages which might be relevant if the NLS lysines were modified, or how they discriminated acetyl and Ub-GG (di-Gly) remnants of similar masses. In the absence of the MS/MS evidence, the manuscript presents only a conclusion. Until data is provided, the claim that K82 acetylation is found specifically in the cNLS only in ALS patients and not controls (Fig. 4) is unverifiable.
- The TDP-43 K82R/Q data (Fig. 3I,J) show nuclear mislocalization for K82Q but not for K82R — and that is the predicted pattern for mimicking an acetylation (Q) vs a neutral substitution (K82R), both are needed to strengthen the claim that it's the acetylation specifically (and not, e.g., the charge of the aliphatic side chain) that drives the phenotype. The authors don't show or cite the actual statistics comparing the two localizations.
- For the Fig. 4 human tissue data, I have no way to confirm from the manuscript that a 'motor cortex from sporadic ALS patients' (n=?) and 'normal control subjects' (n=?) with 12 possible TDP-43 proteinopathy cases stained positive as cited. The tissue samples were reported to be fixed with 10% NBF and paraffin-embedded — but the reference to the ethics statement for the use of postmortem tissues is provided only in the Methods. If the review is double-blind, this may not matter, but the exact institutional approval code was omitted from the main text for the human tissue work (which should be stated in the Methods).
- The statement in the methods that 'unbiased proteomics screens' in soluble fractions were performed to identify modifications in an unbiased fashion. It also states that the observed peptide modifications were 'quantified as ratios [peptide counts mutated to unmodified and read out as apparently elevated in disease]' — these sites (K82/K84) were detected in WT and M337V as well, without mention of the allele-specific origin of the NLS.
- Mass spectrometry data: The authors provide no data repository number for the mass spectrometry, RNA-Seq, or ribosome profiling — and the report is not yet available as a preprint, so independent reviewers cannot check the underlying evidence for the claimed post-translational modifications at K82. No raw files, no annotated MS/MS spectra, no database search config files, no quant tables. The results section states that proteomics identified K84 ubiquitination and K82 acetylation but does not show how peptides were distinguished whether 'K82ac' vs 'K82-ub' vs K82-methyl or cross-talk of modifications at neighboring; the evidence is not present. Minimal fix: provide annotated MS/MS spectra for the key peptides and a repository ID whose content matches the claim in the text.
- Reporting of time-course in Fig. 1: The description says cells were treated with 2 nM BTZ every 24h for 72h, yet the reader is never told when imaging or lysis was performed relative to the last dose, nor whether the cells were kept in BTZ-containing medium. The manuscript's claim 'TDP-43 mislocalization was evident 2h after BTZ addition' is not accompanied by the time course of expression changes after 2h, and figures showing this time course are absent from main or supplements.
- Experimental evidence for the claim that proteasome inhibition by BTZ at 20 nM is 'partial': The authors have not shown a partial inhibition of proteasome activity at this concentration. The only data quantifying proteasome activity in human iPSC-derived neurons was a chymotrypsin-like activity assay done at one concentration or one condition display, whose exact values were reported only relative and only for the mixed glial/neuronal mixture, not separated. The claim 'partial' is key, and must be substantiated either by a dose-response curve of proteasome activity for BTZ at 0-100 nM (showing approximately 50% inhibition at 20 nM), or by stating that 'the concentration was chosen to avoid general proteotoxicity.'
- The arguments made in the final paragraph, in which the authors claim the acetylated K82 may cause cytoplasmic aggregation (because acetylated K82Ac, K84Ac, and K79Ac are found in cytoplasmic inclusion in sALS) rely on data from fixed tissue. The authors assert that P62-associated degradation is compromised by inhibition; however, p62 bodies may occur as a consequence of the experimental system (overexpression of TDP-43 in non-neuronal cells), and generalize to neurons only with the caveat that primary neurons – which express p62 at much higher levels – would need to be tested.
- (HARD) Provide a summary table AND the annotated MS/MS spectra for the key modified NLS peptides (K82Ac, K84Ac, K79/82 ubiquitination) deposited in a public repository (PRIDE/MassIVE) or as supplementary tables. The authors report 'immunoblotting of TDP-43 and mass spectrometry identified ubiquitination at K79, K82, K84 and acetylation at K84,' but the MS evidence is not visible. If the spectra cannot be shown, the claim that these events increase 'when proteasome activity is partially inhibited' is unverifiable.
- (HARD) Clarify the stoichiometry claim 'protein levels of TDP-43 were unchanged' in Fig. 1 legend vs the statement in Results on 'proteasome 20S activity' in Fig. S1A/B, and provide the uncropped immunoblot panels as supplements, with molecular weight markers.
- (SOFT) Provide an explication of the term 'aged' in the context of the aging experiments with differentiation of the human iPSC-derived NGN2 neurons (which the authors describe as 'aged' in culture) - to clarify whether it is just days in culture or their 'aging' method.
- Which cell line is used to generate the cortical neurons shown in use in all Figures, and is it an allelic series on the same background? Were male and female lines used? This should be stated once in the methods.
