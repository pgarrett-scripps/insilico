# Debate Transcript

## Advocate — round 1

The manuscript presents a coherent, mechanistically grounded chain of evidence connecting partial proteasome inhibition to TDP-43 nuclear loss of function through a specific post-translational modification at K82. The reviewers' concerns—about statistical rigor, dose mismatches, human tissue sample size, and overclaimed causality—are real but addressable in revision without undermining the core contribution.

First, the identification of K82 acetylation in the TDP-43 cNLS by unbiased mass spectrometry (98.3% coverage, Fig. 3A–B) followed by validation with custom acetylation-specific antibodies in both cultured neurons (Fig. S5) and six of six sALS motor cortices versus zero of four controls (Fig. 5B) is a significant translational finding. The peptide-binding assay (Fig. 3F) directly demonstrates that K82-acetylated peptides—not just glutamine mimics—fail to bind importin-α1, which is a stronger biochemical test than the data reviewers appear to credit.

Second, the rescue experiment swapping the TDP-43 cNLS for the FUS PY-NLS (Fig. 2E–I) cleanly establishes that mislocalization depends on the import pathway, not on general nuclear-import failure. Co-expression of PY-NLS and cNLS variants even protects wild-type TDP-43, strongly suggesting the mechanism is pathway-specific.

Third, the comprehensive lysine-to-arginine mutagenesis (Fig. 4) systematically maps the bipartite NLS and identifies K82 paired with K95 or K97 as the minimal import-competent configuration, providing structural rationale for why K82 modification is disruptive.

On the dose mismatch (20 nM BTZ for imaging vs. 2 nM for fractionation): the fractionation data at 2 nM (Fig. 1B) independently show mislocalization, so the core claim holds. The imaging at 20 nM is a separate experiment that confirms mislocalization at a higher dose; the authors should simply note this. The 25% expression level claim for K82R (Fig. S4A) can be fixed by adding densitometry. The multiple-testing issue in the nuclear proteome (Fig. 1E) is genuine: the volcano plot uses uncorrected p-values. However, TDP-43 is shown to be the most sensitive with >4-fold reduction, and the primary evidence for mislocalization does not depend on this single analysis—it is confirmed by fractionation (Fig. 1B), live-cell imaging (Fig. S1G), and immunofluorescence (Fig. 1C–D). The mass spec screen is exploratory and flagged as such; appropriate FDR correction can be applied in revision.

The human tissue data (Fig. 5B–C) are preliminary but striking: ac-K82 signal in all six sALS cases and none of four controls is a promising observation, even from a small cohort. The authors should add densitometry and a statistical test, and acknowledge the limited sample size. The 'initiation' claim in the title can be softened to 'disruption' or 'trigger' to match the evidence, which shows sufficiency of K82 modification to block import, not temporal priority.

In summary, the paper's strengths—mechanistic depth, clean rescue, and the first detection of K82 acetylation in ALS tissue—constitute a genuine advance. The weaknesses are fixable or involve rephrasing, not redesign. This work merits acceptance with revision.

## Skeptic — round 1

The advocate concedes that several load-bearing claims outrun the evidence. I will press those concessions into a case for rejection rather than revision.

**Fatal flaw 1: the title and abstract assert initiation; the data show correlation.** The advocate acknowledges that the 'initiation' claim should be softened, but this is not a cosmetic fix. The paper's entire framing—"acetylation of lysine 82 initiates TDP-43 nuclear loss of function"—is what makes it a mechanistic advance rather than an incremental observation. Once that framing is removed, what remains? Ko et al. (2024) already showed K82Q disrupts importin binding. The present additions (peptide-binding with true acetyl-lysine, and tissue data) are meaningful extensions, but they are not the breakthrough the title promises. A paper whose central causal claim must be abandoned in revision has not earned publication in its current form.

**Fatal flaw 2: the human tissue evidence cannot bear the weight placed on it.** Six ALS samples and four controls, with no densitometry, no statistical test, and no confirmation that the antibody does not cross-react with other acetyl-lysines in tissue (e.g., K136ac or K145ac, which are known to be enriched in pathology). The advocate says these are 'promising observations' that need quantification. That is exactly the problem: the most translational claim in the paper—that K82 acetylation is a disease-initiating event—rests on data that are preliminary, unquantified, and uncontrolled. A revision might fix this, but the manuscript as submitted does not support it.

**Fixed flaw that remains fatal: the 25% expression claim.** The advocate says to fix Fig. S4A with densitometry. But this claim—that K82R causes mislocalization at 25% of endogenous levels—is central to arguing that K82 is a gatekeeper. Without it, the strength of the mutant analysis is undermined. The fact that a load-bearing quantitative claim was published without supporting measurement is a rigor failure, not a formatting issue.

**Final point: the logic of the causal chain is broken.** The advocate points to the PY-NLS rescue as clean evidence that import pathway matters. I agree. But the paper never shows that K82 acetylation <i>precedes</i> mislocalization, only that it correlates with it at 24 hr. The proteasome-to-acetylation-to-mislocalization sequence is inferred from co-occurrence, not tested. Without a time course of acetylation or an acetyltransferase perturbation, the model remains correlative. The advocate's concession that 'the mass spec identifies K82 acetylation after 24 hr of proteasome inhibition, at which point TDP-43 is already mislocalized' is devastating: the temporal order required for causation is not established.

These are not minor fixes. They are fundamental gaps in the evidence for the central claim. Rejection with encouragement to resubmit a revised version that addresses them is the appropriate outcome.

**Key points:**
- The causal 'initiation' claim is the paper's core framing, and the advocate concedes it must be softened — without it, the advance over Ko et al. (2024) is incremental, not transformative.
- Human tissue evidence (6 ALS, 4 controls) is unquantified, lacks statistics, and is uncontrolled for cross-reactivity with other acetyl-lysines — too preliminary to support translational claims.
- A load-bearing quantitative claim (25% expression of K82R) was made without supporting densitometry, a rigor failure.
- The temporal order required for causation (acetylation precedes mislocalization) is not shown; the mass spec identifies modifications at 24 hr when mislocalization is already established.
