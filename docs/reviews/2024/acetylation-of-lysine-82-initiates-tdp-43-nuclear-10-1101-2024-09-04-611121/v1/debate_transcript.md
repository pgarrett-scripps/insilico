## Advocate — round 1

# Advocate's Case for Acceptance

This manuscript deserves acceptance because it delivers a mechanistically coherent, multi-pronged answer to a central question in ALS research: what initiates TDP-43 nuclear loss? The convergence of evidence across orthogonal methods is the paper's greatest strength.

**The core claim is multiply supported.** The K82-acetylation-disrupts-import finding rests on three independent pillars: the K82Q mimic abolishing nuclear import (Fig. 3C–E), the direct peptide-binding assay showing K82-acetylated peptides fail to bind importin-α1 (Fig. 3F), and the mutagenesis screen showing even the charge-preserving K82R disrupts binding (Fig. 4C–E). The rigor reviewer correctly notes this convergence: "both cell-based and biochemical assays converge; the peptide assay is the cleaner evidence and it directly shows loss of binding."

**The genuinely novel contribution is the proteasome-trigger link.** As the novelty reviewer establishes, the K82-import mechanism was preempted by Ko et al. 2024 (cited), but "reduced proteasome activity triggers TDP-43 mislocalization via cNLS modification" is "the genuinely novel element" — no prior work links age-related proteasome decline to TDP-43 mislocalization through NLS acetylation. The PY-NLS rescue (Fig. 2D–I) cleanly isolates the cNLS as the relevant element, and the stathmin-2 cryptic splicing readout (Fig. 1G) ties mislocalization to functional loss.

**The sALS tissue finding is a real extension.** Antibody-based detection in 6/6 sALS versus 0/4 controls, with the soluble/insoluble distribution suggesting acetylation precedes phosphorylation (Fig. 5B–C), extends Kametani 2016's mass-spec detection in 1/2 samples.

**The weaknesses are fixable, not fatal.** The missing MS spectra, antibody knockdown controls, and statistical details are all addressable in revision. The "initiates" language should be hedged to "can initiate" — a wording change, not an evidence gap. The data-analysis reviewer's demand for n's and tests is reasonable and standard. None of these undermine the mechanistic logic; they demand fuller reporting.

## Skeptic — round 1

The advocate's case rests on the convergence of three pillars for the K82-acetylation claim. But convergence among flawed pillars does not produce a sound structure — it produces a plausible narrative. Let me be precise about which objections are genuinely fatal versus fixable, and where the advocate has conceded ground that matters.

**The most serious problem is not the missing MS spectra — it is that the causal claim is untested by any experiment that would distinguish acetylation from other K82 perturbations.** The literature reviewer and methodology reviewer independently converged on this: the manuscript itself shows that even the charge-preserving K82R mutation disrupts importin-α1 binding (Fig. 4C–E). The K82Q acetylation mimic is therefore not evidence that acetylation *specifically* drives the effect — it is evidence that *any* perturbation at K82 does. The peptide-binding assay (Fig. 3F) tests only acetylated and phosphorylated peptides, not ubiquitinated ones, even though the MS identified ubiquitination at the same lysines (Fig. 3B). The claim that acetylation "initiates" the proteinopathy requires distinguishing acetylation from ubiquitination at K82 — both were detected, both neutralize the charge, and neither was tested against the other. This is **FATAL as framed**, not fixable by adding spectra. What would fix it: a ubiquitinated-K82 peptide in the binding assay, or a K82E (charge-neutral, non-acetyl-mimicking) comparison. Without that, the title claim is not supported by the evidence presented.

**The "initiates" language is not merely a wording change — it encodes an untested temporal claim.** The rigor reviewer correctly notes that the human data are cross-sectional; the soluble-versus-insoluble distinction (Fig. 5C) is a single case and is not a temporal marker. The advocate says this is "a wording change, not an evidence gap." That is wrong: the paper's entire framing — "initiates TDP-43 nuclear loss of function" — asserts temporal priority that no experiment in the manuscript establishes. The time-course experiments (Fig. 1B) show mislocalization at 12 hr but do not measure acetylation at time points before mislocalization. The reproducibility reviewer's demand for a time-course where acetylation precedes the splicing change is the correct test, and it is absent. This is **FIXABLE** by requalification ("can initiate" or "is sufficient to initiate") — but the advocate's dismissal of it as cosmetic understates what the title currently claims.

**The antibody specificity gap is load-bearing and the advocate has not addressed it.** The translational claim — K82 acetylation in 6/6 sALS — rests entirely on a new antibody validated only against synthetic peptides (Fig. 5A). The rigor reviewer's demand for a K82R or knockdown control in the same immunoblot format is not a formality: without it, the signal could track total TDP-43 load or cross-react with a co-migrating acetylated species. The reproducibility reviewer flags the same gap independently. This is **FATAL** for the human-tissue claim as presented, though fixable with the control.

**The advocate has genuinely answered one concern:** the novelty objection. The manuscript cites Ko et al. 2024 and Kametani 2016, and the proteasome-trigger link is a real delta. I concede that point — it is not unacknowledged duplication.

**A collective blind spot worth naming:** none of the specialist reports tested whether the 2 nM BTZ dose used in most experiments actually produces the ~50% proteasome inhibition claimed, versus the 20 nM used in the proteomics (Fig. 1E). The dose-response (Fig. 1A) shows activity at both doses, but the mislocalization experiments (Fig. 1B–D) and the PTM identification (Fig. 3A–B) use different doses. If the PTM mass spec was done at 20 nM and the functional assays at 2 nM, the link between the identified modifications and the phenotype is indirect. This is a gap the panel did not collectively notice.

**Verdict:** The mechanistic logic is coherent and the proteasome-trigger link is genuinely novel, but the central claim — that acetylation *specifically* at K82 initiates the proteinopathy — is not supported by the evidence as presented. The K82R result actively undermines the acetylation-specificity claim, and the antibody control is missing for the human data. These are fixable, but they are not cosmetic. The manuscript should not be accepted in its current form.
