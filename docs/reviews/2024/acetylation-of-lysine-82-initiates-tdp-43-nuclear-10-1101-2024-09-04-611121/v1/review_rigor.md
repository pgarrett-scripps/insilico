# Rigor & Overclaiming Reviewer

## Summary
The core biochemical finding — that K82 acetylation disrupts TDP-43 binding to importin-α1 and is sufficient to block nuclear import — is well supported by the direct peptide-binding assay and the K82Q mimic, and the mutagenesis mapping of the bipartite NLS is careful. But the title's causal claim ("initiates") and the sALS antibody finding both outrun the evidence as presented, and a dose inconsistency weakens the "partial proteasome inhibition" framing of the headline experiments. The paper is worth revising rather than rejecting: the sufficiency claim is solid, the mediation and human-detection claims are not yet established.

## Weaknesses
- HARD — title and Discussion causal claim. "Acetylation of lysine 82 initiates TDP-43 nuclear loss of function" (title) and "acetylation initiates and drives TDP-43 proteinopathy" (Discussion) assert that K82 acetylation is the causal mediator of the proteasome-inhibition phenotype. The evidence establishes sufficiency (K82Q mimic, Fig 3C-E; acetylated K82 peptide fails to bind importin-α1, Fig 3F) and co-occurrence (mass spec under BTZ), but no experiment prevents K82 acetylation and shows mislocalization is blocked. The same mass spec identifies ubiquitination at K79/82/84 — an equally plausible mediator that the abstract itself acknowledges ("driven by ubiquitination or acetylation") but the title drops. What would settle it: block K82 acetylation (acetyltransferase inhibition or deacetylase overexpression) and show BTZ-induced mislocalization is prevented, or show a non-acetylatable K82 variant that retains import resists it — noting K82R is confounded because it fails import on its own.
- HARD — sALS antibody specificity. The ac-K82 antibodies are validated only against unmodified vs K82-acetylated peptide (Fig 5A), not against K79-acetylated or K84-acetylated peptides, which the same mass spec shows are also acetylated under BTZ. The increased signal in BTZ-treated lysates (Fig S5) is therefore consistent with K79/K84 cross-reactivity, and the abstract's claim "we detected acetylation of lysine 82 in the motor cortex of sporadic ALS patients but not control subjects" inherits this. What would settle it: ELISA of each antibody against K79-ac and K84-ac peptides, plus competition with the K82-ac peptide.
- SOFT-to-HARD — "TDP-43 was the protein whose nuclear localization was the most sensitive to a reduction in proteasome activity." The mass spec, immunofluorescence and stathmin-2 experiments use 20 nM BTZ, while 2 nM is stated as achieving ~50% inhibition (Fig 1A); if 20 nM gives substantially more than 50%, the "partial reduction" framing of the headline experiments is not supported. The mass spec also measures nuclear content, which is equated with localization. State the % inhibition at 20 nM, or repeat the proteomics at 2 nM.
