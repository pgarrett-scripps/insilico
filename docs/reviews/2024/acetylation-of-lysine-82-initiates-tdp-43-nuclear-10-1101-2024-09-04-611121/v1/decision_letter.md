# Decision Letter

**Decision:** major

## Summary of Evaluation
The manuscript reports a mechanistically interesting and potentially important pathway linking reduced proteasome activity to TDP-43 nuclear loss via acetylation of lysine 82 within its cNLS, with detection of K82 acetylation in sALS motor cortex. The core sufficiency claim (K82 acetylation can disrupt importin-α1 binding and block nuclear import) is supported by the peptide binding assay, K82Q mimic, and PY-NLS swap, and the mutagenesis mapping of the bipartite NLS is careful. However, the panel identified several load-bearing issues that must be addressed before the paper meets the standards required for In Silico review publication. The TMT nuclear proteome and PTM mass spectrometry data are not deposited, and the acetylation-vs-ubiquitination ambiguity at K79/K82/K84 is unresolved. The ac-K82 antibodies lack validation against K79- and K84-acetylated peptides, and the sALS tissue immunoblot lacks a competing peptide control and blinding. A dose inconsistency undermines the 'partial proteasome inhibition' framing of the headline experiments (omics, imaging, stathmin-2 analyses use 20 nM BTZ, while the ~50% inhibition point is 2 nM). The title's causal claim ('initiates') and the Discussion's 'initiates and drives' outrun the correlational evidence. The manuscript lacks a data-availability statement, and load-bearing reagents (plasmids, antibodies, knock-in cell line) are not deposited or described. Animal ethics approval for the mouse data is absent. All required revisions are concrete and checkable; the editorial debate, the specialist reports and the compliance audits converge on major revision as the appropriate verdict.
