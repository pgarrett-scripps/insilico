# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript reports a mechanistically interesting pathway linking reduced proteasome activity to TDP-43 nuclear loss via K82 acetylation, but the statistical support for the three load-bearing claims — TDP-43 as the most nuclear-sensitive protein, K82 acetylation as the driver of mislocalization, and K82 acetylation as elevated in sALS — is incomplete as reported. The omics claim rests on unadjusted p-values across 5202 proteins with n=3; the sALS comparison lacks a test and a defined normalization denominator; and the imaging and binding quantifications have undefined n structure. The design is thoughtful (dye-swap TMT, three inhibitors, PY-NLS rescue), and the gaps are addressable, but the numbers as presented do not yet support the strength of the conclusions.

## Strengths
- The dye-swap design (three forward + three reverse TMT labelling groups) is good practice for controlling labelling bias in the nuclear proteome experiment.
- Confirming the mislocalization phenotype with three mechanistically distinct proteasome inhibitors (BTZ, MG132, MRZ) strengthens the attribution to proteasome inhibition rather than inhibitor-specific toxicity.
- The PY-NLS swap rescue provides a strong internal control isolating the cNLS as the site of the effect.

## Weaknesses
- Unadjusted p-values across 5202 proteins do not support the "most sensitive" ranking claim (Fig 1E). The legend states "Unadjusted P values were calculated using a one-sample two-sided Student's t-test" across the nuclear proteome. With n=3 biological replicates (three forward + three reverse labelling groups), each t-test has 2 degrees of freedom, and at an unadjusted p<0.05 threshold ~260 false positives are expected by chance. The claim that TDP-43 is "the protein whose nuclear localization is most sensitive" is a genome-wide ranking and requires FDR control with hits reported against the corrected threshold. The companion claim that nuclear clearance is "selective for TDP-43" (Fig 1F) is a null claim from an underpowered comparison: with n=3, failure of other ALS-linked RBPs to reach significance is not evidence of no change. Report the FDR-corrected q-value for TDP-43 and the number of proteins passing a 5% FDR threshold.
- The sALS vs control acetylation comparison (Fig 5B) lacks a test, a defined denominator, and matching information. The claim "all six of six sALS patients" positive with "no signal in four controls" is a binary outcome that should be tested (Fisher's exact test gives p=0.0048 for a 6/6 vs 0/4 split), but no test is reported. The "varying levels" across sALS and the correlation with pTDP-43 are asserted without quantification or test. Critically, the ac-K82 signal is not stated to be normalized to total TDP-43 per sample. Since sALS motor cortex accumulates TDP-43, a raw ac-K82 signal could track total TDP-43 abundance rather than increased acetylation per molecule. The claim requires ac-K82/total TDP-43 ratios per sample, and controls must be matched to sALS for age and postmortem interval if the difference is to be attributed to disease rather than sample quality. Additionally, the antibody was validated against unmodified peptide but not against K79- or K84-acetylated peptides, which the manuscript itself shows are also modified (Fig 3B); cross-reactivity would inflate the ac-K82 signal.
- The imaging quantifications (Fig 1D, 3D, 4E, 4G, 2G-I) have undefined n structure. The methods state "Each data point represents an independent biological replicate (distinct wells of independently treated cells)," but the number of cells averaged per well, the number of wells per condition, and the test applied are not stated in the text. If plotted points are individual cells, this is pseudo-replication — cells within a well are not independent. The claim that K82Q "eliminated its nuclear import" (Fig 3D) needs the n and the test supporting "eliminated" rather than "reduced," as does the "complete cytosolic localization" claim for TDP-43-6KR (Fig 4B).
- The peptide binding assay (Fig 3F) — n and replicate structure not stated. The claim that K82-acetylated peptides "did not bind to importin-α1" is a null claim. With what n? Is this a single experiment? The binding curves need replicate structure and error bars to distinguish "no binding" from "below detection," and the "reduced binding" claims for K79-acetylated, K84-acetylated, and S92-phosphorylated peptides need a test with stated n.
- Sweep: Fig 1A: n per dose not stated; the "~50% inhibition" point — how was it derived from the dose-response, and what is its precision? Fig S1A: mouse proteasome activity — n per age group not stated; the "~50% decline by one year" claim has no reported test. Fig S1C: "viability unaffected" — non-significance in a small sample is not evidence of no effect; an equivalence test is needed. Fig 1G: stathmin-2 RT-PCR — n and test not stated. SEM is used throughout with small n (n=4–6); error bars are undefined per figure, and SEM on small samples can imply false precision. Fig 5C: soluble vs insoluble acetylation is n=1 per group; the "earlier event than phosphorylation" claim is unsupported by n=1. Exact p-values are not reported anywhere in the text; only threshold symbols (e.g., *P<0.05), which prevents readers from assessing marginal results.

## Questions
- For Fig 1E, what is the FDR-corrected q-value for TDP-43, and how many proteins pass a 5% FDR threshold?
- For Fig 5B, is the ac-K82 signal normalized to total TDP-43 per sample, and what are the ac-K82/total TDP-43 ratios for each of the 10 samples?
- For the imaging quantifications (Fig 1D, 3D, 4E, 4G), what is the n per condition — wells or cells — and how many cells were averaged per well?
- For Fig 3F, how many independent replicates of the peptide binding assay were performed, and what do the error bars represent?
