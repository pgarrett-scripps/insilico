# Ethics & Compliance Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript's use of human postmortem tissue is properly documented, with named IRBs and protocol numbers and HIPAA-compliant consent, and all tissue data are de-identified. However, the mouse tissue work reported in Fig. S1A has no animal care and use approval statement, and the manuscript lacks both a funding disclosure and a competing-interests declaration — all hard requirements for this venue. These are fixable additions, but the manuscript is not compliant as worded. The iPSC line's donor consent is also undocumented, though WTC11 is a widely distributed line with known provenance.

## Weaknesses
- (HARD) Animal research without approval statement. Fig. S1A reports chymotrypsin-like proteasome activity in "fresh frozen cortex of mice of different ages" (3–24 months), and the Materials and Methods describe mouse tissue use, but no IACUC or animal welfare approval is stated anywhere in the manuscript. Per the venue's rule that silence is not compliance, this is a hard gap. The one thing that would settle it: an explicit statement of the institutional animal care and use committee approval (with protocol number) covering the mouse tissue collection, or a statement that the tissue was obtained from an approved source.
- (HARD) No funding disclosure. The manuscript contains no funding statement; the venue requires funding to be disclosed. The one thing that would settle it: a funding statement listing all sources of support.
- (HARD) No competing-interests declaration. The manuscript contains no competing-interests statement, not even an explicit declaration of none. The one thing that would settle it: a statement declaring competing interests or explicitly declaring none.
- (SOFT) iPSC donor consent not documented. The iPSCs are described as "a kind gift of Michael Ward" in the WTC11 background, but no donor consent statement or citation to the line's derivation is given. WTC11 is a widely distributed line with documented consent, so this is a soft request: cite the original derivation or state the consent status.
- (SOFT) Biosafety statement. Lentiviral production and infection are described; an institutional biosafety approval statement is commonly required for recombinant DNA work and should be added if applicable.
