# Ethics & Compliance Reviewer

SCORE: 4  
CONFIDENCE: 4  

**Overall take (ethics/compliance):** The manuscript is compliant on the categories that matter most: human postmortem tissue use is covered by an IRB approval statement, and no clinical trial, animal, or dual-use concerns are triggered. The one gap is that the IRB approval is named but lacks an identifier, which is a soft request rather than a hard failure. Funding and competing interests are not disclosed anywhere in the manuscript, which is a hard requirement for this venue. This is a fixable revision, not a substantive scientific problem.

**Strengths:**  
- Explicit IRB approval statement for human tissue acquisition, including the two institutions involved.  
- Clear description of tissue provenance (short postmortem interval, consent procedures) that supports reproducibility.  
- No evidence of data fabrication or internal contradiction between the described methods and the reported results.

**Weaknesses (load-bearing first):**  
1. **Funding and competing interests are not disclosed.** The manuscript has no funding statement, no author contributions section, and no competing-interests declaration. For a study involving human tissue and potential commercial applications (e.g., the acetylation-specific antibodies and the therapeutic implications for deacetylase inhibitors), this is a hard requirement. The absence is not evidence of misconduct, but it must be stated explicitly as “none” or declared. This is the single most significant compliance gap.

2. **IRB approval is named but lacks protocol or committee identifiers.** The text states “approved by Institutional Review Boards (either Benaroya Research Institute, Seattle, WA IRB# 10058 or University of California San Diego, San Diego, CA IRB# 120056).” The IRB numbers are present, so this is actually compliant — I initially misread this as missing. On re-reading, the identifiers are there. No issue.

3. **Human iPSC-derived neurons are used without a statement on donor consent for the iPSC line.** The WTC11 line is a well-known commercial/consented line, but the manuscript does not state whether the original donor consent covers the differentiation and molecular analyses performed here. This is a soft request — the line is widely used and the consent is likely in place, but a one-line statement would close the loop.

4. **Postmortem tissue from sALS patients is described as “fresh frozen” but the consent statement covers “fixed” tissue.** The methods say tissues were “immediately dissected… and fixed in neutral buffered formalin” for the repository, yet the study uses fresh frozen motor cortex. This is an internal inconsistency in the tissue handling description. It does not affect the ethics finding — the IRB covers the repository — but it should be clarified whether the fresh frozen samples were collected under the same protocol or a separate one.

**Sweep:**  
- No clinical trial registration is needed; this is not an interventional study.  
- No animal research is described; the mouse data in Fig. S1A are from tissue lysates, not live animals, and no animal protocol is required for postmortem tissue.  
- No dual-use or biosafety concerns; the work involves standard molecular biology and cell culture.  
- No restricted-consent or biobank-specific issues beyond the iPSC line noted above.  
- The manuscript does not report any patient-identifiable data; all human samples are anonymized.  

**Questions:**  
1. Please add a funding statement and a competing-interests declaration (even if “none”) to the manuscript.  
2. Please confirm that the fresh frozen motor cortex samples used in Fig. 5 were collected under the same IRB protocol as the fixed tissue, or state the separate approval.  
3. Please state whether the WTC11 iPSC line’s original donor consent covers the differentiation and molecular analyses performed here.
