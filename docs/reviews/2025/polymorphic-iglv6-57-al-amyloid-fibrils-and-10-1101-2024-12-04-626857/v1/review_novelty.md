# Novelty & Contribution Reviewer

## Summary
This manuscript reports cryo-EM structures of IGLV6-57 AL amyloid fibrils with single and double protofilament morphologies, including the first cardiac amyloid fibril with 2-fold rotational symmetry perpendicular to the fibril axis. The work is technically competent and the dominant morphology is well-resolved (3.0 Å), but novelty is incremental: concurrent work (Hicks et al. 2025) already reported polymorphism in λ6 fibrils, and the authors' contribution is structural characterization of both morphologies rather than discovery of polymorphism itself. The proposed shared folding pathway is a hypothesis based on comparative structural analysis of three highly similar sequences, appropriately framed as speculative but lacking experimental support. The contribution is solid but does not establish fundamentally new principles of light chain aggregation.

## Strengths
- High-resolution structure of dominant single protofilament morphology (3.0 Å) with clear cross-beta architecture and detailed atomic modeling, supported by de novo MS sequencing and proteinase K enrichment validation.
- Rigorous acknowledgment of cryo-EM limitations and biases (fibril extraction, grid optimization, particle picking, class averaging) that could skew the observed morphology distribution, demonstrating methodological self-awareness.
- Comprehensive comparative analysis of three IGLV6-57 structures revealing conserved structural motifs (C-shaped turn, Pro 14 bend) that suggest sequence-dependent yet structurally constrained aggregation despite 80–84% sequence identity in the core.

## Weaknesses
- Concurrent work not clearly positioned as such. The manuscript cites Hicks et al. (2025, reference 21) as showing single and double protofilament morphologies in λ6 light chains, yet the abstract and introduction frame the polymorphic finding as a novel contribution without explicitly stating that concurrent work already reported this observation. The distinction—that the authors characterize both morphologies structurally while Hicks et al. focused on the dominant single morphology—is real but narrow and should be foregrounded in the abstract rather than buried in the Discussion.
- Shared folding pathway hypothesis lacks mechanistic evidence. The proposed shared folding pathway rests entirely on identifying conserved structural motifs across three structures with 80–84% sequence identity. The authors provide no experimental evidence—kinetics, intermediate trapping, mutagenesis, or molecular dynamics—that these motifs represent obligatory steps in folding rather than convergent solutions to packing constraints imposed by the disulfide bond and hydrophobic core.
- Double protofilament structure modeled by docking into lower-resolution map (3.78 Å) rather than de novo modeling. The authors state the map quality was insufficient to directly model the structure, which limits confidence in the protofilament interface assignment and the three proposed assembly mechanisms.
- Solvation energy analysis does not explain fibril stability. The authors note their IGLV6 fibrils have the lowest estimated solvation energy of all ex-vivo AL fibril structures, yet they are stable. They then invoke interlocking monomers and specific interactions to explain stability, which is post-hoc rationalization rather than predictive explanation.
- Particle tracing analysis of mixed polymorphs is descriptive, not quantitative. The claim that a minor population of fibrils were observed with both polymorphs co-existing on the same total fibril is based on visual inspection of micrographs with no quantitative estimate of frequency, no statistical test of whether co-occurrence is random, and no analysis of whether mixed fibrils represent true coexistence or artifacts of 2D projection.
- De novo MS sequencing validation is indirect. The authors validate their predicted sequence by showing that proteinase K-resistant regions match predicted fibril core residues, but proteinase K resistance only confirms that the core region is proteolytically protected; it does not uniquely identify the sequence. Three residues in the constant domain were flagged as uncommon substitutions and left as picked by Stitch, introducing uncertainty into the full-length sequence.
- Structural comparison with 6HUD and 9OKA is qualitative, not quantitative. The authors compare three IGLV6-57 structures visually but do not perform quantitative structural superposition, RMSD calculations, or statistical measures of fold similarity. The claim of shared structural motifs relies on manual annotation and visual inspection rather than objective metrics.
- Generalizability not addressed. The manuscript presents one patient sample. Whether the polymorphism, the 2-fold rotational symmetry, and the shared folding pathway are general features of IGLV6-57 fibrils or specific to this patient's sequence variants and tissue environment is not discussed.

## Questions
- How does the frequency of mixed single/double protofilament fibrils compare to the frequency expected if morphologies assembled independently? Is the co-occurrence statistically significant?
- Can the authors provide quantitative structural superposition (RMSD, TM-score) between the three IGLV6-57 structures to support the claim of a shared fold, or does visual inspection alone suffice for the hypothesis?
- Why does the solvation energy metric predict low stability for IGLV6 fibrils when they are experimentally stable, and does this invalidate the use of solvation energy as a predictor of fibril stability across different germline genes?
