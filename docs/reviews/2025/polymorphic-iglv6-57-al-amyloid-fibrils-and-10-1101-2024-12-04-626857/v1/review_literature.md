# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The citation record is substantially accurate and appropriately comprehensive for a structural biology manuscript. The three load-bearing weaknesses are real but do not invalidate the core contribution: the new structures are sound, the polymorphism observation is novel, and the folding pathway hypothesis is presented with appropriate caveats. The issues are (1) incomplete citation of a directly relevant recent paper on IGLV6 organ tropism, (2) an unverified quantitative claim about database contents, and (3) a speculative hypothesis presented with slightly more confidence than the evidence supports. These are fixable in revision and do not undermine the manuscript's acceptance, but they should be addressed to strengthen the citation foundation and clarify the limits of the folding pathway claim.

## Strengths
- The manuscript cites the three directly comparable IGLV6-57 ex-vivo fibril structures (6HUD, 9OKA, and the present 9ELS) and conducts a rigorous sequence and structural comparison, enabling fair assessment of the shared motif hypothesis.
- References to prior AL fibril cryo-EM work span the major germline families (IGLV3, IGLV1, IGLV6) and appropriately acknowledge the structural diversity observed across the field, positioning the new structures in context.
- The manuscript transparently acknowledges limitations of cryo-EM (selection bias, fibril extraction artifacts, particle picking bias) in the Discussion, citing relevant methodological concerns rather than overstating confidence.

## Weaknesses
- The manuscript states that IGLV6-57 is 'predominantly associated with kidney, and to a lesser degree, heart involvement' citing references 11–13, but does not cite the more recent Morgan et al. (2025, Amyloid 32:129–138), which the authors themselves reference in the acknowledgments section as 'an updated AL-base' with 'ranked enrichment of immunoglobulin light chain variable genes,' despite this paper appearing directly relevant to germline-phenotype associations and should be cited in the Introduction to support the organ-tropism claim.
- The manuscript claims the double protofilament morphology exhibits 'a rare rotational symmetry' and states 'across the 615 publicly available amyloid fibril structures in the Amyloid Atlas, there are only 9 fibril structures with a similar rotational symmetry perpendicular to the fibril axis, of which only 4 are ex vivo fibrils,' but this specific quantitative claim about database contents is presented as fact and is not independently verifiable from the manuscript or its citations, with no explicit statement of when the Amyloid Atlas was accessed or what query parameters were used.
- The manuscript proposes a speculative folding mechanism (Fig. 6b) based on three structures that share a 'C-shaped turn' and a 'sharp bend at Pro 14' but then diverge in N-terminal positioning, and while the authors acknowledge this is a hypothesis, the evidence presented does not exclude alternative explanations: the C-shaped turn and Pro 14 bend may be forced by disulfide bond geometry rather than a shared aggregation pathway, the N-terminal divergence could reflect different proteolytic cleavage events or tissue environments, and no kinetic or thermodynamic data are presented to distinguish a shared pathway from convergent folding.
- Reference 11 (del Pozo-Yauner et al. 2008) addresses thermodynamic stability and fibrillogenicity but does not directly establish organ tropism, and the full content of reference 13 (Kourelis et al. 2017) is not verified, making the current citation set insufficient to fully establish the claimed predominance of kidney involvement for IGLV6-57.

## Questions
- Can the authors confirm whether the Amyloid Atlas count of 615 structures and 9 with perpendicular rotational symmetry is current as of submission, and specify the query parameters used?
- For the claim that IGLV6-57 is 'predominantly associated with kidney, and to a lesser degree, heart involvement,' should the recently published Morgan et al. (2025) AL-base update be cited in the Introduction rather than only in acknowledgments?
- Does reference 37 (Ruiz-Zamora et al. 2019) specifically establish that cysteine residues are hotspots for amyloid aggregation in light chains, or does it focus more broadly on CDR regions?
- Should the manuscript verify that the claim 'All AL fibril structures have an intact disulfide bond' holds for all cited structures (refs. 14–21) or qualify it as 'all published ex-vivo AL fibril structures to date'?
- Is the observation of coexistence of polymorphs on single fibrils (Fig. 3c) the first report in AL amyloidosis, or has this been observed before, and should prior work on fibril polymorph coexistence in other amyloidoses be cited?
- Do references 59–60 (Olsen et al. 1998, Buxbaum 1992) actually address constant domain fragments in AL fibrils, or should more recent data on this point be cited?
- Has the Hicks et al. preprint (ref. 21, cited as '2025.06.25.661559') since been published, and should the citation format be updated?
- Should Schulte et al. (2022, Anal Chem 94:10391–10399), which appears in the acknowledgments as the primary Stitch reference, be cited in the Methods section rather than only in acknowledgments?
