# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
This cryo-EM structural study reports two morphologies of IGLV6-57 AL amyloid fibrils with de novo mass spectrometry sequencing. The quantitative claims are modest and appropriately supported: resolution estimates follow standard FSC criteria, sequence identity percentages are correctly calculated, and the particle tracing analysis is transparent about selection thresholds. The main statistical limitation is the absence of formal hypothesis testing or confidence intervals around the structural comparisons and solvation energy estimates, but these are descriptive rather than inferential claims and the manuscript is candid about the exploratory nature of the mechanistic speculation. The work merits publication with minor clarifications on statistical reporting.

## Strengths
- De novo sequencing validation is rigorous: MS data before and after proteinase K digestion are compared to identify core-resident residues, and the sequence is manually reviewed and cross-checked against structural data, reducing the risk of spurious assignments.
- Resolution reporting follows gold-standard practice: FSC curves at 0.143 threshold are provided for both morphologies (3.0 Å and 3.78 Å), local resolution maps are shown, and the authors acknowledge map quality limitations for the double protofilament structure.
- Particle selection for morphology tracing is transparent: a 5 Å resolution cutoff is stated a priori to balance particle count against confidence in morphology assignment, and the authors report the absolute numbers (388,527 and 341,845 particles) and acknowledge that many particles fail to align into final maps.

## Weaknesses
- Solvation energy comparison lacks confidence intervals and statistical testing. The authors state their IGLV6 fibrils have 'the lowest' solvation energy of all ex-vivo AL fibril structures (Supplementary Fig. 6b,c) and claim this is notable despite 'low predicted fibril stability,' but no error bounds, replicates, or formal test distinguishes this from the other structures. The claim rests on a single point estimate per structure with no disclosure of sensitivity to buried surface area calculation method or coordinate uncertainty. What would resolve this: report the range of solvation energies across the ensemble of conformations sampled during refinement, or provide a sensitivity analysis showing how the ranking changes with ±10% variation in buried surface area.
- Sequence identity percentages (80.4%, 83.9%) are reported without confidence intervals or alignment uncertainty. The Needleman-Wunsch algorithm is deterministic, but the choice of gap penalties and the presence of insertions/deletions of different lengths between the three IGLV6 structures mean the alignment is not unique. The claim that 'all three sequences have untemplated insertions' and that specific residues like Tyr 100 'explain differential folding' rests on the alignment being correct, but no alternative alignments are shown or their impact on the conclusions assessed. What would resolve this: report the top 3–5 alternative alignments ranked by score, and show that the mechanistic conclusions (e.g., π–π interactions with Tyr 100) hold across all plausible alignments.
- APR prediction scores (0–10, based on how many of ten methods agree) are presented without a threshold or decision rule. Figure 4 and Supplementary Data 2 show raw scores for each residue, but the text does not state what score constitutes 'strongly predicted' or how many methods must agree to call a region an APR. The authors note that 'strongly predicted APRs' are at residues 33–38, 74–79, and 98–104, but these appear to be selected post-hoc by visual inspection of the heatmap rather than by a pre-specified threshold. This matters because the claim that CDR 2 APRs 'play a role' in aggregation is speculative and depends on which residues are counted as APRs. What would resolve this: state the threshold score used to define an APR, apply it consistently, and report how many residues meet it; alternatively, acknowledge that APR assignment is exploratory and not hypothesis-driven.

## Questions
- Figure 2e shows three 'disconnected densities unlikely to result from sidechain orientation' (asterisks): are these modeled as water molecules, and if so, how many independent water molecules are resolved in the final model and with what B-factor range?
- The helical rise of 5.03 Å is described as 'atypical for amyloid fibrils' and attributed to 'imperfections in the calibration of the electron microscope'—was the microscope calibrated against a standard (e.g., catalase crystals) before or after data collection, and does the reported rise value affect the cross-β geometry or only the axial spacing?
- For the double protofilament morphology (3.78 Å resolution), the authors state 'map quality was insufficient to directly model the structure' and used docking instead: what is the FSC value at the 0.5 threshold (half-map correlation), and does it fall below the 0.143 threshold at any resolution shell?
- Supplementary Figure 6b,c compares solvation energy across AL fibril structures: are all structures refined against the same force field and with the same buried surface area calculation method, or do differences in refinement protocol contribute to the ranking?
