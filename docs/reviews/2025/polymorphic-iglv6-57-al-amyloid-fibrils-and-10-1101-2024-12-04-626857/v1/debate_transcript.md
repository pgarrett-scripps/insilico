# Debate Transcript

## Advocate — round 1

The core contribution here is a genuine, well-validated structural finding: a 3.0 Å ex-vivo cryo-EM structure of an IGLV6-57 AL fibril plus a second, distinct double-protofilament morphology with a documented rotational symmetry rarely seen even across the 615-structure Amyloid Atlas. The reproducibility reviewer confirms the load-bearing cryo-EM maps and models are properly deposited (PDB 9ELS/9ELT, EMDB EMD-48160/48161) with gold-standard FSC validation, and the data_analysis reviewer notes resolution reporting 'follows gold-standard practice.' The sequence assignment—critical since no prior sequence was known—is cross-validated three independent ways: de novo MS via Casanovo/Stitch, proteinase K protection mapping (Fig. 1f) matching predicted core boundaries, and intact-mass/western blot sizing, which the methodology reviewer calls 'strong orthogonal validation.' The particle-tracing evidence that single and double protofilaments coexist on the same fibril (Fig. 3c) is a genuinely novel observation, independently corroborated by the concurrent Hicks et al. preprint, which the novelty reviewer concedes is 'real but narrow' territory the authors do occupy.

Importantly, the authors themselves flag the 'shared folding pathway' (Fig. 6) as hypothesis-generating, not proven—explicitly writing 'more data is needed to support this hypothesis' and 'our data are not sufficient to support or eliminate these models.' This self-restraint is precisely what responsible speculative discussion should look like, and it is separable from the solid structural core. Remaining issues—MassIVE accession disclosure, docking validation metrics, APR thresholds—are enumerated precisely because the methods are transparent enough to critique, and are straightforwardly addressable in revision without touching the primary structural claims.

**Key points:**
- Two independently validated, deposited cryo-EM structures (3.0 Å single protofilament, docked double protofilament) represent a genuine new addition to the AL structural landscape, including a rare 2-fold symmetric morphology.
- Sequence assignment is triangulated via de novo MS, proteinase K protection, western blot, and intact MS—reviewers call this 'rigorous' and 'strong orthogonal validation.'
- Particle tracing showing coexisting polymorphs on single fibrils is a novel, methodologically sound observation per the methodology reviewer.
- The 'shared folding pathway' is explicitly framed by the authors as a hypothesis needing further data, not an overclaimed finding—appropriate scientific hedging rather than overreach.
- Outstanding concerns (MS data accession, docking metrics, APR thresholds) are well-defined, revision-fixable issues that do not undermine the primary structural contribution.

## Skeptic — round 1

The advocate's strongest move—citing the authors' hedging on the folding pathway—doesn't survive contact with the manuscript's own title: 'Polymorphic IGLV6-57 AL amyloid fibrils and features of a shared folding pathway.' A hypothesis the authors admit is unsupported by data ('our data are not sufficient to support or eliminate these models') is nonetheless promoted to the paper's headline claim and given its own figure (Fig. 6b) as if it were a finding. The rigor reviewer is explicit: the C-shaped turn and disulfide reversal are observed across AL fibrils regardless of germline, so three related sequences sharing a turn doesn't distinguish a 'pathway' from convergent sampling of a conformational landscape. This is FIXABLE—retitle and reframe Fig. 6 as speculative—but as submitted it is overclaiming baked into the paper's identity, not a footnote.

More serious: the reproducibility reviewer identifies that the de novo MS sequence—the single input enabling all atomic modeling—rests on raw spectra that are not deposited, only 'available to reviewers upon request,' with three ambiguous residues (D134, D175, D176) the authors admit 'may also be asparagines.' The advocate calls the sequence validation 'triangulated,' but proteinase K protection and intact-mass sizing only confirm approximate boundaries and mass, not amino-acid identity; only the raw Casanovo/Stitch spectra can verify that. Until deposited, no one can check whether the model itself is built on a correct sequence. This is FIXABLE via deposition, but currently it is a hard availability gap on the most load-bearing artifact in the paper, not a minor housekeeping item.

**Key points:**
- FATAL-unless-fixed: the de novo MS sequence underlying the entire atomic model rests on raw spectra available only 'upon request,' with acknowledged ambiguous residues—no independent party can currently verify the sequence that the structure depends on.
- FIXABLE but currently overclaiming: the title itself ('...features of a shared folding pathway') elevates a hypothesis the authors admit is unsupported ('data are not sufficient to support or eliminate these models') to the paper's core identity, not merely a hedged discussion point.
- Advocate's 'triangulated validation' overstates what proteinase K protection and intact-mass sizing can show—those confirm boundaries/mass, not amino acid identity, so they don't substitute for accessible raw MS data.
- Concede: the cryo-EM maps/models themselves (9ELS/9ELT) are properly deposited and well-validated by FSC; this specific critique is about the MS foundation, not the density maps.
