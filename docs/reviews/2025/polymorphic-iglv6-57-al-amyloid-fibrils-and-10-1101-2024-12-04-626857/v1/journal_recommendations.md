# Journal Recommendations

## As-is (current quality)
_No headline venue is realistic at the current quality — see Alternative._

## After required revisions
- **Nature Communications**
  - Fit: This venue publishes high-impact structural biology and amyloidosis research with cryo-EM data. The 3.0 Å IGLV6-57 fibril structure and polymorphic double-protofilament morphology are genuine contributions to understanding AL amyloidosis mechanisms. After reframing the folding-pathway claim as hypothesis rather than headline finding and depositing MS data publicly, the work meets the venue's standards for rigor and reproducibility.
  - Realism: Realistic but competitive. The structural core is sound and novel (first cardiac AL fibril with 2-fold rotational symmetry), but Nature Communications will expect quantitative structural comparison metrics and full transparency on the MS sequence validation—both addressed by the required revisions. Acceptance odds improve substantially once the title and Fig. 6 are reframed.
- **Journal of Structural Biology**
  - Fit: Specializes in cryo-EM structures and amyloid fibril characterization. The dual morphologies, high-resolution density maps, and detailed atomic modeling of IGLV6-57 fibrils are directly on-scope. The journal values methodological transparency and reproducibility, which the required revisions (public MS deposition, quantitative RMSD/TM-score comparisons) directly address.
  - Realism: Good fit after revision. The structural data quality and novelty are appropriate for this venue. The major revisions required are standard for this journal (MS data deposition, quantitative structural metrics), and once completed, the work should be competitive.
- **eLife**
  - Fit: eLife emphasizes reproducibility, data transparency, and mechanistic clarity. The paper's structural contributions are strong, but the current overclaiming of the folding-pathway mechanism and undeposited MS spectra are precisely the kinds of reproducibility gaps eLife reviewers flag. After public MS deposition, reframing the hypothesis, and adding quantitative structural comparisons, the manuscript aligns well with eLife's standards.
  - Realism: Realistic after revision. eLife's review process is rigorous on reproducibility and transparency, but the authors have already committed to all major fixes. The structural core is sound and the revisions are concrete, making this a strong target post-revision.

## Alternative outlets
- **bioRxiv**
  - Fit: The manuscript is already positioned as a preprint-ready work with deposited structures (PDB 9ELS/9ELT, EMDB EMD-48160/48161). Posting to bioRxiv with the required revisions (public MS accession, reframed title/Fig. 6, quantitative metrics) would establish priority and allow community feedback before journal submission. This is a natural intermediate step.
  - Realism: Immediate and appropriate. bioRxiv accepts all original research; this work qualifies. Posting here after addressing the major revisions would strengthen the journal submission package and allow rapid dissemination.
- **Amyloid**
  - Fit: Specialist journal focused on amyloid diseases and structures. The IGLV6-57 AL fibril characterization, comparison with other IGLV6 structures (6HUD, 9OKA), and discussion of germline-sequence-dependent folding are directly on-scope. The journal values detailed structural and mechanistic work on light-chain amyloidosis.
  - Realism: Good fit if Nature Communications or eLife decline. Amyloid is more specialized and may have lower acceptance thresholds for incremental structural contributions, though the novelty here (polymorphic morphologies, rare symmetry) is sufficient. The required revisions strengthen the case.
- **Structure**
  - Fit: Publishes cryo-EM structures and structural biology with emphasis on mechanistic insight. The dual IGLV6-57 morphologies and atomic models are appropriate in scope. The journal values clear presentation of structural data and honest framing of mechanistic claims—both directly addressed by the required revisions (reframing the folding-pathway hypothesis, adding quantitative comparisons).
  - Realism: Reasonable fallback. Structure is competitive but slightly lower tier than Nature Communications or eLife. After revisions, the work's structural novelty and rigor should be competitive, particularly if the folding-pathway claim is properly hedged as exploratory.

## Notes
The manuscript's structural core (3.0 Å IGLV6-57 fibril, rare double-protofilament morphology with 2-fold rotational symmetry) is sound and novel. The major revisions required are all concrete and revision-fixable: (1) public MS/MS deposition (currently 'available to reviewers upon request'), (2) reframing the 'shared folding pathway' from title/Fig. 6 to an explicit untested hypothesis, (3) adding quantitative structural metrics (RMSD, TM-score) for the three-structure comparison, and (4) resolving solvation-energy sign convention and APR thresholds. The editor's decision (major, not reject) reflects that the panel validated rather than dismissed the two heaviest critiques, and the authors have already committed to all fixes. Venues listed in 'after_revision' are realistic targets once these revisions are completed. The alternative venues are appropriate fallbacks if the headline journals decline, but the work should be competitive at Nature Communications or eLife post-revision given the structural novelty and the authors' demonstrated commitment to transparency.
