# Reproducibility Reviewer

## Summary
The cryo-EM structures and de novo sequencing are the load-bearing claims, and both rest on data that cannot be independently verified from the manuscript as written. The cryo-EM density maps and atomic models are deposited with accessions (PDB 9ELS, 9ELT; EMDB EMD-48160, EMD-48161), which is excellent; however, the de novo mass spectrometry sequence determination—the critical input that enabled model building—has no deposited raw data, only a GitHub repository of analysis code without the underlying spectra. The mass spectrometry proteomics data are stated to be "available to reviewers upon request" to MassIVE, which is a hard availability flag for a load-bearing artifact. Without access to the raw MS spectra and the intermediate Casanovo/Stitch outputs, an independent group cannot verify that the sequence assignments are correct or reproduce the model-building step. The cryo-EM reconstruction itself is well-documented, but its interpretation depends entirely on a sequence that cannot be checked.

## Strengths
- Cryo-EM density maps and atomic coordinates are deposited with working accessions (PDB, EMDB), enabling independent inspection of the final structures and their quality metrics (resolution, FSC curves, local resolution maps all provided).
- Detailed cryo-EM methods including software versions (RELION 4.0, CTFFIND 4.1, Topaz), imaging parameters, and helical reconstruction workflow are specified with sufficient granularity to attempt replication of the reconstruction from raw micrographs.
- Particle tracing methodology is transparent: the authors provide the logic for selecting particles by resolution cutoff (≥5 Å) and directly map particle coordinates back to micrographs, making the morphology assignment reproducible if raw micrographs are available.

## Weaknesses
- **De novo MS sequence determination cannot be independently verified.** The sequence that enabled atomic model building was determined by Casanovo v4.2.0 and Stitch v1.5.0 applied to tryptic, chymotryptic, and elastase digests. The authors state "The mass spectrometry proteomics data have been deposited to MassIVE (ProteomeXchange). MassIVE accession number: (available to reviewers upon request)." This is a hard flag: the accession is not provided in the manuscript, and 'available on request' means the raw spectra are gated behind author discretion. Without the raw .mgf files or the original Orbitrap .raw files, an independent group cannot re-run Casanovo, validate the de novo peptide assignments, or verify that the sequence inferred is the only or most likely solution. The GitHub repository (https://github.com/pgarrett-scripps/LSPB_AL2_Antibody) contains analysis code and custom Stitch templates but not the spectra themselves. This is the critical input to the entire structural interpretation: if the sequence is wrong, the model is wrong. The authors note that three residues in the constant domain (D134, D175, D176) "were picked by Stitch but are uncommon substitutions" and "may also be asparagines," indicating uncertainty in the de novo call that cannot be resolved without the spectra. Until the raw MS data are deposited with a public accession, this claim is not reproducible.
- **Cryo-EM raw micrographs are not deposited.** The authors collected data at Stanford-SLAC and state that "Images were collected using a 300keV Titan Krios microscope... at the Stanford-SLAC Cryo-EM Center (S2C2)." The density maps (EMDB EMD-48160, EMD-48161) are deposited, but the raw movie frames are not mentioned as deposited anywhere. Without the raw micrographs, an independent group cannot re-run motion correction, CTF estimation, particle picking, or 2D/3D classification to verify the reconstruction or explore whether other morphologies or classes exist. The authors acknowledge in their Discussion that "Particle picking in RELION using Topaz is influenced by the hand-picked training set, which may not capture the full ensemble of fibril morphologies" and that "It remains unclear why so many particles fail to align into the final density maps—possibly reflecting a broader polymorphism than can be resolved." This uncertainty is honest but underscores that reproducibility of the reconstruction requires the raw data.
- **Proteinase K digestion and subsequent MS analysis lack sufficient detail for replication.** The authors used proteinase K digestion to enrich for fibril-core peptides (Fig. 1f), but the digestion protocol is sparse: "27.25 µg of extracted fibrils were digested for 45 minutes at 37° C while shaking at 400 rpm with 5.45 µg of proteinase K." No buffer composition, pH, or stopping conditions beyond "2 µL of phenylmethylsulfonyl fluoride (PMSF)" are given. The resulting peptides were then subjected to tryptic or chymotryptic digestion, but the exact workflow (whether the same sample was split or separate aliquots used, whether desalting occurred between steps) is not specified. This multi-step protocol is presented as supporting evidence for the fibril core boundaries (Fig. 1f, Supplementary Fig. 5) but cannot be independently executed without contacting the authors.
- **Solvation energy calculations are not reproducible without the code.** The authors state: "The stabilization energy per residue was calculated by the sum of the products of the area buried for each atom and the corresponding atomic solvation parameters." They cite a previous publication for the method but do not provide the code, parameters, or intermediate outputs (buried surface areas per residue). Supplementary Fig. 6b,c shows the results, but the calculation cannot be verified or extended without the implementation.
- **APR prediction methodology uses a consensus of ten methods with no threshold specified.** The authors state: "We present the data without a consensus threshold to better showcase the breadth of APRs in the 9ELS fibrils. The score for each residue was calculated as the total number of prediction methods that positively predicted a residue as part of an APR." This is a soft issue (the choice is defensible and disclosed), but the lack of a threshold and the reliance on a consensus score without validation against experimental data means the APR predictions are exploratory and not load-bearing for any claim about aggregation mechanism.
- **Rigid body docking of the single protofilament into the double protofilament map is not quantitatively validated.** The authors state: "The docking resulted in a visually reasonable fit and indicated that Asn 72 and Ser 73 are the residues involved in the protofilament interface." No docking score, RMSD, or cross-correlation metric is provided; the fit is assessed qualitatively. This is a soft issue because the double protofilament structure is not the main claim, but the interface assignment is speculative without quantitative support.
- **Gel electrophoresis densitometry and band assignment (Supplementary Fig. 5) lack quantification.** The authors observe "two species with a molecular weight of ~12 kDa and ~10 kDa" after proteinase K digestion and heat denaturation, and assign these to residues 1–120 with or without residues 41–60. No densitometry, band intensity ratios, or molecular weight standards are reported, so the assignment cannot be verified from the figure alone.
- **Sequence alignment parameters and identity/similarity calculations are not fully specified.** The authors used the Needleman-Wunsch algorithm via EMBOSS Needle but do not state the gap opening/extension penalties or the substitution matrix used (BLOSUM62, PAM, etc.), which affect the reported identity and similarity percentages.

## Questions
- What is the MassIVE accession number for the raw MS data, and when will it be made public (not 'on request')? If the accession is not yet assigned, provide the timeline for deposition.
- Are the raw cryo-EM micrographs deposited in EMPIAR or another public repository, and if so, what is the accession? If not, will they be deposited, and on what timeline?
- For the proteinase K digestion (Fig. 1f), what was the buffer pH and composition, and was the digestion quenched by heating, pH change, or PMSF alone?
- What is the docking score or cross-correlation coefficient for the rigid body fit of the single protofilament model into the double protofilament density map (Fig. 3b)?
- For the solvation energy calculations (Supplementary Fig. 6), what atomic solvation parameters were used, and is the code available?
