# Citation Integrity Auditor

## Summary
This manuscript contains 77 numbered references spanning cryo-EM structures, mass spectrometry methodology, amyloidosis literature, and structural biology. The audit examined reference resolvability, claim–citation support, and numeric fidelity across load-bearing citations. All major references are resolvable via PubMed, DOI, or standard preprint repositories. Four checklist categories were detected and evaluated: reference resolvability, claim–citation support, quotation/number fidelity, and retracted/predatory sources. No critical failures detected. Most primary citations are resolvable, though several claims rest on references that could not be fully verified through available tools (expected for specialized structural biology), and one reference (Hicks et al. 2025 bioRxiv preprint) requires clarification regarding public availability.

## Unverifiable (raise as questions)
- **[Reference resolvability] Hicks et al. (2025) bioRxiv preprint** — Cited as '2025.06.25.661559 Preprint at https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.06.25.661559'. Preprint DOI format is correct, but manuscript is dated as future publication. Cannot confirm contents match claim.
- **[Claim–citation support] IGLV6-57 is the most common LC germline gene implicated in AL amyloidosis** — Manuscript cites refs 11–13 (del Pozo-Yauner et al. 2008, Berghaus et al. 2022, Kourelis et al. 2017). The claim is plausible given Berghaus et al. title mentions 'complete lambda light chain germline usage,' but exact prevalence statement cannot be directly verified from manuscript text alone.
- **[Claim–citation support] IGLV6-57 is predominantly associated with kidney, and to a lesser degree, heart involvement** — Refs 6, 11–13. The claim is specific and quantitative. Berghaus et al. (2022) title explicitly mentions 'heart or kidney involvement,' but exact prevalence ratios are not quoted in the manuscript.
- **[Claim–citation support] IGLV3-1 and IGLV3-19 genes may be structurally related despite somatic variability** — Refs 18, 20 (Radamaker et al. 2021 Nat Commun 12:6434; Karimi-Farsijani et al. 2024 Nat Commun 15:5121). Manuscript states 'Although not yet confirmed, this observation suggests...' — the claim is appropriately hedged as a hypothesis. Cannot verify the specific structural comparison without accessing the papers.
- **[Claim–citation support] The double protofilament morphology has a rare rotational symmetry** — Manuscript claims 'across the 615 publicly available amyloid fibril structures in the Amyloid Atlas, there are only 9 fibril structures with a similar rotational symmetry perpendicular to the fibril axis, of which only 4 are ex vivo fibrils, all derived from brain tissue.' Refs 45–53 cited. The numeric claim (615 structures, 9 with symmetry, 4 ex vivo from brain) is very specific but cannot be independently verified from the manuscript.
- **[Quotation/number fidelity] 80.4% sequence identity between 9ELS and 6HUD (first 112 residues)** — Manuscript states 'The first 112 residues of our structure 9ELS had an 80.4% sequence identity and an 87.5% sequence similarity when compared to 6HUD.' Needleman-Wunsch method cited correctly, but numeric result cannot be verified without access to both sequences. Claim is internally consistent (identity ≤ similarity) and plausible.
- **[Quotation/number fidelity] 83.9% sequence identity between structure and 9OKA** — Numeric precision is stated (83.9%, not ~83%). Cannot verify without running the alignment. Internally consistent with identity < similarity pattern.
- **[Quotation/number fidelity] 11 different amino acids between 6HUD and the authors' structure** — Specific numeric claim stated in manuscript. Cannot verify without inspecting both sequences directly.
- **[Quotation/number fidelity] 8 different amino acids between 9OKA and the authors' structure** — Specific numeric claim stated in manuscript. Cannot verify without inspecting both sequences directly.
- **[Quotation/number fidelity] 615 publicly available amyloid fibril structures in the Amyloid Atlas** — Very specific count attributed to Ref 29 (Sawaya et al. 2021 Cell 184:4857–4873). Cannot verify this exact number without consulting the Amyloid Atlas database directly.
