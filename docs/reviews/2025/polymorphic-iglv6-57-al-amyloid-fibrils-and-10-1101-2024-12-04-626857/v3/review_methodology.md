# Methodology Reviewer

## Summary
The cryo-EM structural determination is technically sound and the fibril extraction and characterization are well-executed, but the core claim about a 'shared folding pathway' for IGLV6-57 fibrils rests on visual inspection of three structures without a quantitative framework to distinguish genuine mechanistic convergence from coincidental structural similarity. The polymorphism observation is novel but the design does not establish how the two morphologies arise or coexist. The paper makes a solid incremental contribution to AL amyloid structure but overstates the evidence for its mechanistic conclusions.

## Strengths
- The cryo-EM reconstruction achieves high resolution (3.0 Å for single protofilament) with appropriate validation through FSC curves, local resolution analysis, and independent half-map refinement, supporting the atomic model reliability.
- De novo mass spectrometry sequencing with multiple proteases and proteinase K enrichment provides strong orthogonal validation of the fibril composition and core boundaries, reducing ambiguity in sequence assignment.
- Particle tracing directly correlates morphology to position in raw micrographs, providing genuine evidence that single and double protofilaments coexist on the same fibrils rather than being separate populations.

## Weaknesses
- The 'shared folding pathway' claim (Figure 6, Discussion) is the paper's central mechanistic contribution, yet it rests entirely on visual comparison of three structures without quantitative support. The authors identify a conserved C-shaped turn and note that the N-terminus adopts different positions, then propose a branching pathway where structures 'diverge' based on residue differences (Tyr 100, position 32, position 24). However, this is post-hoc pattern recognition: the three sequences differ at 8–11 residues in the core, and the paper provides no calculation showing which differences are necessary or sufficient to produce the observed conformational variants. A mutation-scanning experiment, energy landscape analysis, or even a systematic enumeration of which residue combinations predict which N-terminus position would be needed to establish causation. As written, the pathway is a narrative fit to existing data, not a testable mechanism. An alternative explanation—that the observed structures represent a continuum of conformations sampled during fibril growth, with the three examples being arbitrary snapshots—fits the data equally well and is not ruled out.
- The double protofilament morphology is described as having 'rare rotational symmetry' and raising 'new questions about fibril formation,' but the design does not establish whether this is a genuine distinct assembly pathway or a transient intermediate. The authors observe both morphologies on the same fibrils and propose three speculative assembly mechanisms (secondary nucleation, independent nucleation with wrapping, or simultaneous elongation at different rates) but provide no evidence to distinguish them. The particle tracing shows a minor population with both morphologies coexisting, but does not establish directionality, kinetics, or whether one converts to the other. The claim that 'each of the protofilaments in the double protofilament morphology are oriented in an antipolar fashion' (Supplementary Fig. 8) is stated without showing the figure or explaining what this implies mechanistically. Without time-resolved data, kinetic measurements, or a structural mechanism linking the two forms, the morphology observation is descriptive, not mechanistic.
- The solvation energy calculation (Supplementary Fig. 6) reports that IGLV6 fibrils have 'the lowest solvation energy of all ex-vivo AL fibril structures,' yet the authors then argue that 'multiple interactions' stabilize the structure despite this apparent thermodynamic disadvantage. This is internally inconsistent: if the solvation energy is lowest (most favorable), the structure should be more stable, not less. The resolution of this contradiction—whether the calculation is relative to unfolded monomer, whether it accounts for entropy, or whether it is simply a per-layer metric that does not capture the full fibril—is not explained. This undermines the claim that the structure is stabilized by specific electrostatic and hydrophobic interactions rather than by favorable solvation.
- The comparison of three IGLV6-57 structures (9ELS, 6HUD, 9OKA) is presented as evidence for a shared fold, but no quantitative structural alignment, RMSD calculation, or overlay is provided; the claim rests on visual inspection of Figure 5 and text description of the C-shaped turn. Without metrics, it is unclear whether the structures are truly similar or whether the authors are emphasizing superficial resemblances (both have a turn, both have an N-terminus) while downplaying differences in backbone geometry, side-chain orientation, or packing density.
- The APR analysis (Figure 4, AmylPred2) identifies multiple aggregation-prone regions across the sequence, including in CDR 2, which is absent from the fibril core in all three IGLV6 structures but present in IGLV1 and IGLV3 structures. The authors note this discrepancy but do not explain it mechanistically or test whether CDR 2 is genuinely non-aggregating in IGLV6 or whether it is excluded by a different mechanism (e.g., proteolytic cleavage, steric occlusion). This leaves a key observation unresolved.
- The paper does not report whether the two morphologies differ in stability, growth rate, or propensity to seed further fibrils, which would be necessary to understand their biological relevance or assembly hierarchy.
- No analysis is provided of whether the observed structures are artifacts of the extraction, grid preparation, or cryo-freezing conditions, which the authors themselves acknowledge as potential sources of bias in the Discussion but do not experimentally address.
- The claim that Arg 24 'stabilizes electrostatic forces' (Discussion) by interacting with Asp 85, Asp 88, and Tyr 90 is not quantified; no energy decomposition, hydrogen-bonding analysis, or comparison to the 6HUD Gly 24 variant is provided to show that this interaction is stabilizing rather than neutral or destabilizing.

## Questions
- Figure 6b proposes a branching point where the N-terminus adopts an interior or exterior position based on 'specific residue differences' or 'interactions with cofactors'; what experimental or computational evidence would distinguish these two mechanisms, and have the authors tested whether the observed structures can interconvert under different conditions?
- The double protofilament morphology is resolved at 3.78 Å but the authors state 'the map quality was insufficient to directly model the structure' and instead use docking of the single protofilament model; how confident is the docking fit, and does the resolution limit permit reliable identification of the protofilament interface residues (Asn 72, Ser 73)?
- Supplementary Figure 8 is cited to support the claim that antipolar protofilaments expose both 'plus' and 'minus' elongation sites, but this figure is not shown in the main text; what is the structural basis for this polarity assignment, and how does it differ from the single protofilament morphology?
- The authors note that 'it remains unclear why so many particles fail to align into the final density maps' (Discussion), with only ~80,000 particles from 1.4 million contributing to the two final maps; could this reflect a broader ensemble of morphologies not captured by the two reported structures, and how would this affect the claim of a shared folding pathway?
