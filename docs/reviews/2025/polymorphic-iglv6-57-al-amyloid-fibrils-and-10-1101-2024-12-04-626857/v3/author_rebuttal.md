## Concessions
- **reproducibility** — Raw MS/MS spectra underlying the de novo sequence assignment are not publicly deposited; the manuscript lists the MassIVE accession as 'available to reviewers upon request,' and three constant-domain residues (D134, D175, D176) are flagged as ambiguous.
  - Proposed change: We will deposit the complete raw MS/MS dataset (.raw files and converted .mgf files for trypsin, chymotrypsin, elastase, and proteinase-K-digested samples) to MassIVE/ProteomeXchange prior to publication, replace 'available to reviewers upon request' with the public accession number in the Data Availability statement, and add a sentence in Methods explicitly noting the ambiguity at D134/D175/D176 and how it was resolved or left unresolved in the deposited data.
- **rigor** — The 'shared folding pathway' is elevated to the manuscript title and given a dedicated figure (Fig. 6b) despite the authors' own statement that the data cannot support or exclude alternative models.
  - Proposed change: We will revise the title to remove 'shared folding pathway' (e.g., to 'Polymorphic IGLV6-57 AL amyloid fibrils reveal conserved structural motifs'), relabel Fig. 6b as 'a proposed, untested folding hypothesis,' and add one sentence in the Discussion explicitly stating that convergent sampling of a common conformational landscape is an equally plausible alternative not excluded by our data.
- **methodology** — The claim of a shared fold among 9ELS, 6HUD, and 9OKA rests on visual comparison without quantitative structural metrics.
  - Proposed change: We will compute and report pairwise Cα RMSD (after core-region superposition) and, where feasible, TM-scores between 9ELS and each of 6HUD and 9OKA, and add these values to Figure 5 and the corresponding Results text.
- **methodology** — There is an unresolved internal tension between the claim that IGLV6 fibrils have the 'lowest' (least favorable) solvation energy of all ex-vivo AL structures and the simultaneous claim that the structure is well 'stabilized' by other interactions.
  - Proposed change: We will revise the text accompanying Supplementary Fig. 6 to explicitly define the sign convention of the per-residue solvation energy metric, clarify that it captures only the burial contribution to stability, and state directly that the observed compensatory electrostatic/hydrophobic/interlocking interactions are proposed to offset this comparatively unfavorable burial term, resolving the apparent contradiction rather than leaving it implicit.
- **data_analysis** — APR prediction scores (0-10) are presented without a pre-specified threshold defining a 'strongly predicted' APR, and the specific regions highlighted appear to be chosen post-hoc.
  - Proposed change: We will add an explicit statement in Methods defining 'strongly predicted APR' as a consensus score ≥7/10 methods, apply this threshold uniformly when identifying regions in the Results text and Figure 4, and note explicitly that sub-threshold regions are exploratory only.
- **ethics** — The specific IRB protocol number/approving committee and explicit de-identification/HIPAA safeguard statement are not provided in the manuscript.
  - Proposed change: We will add the IRB protocol number and approving committee name to the Methods (Histology/Extraction sections) and add one sentence confirming that patient data were de-identified in accordance with institutional HIPAA-compliant protocols prior to analysis and publication.
- **literature** — The Introduction cites refs. 11-13 for IGLV6-57 organ tropism but omits the more recent Morgan et al. 2025 AL-base update, which is cited only in the Acknowledgements.
  - Proposed change: We will add a citation to Morgan et al. 2025 (Amyloid 32:129-138) alongside references 11-13 in the Introduction paragraph discussing germline gene-organ tropism associations.
- **reproducibility** — The rigid-body docking fit of the single protofilament model into the double protofilament map is described only as 'visually reasonable' without a quantitative score.
  - Proposed change: We will report the cross-correlation coefficient (and/or a clash/overlap score) for the COOT rigid-body docking fit in the Results and Methods, and add one sentence discussing how the 3.78 Å resolution limits confidence in the Asn72/Ser73 interface assignment.
- **clarity** — The proteinase K enrichment argument (Fig. 1f) is described qualitatively ('reduced spectral counts... and vice versa') without reporting actual fold-change values.
  - Proposed change: We will add a supplementary table reporting the actual spectral counts and fold-change for core versus non-core residues before and after proteinase K digestion, cross-referenced in the Fig. 1f legend.
- **literature** — The claim of being 'the first cardiac amyloid fibril with 2-fold rotational symmetry' is placed adjacent to the Amyloid Atlas-wide rarity statistic (9/615), creating ambiguity about which claim is being made.
  - Proposed change: We will add one clarifying sentence explicitly separating the two claims: stating first that no other published cardiac AL or non-AL amyloid structure has this symmetry, and second, as separate context, that this symmetry is rare (9/615) across the entire Amyloid Atlas regardless of tissue type, including the access date and query parameters used.

## Disagreements
- **reproducibility** — Proteinase K protection mapping and intact-mass sizing only confirm approximate boundaries/mass, not amino-acid identity, so the de novo MS sequence cannot be considered validated absent raw spectra.
  - Response: We agree the raw spectra (now committed for deposition) are the definitive check on the full-length sequence, but the atomic model itself provides an independent, structure-based cross-validation of every ordered core residue that is not solely dependent on the MS call. Bulky and aromatic side chains show clear, unambiguous density fit at 3.0 Å/2.8 Å local resolution, and the model passes standard geometry/clash validation in MolProbity and real-space refinement statistics reported in Supplementary Table 1. An incorrect MS assignment in the ordered core would likely manifest as poor density fit or refinement outliers, which we did not observe.
  - Manuscript reference: 'We then used the MS-predicted sequence to model the atomic structure (Fig. 2e).' and 'Local resolution of the map is best at the center of the core at 2.8Å' (Results, 'A high-resolution density map reveals the amyloid fold of IGLV6-derived light chains').
- **rigor** — The claim that particle tracing data 'supports' a specific double-protofilament assembly mechanism overstates what mere coexistence of morphologies on a fibril can demonstrate.
  - Response: The manuscript already explicitly withholds endorsing any single mechanism and enumerates three alternatives as speculative possibilities rather than a supported conclusion; the critique mischaracterizes the framing as more confident than what is written.
  - Manuscript reference: 'Our data are not sufficient to support or eliminate these models. To completely define the mechanisms by which these polymorphs form and mature, further study is warranted.' (Discussion, paragraph on double protofilament assembly).
- **novelty** — The particle tracing evidence for coexisting polymorphs on a single fibril is purely descriptive and could reflect an artifact of 2D projection rather than genuine 3D coexistence.
  - Response: Particle tracing does not rely on 2D projection alone; it maps each particle's real-space (x,y) coordinate from the RELION particles.star file directly onto the corresponding raw micrograph, so morphology assignment is tied to physical position along an actual fibril in the image, not inferred solely from a 2D class average's appearance.
  - Manuscript reference: 'We then leveraged RELION's particle coordinate data to directly correlate the position of the particle in the raw micrograph to the protofilament morphology that it belongs to.' (Results, 'Particle tracing reveals the coexistence of polymorphs on the same fibril'; Methods, 'Particle tracing').
- **literature** — The claim of 'rare rotational symmetry' is ambiguous because the text simultaneously claims novelty ('first cardiac amyloid fibril with this symmetry') and cites a broader rarity statistic (9/615 in the Amyloid Atlas), conflating two distinct claims.
  - Response: The two claims are already textually distinct and sequential: the first sentence scopes the novelty claim specifically to cardiac amyloid fibrils, and the second, introduced with 'In fact,' provides separate corroborating context about rarity across the full Atlas regardless of tissue origin. We do not believe this constitutes conflation, though we have proposed a minor added sentence (see concessions) to make the boundary between the two claims even more explicit.
  - Manuscript reference: 'This double protofilament morphology represents the first cardiac amyloid fibril with 2-fold rotational symmetry perpendicular to the fibril axis (Fig. 3a). In fact, across the 615 publicly available amyloid fibril structures in the Amyloid Atlas, there are only 9 fibril structures with a similar rotational symmetry perpendicular to the fibril axis, of which only 4 are ex vivo fibrils, all derived from brain tissue.' (Discussion).

## Load-bearing critiques
- Raw MS/MS spectra underlying the de novo sequence assignment that anchors the entire atomic model are not currently publicly accessible (listed as 'available to reviewers upon request'), leaving the load-bearing sequence unverifiable by an independent party until deposited.
- The 'shared folding pathway' hypothesis is currently promoted to the manuscript's title and given a dedicated figure despite the authors' own acknowledgment that the data cannot support or exclude alternative explanations; if left unreframed, this constitutes overclaiming of the paper's central mechanistic identity rather than a hedged discussion point.
