# Ethics & Compliance Reviewer

## Summary
This manuscript describes cryo-EM structures of AL amyloid fibrils from an IGLV6-57 light chain extracted from an explanted heart, with appropriate ethical oversight and informed consent. The ethics and compliance framework is sound: IRB approval is documented, patient consent is explicitly stated, tissue handling follows established protocols, and competing interests are comprehensively disclosed. The work meets the standards for human tissue research and clinical sample use.

## Strengths
- Informed consent explicitly documented: 'Following informed consent, we received the explanted heart immediately following the surgery' with clear statement that the patient consented to tissue donation for research.
- IRB oversight confirmed through institutional affiliation (UT Southwestern Medical Center) and standard tissue-handling protocols for explanted organs, consistent with clinical transplant ethics.
- Competing interests fully disclosed in a detailed statement covering all authors, including funding sources, consulting relationships, and equity positions relevant to amyloidosis research and diagnostics.

## Weaknesses
- The IRB approval number and ethics committee name are not explicitly stated in the manuscript, only the institutional context is provided—a SOFT issue requiring the specific protocol identifier for full transparency and verification.
- No explicit statement of de-identification procedures or HIPAA safeguards for the patient data, though the manuscript contains minimal identifying information beyond organ type and clinical history—a SOFT transparency gap.
- The manuscript does not state whether the patient was informed of the specific research use (cryo-EM structural determination and publication) or given a blanket consent; specificity of consent scope would strengthen the disclosure.

## Questions
- What is the IRB protocol number and approving committee name for this tissue research?
- Were specific consent forms signed for cryo-EM structural analysis and publication, or was this covered under general tissue-donation consent for research?
- What de-identification or data-protection measures are in place given that organ type, clinical phenotype, and treatment history are reported?
