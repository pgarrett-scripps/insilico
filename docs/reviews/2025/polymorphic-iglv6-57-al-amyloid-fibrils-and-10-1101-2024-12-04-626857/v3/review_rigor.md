# Rigor & Overclaiming Reviewer

## Summary
The structural characterization is rigorous and the cryo-EM data are of high quality, but the paper makes several claims about shared folding pathways and mechanistic insights that exceed what the evidence supports. The comparison of three IGLV6-57 structures is observational and cannot establish a unified pathway; the double-protofilament morphology's assembly mechanism is speculative; and claims about sequence-driven aggregation are not tested experimentally. The work is a solid structural contribution but requires substantial hedging of its mechanistic conclusions.

## Strengths
- De novo mass spectrometry sequencing and validation against proteinase K digestion provides strong evidence for the fibril composition and core boundaries.
- High-resolution cryo-EM structures (3.0 Å for single protofilament) with detailed atomic modeling and multiple independent validation approaches (western blot, intact MS, proteolysis patterns) establish the structural data reliably.
- Particle tracing to identify coexisting polymorphs on single fibrils is a methodologically sound approach that adds genuine insight into fibril heterogeneity.

## Weaknesses
- **Shared folding pathway claim (title, abstract, Fig. 6).** The authors propose a unified folding mechanism based on three IGLV6-57 structures with 80–84% sequence identity and 8–11 residue differences in the core. The evidence is purely structural comparison: all three share a C-shaped turn and a disulfide bond reversal. However, this is consistent with multiple alternative explanations: (1) the C-shaped turn may be a local energetic minimum accessible from multiple unfolded states without implying a single pathway; (2) the disulfide reversal is observed in all published AL fibrils regardless of germline origin, suggesting it is a generic consequence of fibril formation rather than evidence of a shared pathway; (3) the N-terminal positioning differences (interior vs. exterior) are attributed to specific residues (e.g., Tyr 100) but no mutagenesis or kinetic data test whether these residues actually control the divergence. The paper presents a plausible story but does not distinguish it from the null hypothesis that sequence variation simply samples different local minima of a broader conformational landscape. The claim should be reframed as 'consistent with a shared pathway' or supported by experimental perturbation.
- **Assembly mechanism of double protofilament morphology.** The authors state 'This is supported by the particle tracing data, in which we observe the presence of single protofilaments extended from double protofilament morphologies' (Discussion). However, the tracing data show coexistence of morphologies on the same fibril, not directionality of assembly. A single protofilament extending from a double protofilament is equally consistent with secondary nucleation, lateral association, or dissociation. The three assembly models proposed (secondary nucleation, independent nucleation followed by wrapping, simultaneous elongation at different rates) are all speculative and the data do not distinguish among them. The statement that particle tracing 'supports' a specific mechanism overstates what coexistence demonstrates.
- **Sequence-driven aggregation and APR analysis.** The paper identifies aggregation-prone regions (APRs) computationally and notes that CDR2 is absent from the fibril core in IGLV6-derived structures but present in IGLV1 and IGLV3 structures. The authors state 'What role APRs near CDR 2 play in the aggregation of AL fibrils is yet to be determined' but do not test this experimentally. The claim that specific residues (e.g., Arg 24, Tyr 100) 'drive aggregation' or 'contribute to fibril stability' is inferred from structural proximity and energetic calculations, not from kinetic assays, mutagenesis, or stability measurements. The solvation energy calculation (Supplementary Fig. 6) shows IGLV6 fibrils have the lowest predicted energy, but this is a post-hoc observation on three structures and does not establish that sequence differences drive the observed morphologies or that the pathway is sequence-dependent as claimed in the abstract.

## Questions
- Figure 5 shows 8–11 amino acid substitutions between the three IGLV6-57 structures in the fibril core; which of these are necessary or sufficient to produce the observed structural differences, and has mutagenesis been performed to test this?
- The paper states the double protofilament morphology has 'rare rotational symmetry' (180° perpendicular to the fibril axis) and cites only 9 of 615 amyloid structures in the Amyloid Atlas with similar symmetry—is this frequency sufficient to call it rare, or is it a known feature of certain fibril classes?
- Proteinase K digestion shows reduced spectral counts for residues predicted outside the fibril core (Fig. 1f), but the authors do not quantify the fold-enrichment or statistical significance of this difference; what is the magnitude of proteolytic protection?
- The paper proposes that the N-terminus adopts interior or exterior positions based on 'specific residue differences' and 'interactions with cofactors found within the tissue environment'—were any cofactors identified in the mass spectrometry analysis of the fibril extract, and do they correlate with morphology?
