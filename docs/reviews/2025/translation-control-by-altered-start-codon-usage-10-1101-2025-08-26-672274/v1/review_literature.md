# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript presents a well-grounded study of translational control via start codon usage in Listeria monocytogenes, with generally accurate attribution of prior work and appropriate citation of foundational literature on SigB regulation, acid resistance, and translation initiation. The core claims about SigB function, the stressosome, and the role of RsbW are well-supported by cited sources. However, there is one significant gap in the literature on start codon flexibility in Listeria, and one claim about translation efficiency hierarchy requires clarification against the cited evidence. Three load-bearing claims need minor strengthening: the start codon efficiency hierarchy (ATG > ATA > ATT) relies on Hecht et al. 2017 which does not directly rank these three codons in the same genomic context; the flexible start codon usage in 39 conserved genes lacks comparison to prior work on start codon polymorphism in Listeria or related pathogens; and the RsbW stoichiometric perturbation inference lacks direct measurement of endogenous RsbW protein levels in the ARD strains.

## Strengths
- The manuscript correctly cites and accurately represents the cryo-EM structure of the stressosome (Williams et al. 2019, ref. 8) and the 2:1 RsbR:RsbS stoichiometry it revealed, grounding the mechanistic model.
- The recent Cherrak et al. (2024, ref. 36) citation on non-canonical start codons conferring selective advantage in E. coli is appropriately positioned as a parallel example and is accurately described—the paper does show GTG reducing lacI translation to enhance fitness in the murine gut.
- Key citations on SigB-dependent acid resistance (Cotter et al. 2001 on glutamate decarboxylase; Guerreiro et al. 2020 on SigB-mediated GSR; Wu et al. 2023 on GadR) are primary sources and correctly attributed.
- All references to the stressosome structure, RsbX function, and the sigB operon organization are consistent with cited sources (refs. 8, 12, 19, 33); no contradictions detected.
- The manuscript cites ref. 60 (Asakura et al. 2012) for the observation that intrahost passage alters SigB-dependent acid resistance, and the citation is accurate.
- The claim that Shine-Dalgarno strength does not correlate closely with translational efficiency in Listeria (line 410) appropriately cites ref. 62 (Bryant et al. 2023), a recent comparative study of Salmonella and Listeria translational landscapes.

## Weaknesses
- The manuscript reports (Figure 4B, C) that ATG > ATA > ATT translation efficiency and cites Hecht et al. (2017, ref. 61), but that paper measured initiation rates from all 64 codons in E. coli and reported absolute initiation frequencies, not a direct comparison of ATG vs. ATA vs. ATT in the same genomic context; the manuscript's own reporter data directly demonstrates the hierarchy in L. monocytogenes, which is stronger evidence than the E. coli reference.
- The manuscript identifies 39 genes with flexible start codon usage across 60,692 L. monocytogenes genomes and reports that 13 are involved in virulence or stress response, but does not cite prior work systematically documenting start codon polymorphism in Listeria or comparing it to other pathogens, leaving the novelty claim underexamined.
- The manuscript proposes that reduced RsbW translation (via non-canonical start codons) or loss of RsbW (via nonsense mutations) increases SigB activity, but does not directly measure RsbW protein levels in the ARD strains to confirm that the stoichiometric perturbation actually occurs; the inference relies on the reporter assay in a heterologous context (pIMK3 plasmid) rather than measurement of endogenous RsbW in the ARD strains themselves.
- The manuscript states that GTG SC usage for rsbS might achieve correct stressosome subunit stoichiometry (line 277) but does not cite Listeria-specific experimental evidence that GTG initiation is weaker than ATG; ref. 36 (Cherrak et al. 2024) and ref. 61 (Hecht et al. 2017) provide E. coli data only.
- The manuscript does not cite any work on the evolutionary drivers of start codon selection in pathogens under host-specific selective pressure, beyond the Cherrak et al. (2024) example, which is a gap in the discussion of mechanism.

## Questions
- For Figure 4B–C (start codon efficiency): does Hecht et al. (2017) specifically rank ATG, ATA, and ATT in that order, or are you inferring the hierarchy from their broader codon table? If the latter, please clarify that your own reporter data are the primary evidence.
- Has flexible start codon usage in Listeria core genes been previously documented in the literature, or is the population-level analysis of 39 genes with variable start codons novel to this work? A brief statement would clarify the novelty.
- Do you have quantitative Western-blot measurements of RsbW protein levels in ARD1, ARD6, ARD10, and ARD12 compared to MQ140025, or does the stoichiometric perturbation rest entirely on the reporter assay inference?
