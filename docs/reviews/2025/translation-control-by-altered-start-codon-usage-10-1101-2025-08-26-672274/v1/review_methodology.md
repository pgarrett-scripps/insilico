# Methodology Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript presents a well-executed experimental investigation of translational control via start codon usage in Listeria monocytogenes, with solid evidence for the core mechanism in rsbW. However, the leap from a single-gene mechanism (rsbW) to a genome-wide claim about start codon selection as a general regulatory strategy is not fully supported by the design. The population-level analysis of 60K genomes identifies correlations between start codon usage and genetic clades but cannot establish that these codons were selected for their regulatory effect rather than fixed by drift or other mechanisms. The reporter assays for plcB and lftR provide only weak evidence that canonical start codon differences modulate expression in vivo.

## Strengths
- The in vitro evolution experiment is well-designed with appropriate controls (parental strain, reference strain F2365, multiple independent replicates) and clearly isolates rsbW mutations as the driver of acid resistance recovery.
- Translational reporter assays convincingly demonstrate that ATG > ATA > ATT in translation efficiency for rsbW, with both fluorescence and Western blot corroboration, establishing the mechanism for the ARD phenotypes.
- The phylogenetic analysis of MQ140025 contextualizes the mutations within ST1 evolutionary history and strengthens the claim that the rsbW lesions are transient, niche-specific adaptations.

## Weaknesses
- The central claim—that start codon usage is 'under selection for stress response and virulence genes'—rests on observing that 39 conserved genes show flexible start codon usage and that 13 of these are involved in virulence/stress response. This is a correlation, not evidence of selection. The design does not exclude drift, lineage-specific mutational biases, or structural/codon-usage constraints as explanations for the observed patterns. To establish selection, the authors would need to show that the alternative start codon confers a fitness advantage in the relevant niche, or demonstrate that the pattern is inconsistent with neutral evolution (e.g., via dN/dS analysis or phylogenetic comparative methods). The statement 'these data suggest an evolutionary preference for the usage of specific SC for these genes possibly associated with niche-specific selective pressures' (line 425) is appropriately hedged, but the title and abstract overstate the evidence by claiming start codons are 'under selection' rather than 'show clade-associated variation.'
- The reporter assays for plcB and lftR (Figure 5C) show only modest differences in fluorescence between ATG and GTG (described as 'small reduction'), and the manuscript does not establish that this magnitude of difference is biologically meaningful in the context of virulence or stress response. No dose-response relationship is tested, no measurement of endogenous expression levels is provided, and no functional assay (e.g., cell invasion, survival in macrophages) links the altered translation rate to a phenotypic outcome. The claim that 'SC flexibility might provide a genetic means of adjusting gene expression, potentially as a means of enhancing niche specific fitness' (line 302) is speculative without this functional validation.
- The design does not rule out the alternative explanation that the rsbW start codon mutations in ARDs are a byproduct of selection for loss-of-function of RsbW itself (via nonsense mutations in ARD3 and ARD5) rather than selection for reduced translation rate. The fact that both nonsense mutations and start codon substitutions occur in the same gene, and both increase SigB activity, suggests that the common target is RsbW inactivation, not fine-tuning of RsbW stoichiometry. The authors argue that 'the primary SC of rsbW accounts for the functional expression under laboratory conditions' (line 248), but this does not exclude the possibility that the start codon mutations are simply a less-efficient route to the same end (loss of RsbW function) that happens to be selected when nonsense mutations are unavailable or deleterious in some genetic background.

## Questions
- Does the phylogenetic distribution of start codon variants in the 39 flexible genes show a pattern inconsistent with neutral drift (e.g., are changes concentrated at recent branching points, or do they predate lineage divergence)?
- What is the endogenous expression level of plcB and lftR in strains carrying ATG vs. GTG, and does this correlate with virulence phenotypes in infection models?
- For the ARDs, is the fitness cost of hyperactive SigB (reduced growth, smaller colonies) the same for start codon mutants as for nonsense mutants, or does the reduced translation rate confer a selective advantage by partially restoring RsbW function?
- The manuscript states that 'the Shine-Dalgarno strength does not correlate closely with translational efficiency' in L. monocytogenes (line 462)—is there evidence that start codon identity, rather than RBS context, is the primary determinant of translation initiation rate in this species?
