## Concessions
- **reproducibility** — The genome-wide start-codon calling pipeline, code, and processed dataset underlying the 39-gene/60,690-genome analysis are not deposited or accessible, making the claim unverifiable.
  - Proposed change: Deposit the custom tblastn scripts, parameter files, and full processed output (start codon call per gene per genome, gene presence/absence matrix, list of genome accessions used) to a public repository (Zenodo/GitHub) with a permanent DOI, cite this in Methods and add a Data Availability statement; include Table S1 as a downloadable supplementary spreadsheet with the revised submission.
- **reproducibility** — ARD Illumina reads/VCFs, MQ140025 plasmid sequences, and reporter plasmid sequences are not deposited, preventing independent verification of the rsbW mutations and constructs.
  - Proposed change: Deposit ARD1, 3, 5, 6, 10, 12 Illumina reads to SRA under BioProject PRJNA1305691 along with resulting VCF files as supplementary data; deposit the pLM7UG1-like and pLMGC1 plasmid sequences to GenBank; deposit the sequences of the three pIMK3-rsbW-eGFP constructs (ATG/ATA/ATT) and the pIMK3-plcB/lftR constructs as supplementary FASTA files.
- **data_analysis** — Statistical test details for qPCR (exact p-values, multiple-comparison correction) and definition of error bars are not disclosed.
  - Proposed change: Add to Methods the exact statistical test used (paired two-tailed t-test), report exact p-values for all Figure 2 comparisons in a supplementary table, apply Benjamini-Hochberg correction across the gene x strain comparisons and report corrected q-values, and state explicitly in each figure legend whether error bars are SEM or SD and how many independent experiments were averaged.
- **data_analysis** — Motility phenotype (LM/IM/HM) is categorical without a quantitative metric or reported n.
  - Proposed change: Report a quantitative motility metric (halo diameter in mm at a fixed time point, n≥3 independent platings per strain) in a supplementary table and cross-reference it against the LM/IM/HM categories used in Figure 2B.
- **clarity** — Abbreviations (GSR, ARD, CC, ST, PMSC) are used before being defined, and Table S1 is cited but not provided.
  - Proposed change: Define GSR, ARD, CC, ST and PMSC at first use in the Abstract/Introduction, and include Table S1 as a downloadable supplementary spreadsheet accompanying the revised manuscript.
- **methodology** — The title and Results header assert start codon usage is 'under selection' when the evidence is correlational (clade clustering) without dN/dS analysis or a direct fitness assay.
  - Proposed change: Revise the title, abstract, and the Results header ('SC are under selection for stress response and virulence genes') to 'SC usage shows clade-associated variation in stress response and virulence genes,' and add a paragraph to the Discussion explicitly acknowledging drift, founder effects, and hitchhiking as alternative explanations not excluded by the current phylogenetic association data.
- **novelty** — RsbV G111C/S substitutions co-occur with the rsbW start-codon changes in all relevant ARDs, and the reporter assay tests SC identity only in a heterologous pIMK3/MQ140025 background, not in the ARDs' own genetic background, leaving the causal driver of SigB hyperactivity ambiguous.
  - Proposed change: Construct an isogenic rsbW-ATA (and/or ATT) allelic-exchange mutant at the native locus that lacks the linked RsbV G111C/S substitution, and measure colony morphology, motility, and SigB-dependent transcripts (gadD3, lmo2230, inlA) to directly test whether the start-codon change alone reproduces the ARD phenotype.
- **clarity** — The connection between the rsbW finding and the genome-wide start-codon survey is only stated retrospectively (line 248), obscuring the paper's argument structure.
  - Proposed change: Add an explicit sentence at the end of the Introduction stating that the rsbW findings motivate a genome-wide survey of start codon usage across the species, and move the transition sentence connecting rsbW to the genome-wide analysis to precede rather than follow the sigB-operon SC section.
- **ethics** — No explicit biosafety statement is given for handling a food-borne pathogen.
  - Proposed change: Add one sentence to Methods stating the biosafety containment level (BSL-2) and institutional biosafety approval/reference under which the work was conducted.
- **clarity** — IPTG induction concentration (1 mM) is not stated until very late in the Methods (line 516), creating ambiguity about consistency across reporter experiments.
  - Proposed change: State the inducer concentration (1 mM IPTG) explicitly at first mention in the Fluorescence quantification Methods subsection and confirm in the text that it was used consistently for all reporter assays (Fig 4B, 5C).
- **data_analysis** — Western blot quantification procedure, replicate numbers, and the threshold for 'no detectable signal' are not specified.
  - Proposed change: Add to Methods a description of the densitometry procedure (software, normalization to loading control), state the number of blots quantified (n=3), and define 'no detectable signal' as below a specified fold-over-background threshold determined from the pIMK3-only control.

## Disagreements
- **novelty** — The rsbW start-codon effect cannot be distinguished from the co-occurring RsbV G111C/S substitution or from simple RsbW loss-of-function, since nonsense mutants (ARD3/ARD5) produce the same SigB-hyperactive phenotype.
  - Response: Our existing data already show a graded, dose-dependent phenotype inconsistent with a uniform binary loss-of-function model. ARD1 (rsbW ATT, the lowest-translating codon in our reporter assay) phenocopies the nonsense mutants ARD3 and ARD5 in colony size and motility (grouped as LM), whereas ARD6/10/12 (rsbW ATA, higher residual translation) show a distinct intermediate phenotype (IM) with correspondingly lower induction of gadD3, lmo2230 and inlA than the LM group. This graded correspondence between measured translation efficiency (ATT<ATA<ATG) and phenotypic severity is difficult to reconcile with all rsbW lesions acting as simple binary loss-of-function alleles, and instead supports the stoichiometric-perturbation model, independent of whether RsbV is also altered.
  - Manuscript reference: "While the parental strain MQ140025 displayed hyper-motility (HM); ARD1, ARD3, and ARD5 were characterized by smaller colony size and low motility (LM)...ARD6, ARD10, and ARD12 were characterized by normal colony size and intermediate motility (IM)...gadD3, lmo2230, and inlA were all induced to a higher level in LM group (ARD1, 3 and 5) than IM group (ARD6, 10 and 12; Figure 2 C,F,G)."
- **literature** — The ATG>ATA>ATT translation-efficiency hierarchy is attributed to Hecht et al. 2017, which measured all 64 codons individually in E. coli but did not directly rank ATG, ATA and ATT together in one context.
  - Response: We agree the E. coli literature is cited only as general precedent that non-canonical start codons remain functional at reduced rates; the specific ATG>ATA>ATT hierarchy for rsbW in L. monocytogenes is established entirely by our own reporter data, using both fluorescence and independent Western blot quantification, not inferred from Hecht et al. We will clarify in the text that the citation provides supporting precedent rather than the source of the ranking.
  - Manuscript reference: "The induction of a fluorescence signal by IPTG was clearly decreased when using ATA as the SC compared to ATG, while there was no detectable signal when using ATT as SC (Figure 4B)...These data suggested that the relative translation rates from these three start codons were ATG > ATA > ATT."
- **methodology** — The in vitro evolution protocol's 1:2500 dilution bottleneck may not accurately mimic gastric passage, and the relevance of selected mutations to natural infection is not established.
  - Response: The acid-challenge protocol was designed to impose repeated bottleneck/recovery cycles at lethal pH analogous to the selective pressure of gastric passage, not to precisely replicate in vivo gastric transit kinetics. It reproduces the specific selection dynamic of interest (survival of lethal, GI-tract-relevant pH followed by recovery and regrowth), following a validated protocol from our own prior work, and the reproducible emergence of independent rsbW mutations under this pressure across three independent lineages is itself a meaningful empirical result regardless of the precise fidelity to in vivo transit times.
  - Manuscript reference: "Protocols for acid challenge experiments were adapted from a previous study [28]...For in vitro adaptation experiments, at each indicated time point 2 µL of sample was removed and diluted into 5 mL BHI unacidified and incubated at 37°C with agitation (160 rpm) to recover for 24 h."
- **rigor** — The claim that GTG usage in rsbS achieves correct stressosome stoichiometry lacks Listeria-specific evidence beyond citations of E. coli/general bacterial studies.
  - Response: This statement is explicitly presented as a hypothesis-generating interpretation using hedged language, not as an established mechanistic claim, and is not load-bearing for any of the paper's central conclusions regarding rsbW or the ARD phenotypes.
  - Manuscript reference: "It seems plausible that GTG SC usage for rsbS might be a means of achieving the correct stressosome subunit stoichiometry, which is reported to be 2:1 for RsbR:RsbS."
- **novelty** — The 5% threshold for defining 'flexible' start codon usage is arbitrary and could reflect sequencing or assembly artifacts rather than genuine variation.
  - Response: We deliberately chose this threshold in the context of a very large sample (60,690 genomes) specifically to guard against this concern, and additionally cross-validated the calls against phylogenetic structure. The alternate codons consistently associate with specific clonal complexes or lineages rather than being randomly scattered across the tree, which would be the expected pattern for sequencing/assembly noise; phylogenetic clustering of the signal argues that it reflects genuine, heritable variation rather than artifact.
  - Manuscript reference: "SC usage was found to be flexible for 39 conserved genes (1.8%)...The majority of these genes use different SCs between lineage I and lineage II, and some of them use different SCs between globally prevalent clonal complexes (Figure S2)."
