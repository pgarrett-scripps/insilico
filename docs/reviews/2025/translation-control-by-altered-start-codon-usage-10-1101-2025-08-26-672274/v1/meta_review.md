# Meta-Review

**Draft recommendation:** major

## Synthesis
The panel agrees the experimental core of the paper is sound: the in vitro acid-evolution experiment, the isolation of six independent rsbW mutations, and the ATG>ATA>ATT reporter hierarchy (Fig. 4B-C) are consistently described as well-designed and convincingly demonstrated (data_analysis 4/5, ethics 4/5, ambient praise even from the harshest reviewers). Divergence centers on the paper's second, more ambitious pillar — the 60,690-genome start-codon survey and its framing as evidence of 'selection' on stress/virulence genes. Reproducibility (2/5, conf 5) identifies a hard, load-bearing gap: the custom tblastn pipeline, gene-presence thresholds, and processed start-codon calls underlying the headline '39 genes, 13 virulence/stress-linked' claim are not deposited, archived, or code-available, making this half of the paper's contribution currently unverifiable by any reader. Independently, methodology, novelty, and rigor (3/5 each, conf 4-5) converge on a second, related problem: the genome-wide clustering is correlational (phylogenetic co-occurrence with clade), and the manuscript's title/abstract language ('under selection') outruns what phylogenetic association alone can show, since drift, founder effects, and lineage-specific bias are not excluded. A third convergent critique, raised independently by novelty and literature reviewers, is that the rsbW mechanistic model (Fig. 4D) is not isolated from a confound — the RsbV G111C/S substitution co-occurs with the start-codon changes in the ARDs, and no construct separates the two effects, so the causal claim that translation-rate tuning (rather than RsbV alteration or simple RsbW loss-of-function, as suggested by the nonsense-mutant ARD3/ARD5 phenotype) drives SigB hyperactivity rests on inference across genetic backgrounds rather than direct measurement in the ARDs themselves. Clarity issues (undefined abbreviations, missing Table S1, an argument structure that buries the connection between the single-gene and genome-wide analyses) are real but secondary and readily fixable. In debate, the advocate argued the reporter assay stands 'independent of RsbV entirely' and that hedged language ('possibly associated with... selective pressures') shows the authors are not overclaiming; the skeptic countered — and this held — that the reporter tests SC identity in a heterologous pIMK3 background, never in the ARDs themselves where the RsbV confound actually sits, and that the reproducibility gap is not addressed by textual hedging since the title and Results headers still assert 'under selection' as the paper's contribution. The reproducibility critique was not meaningfully rebutted in the debate at all.

## Decisive Factors
The confidence-weighted average (3.25/5, modal recommendation 'major') is consistent with the recommendation here, so no divergence needs defending. The decisive factors pulling the verdict to 'major' rather than 'minor' are: (1) the reproducibility reviewer's high-confidence (5/5) finding that the entire genome-wide start-codon dataset — the empirical basis for the paper's second major claim — is undeposited and unverifiable, which is a load-bearing gap rather than a cosmetic one; (2) convergent, independent flags from methodology, novelty, and rigor reviewers that the manuscript's 'under selection' framing outpaces correlational phylogenetic evidence, unaddressed by the debate; and (3) the unresolved RsbV/start-codon confound in the rsbW mechanistic model, which the advocate's rebuttal did not actually resolve (the reporter assay does not test the ARDs' own genetic background). None of these amount to grounds for rejection — the rsbW mechanism, acid-evolution phenotype, and reporter assays are independently well-supported and no reviewer scored below 2 or called for outright rejection with high confidence — but they require concrete, specified revisions (deposit the genome analysis pipeline/data/code and accessions; soften 'selection' language to 'clade-associated variation' or provide dN/dS or fitness evidence; construct a genetic separation of the RsbV and rsbW-start-codon effects, or explicitly flag this as an open question) before the paper's full set of claims is supported by verifiable evidence.
