# Journal Recommendations

## As-is (current quality)
_No headline venue is realistic at the current quality — see Alternative._

## After required revisions
- **Applied and Environmental Microbiology**
  - Fit: This manuscript's experimental core—in vitro acid adaptation, recovery of independent rsbW mutations, and translational reporter assays—is well-suited to AEM's scope in bacterial physiology and stress response. The Listeria monocytogenes system and SigB-mediated GSR are established topics in this venue, and the acid-survival phenotype directly addresses applied food safety microbiology.
  - Realism: After addressing the major revisions (genome-wide dataset deposition, softening 'selection' language, and resolving the RsbV confound), this paper would be a realistic fit for AEM. The experimental rigor and reproducibility improvements required by the editor align well with AEM's standards; acceptance would depend on how thoroughly the confound is resolved (either by isogenic mutant or explicit limitation statement).
- **mBio**
  - Fit: mBio publishes original research on microbial physiology, evolution, and pathogenesis. The paper's combination of experimental evolution, genomic analysis across 60K+ genomes, and mechanistic dissection of a virulence regulator fits mBio's scope. The clade-associated start-codon variation and its link to stress response and virulence are directly relevant to the journal's audience.
  - Realism: With required revisions completed—particularly deposition of the genome-wide analysis pipeline and data, and reframing of 'selection' claims as clade-associated variation with explicit discussion of drift and founder effects—this paper would be competitive for mBio. The experimental half is strong; the revision hinges on making the computational half fully verifiable and the causal language proportionate.
- **Molecular Microbiology**
  - Fit: This journal emphasizes molecular mechanisms of bacterial adaptation and gene regulation. The rsbW translational control mechanism, the ATG>ATA>ATT hierarchy, and the connection to SigB-mediated stress response are core molecular microbiology topics. The genome-wide survey of start-codon usage in virulence and stress genes adds evolutionary perspective.
  - Realism: After revisions, this would be a solid fit for Molecular Microbiology, particularly if the RsbV confound is resolved by isogenic mutant construction or clearly stated as an open question. The journal values mechanistic rigor; the required clarifications on causality and reproducibility align with that standard.

## Alternative outlets
- **bioRxiv**
  - Fit: As a preprint server, bioRxiv is appropriate for this work at any stage of revision. The experimental core is sound and the genome-wide analysis, once deposited, will be verifiable. Authors can post the manuscript with a clear statement of which revisions are in progress, allowing community feedback before formal submission.
  - Realism: bioRxiv acceptance is essentially assured; the value lies in rapid dissemination and community input. This is a sensible intermediate step if the authors wish to solicit feedback on the genome-wide dataset deposition strategy or the RsbV confound resolution before committing to a specific journal.
- **Infection and Immunity**
  - Fit: This journal covers bacterial pathogenesis and host-pathogen interactions. The paper's focus on SigB-mediated virulence, acid resistance during gastric transit, and the link between start-codon usage and virulence-gene expression are directly relevant. The clinical isolate (CC1, hypervirulent clade) and in vivo relevance strengthen the fit.
  - Realism: After revisions, this is a plausible secondary target, though the genome-wide start-codon survey may be less central to Infection and Immunity's core scope than to AEM or mBio. Acceptance would depend on how prominently the virulence-gene start-codon variation is emphasized relative to the mechanistic rsbW story.
- **Frontiers in Microbiology**
  - Fit: Frontiers is a broad, open-access venue that welcomes original research in microbial physiology, evolution, and pathogenesis. The paper's combination of experimental evolution, genomic analysis, and mechanistic studies fits well. The journal's transparent review model aligns with the In Silico ethos of public, detailed evaluation.
  - Realism: Frontiers is a realistic fallback if the paper does not reach AEM, mBio, or Molecular Microbiology. Acceptance odds are higher due to the journal's broader scope and lower rejection rate, though the required revisions (dataset deposition, causal language refinement) would still be expected.

## Notes
The manuscript's experimental core (acid adaptation, rsbW mutations, translational reporter assays) is sound and well-reported; the major revisions center on verifiability of the genome-wide analysis and proportionality of causal claims. The single load-bearing condition is deposition of the custom tblastn pipeline, gene-presence matrix, start-codon calls, and genome accessions in a public repository with a permanent DOI. The RsbV G111C/S confound is resolvable either by isogenic mutant construction or by explicit limitation statement in Results and Discussion; the latter is less ideal but acceptable if clearly stated. After these revisions, the paper is realistic for mid-tier specialty journals (AEM, mBio, Molecular Microbiology) but not for top-tier venues (Nature, Science, Cell) given the correlational rather than causal evidence for start-codon 'selection' and the unresolved mechanistic confound in the ARDs themselves.
