# Decision Letter

**Decision:** major

## Summary of Evaluation
The panel converged on a consistent picture: the experimental core of this manuscript — the in vitro acid-adaptation experiment, the recovery of six independent rsbW lesions across separate lineages, and the ATG>ATA>ATT translational hierarchy demonstrated by both eGFP fluorescence and Western blot — is well designed and convincingly reported. No reviewer challenged that core, and the weighted score (3.25/5, modal 'major', no low-confidence reject) reflects concerns about the paper's second pillar and its verifiability rather than doubt about its central experimental finding.

Three issues drive the 'major' verdict. First, and decisively, the reproducibility reviewer's high-confidence finding that the custom tblastn pipeline, gene-presence thresholds, processed start-codon calls, genome accession list, ARD reads/VCFs, and construct sequences are not deposited. This is load-bearing: the '39 genes / 60,690 genomes / 13 virulence-stress-linked' claim is currently unverifiable by any reader. The authors conceded this fully and proposed a specific, adequate deposition plan; that concession is why the paper is revisable rather than rejectable, but the deposits must actually exist and be cited before acceptance. Second, methodology, novelty and rigor independently flagged that 'under selection' in the title and Results header outruns correlational clade-clustering evidence. The authors conceded this and proposed precise replacement wording plus a Discussion paragraph on drift, founder effects and hitchhiking; I regard that as the right fix and have made it a required, checkable revision rather than demanding dN/dS or fitness data.

Third, the RsbV G111C/S confound. Here I weighed the rebuttal carefully and find it partially persuasive. The authors' point that ARD1 (ATT) phenocopies the nonsense mutants while ARD6/10/12 (ATA) show a distinct intermediate phenotype with graded gadD3/lmo2230/inlA induction is a real, quoted, dose-response argument against a uniform binary loss-of-function model, and it deserves to be made explicitly in the manuscript. It does not, however, dissolve the RsbV confound, since RsbV G111C and G111S themselves differ between the LM and IM groups. The authors also offered to build an isogenic native-locus rsbW-ATA/ATT allelic-exchange mutant. I am not making that construct a condition of acceptance — it is a substantial new experiment and the paper's translation-rate claim is defensible if properly scoped — but the manuscript must either include it or state the confound plainly as an unresolved alternative in both Results and Discussion, rather than presenting Fig. 4D as an established causal model.

I accept the authors' responses on three further points and will not require action beyond a sentence of clarification: the Hecht et al. citation (correctly reframed as general precedent, with the hierarchy established by their own reporter data), the rsbS/GTG stoichiometry statement (explicitly hedged and not load-bearing), and the acid-challenge protocol's fidelity to gastric transit (the design goal is stated honestly and the reproducible emergence of independent rsbW mutations stands on its own). The 5% flexibility threshold rebuttal is reasonable but should be written into the manuscript, since the phylogenetic-clustering argument against artifact is currently implicit.

The compliance audits add a substantial but tractable list of methods and traceability gaps — tool versions, sequencing metrics, statistical definitions, construct validation, a data-availability statement, and one internal numerical inconsistency (60,692 vs 60,690 genomes). These are additions, not defects in the science, and are folded into the requirements below. One citation issue (RsbU Q317* Mn2+ site attributed to a paper on RsbX) needs checking, as a misattribution there touches a mechanistic statement in the Introduction.

This is a solid paper whose experimental half I would recommend to a colleague; the revision is about making the computational half checkable and the causal language proportionate.

## Required Revisions
1. Deposit the genome-wide start-codon analysis in full and cite it: custom tblastn scripts and parameter files, the gene presence/absence matrix, the per-gene per-genome start-codon call table, and the complete list of the 60,690 genome accessions used, in a public repository (Zenodo/GitHub) with a permanent DOI. Add a Data Availability statement and reference the DOI in Methods. This is the single condition on which acceptance most directly depends.
2. Deposit the primary experimental data: ARD1/3/5/6/10/12 Illumina reads to SRA under PRJNA1305691 with the Breseq VCFs as supplementary files; the pLM7UG1-like and pLMGC1 plasmid sequences to GenBank; and the full annotated sequences of the three pIMK3-rsbW-eGFP (ATG/ATA/ATT) and the pIMK3-plcB/lftR constructs as supplementary FASTA. List individual read and assembly accessions, not only the project accession.
3. Revise the title, Abstract and the Results header 'SC are under selection for stress response and virulence genes' to language reflecting association rather than selection (e.g. 'clade-associated variation'), and add a Discussion paragraph naming drift, founder effects, lineage-specific bias and hitchhiking as alternatives not excluded by phylogenetic co-occurrence. If any 'selection' claim is retained anywhere, it must be supported by dN/dS or fitness evidence.
4. Address the RsbV G111C/S confound explicitly. Either (a) include the isogenic native-locus rsbW-ATA/ATT allelic-exchange mutant assayed for colony morphology, motility and SigB-dependent transcripts, or (b) state clearly in Results (at Fig. 4D) and Discussion that the reporter assay tests start-codon identity only in a heterologous pIMK3/MQ140025 background, that RsbV substitutions co-vary with the start-codon changes in every relevant ARD, and that the causal contribution of each cannot be separated by the present data. Incorporate the graded LM/IM dose-response argument from the rebuttal into the manuscript text as positive evidence against a purely binary loss-of-function model.
5. Complete the statistical reporting: state the exact test for every panel in Figs. 1, 2, 4 and 5 (not only Fig. 2), define error bars as SD, SEM or CI in each legend, state the number of biological versus technical replicates for each panel, justify the paired t-test, report exact p-values for Fig. 2 in a supplementary table, and apply and report Benjamini-Hochberg-corrected q-values across the gene x strain comparisons.
6. Supply the missing computational provenance: version numbers and key parameters for Autocycler (and canu/flye/metaMDBG/unicycler), Prokka, BRIG, geneviewer, Mauve, tblastn (e-value, word size, coverage/identity cutoffs), Breseq, Parsnp2 and mlst; the exact filtering and counting logic for the >95% conservation and >5% flexibility thresholds; and a justification of the 5% threshold that incorporates the rebuttal's phylogenetic-clustering argument against sequencing/assembly artifact.
7. Add sequencing metadata: Nanopore read-length statistics (N50, mean), total bases and coverage depth for the MQ140025 assembly; and the Illumina platform, read length, insert size and per-isolate coverage depth for the ARD isolates.
8. Resolve the internal numerical inconsistency between the Abstract ('60,692 genomes') and Methods ('n=60,690'), and use the correct figure consistently throughout.
9. Check and correct the citation at line 135: the RsbU Q317* C-terminal Mn2+ binding site claim is attributed to Ref 25 (Teh et al. 2015), which concerns RsbX phosphatase structure. Supply the correct primary source or restate the claim to match what Ref 25 supports.
10. Complete the Western blot methods: state whether densitometry was performed and with what software, the normalisation/loading control used, the number of blots quantified, and an explicit fold-over-background threshold defining 'no detectable signal' relative to the pIMK3-only control.
11. Include Table S1 (and S2) as downloadable supplementary files, and define GSR, ARD, CC, ST and PMSC at first use in the Abstract and Introduction.
12. Report a quantitative motility metric (halo diameter in mm at a fixed time point, n>=3 independent platings per strain) in a supplementary table and cross-reference it to the LM/IM/HM categories used in Fig. 2B, so the categorical assignment is auditable.
13. State whether the pIMK3 reporter constructs were sequence-verified before transformation, and specify the NcoI/BamHI insertion sites and selection marker; provide a plasmid map or Addgene/accession reference for pIMK3.

## Minor Suggestions
- Add the explicit signposting sentence at the end of the Introduction stating that the rsbW finding motivates the species-wide start-codon survey, and move the rsbW-to-genome-wide transition to precede the sigB-operon SC section.
- State the 1 mM IPTG concentration at first mention in the Fluorescence quantification subsection and confirm in the text that it was used identically for all reporter assays (Figs. 4B and 5C).
- Clarify in the text that Hecht et al. 2017 is cited as general precedent for functional non-canonical start codons, and that the ATG>ATA>ATT ranking for rsbW derives entirely from the reporter and immunoblot data in this study.
- Add a one-sentence biosafety statement (containment level and institutional approval reference) to Methods.
- Specify the acidifying agent used to prepare the pH 2.5 / pH 3.0 BHI, and state which aspects of the acid-challenge and RT-qPCR protocols were adapted from Ref 28 and where they deviate, so a reader need not retrieve that paper to repeat the work.
- Give the NCBI accession for the F2365 reference assembly used in the Mauve/BRIG comparisons.
- Consider adding catalog numbers (and RRIDs where available) for the anti-GFP primary and anti-rabbit secondary antibodies.
- Explain the 500x factor in the 500 x (RFU/OD) fluorescence calculation, and note the plate type and gain setting.
- Confirm whether the sigB-null phenotype of MQ140025 was verified experimentally before the evolution experiment began, or state that it was inferred from genotype.
