# Debate Transcript

## Advocate — round 1

This manuscript makes a genuine, well-substantiated contribution: it identifies six independent rsbW mutations arising under lethal acid selection, directly demonstrates via reporter assays that ATG>ATA>ATT translation efficiency (Fig. 4B-C, corroborated by both fluorescence and Western blot), and extends this to a systematic 60,690-genome survey showing clade-associated start codon flexibility in 39 conserved genes, 13 tied to virulence/stress. Reviewers uniformly praise the core experimental rigor: the data_analysis reviewer calls the reporter assays 'appropriate quantification...consistent dose-response,' the ethics reviewer finds the work 'sound' with clean compliance, and even the harshest critiques (novelty, rigor) concede the in vitro evolution experiment is 'well-designed' and the rsbW mechanism for one gene is 'clear.' The most serious-sounding objection—that genome-wide start codon clustering reflects drift rather than selection—is honestly hedged in the text itself: line 425 says variants are 'possibly associated with niche-specific selective pressures,' not proven selection. That's appropriately cautious language, not overclaiming; the title's 'under selection' is a framing choice revisable in minor text edits, not a fatal flaw invalidating the data. Similarly, the RsbV-confound concern (G111C/S co-occurring with start codon changes) is a legitimate mechanistic caveat, but it doesn't undermine the paper's actual demonstrated finding—that start codon identity alone changes translation output in a controlled reporter system independent of RsbV. That causal claim stands on its own reporter data, isolated from the RsbV question entirely. This is a rich, multi-pronged dataset combining genetics, genomics, and biochemistry that meaningfully advances understanding of translational control in a major pathogen, with weaknesses that are for the most part textual and clarificatory rather than structural.

**Key points:**
- Reporter assays (Fig 4B-C) rigorously establish ATG>ATA>ATT hierarchy independent of RsbV confound—reviewers confirm this mechanism is sound.
- In vitro evolution experiment is well-controlled and reproducibly yields rsbW mutations across 18 ARDs, a genuine and novel empirical finding.
- Genome-scale analysis of 60,690 genomes is transparent and systematic per the clarity/data reviewers, providing real value regardless of causal interpretation.
- Manuscript's own hedged language ('possibly associated with niche-specific selective pressures') shows the authors are not overclaiming causation from correlation—critics are attacking phrasing, not substance.
- Most flagged weaknesses (undefined abbreviations, missing Table S1, deposition timing) are textual/administrative issues fixable in revision, not core scientific flaws.

## Skeptic — round 1

The advocate's strongest move—claiming the reporter assay isolates start-codon effects 'independent of RsbV entirely'—is only half true, and that half doesn't rescue the paper's actual mechanistic claim. The reporter tests SC identity in a heterologous pIMK3 fusion in the parental background, not in the ARDs themselves; it never measures endogenous RsbW protein or RsbW:SigB ratios in ARD1/6/10/12, where the SC changes co-occur with RsbV G111C/S substitutions. As the novelty reviewer states plainly, 'no construct separates these dual effects, leaving unclear whether reduced RsbW translation or altered RsbV function is the primary driver.' That's the paper's central causal model (Fig 4D) left unvalidated in the actual strains under study—FIXABLE with a genetic separation construct, but not resolved by anything currently in the text.

More serious is the reproducibility reviewer's finding, which the advocate never engages: the entire genome-wide start-codon analysis—the empirical basis for the abstract's headline claim of clade-associated flexibility in 39 genes, 13 virulence/stress-linked—rests on a custom tblastn pipeline over 60,690 genomes with no deposited database, no code, no accession, and no intermediate outputs. This is not textual polish; it is the load-bearing dataset for half the paper's contribution, and as submitted it is unverifiable by any reader. I'd call this FIXABLE (deposit the data/code) but it currently blocks independent verification of the paper's second major claim, exactly as the rigor reviewer's drift-vs-selection concern (also unaddressed by hedged phrasing alone, since the title and Results header assert 'under selection' as fact) compounds an unverifiable correlational claim.
