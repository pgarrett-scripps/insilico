# Novelty & Contribution Reviewer

## Summary
This manuscript reports rapid evolution of acid resistance in a clinical L. monocytogenes isolate through mutations in rsbW that alter its start codon, and surveys start codon usage across 60,692 genomes to argue that start codon choice is under selection for stress and virulence regulation. The work is technically sound in execution (genome sequencing, reporter assays, transcriptional analysis) and the in vitro evolution experiment is well-designed. However, the central novelty claims are overstated. The principle that non-canonical start codons reduce translation efficiency and can regulate gene expression is established (Hecht 2016, Cherrak 2024); the manuscript applies it to a new system but does not demonstrate a new mechanism. The evidence that start codon variants are under selection is correlational (phylogenetic clustering) rather than causal (no fitness assay, no environmental dependence shown). Most critically, the mechanism by which rsbW start codon mutations increase SigB activity is not isolated from a confounding RsbV amino acid change, leaving the primary claim incompletely supported. The work is a solid incremental contribution to understanding SigB regulation in L. monocytogenes but does not establish the novel regulatory principle its framing suggests.

## Strengths
- Comprehensive genome-wide analysis of start codon usage across >60,000 L. monocytogenes genomes provides a population-level perspective on codon evolution that is well-executed and informative for the species.
- The in vitro evolution experiment is well-designed with biological replicates and appropriate controls, and the isolation of six independent acid-resistant derivatives carrying mutations in the same gene (rsbW) strengthens the inference that this locus is under strong selection.
- Reporter assays directly measuring translation efficiency from different start codons provide quantitative evidence for differential initiation rates and are a clear methodological strength.

## Weaknesses
- The mechanism by which rsbW start codon mutations increase SigB activity is not experimentally isolated: all ARDs carrying start codon changes simultaneously carry SNPs that alter the first amino acid of RsbV (G111C or G111S), a direct binding partner of RsbW, and no construct separates these dual effects, leaving unclear whether reduced RsbW translation or altered RsbV function is the primary driver.
- The evidence for selection on start codon variants is correlational, not causal: phylogenetic clustering is consistent with selection but equally consistent with drift or founder effects, and no fitness assay, environmental condition test, or demonstration that measured translation differences are large enough to be selected for is provided.
- The novelty of the core claim is overstated relative to prior work: Hecht et al. (2016) systematically measured translation initiation from all 64 codons and showed non-canonical codons are functional with reduced rates, and Cherrak et al. (2024) explicitly demonstrated that non-canonical start codons confer context-dependent fitness advantages in bacteria; the present manuscript applies this known principle to L. monocytogenes and the SigB system but does not establish a new mechanism.
- The GadR R154Q substitution is inferred to impair function based on sequence conservation and low gadT2D2 expression but is not experimentally validated.
- The definition of 'flexible' start codon usage as >5% of sequences is arbitrary and not justified; genes with 5–10% variant usage could represent sequencing errors, assembly artifacts, or genuine rare variants, and this distinction is not addressed.
- The secondary start codon of rsbW (6 bp downstream) is not tested in the ATA or ATT reporter constructs, leaving ambiguity about whether reduced fluorescence reflects true initiation failure or reinitiation.
- The in vitro evolution protocol uses a 1:2500 dilution between acid cycles, which may not accurately mimic gastric passage bottlenecks, and the relevance of selected mutations to natural infection is not established.
- The claim that start codon flexibility 'might provide a genetic means of adjusting gene expression' is based on only two genes (plcB, lftR) and is insufficient to support a general principle.

## Questions
- Can the authors provide a construct with the rsbW start codon change (ATG→ATA) but without the RsbV G111C/S substitution to isolate the contribution of each mutation to SigB activation?
- For the 39 genes with flexible start codon usage, is there evidence (fitness assay, growth under specific stress, or environmental dependence) that the start codon variants confer a selective advantage, or is the phylogenetic clustering consistent with neutral divergence?
- Do the secondary start codons of rsbW (and other genes with flexible start codons) contribute to translation initiation in the ATA and ATT constructs, and if so, how does this affect interpretation of the reporter assay results?
