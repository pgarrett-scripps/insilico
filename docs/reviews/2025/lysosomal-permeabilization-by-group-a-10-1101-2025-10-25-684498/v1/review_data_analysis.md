# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
The paper's central quantitative claims — that cytosolic fractions from WT- and DSLO-infected cells stimulate IL-1β secretion, that M1 protein accounts for most of the WT response, and that histones appear in the cytosol of GAS-infected cells — are supported by the ELISA and Western blot data, but the statistics as reported are too thin to verify: no n is stated for the ELISA experiments, no test is named for most comparisons, error bars are undefined, and the proteomics analysis rests on a normalization/imputation pipeline whose details are not given. The claims are plausible and the design is largely sound, but the quantitative reporting needs to be completed before the results can be checked.

## Strengths
- The experimental design is well-controlled: uninfected, WT, DSLO, DM1, heat-killed and GBS conditions provide appropriate comparators for the IL-1β and histone claims.
- The authors pre-empt the obvious contamination objection by showing LAMP-2/LAMP-1 and Lamin A/C are absent from cytosolic fractions, and by filtering samples to exclude bacteria.
- The proteomics analysis is transparent about filtering (proteins in ≥2 replicates, contaminants removed) and the data are deposited with a public accession.

## Weaknesses
- LOAD-BEARING: The IL-1β ELISA claims — that WT cytosol stimulates more IL-1β than UI cytosol (Fig. 1C), that DSLO cytosol also stimulates (Fig. 2C), and that DM1 cytosol stimulates less (Fig. 4A) — are the load-bearing quantitative results, but no test is named, no n is stated, and the error bars are undefined. The text says 'analyzed by one-way ANOVA with Tukey's multiple comparison' but does not give the number of independent replicates per condition, whether the comparisons are paired or unpaired, or whether the data met ANOVA assumptions. As reported, the p-values are uninterpretable. The one thing that would settle this: state n per condition, name the test for each comparison, and define the error bars.
- LOAD-BEARING: The claim that M1 protein 'accounts for the majority of the inflammasome activation' (Fig. 4A) rests on a comparison between WT cytosol and DM1 cytosol that is not shown to be statistically significant in the text — the figure legend says 'ns' for some comparisons but the specific WT-vs-DM1 comparison is not identified. If the WT-vs-DM1 difference is not significant, the claim that M1 is the main driver is unsupported; if it is significant, the test and n must be stated. Report the specific comparison with its test, n and effect size.
- LOAD-BEARING: The proteomics analysis (Fig. 3, Supp. Tables) reports a normalization pipeline — log2-transformed NSAF, scaling, correlation-slope normalization, missForest imputation, and imputation of the least-expressed value for consistently absent proteins — but the effect of this pipeline on the presence/absence calls is not assessed. The headline finding that histones appear only in infected cytosol, and that M1 appears only in WT cytosol, depends on the imputation and filtering choices. A sensitivity analysis showing the presence/absence calls are robust to the imputation method and to the ≥2-replicates filter would settle whether these are real differences or artefacts of the pipeline.
- Sweep: The cathepsin B inhibitor experiment (Fig. 4B) concludes 'no significant reduction' in IL-1β, but no power analysis or equivalence test is reported, so the 'no effect' claim is unsupported by a non-significant result in an unpowered sample.
- Sweep: The DNA quantification (Fig. 5C) reports 'no significant difference' between conditions, again without a stated n, test, or power consideration — the 'no DNA in cytosol' claim is a negative result that needs an equivalence framing or at least a stated detection limit.
- Sweep: The recombinant SLO dose-response (Fig. 2A) shows a threshold effect but the text does not state the n per dose or whether the doses were tested in independent replicates; the 'high concentrations' threshold is not quantified.
- Sweep: Multiple comparisons are made across many conditions (UI, WT, DSLO, DM1, HK, GBS, plus inhibitor and dose conditions) but no multiple-comparison correction beyond the per-figure ANOVA is described; the family of tests across figures is not pre-registered or corrected.
- Sweep: The PCA and heatmap (Fig. 3A, Supp. Fig. 2D) are presented as evidence of reproducibility across replicates, but the number of replicates per condition (4 UI, 4 WT, 3 SLO) is only stated in the methods, not in the figure legends, and the PCA is not annotated with the variance explained.

## Questions
- For each ELISA comparison (Figs. 1C, 2A-C, 4A-B), what is the n per condition, is the comparison paired or unpaired, and what do the error bars represent (SD, SEM, or CI)?
- In Fig. 4A, is the WT-vs-DM1 difference in IL-1β statistically significant, and with what test and effect size?
- For the proteomics presence/absence calls (histones in infected cytosol, M1 only in WT), how sensitive are the calls to the imputation method and the ≥2-replicates filter?
- For the 'no effect' claims (cathepsin B inhibitor, DNA quantification), what is the smallest effect the experiment could detect, and was the study powered to detect it?
