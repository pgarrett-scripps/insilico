# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
The paper reports a careful isolation of cytosolic proteins from GAS-infected macrophages and identifies candidate host and bacterial proteins released by phagosomal permeabilization, with the proteomics and Western blot findings being plausible and well-described. However, the central IL-1β readout in the cytosol-transfer experiments is confounded by the possibility of carried-over pro-IL-1β from the producer cells, and the M1 attribution plus several negative controls are under-supported as stated. The statistical reporting is generally transparent (n stated, SD used, tests named), but the load-bearing claims need additional controls before the conclusions hold.

## Strengths
- Independent replication is stated (n=3–4 separate preparations) and error bars are defined as SD, not SEM.
- The proteomics pipeline (NSAF normalization, missForest imputation, ≥2-replicate filter) is described in enough detail to be reproduced.
- The authors candidly acknowledge limitations (e.g., cathepsin B near the MWCO) rather than overclaiming.

## Weaknesses
- IL-1β carry-over confound in the cytosol-transfer experiments (Figs. 1C, 2C, 4A, 4B): the cytosol is isolated from PMA-primed THP-1 cells, which express pro-IL-1β (~31 kD); the 30 kD MWCO concentration step retains pro-IL-1β in the transferred fraction; the ELISA measures total IL-1β in the supernatant and cannot distinguish newly synthesized IL-1β from the naïve cells from pro-IL-1β carried over in the cytosol and cleaved in vitro by proteases (cathepsin B, SpeB) present in the same fraction; the 'inflammasome-stimulating activity' could therefore be an in vitro cleavage artifact rather than activation of the naïve cells; the difference between WT and UI cytosol could reflect the amount of cleaving protease in the fraction, not inflammasome activation; a control measuring IL-1β directly in the cytosol input, or blocking new protein synthesis in the naïve cells (e.g., cycloheximide), would settle it.
- M1 attribution confound (Fig. 4A): the reduced IL-1β response to ΔM1 cytosol is attributed to the absence of M1, but ΔM1 bacteria may permeabilize phagosomes less or be phagocytosed differently, so ΔM1 cytosol could contain less of all leaked agonists, not just M1; normalizing to equal total protein (20 µg) does not control for composition — if ΔM1-infected cells leak less total protein, the same 20 µg contains proportionally more host cytosolic protein and less leaked bacterial/lysosomal protein; the authors should show comparable levels of another leaked protein (e.g., SLO, GAPDH) in WT vs ΔM1 cytosol, or add back recombinant M1 to ΔM1 cytosol to restore the response.
- CA-074 negative result lacks a positive control (Fig. 4B): 'No significant reduction' with n=3 does not establish that cathepsin B is uninvolved, particularly since cathepsin B is below detection in the >30 kD fraction (Supp. Fig. 3); without a demonstration that CA-074 actually inhibited cathepsin B in this system (activity assay, or a positive control where cathepsin B is known to drive the response), the negative result is uninterpretable — it is equally consistent with the inhibitor not working.
- DNA 'no difference' is an underpowered null (Fig. 5C): the claim 'histones in the absence of DNA' rests on a non-significant result with n=3, at values near the Qubit detection floor, and GAS DNase could degrade released DNA; the claim should be 'no DNA detected above background,' not 'no DNA present'; equivalence testing or reporting the assay's detection limit in this sample matrix would strengthen this.
- Fig. 2B: 'not significantly different' between WT and ΔSLO with n=3 is an underpowered null; the authors' own observation that ΔSLO 'always' trended lower supports this — report effect sizes and CIs rather than relying on the non-significant p-value.
- Proteomics presence/absence calls (Fig. 3, Supp. Table 2): with label-free MS and 3–4 replicates, absence of a protein in one condition could be a detection artifact; the M1-only-in-WT call is a single presence/absence observation and should be verified by targeted MS or Western blot.
- Fig. 1C axis: the media-only control appears to produce far more IL-1β than any cytosol treatment — please clarify the axis values and confirm the WT cytosol 'increase' is not a suppression relative to untreated cells.
- Multiple testing: 1532 proteins tested; ShinyGO enrichment uses FDR (good), but the presence/absence Venn analysis is uncorrected — acceptable as descriptive, but this should be stated.

## Questions
- What is the IL-1β concentration in the cytosol fractions themselves (input), before addition to naïve cells?
- Please clarify the Fig. 1C axis values — does the media-only control exceed the WT cytosol treatment, and if so, how is the 'increased IL-1β' claim reconciled with that baseline?
