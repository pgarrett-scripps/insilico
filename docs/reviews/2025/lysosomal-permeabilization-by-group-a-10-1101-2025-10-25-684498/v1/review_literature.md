# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript's citation practice is largely sound — the foundational prior work on GAS lysosomal permeabilization (Nishioka 2024), M1/NLRP3 activation (Valderrama 2017), SpeB/IL-1β (LaRock 2016, 2022), and cGAS-independent STING (Movert 2018) is correctly identified and attributed. However, one load-bearing citation is misattributed to a source that supports the opposite of the claim it is cited for, and the natural-infection inference about M1 rests on a comparison whose key control is not reported. These are correctable but substantive issues on my dimension.

## Strengths
- The manuscript's citation practice is largely sound — the foundational prior work on GAS lysosomal permeabilization (Nishioka 2024), M1/NLRP3 activation (Valderrama 2017), SpeB/IL-1β (LaRock 2016, 2022), and cGAS-independent STING (Movert 2018) is correctly identified and attributed.
- Currency is good: the post-pandemic iGAS surge is anchored to current sources (CDC 2023, Gregory 2025), and the field's active lines (LaRock 2022, Nishioka 2024) are represented.
- Self-citation (Nishioka 2024) is proportionate and appropriate as the direct foundation of this study.

## Weaknesses
- HARD — Ref 17 (O'Neill et al., "Cytosolic Replication of Group A Streptococcus in Human Macrophages," mBio 2016) is cited for a claim its central finding contradicts. The text states: "Following lysosomal permeabilization, GAS remains in lysosomes, where it neither replicates nor escapes into the cytosol, enabling persistence in a cell that normally functions to kill them (7,12,16,17)." O'Neill, Thurston and Holden's paper is titled and reported as demonstrating that GAS *does* escape the phagosome and *does* replicate in the cytosol of human macrophages. Citing it in support of "neither replicates nor escapes into the cytosol" is either a misattribution or an unstated strain/cell-type/timepoint qualification. The contradicting finding must be acknowledged in the text and the citation corrected or qualified — this is exactly the "contradicting prior findings silently ignored" case, and it sits at the center of the paper's framing of GAS persistence.
- The M1-in-cytosol inference (Fig 4A) lacks the control that would exclude a confound. The claim that M1 protein "accounts for the majority of the inflammasome activation" rests on comparing cytosol from WT- versus ΔM1-infected cells. But the manuscript does not report whether the ΔM1 strain permeabilizes phagolysosomes to the same extent as WT — SLO expression, dextran-leakage, and CD63/LAMP-3 recruitment are not shown for ΔM1. If the ΔM1 strain permeabilizes less, the reduced IL-1β would be produced without M1 being the active agent. The Valderrama citation (ref 22) is accurate for recombinant M1 activating NLRP3, but the natural-infection extension needs the permeabilization control to stand.
- The parsed reference list contains only ref 52 (Venny); refs 1–51 are absent from the parse, so I could not verify the full bibliography resolves — please confirm all entries.
- The "remains in lysosomes" framing is in tension with O'Neill's cytosolic-replication finding beyond the citation error itself; the contradiction should be discussed in the text, not only fixed in the reference list.
- Consider whether prior work on cytosolic histones in bacterial infection (beyond ref 47) should be cited for the novel histone-in-cytosol finding (SOFT).
- My related-work searches (Semantic Scholar/arXiv) returned mostly irrelevant results for this biomedical topic, so I could not independently confirm the absence of directly-competing proteomics of GAS-infected macrophage cytosol — a verification gap, not a confirmed omission.

## Questions
- Does the ΔM1 strain permeabilize phagolysosomes to the same extent as WT (SLO expression, dextran leakage, CD63/LAMP-3 recruitment)? If not, the reduced IL-1β in Fig 4A could reflect reduced permeabilization rather than loss of M1.
- Can you confirm ref 17 (O'Neill 2016) is intended to support the "remains in lysosomes, neither replicates nor escapes" claim, given that paper's title and findings — and if so, what strain/cell-type/timepoint distinction is being relied on?
