# Decision Letter

**Decision:** major

## Summary of Evaluation
The panel's signal (3.41/5; 1 accept, 1 minor, 6 major) and the debate converge on a clear picture: the two load-bearing contributions — SLO-mediated delivery of M1 into the cytosol as the mechanism for M1-driven NLRP3 activation, and cytosolic histones in GAS-infected macrophages — are genuinely novel and survive the panel's related-work searches, and the ΔM1 isogenic control plus GBS negative control provide clean genetic tests. However, several load-bearing claims outrun their evidence as written, and these are substantive evidence gaps rather than reporting completions. The M1 'accounts for the majority' attribution rests on a WT-vs-ΔM1 comparison whose significance is never stated; the ΔSLO IL-1β response (a headline finding) has an untested mechanism (SpeB suggested, no mutant shown); ref 17 (O'Neill 2016) is misattributed to support the opposite of its central finding, and the contradiction sits at the center of the paper's persistence framing; and the 'all histone proteins' / 'in the absence of DNA' claims are over-strong relative to the data. The statistics reporting (n, named tests, defined error bars) and the proteomics normalization/imputation sensitivity are incomplete. These are fixable in revision — they require new experiments (M1 neutralization/add-back, SpeB mutant, nucleosome-specific assay, permeabilization control for ΔM1) and reanalysis, so the verdict is major rather than minor. The audits found no HARD compliance gaps; the citation-integrity auditor's inability to verify refs 1–51 is a parse artifact, not evidence of misattribution, and the literature reviewer's specific ref 17 concern is the substantive citation issue.

## Required Revisions
1. 1. Report the specific WT-vs-ΔM1 IL-1β comparison (Fig 4A): state the test, n per condition, whether paired/unpaired, the effect size, and define the error bars. If the difference is not significant, the 'M1 accounts for the majority' claim must be withdrawn or requalified.
2. 2. Reconcile the M1 attribution with the ΔSLO data: either (a) add an experiment neutralizing M1 in WT-infected cytosol (antibody) or adding recombinant M1 back to ΔM1 cytosol to show the response tracks M1 specifically, or (b) explicitly requalify the claim as 'M1 accounts for the majority of the WT-infected-cytosol response' and acknowledge the redundant-triggers account (M1, SpeB, cathepsin B, other leaked proteins) as consistent with all data.
3. 3. Identify the driver of the ΔSLO IL-1β response: test the SpeB hypothesis with a SpeB-deficient mutant or inhibitor, or explicitly state that the mechanism is unidentified and scale the 'regardless of SLO' claim accordingly.
4. 4. Correct the ref 17 (O'Neill 2016) misattribution: that paper demonstrates GAS escapes and replicates in the cytosol of human macrophages; it cannot be cited for 'neither replicates nor escapes.' Acknowledge and discuss the contradiction in the text, and qualify the persistence framing by strain/cell-type/timepoint.
5. 5. Add a permeabilization control for the ΔM1 strain: show SLO expression, dextran leakage, and CD63/LAMP-3 recruitment are comparable to WT, so the reduced IL-1β in Fig 4A reflects loss of M1 rather than reduced permeabilization.
6. 6. Temper the histone claims: change 'all histone proteins' to 'almost all histone subunits'; confirm nucleosome release with a nucleosome-specific assay (H3-H4 tetramers or H2A-H2B dimers) or explicitly state that full nucleosomes are not established; and requalify 'in the absence of DNA' given the Qubit detection limit.
7. 7. Complete the statistics reporting for every ELISA comparison (Figs 1C, 2A-C, 4A-B): state n per condition, name the test, and define error bars (SD/SEM/CI). For the 'no effect' claims (cathepsin B inhibitor Fig 4B, DNA quantification Fig 5C), state the smallest detectable effect and whether the study was powered to detect it, or reframe as negative results with stated detection limits.
8. 8. Add a sensitivity analysis for the proteomics presence/absence calls (histones in infected cytosol, M1 only in WT): show the calls are robust to the imputation method and the ≥2-replicates filter, and quantify 'consistently absent'.
9. 9. Deposit the R analysis script (normalization, imputation, PCA, heatmap) at a resolvable, versioned location with package versions and a stated random seed for missForest.
10. 10. Reconcile the internal inconsistency about histone H3 (text says H3 present in WT but not ΔSLO cytosol in Supp. Table 1, then says the Western blot showing H3 in both is 'consistent' with the proteomics).
11. 11. Rewrite the abstract: define 'new proteins' (bacterial and lysosomal proteins), acknowledge the ΔSLO IL-1β response, qualify M1's role as specific to WT-infected cytosol, and temper the histone claims.
12. 12. Fix presentation issues: add rSLO concentrations to Fig 2A legend/text, define units for Fig 5C DNA quantification, standardize strain nomenclature (DSLO/ΔSLO/SLO; DM1/ΔM1), repair the garbled Fig 4 legend, retitle Fig 4 to 'cathepsin B' rather than 'lysosomal enzymes', and reconcile 'fractionation buffer' vs 'digitonin acetate buffer' terminology.

## Minor Suggestions
- Add a one-line biosafety statement for the live GAS (BSL-2) work.
- State the number of replicates per condition and variance explained in the PCA/heatmap figure legends (Fig 3A, Supp Fig 2D).
- Consider whether the ΔSLO permeabilization claim needs a CD63/LAMP-3 knockdown or ΔSLO+CD63 double-mutant control to directly test the pathway.
- Address the possibility that complement proteins and immunoglobulins in ΔSLO-infected cytosol are serum-derived contaminants from the opsonization step.
- Consider citing prior work on cytosolic histones in bacterial infection beyond ref 47 for the novel histone-in-cytosol finding.
- State what downstream use the proteomic catalog enables, to demonstrate its resource value.
