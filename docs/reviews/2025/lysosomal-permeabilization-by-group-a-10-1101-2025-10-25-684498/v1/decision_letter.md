# Decision Letter

**Decision:** major

## Summary of Evaluation
The panel is split (4 minor / 4 major; confidence-weighted 3.50/5), and I concur with the major-revision camp. The manuscript makes two genuinely novel contributions — (i) evidence that M1 protein is delivered to the macrophage cytosol via SLO-mediated phagolysosomal permeabilization, closing a gap left by Valderrama et al. 2017, and (ii) the unexpected presence of histones in the cytosol of GAS-infected (but not GBS-infected) cells — and the proteomics resource is deposited with unusually detailed search parameters. However, the two load-bearing mechanistic claims are precisely the ones the panel found materially under-tested, and the objections are substantive rather than stylistic. (1) The IL-1β cytosol-transfer readout is confounded by possible carry-over of donor pro-IL-1β: GAS infection itself primes donor cells, so WT donors plausibly contain more pro-IL-1β than uninfected donors, and the 30-kDa MWCO step retains pro-IL-1β in the transferred fraction. The skeptic's rebuttal of the advocate's "identical priming" mitigation is correct. (2) The M1 attribution confounds "M1 absent from the bacterium" with "M1 absent from the cytosol": the detection uses ΔSLO, the functional test uses ΔM1, and neither strain's bacterial load, SLO levels, or permeabilization phenotype is characterized. (3) The histone claim does not exclude dead-cell leakage into the digitonin-released fraction; the GBS control shows GAS-specificity but not active release from live cells. (4) There is a clear citation error (O'Neill 2016 cited to support the opposite of its central claim), a CA-074 negative result without a positive control, and an unaddressed contradiction with Harder 2009/Richter 2021 on SLO-independent NLRP3 activation. Reproducibility and methods-completeness audits add concrete, fixable gaps (no deposited analysis code, undefined imputation thresholds, ambiguous size-exclusion direction bearing on the nucleosome claim, inconsistent MassIVE accession, missing antibody dilutions, cell-line authentication/mycoplasma, and a missing biosafety statement). None of these is fatal; all are bounded and addressable with targeted controls and reporting. The work is sound and worth publishing, but the specific attributions outrun the evidence as presented, so I am requiring major revision rather than accepting or rejecting.

## Required Revisions
1. Resolve the IL-1β carry-over confound in the cytosol-transfer experiments (Figs. 1C, 2C, 4A, 4B). Measure IL-1β directly in the cytosol input fractions before transfer, and/or add a protein-synthesis inhibitor (e.g., cycloheximide) to the recipient cells to demonstrate that the measured IL-1β reflects new synthesis rather than donor pro-IL-1β carried over and cleaved in the recipient well. Report the IL-1β concentration in the cytosol input for WT vs. uninfected donors.
2. Strengthen the M1 attribution. Characterize the ΔM1 strain's phenotype (intracellular bacterial load at harvest, SLO and other secreted-virulence-factor expression, and extent of phagolysosomal permeabilization) so that the reduced IL-1β response to ΔM1 cytosol can be attributed to the specific absence of cytosolic M1 rather than to generally weaker permeabilization or lower bacterial load. Ideally, add back recombinant M1 to ΔM1 cytosol and show restoration of the response, and/or verify the M1-in-WT-cytosol proteomics call by targeted MS or Western blot.
3. Address the dead-cell alternative for the histone finding. Report cell viability at the 1-h harvest and show that the cytosolic histone signal tracks with a live-cell permeabilization readout (e.g., cathepsin B release) rather than with the dead-cell fraction; or isolate cytosol from cells in which death is blocked (e.g., a pancaspase inhibitor) and show histones persist. Requalify the abstract's 'released into the cytosol' to 'present in the cytosol' unless active release from live cells is demonstrated.
4. Correct the citation error: O'Neill et al. 2016 (ref 17) argues that GAS does escape the phagosome and replicate in the cytosol, the opposite of the claim it is cited to support. Re-cite it as a competing model and engage with the discrepancy, or remove it from that sentence.
5. Add a positive control for the CA-074 experiment (Fig. 4B): demonstrate that the inhibitor actually blocks cathepsin B activity in this system (activity assay, or a positive control where cathepsin B is known to drive the response). Without this, the negative result is uninterpretable; otherwise, drop the claim that cathepsin B does not contribute.
6. Reconcile the ΔSLO-elicits-IL-1β result with Harder 2009 and Richter 2021, which concluded SLO is required for NLRP3 activation. Provide a head-to-head account of the discrepancy (assay, strain, MOI, the 30-kDa cutoff, or the newly described CD63/LAMP-3 pathway), and report effect sizes/CIs for the WT-vs-ΔSLO comparison rather than relying on the underpowered n=3 null.
7. Make the analysis reproducible: deposit the R analysis script (with sessionInfo) at the MassIVE record or a versioned repository; define the imputation thresholds ('consistently absent' replicate criterion; whether 'below the least expressed protein' is within-sample or across-dataset); state which side of the 30-kDa membrane was analyzed for the proteomics input (this bears directly on the nucleosome interpretation, since monomeric histones are 10-23 kDa); and reconcile the MassIVE accession (PXD069795) with the embedded identifier (MSV000099561).
8. Complete the methods reporting: provide working dilutions (and secondary antibodies) for all antibodies; state cell-line authentication (STR) and mycoplasma-testing status for THP-1; and add an institutional biosafety statement confirming BSL-2 containment and approval for the live GAS/GBS work, plus a statement on the IRB-exempt status of the pooled human serum.
9. Requalify over-strong abstract/claims: align 'all histone proteins' with the body's 'almost all'; soften 'M1 corresponded with the IL-1β response' and 'indicating SLO-mediated release' to 'consistent with' and 'accounts for the majority of the response to WT-infected cytosol'; and temper the 'strong' IL-1β response and the speculative exosome interpretation.

## Minor Suggestions
- Add one-sentence numeric summaries (mean ± SD, n) for each ELISA comparison in the Results text so effect sizes are visible without inspecting figures.
- Use a single symbol for the SLO-deficient mutant throughout (recommend ΔSLO, not DSLO).
- State the y-axis units (IL-1β, pg/mL) in the legends of Figs. 1C, 2B, 2C, 4A, 4B, and specify what the Fig. 3A heatmap color scale represents (z-score of log2 NSAF, fold-change from row mean, etc.).
- Clarify the Fig. 1C axis: the media-only control appears to exceed the WT-cytosol treatment; confirm the 'increased IL-1β' claim is not a suppression relative to untreated cells.
- State the rSLO dose range for Fig. 2A in the Methods, and the LPS concentration used in Supp. Fig. 1.
- Clarify whether cells were washed after the CA-074 pre-incubation before adding cytosolic fractions, and whether the inhibitor was present throughout the 2-h exposure.
- Explain the purpose of 0.1 µg/mL penicillin in the digitonin lysis buffer and confirm it does not affect macrophage protein content or viability during the 1-h lysis.
- Provide an RRID/CVCL identifier for THP-1 and reconcile the 24-h (Methods) vs. 2-h (Fig. 4 legend) exposure times for the cytosol-transfer assay.
- Add a cytosol-from-heat-killed-GAS-infected-cells control to separate 'bacterial proteins present but no permeabilization' from 'permeabilization occurred.'
- Resolve the 'streptolysin O precursor' annotation in ΔSLO-infected cytosol by checking the specific peptides identified, to rule out a strain-contamination artifact.
