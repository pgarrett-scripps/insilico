# Citation Integrity Auditor

## Summary
This manuscript is a bioRxiv preprint (Quezada et al., 'Lysosomal permeabilization by Group A Streptococcus releases proteins into the macrophage cytosol'; DOI 10.1101/2025.10.25.684498). It reports a proteomic analysis of cytosolic fractions from GAS-infected THP-1 macrophages, with IL-1β secretion as a readout, and claims that (a) M1 protein released from SLO-permeabilized phagosomes drives the IL-1β response, and (b) histones appear in the cytosol of infected cells. Reference resolvability was found to be present for all in-text citations (1–51) mapping to entries in the manuscript's References section; the separately supplied REFERENCE LIST block contains only ref 52 (Venny) as a parse artifact. The proteomics data are deposited at MassIVE (accession PXD069795) with a reviewer link. Two '(data not shown)' placeholders (SOFT) were identified as missing supporting data that could be converted to shown controls. One claim–citation support check was verified as present (ref 15). Three content-level checks remain unverifiable (ref 22 claim about recombinant M1 stimulation of IL-1β; ref 16 claim about 40 kD size threshold and cathepsin B leakage; and the 43,100 incidence figure from refs 1–2). Self-citations were noted as germane and not problematic. No retracted or predatory sources were detected. No HARD (blocking) citation-integrity findings were identified.

## Unverifiable (raise as questions)
- **[Claim–citation support] Ref 22 — Valderrama et al. claim about recombinant M1 stimulation of IL-1β** — Ref 22 (Valderrama et al., Nat Microbiol 2017) is cited for the claim that 'recombinant M1 protein added to PMA-stimulated THP-1 macrophages could stimulate IL-1β activation and secretion.' This is load-bearing for the paper's central mechanism. Could not retrieve the paper's contents with the available tools.
- **[Claim–citation support] Ref 16 — Nishioka et al. claim about SLO causing 40 kD protein leakage and cathepsin B release** — Ref 16 (Nishioka et al., Infect Immun 2024) is the authors' own prior work, cited for the claim that SLO causes leakage of proteins up to 40 kD into the cytosol including active cathepsin B. This is load-bearing for the premise of the study. Could not verify the paper's contents with the available tools.
- **[Quotation/number fidelity] 43,100 life-threatening infections figure attributed to refs 1–2** — The Introduction states 'there were an estimated 43,100 life-threatening infections in the United States (1,2)' citing the CDC ABCs 2023 report (ref 1) and Gregory et al., JAMA 2025 (ref 2). Could not retrieve either source to check the figure.

## SOFT gaps — recommended
- **[Reference resolvability] (data not shown) placeholders** — The 0.22 µm filtration step 'to eliminate the presence of live bacteria in the sample (data not shown)' and the 'Digitonin acetate buffer spiked with THP-1 genomic DNA … control (data not shown)' both appear in the manuscript as supporting procedural/control claims.

## Documented (for the record)
- **[Reference resolvability] In-text citation-to-reference-list mapping** — All in-text citations (1–51) map to entries in the manuscript's References section, which gives full citations (journal, year, volume, pages) for each. The separately supplied REFERENCE LIST block contains only ref 52 (Venny), which is a parse truncation and is not evidence of missing references.
- **[Claim–citation support] Ref 15 — Bastiat-Sempe et al. claim about SLO/NAD-glycohydrolase** — Ref 15 (Bastiat-Sempe et al., mBio 2014) is cited for the claim that 'Streptolysin O and NAD-glycohydrolase prevent phagolysosome acidification and promote group A Streptococcus survival in macrophages.' Verified via PubMed (PMID 25227466); the paper exists and its title matches the claim.
- **[Self-citation / citation inflation] Self-citations in refs 16, 26, 27** — Refs 16 (Nishioka 2024), 26 (Timmer 2009), and 27 (Lauth 2009) are the authors' own prior work. Each is directly germane to the claims they support (lysosomal dysfunction, SLO-mediated immune evasion, M1 protein function), and the manuscript explicitly builds on ref 16.
