# Debate Transcript

## Advocate — round 1

The manuscript makes two genuinely novel, well-controlled claims that survive the panel's own searches: SLO-mediated delivery of M1 into the cytosol as the mechanism for M1-driven NLRP3 activation (answering the open question left by Valderrama 2017), and cytosolic histones in GAS-infected macrophages. The ΔM1 isogenic control (Fig 4A) is a clean genetic test, and the GBS negative control plus LAMP-1/Lamin A/C absence strengthen the histone finding. The reproducibility reviewer confirms the MS acquisition is specified in unusual detail and raw data are deposited with a working accession.

The panel's HARD objections are mostly fixable in revision, not fatal. The statistics reviewer's demand for n, named tests, and defined error bars is a reporting completion, not a design flaw. The literature reviewer's citation concern (ref 17, O'Neill 2016) is a genuine misattribution that must be corrected — I concede that point — but it does not touch the central claims. The rigor reviewer's 'all histone proteins' overstatement is an abstract-scaling fix.

The one substantive tension is the ΔSLO IL-1β response: the paper argues M1 'accounts for the majority' of the WT response while ΔSLO cytosol (no M1) also stimulates strongly. The novelty reviewer correctly notes the alternative — multiple redundant triggers — fits all data. But the authors' own qualification ('accounts for the majority... of WT-infected cytosol') is honest, and the abstract should be rewritten to match. This is a framing correction, not an evidence failure. The M1 finding remains the strongest, most defensible novelty, and the proposed neutralization/add-back experiment is a reasonable revision path, not grounds for rejection.

- Two load-bearing contributions — M1 cytosolic delivery and cytosolic histones — are genuinely new and survive the panel's related-work searches.
- The ΔM1 isogenic control (Fig 4A) and GBS negative control provide clean genetic tests for the central claims.
- Most HARD objections (n, named tests, error bars, abstract scaling) are reporting completions fixable in revision, not design flaws.
- I concede the ref 17 (O'Neill 2016) misattribution — a real error that must be corrected, but it does not touch the central claims.
- The ΔSLO IL-1β tension is a framing issue the authors already qualify; the abstract should be rewritten to match, and the proposed M1 neutralization/add-back experiment is a reasonable revision path.

## Skeptic — round 1

The advocate's framing — that the HARD objections are 'reporting completions' — collapses the load-bearing problem into a cosmetic one. The central claim that M1 'accounts for the majority of the inflammasome activation' is not merely under-reported; it is contradicted by the manuscript's own data. Fig 2C shows ΔSLO cytosol (no M1) elicits a strong IL-1β response, and the novelty reviewer correctly notes the redundant-triggers account fits all data. The 'majority' attribution rests solely on the WT-vs-ΔM1 comparison (Fig 4A), which the statistics reviewer flags as not shown to be significant — the legend says 'ns' for some comparisons but the specific WT-vs-DM1 test is never identified. If that comparison is not significant, the headline claim fails outright; if it is, the test and n must be stated. Either way, as reported, the claim is unverifiable.

Second, the literature reviewer identified a misattribution at the center of the paper's framing: ref 17 (O'Neill 2016) demonstrates GAS *does* escape and replicate in the cytosol, yet is cited for 'neither replicates nor escapes.' The advocate concedes this but calls it peripheral. It is not peripheral — it is the paper's own persistence framing, and the contradiction must be discussed in text, not just fixed in the reference list.

Third, the rigor reviewer's point stands: what drives the ΔSLO IL-1β response is untested (SpeB suggested but no mutant shown). A headline finding with an unidentified mechanism is not a framing fix.

These are fixable in revision, but they are substantive evidence gaps, not reporting completions.

**Key points:**
- The M1 'majority' claim rests on a WT-vs-ΔM1 comparison whose significance is never stated — if not significant, the headline fails.
- The ΔSLO IL-1β response (Fig 2C) is a headline finding with an untested mechanism (SpeB suggested, no mutant shown).
- Ref 17 (O'Neill 2016) contradicts the persistence framing it is cited for — a central tension, not a peripheral citation fix.
- The redundant-triggers account fits all data; 'majority' attribution is unsupported absolutely.
