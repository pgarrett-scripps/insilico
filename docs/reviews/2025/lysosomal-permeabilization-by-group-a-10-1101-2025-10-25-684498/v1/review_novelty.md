# Novelty & Contribution Reviewer

## Summary
The two load-bearing contributions — SLO-mediated delivery of M1 protein into the macrophage cytosol as the mechanism for M1-driven NLRP3 activation, and the presence of histones in the cytosol of GAS-infected macrophages — survive my related-work and preprint searches and appear genuinely new. No preempting work was found for either claim. The main weaknesses are positioning: the M1 claim is not reconciled with the manuscript's own ΔSLO data, and the significance of the histone finding outruns what the data demonstrate. The contribution is real but its framing needs sharpening against the closest prior work.

## Strengths
- The M1-delivery mechanism directly answers the open question left by Valderrama et al. 2017 (ref 22), who showed M1 activates NLRP3 but not how M1 reaches the cytosol.
- The ΔM1 comparison (Fig 4A) is a clean genetic test that isolates M1's contribution in WT-infected cytosol.
- The histone finding is unexpected and, to my searches, unreported for GAS-infected macrophages.

## Weaknesses
- The M1-cytosol claim is not reconciled with the manuscript's own ΔSLO data. The paper argues M1 'accounts for the majority of the inflammasome activation' in WT-infected cytosol, yet Fig 2C shows ΔSLO-infected cytosol elicits a strong IL-1β response in the absence of M1. The alternative account — that IL-1β in WT cytosol is driven by multiple redundant triggers (M1, SpeB, cathepsin B, other leaked proteins) rather than predominantly M1 — is consistent with all the data shown. The 'majority' attribution is only supported relative to the ΔM1 comparison, not absolutely. What would settle it: neutralize M1 in WT cytosol (antibody or recombinant M1 add-back to ΔM1 cytosol) and show the response tracks M1 specifically, alongside the ΔSLO comparison.
- The M1-cytosol claim does not exclude M1 acting outside the cytosol. M1 is found in WT cytosol (Supp Table 2), but the reduced IL-1β from ΔM1 cytosol could equally reflect loss of M1 acting at the phagosome surface, during uptake, or via released membrane vesicles rather than cytosolic M1 per se. The manuscript asserts the cytosolic route without a control that distinguishes cytosolic M1 from M1 present elsewhere in the infection. A head-to-head with the ΔSLO strain (which lacks SLO pores but still elicits IL-1β) would help isolate whether the SLO pore is specifically required for the M1 effect.
- The histone finding's significance is overstated relative to its evidence. The manuscript presents cytosolic histones as a 'surprising consequence' with implications for sepsis, but the origin (nuclear vs. serum/opsonization-derived), the mechanism of release, and any functional consequence are all undemonstrated. The absence of LAMP-1/Lamin A/C rules out organelle contamination, but the claim that this is a GAS-specific, biologically meaningful event needs a functional readout (e.g., antimicrobial or inflammatory activity of the released histones) before the significance claim is warranted.
- The claim that ΔSLO bacteria also permeabilize phagosomes and release proteins is largely a restatement of the authors' own prior work (Nishioka et al. 2024, ref 16); the delta here is the proteomic catalog, which is incremental.
- The finding that ΔSLO-infected cytosol stimulates IL-1β is in direct tension with Richter et al. 2021 ('Streptolysins are the primary inflammasome activators'), which the manuscript cites but does not fully reconcile; a head-to-head discussion of why the authors' result diverges is needed.
- The proteomics is descriptive and the 'resource' value is asserted rather than demonstrated; the paper would be strengthened by stating what downstream use the catalog enables.
- No explicit 'first/novel' claims are made, so the priority check rests on implied novelty, which passes my searches.
- The M1 finding is the strongest and most defensible novelty; the histone finding is novel but unexplained, and its framing should be scaled to match.

## Questions
- Can M1 be neutralized in WT-infected cytosol (or added back to ΔM1 cytosol) to confirm that cytosolic M1 specifically drives the IL-1β response?
- What is the origin of the cytosolic histones — nuclear release, or carryover from opsonizing serum — and is there a functional consequence of their presence?
