# Novelty & Contribution Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript's core contributions — (i) that M1 protein is delivered to the macrophage cytosol via SLO-mediated phagolysosomal permeabilization, providing the previously-missing delivery mechanism for M1's established NLRP3 activation, and (ii) the unexpected presence of histones in the cytosol of GAS-infected macrophages — are genuinely new. My related-work and preprint searches (queries on GAS macrophage cytosol proteomics, SLO/M1/NLRP3, and histone release) returned no prior work reporting the same results; the only matching entries were the manuscript itself. The contribution is real and appropriately scaled, with two SOFT positioning issues below.

## Strengths
- The M1-cytosolic-delivery claim is a genuine delta over Valderrama et al. 2017 (Nat Microbiol), which showed recombinant M1 activates NLRP3 but left the natural delivery route open; this paper closes that gap with a direct mechanism.
- The histone finding is a surprising, well-controlled observation (absent in GBS-infected and uninfected cytosol, with organellar-contamination controls).
- The manuscript is honest about its own limits (e.g., it cannot determine the origin/mechanism of histone release) rather than overclaiming.

## Weaknesses
- The DSLO-elicits-IL-1β claim contradicts prior published work and the reconciliation is thin. The manuscript states this "in contrast to previous findings" (Harder et al. 2009, J Immunol; Richter et al. 2021, Immunol Cell Biol), which both concluded SLO is required for NLRP3 activation. The authors attribute DSLO activity to SpeB and CD63/LAMP-3-mediated permeabilization, but they do not explain why prior groups saw no DSLO response — a head-to-head account of the discrepancy (assay differences, strain, MOI, the 30-kD cutoff, or the CD63 pathway being newly described) is needed. As it stands, the "regardless of SLO expression" framing rests on a result that the nearest competing work contradicts, and the differentiation is asserted rather than argued. This is a SOFT issue (the work is named and a mechanism offered) but it is the one place where the novelty claim could be preempted or artifact-driven, and it deserves a sharper treatment.
- The "smaller pores in DSLO-infected cells" explanation for M1's absence is inferred, not demonstrated. The claim that CD63/LAMP-3-mediated permeabilization produces pores too small for M1 but large enough for SpeB is used to explain the WT/DSLO difference, yet no pore-size measurement is offered for the DSLO pathway (the >40-kD dextran data cited are from the SLO/WT context). If the DSLO cytosol simply lacks M1 for another reason (e.g., lower bacterial load or different M1 expression), the mechanistic framing overreaches. This is a magnitude/positioning question rather than a preemption.

## Questions
- Can the authors state the residual IL-1β response in ΔM1-infected cytosol as a fraction of the WT response, and does it reach baseline?
- What explains the discrepancy with Harder 2009 and Richter 2021, who found no NLRP3 activation without SLO — assay, strain, or the CD63/LAMP-3 pathway being newly characterized?
- Is there direct evidence (pore-size or dextran-leakage data) for the DSLO/CD63 permeabilization being smaller than SLO pores, or is that inferred from the M1/SpeB size difference alone?
