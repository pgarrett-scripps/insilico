# Journal Recommendations

## As-is (current quality)
- **bioRxiv**
  - Fit: This is a preprint server designed for biology and life-sciences computational work. The manuscript's mathematical modeling of virus-DIP coevolution, public code, and sensitivity analysis are well-suited to bioRxiv's audience, which expects rigorous but not yet journal-polished work. The paper's current state—sound methodology, clear presentation, but with acknowledged gaps in empirical grounding and necessity claims—is appropriate for preprint circulation and community feedback.
  - Realism: bioRxiv accepts all technically sound preprints without peer review, so posting is essentially guaranteed. However, this is a preprint server, not a peer-reviewed venue, so it does not constitute publication in the traditional sense.
- **In Silico**
  - Fit: In Silico is explicitly designed to review preprints already posted elsewhere and publish the complete referee record. The manuscript's claims are checkable from the paper and deposited code; the work is original research with no patient-safety implications; and the central questions (whether continuous diffusion is necessary, whether the DePolo match is quantitative) are exactly the kind of evidence-versus-claim gaps that In Silico's public, transparent review process is built to surface and debate. The panel's major verdict and the specific, actionable critique make this an ideal fit for In Silico's model.
  - Realism: In Silico's scope explicitly includes work with major revisions needed, and its review-as-publication model means the paper's current state—competent but with unsupported claims—is publishable as-is with a transparent report. However, acceptance of a preprint for review does not guarantee a favorable outcome; the published review will flag the necessity and DePolo gaps prominently. Authors should expect a detailed, critical public record.

## After required revisions
- **PLoS Computational Biology**
  - Fit: This venue publishes computational and mathematical models of biological systems, including virus dynamics, with an emphasis on mechanistic insight and reproducibility. After addressing the editor's required revisions—running null-model comparisons to support the necessity claim, rescoping the DePolo match to qualitative structure with quantified magnitude shortfall, and closing the methods-reproducibility gaps—the paper will have demonstrated both the sufficiency of continuous diffusion and honest calibration of empirical contact. The sensitivity analysis and four-regime classification are substantive contributions to understanding virus-DIP coevolution.
  - Realism: PLoS Computational Biology accepts papers at the major-revision stage if the revisions are substantive and the core contribution is sound. This paper's mathematical framework, public code, and sensitivity analysis are genuine strengths; the revisions address the panel's specific critiques without requiring new experiments. Acceptance is realistic but not assured, as the journal will expect the null-model comparison and DePolo rescoping to be executed cleanly.
- **Journal of Theoretical Biology**
  - Fit: This journal is a natural home for phenotype-structured coevolutionary models with explicit mathematical treatment and parameter sensitivity analysis. The paper's framework—continuous diffusion in phenotype space, interference kernels, fitness landscapes—is squarely in JTB's scope. After revisions, the paper will have demonstrated the necessity of continuous diffusion (via null models), honestly stated the limits of empirical contact (DePolo magnitude gap), and provided complete reproducibility details. The four dynamical regimes and their parameter boundaries are the kind of theoretical prediction JTB values.
  - Realism: JTB is a strong fit for the post-revision manuscript. The journal values rigorous mathematical modeling and sensitivity analysis; the panel's major verdict reflects unsupported claims, not flawed mathematics. Once the null-model comparison and DePolo rescoping are in place, the paper will be well-positioned. Acceptance is plausible but depends on whether the null models actually support the necessity claim or reveal that simpler mechanisms suffice.
- **Virus Evolution**
  - Fit: This journal focuses on viral evolution, including coevolutionary dynamics, and explicitly welcomes computational and theoretical work. The paper's focus on virus-DIP coevolution, Red Queen dynamics, and therapeutic implications aligns directly with the journal's scope. After revisions, the paper will have addressed the necessity claim (null models) and calibrated the empirical contact (DePolo), making it a solid contribution to understanding how viruses and their parasitic variants coevolve.
  - Realism: Virus Evolution is a good fit for the post-revision manuscript, though it is a more specialized venue than PLoS Comp Biol or JTB. The journal's audience will appreciate the mechanistic modeling and sensitivity analysis. However, the paper's therapeutic framing—while improved by mapping phenotype axes to measurable traits—may still be viewed as aspirational rather than actionable, which could limit enthusiasm.

## Alternative outlets
- **arXiv q-bio.PE (Quantitative Methods)**
  - Fit: If the paper cannot reach peer-reviewed venues after revision, arXiv is a permanent, citable preprint repository for quantitative biology and population ecology. The manuscript's mathematical rigor, public code, and sensitivity analysis make it a solid arXiv contribution. Posting here ensures the work is discoverable and citable by the community, even if journal publication is delayed or unsuccessful.
  - Realism: arXiv accepts all technically sound submissions without review. This is a fallback to ensure the work reaches the community, but it does not constitute peer-reviewed publication.
- **SIAM Journal on Applied Mathematics**
  - Fit: If the paper's biological framing is de-emphasized and the focus is shifted to the mathematical framework itself—coupled PDEs with convolution kernels, phenotype-space diffusion, sensitivity analysis—it could fit SIAM's scope for applied mathematics. The paper's mathematical contributions (the zero-sum resource-transfer identity, the Gaussian interference kernel, the four-regime classification) are sound and could appeal to a mathematical audience.
  - Realism: SIAM is a stretch for this manuscript because the biological motivation and empirical grounding are central to the paper's value; stripping them away would weaken the contribution. However, if the authors choose to reframe the work as a mathematical study of coevolutionary PDEs with applications to virology, SIAM could be a fallback. Acceptance would be uncertain and would require substantial rewriting.
- **Epidemiology or Evolution workshop (e.g., SMBE, Evolution conference proceedings)**
  - Fit: If the paper does not reach a major journal, presenting it at a specialist conference (Society for Molecular Biology and Evolution, Evolution annual meeting) and publishing in the proceedings is a viable path. The work's focus on viral coevolution and therapeutic design is of interest to these communities. A workshop or poster presentation would allow the authors to gather feedback and refine the work before a second journal submission.
  - Realism: Conference proceedings are less selective than journals and provide a venue for work in progress. This is a genuine fallback if journal rejection occurs, but it does not replace peer-reviewed publication.
