# Decision Letter

**Decision:** major

## Summary of Evaluation
The panel converged on a consistent picture: the PDE framework for virus–DIP phenotypic coevolution is competently constructed, clearly presented, well parameterised, and accompanied by public code (clarity 4, data_analysis 4, reproducibility 4, ethics 5). The 10,000-sample Latin Hypercube sensitivity analysis mapping four dynamical regimes is regarded across the panel as a genuine contribution. No reviewer recommended rejection and no fatal flaw was identified, so rejection is not warranted. However, four independent high-confidence reviewers (rigor, methodology, novelty, literature) converged on two load-bearing gaps: (i) the paper's only contact with experimental data — the DePolo et al. time-shift comparison in Fig 6 — is described as 'reproducing'/'matching' a pattern that the manuscript itself acknowledges differs by orders of magnitude in scale; and (ii) the paper never shows that continuous phenotype-diffusion is *necessary* rather than merely sufficient for the observed oscillations and chase dynamics, because no discrete-mutation or fixed-phenotype null model is run. In the debate the advocate treated both as rewording issues; the skeptic's reply — that the necessity claim requires an actual null-model comparison — went uncontested and matches four independent reports. That is what places this at major rather than minor revision.

The rebuttal materially improved the picture and I have weighed it. The authors concede seven items and propose concrete, tractable fixes (softened DePolo language plus a quantitative fold-change comparison; a supplementary discrete-substitution/fixed-phenotype null run; bootstrap CIs, seeds, convergence plot and threshold sensitivity for the LHS; an explicit novelty-versus-Alfaro delineation; a fixed-DIP-density control for the Fig 7 conflation; citation-hygiene fixes; a resource-transfer/carrying-capacity assumption sweep). These are exactly the right remedies and are within reach in one revision cycle. I also accept three of the four disagreements. The 'tautology' charge against the high-α trapping result is answered by the quoted text: the α–κ escape threshold and its location are simulation outputs, not restatements of the definition of α, and I do not require further work there beyond making that argument explicit in the Discussion. The objection that pre-1955 von Magnus references 'cannot be verified through modern indexing' is not a defect — absence from PubMed for 1951–54 papers is expected, the citations are formally complete per the citation audit, and the claims are corroborated by cited modern reviews. The μ-insensitivity point is already flagged in the Results as a parameter-bound caveat, and the authors correctly note that the γ and η sweeps are nonmonotonic rather than intuitive; that is a framing improvement, not new work.

On the one load-bearing critique the authors say they cannot meet: I agree that a quantitative, order-of-magnitude match to DePolo et al. requiring a new higher-dimensional 'corkscrew' model is out of scope for this revision, and I am not requiring it. What I do require is that the claim be scaled to the evidence — the manuscript may claim qualitative reproduction of cyclical/transient resistance structure with a stated magnitude shortfall, and must not claim reproduction of the experimental data. That is a claims-calibration requirement, not a new-experiment requirement.

The methods audit adds two HARD reproducibility gaps (solver/discretisation/convergence details; software, library versions and hardware) that must be closed regardless of the scientific revisions. The citation audit found no hard gaps.

## Required Revisions
1. Rescope the DePolo et al. comparison. Replace 'reproduces'/'matches' in the Abstract, Introduction, Results and Fig 6 caption with language stating that the model qualitatively reproduces the cyclical/transient structure of resistance but substantially underestimates its magnitude. Add an explicit quantitative statement in Results giving the modeled peak-to-baseline resistance fold-change alongside the experimentally reported fold-change, so readers can size the discrepancy without inferring it from prose. Retain and make prominent the Discussion statement that a higher-dimensional extension is required to close this gap; the extension itself is not required for this revision.
2. Run and report the null-model comparison for the necessity claim. Add a supplementary simulation at matched parameters of (a) a discrete-substitution variant with μ driven toward zero and/or (b) a fixed-phenotype virus/DIP predator–prey ODE lacking the diffusion term, and report whether oscillatory/chase behaviour is attenuated or absent. Change all 'requires' framing in the Introduction and Discussion to 'sufficient to generate', with the null-model result cited as the evidence for whatever necessity-adjacent claim survives. If the null model unexpectedly reproduces the chase, report that outcome and revise the mechanistic claim accordingly.
3. Close the two HARD methods-reproducibility gaps flagged by the compliance audit. In Methods, state: the PDE solution method (finite difference/element/spectral), spatial grid spacing and time step, convergence criteria/tolerance, and how the 10^2 extinction threshold is enforced numerically; and the programming language, PDE solver and core library names with versions, plus the hardware used and an approximate compute budget for the 10,000-sample LHS.
4. Strengthen the LHS sensitivity analysis as conceded in the rebuttal: report bootstrap confidence intervals on the regime proportions in Fig 5a–f; state the LHS implementation (library, version, stratification/maximin option) and the random seed(s); add a convergence plot showing regime proportions at n = 2,000/5,000/10,000; and add a supplementary check varying the chase (centroid-distance variation) and extinction thresholds by an order of magnitude to show the four-regime classification is not an artefact of the chosen cutoffs.
5. Disambiguate the Fig 7 evolutionary-conflict result. Add the control simulation the authors propose, holding DIP density fixed and nonzero independently of α, and report the resulting virus–DIP phenotypic distance in a supplementary figure, so that 'trapping' at high α is shown to reflect a genuine fitness constraint rather than DIP suppression/extinction. Also make explicit in the Discussion the non-tautological content of the α–κ threshold result (that the threshold location and its κ-dependence are simulation outputs), since this was contested in review and the authors' reply is persuasive but is not currently stated in the manuscript.
6. Delineate novelty relative to Alfaro et al. [39]. Add a short paragraph or table in Methods stating precisely which components are adapted (diffusion term, centering α and aggregation β fitness terms) and which are introduced here (Gaussian convolution interference terms Eqs 1–3, de novo DIP generation term η, zero-sum resource-transfer identity Eq 4).
7. Test the untested structural assumptions. Add a supplementary LHS sweep varying (a) a resource-transfer efficiency multiplier on Eq (2) spanning negative- through positive-sum regimes and (b) a per-particle carrying-capacity weight favouring DIPs, and report in a supplementary figure and a Discussion paragraph whether the Fig 5 regime boundaries shift materially.
8. Specify the initial-condition and simulation setup completely enough to rerun without reading the code: exact functional form and normalisation of the initial Gaussian distributions, total simulation time, output sampling interval, and the criterion used to decide a run has reached steady state versus sustained oscillation.
9. Apply the conceded citation-hygiene corrections: add the journal name (J Virol) to the DePolo et al. (1987) reference; paraphrase the Giachetti & Holland (1988) finding in-text (polymerase mutations reducing DIP-template affinity while preserving genome replication) at the point ref 32 is cited; and specify in-text that Pelz et al. used semi-continuous bioreactor propagation of influenza A virus and its DIPs. Note that ref [31] (Gaba & Ebert 2009) is a methods/review source, not the original experimental report of transient resistance — cite it as such.

## Minor Suggestions
- Deposit an environment specification (requirements.txt, environment.yml or Dockerfile) in the GitHub repository and reference it in the Data/Code Availability statement; add a README mapping each script to the figure it produces. Reviewers could not inspect the repository, so the manuscript should make its completeness self-evident.
- For Fig 6c, state in the caption how the DePolo et al. data were extracted (digitised from which panel, or tabulated from the original text), since the replot cannot otherwise be checked against the source.
- Consider promoting the nonmonotonic γ and η results to the Abstract or a short 'non-obvious findings' paragraph. Two reviewers read the sensitivity analysis as confirming intuition; the authors' rebuttal makes a fair case that these two sweeps are not intuitive, and the manuscript would benefit from saying so directly.
- The μ-insensitivity caveat is already stated in Results; consider carrying it into the Discussion limitations as well, and stating explicitly what wider μ range would be needed to test whether the effect is genuinely weak or bound-limited.
- Map at least one or two of the abstract phenotype axes to a measurable biological quantity (even hypothetically, e.g. polymerase binding affinity or genome length), or state plainly in the Discussion that the axes are abstract and that therapeutic implications are therefore directional rather than quantitative. Two reviewers found the therapeutic framing outran the model's interpretability.
