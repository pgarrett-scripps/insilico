# Novelty & Contribution Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript applies an existing phenotype-space coevolution framework (Alfaro et al. 2024) to virus-DIP systems with biologically motivated extensions (de novo DIP generation, zero-sum interference). It successfully reproduces qualitative experimental phenomena and identifies parameter regimes favoring different outcomes. However, the core methodological novelty is limited—the PDE formalism and strong-mutation approach are not new—and the mechanistic claims about Chase Red Queen dynamics are not uniquely supported by the quantitative evidence (the model underfits experimental data by orders of magnitude). The sensitivity analysis is competent but yields largely intuitive results. The work is a solid application study with useful biological insights, but it does not establish a fundamentally new theoretical framework or make predictions that substantially advance understanding of virus-DIP coevolution beyond what existing models and intuition would suggest.

## Strengths
- The model successfully integrates multiple biological mechanisms (mutation, interference, de novo DIP generation, intrinsic fitness costs) into a unified PDE framework, providing a mechanistically rich foundation for exploring coevolutionary outcomes.
- The authors are transparent about model limitations (two-dimensional phenotype space, simplified kernels, deterministic dynamics) and test robustness by varying interference kernels and dimensionality, demonstrating that core results are not artifacts of specific functional forms.
- The connection to experimental time-shift data (Fig. 6) and the identification of parameter regimes (Fig. 5) provide actionable predictions that could guide future laboratory evolution studies, particularly the prediction that higher bioreactor washout rates should reduce chase dynamics.

## Weaknesses
- Methodological novelty is incremental: the manuscript builds directly on Alfaro et al. [39] (2024), which already develops the phenotype-space PDE framework with convolution kernels and quadratic fitness penalties for host-pathogen coevolution in strong-mutation regimes; the authors acknowledge this but do not clearly specify what is methodologically novel beyond applying Alfaro's framework to a new system with two new terms (de novo DIP generation and zero-sum interference kernel), which are domain-specific extensions rather than methodological innovations.
- Mechanistic explanation of transient resistance is not uniquely supported: the model reproduces the qualitative pattern but the authors acknowledge that quantitative fit is poor (modeled resistance varies linearly while experimental data show orders-of-magnitude changes), and the model cannot capture the directional drift in high-dimensional space that the authors identify as necessary; a simpler alternative explanation (directional drift with occasional phenotypic returns) would produce the same qualitative pattern and may be more parsimonious.
- Sensitivity analysis yields intuitive rather than novel insights: the finding that intermediate interference promotes chase while high interference drives extinction is mechanistically obvious, and the finding that low decay rates promote chase follows directly from the model's structure; the paper does not compare these predictions to prior theoretical work or experiments to establish whether they are novel or merely confirmatory.
- The zero-sum interference assumption is justified as a 'neutral baseline' because true efficiency is ambiguous, but this sidesteps rather than resolves the question; positive-sum and negative-sum scenarios could yield qualitatively different outcomes and deserve exploratory analysis.
- Mutation rate (μ) has negligible effect on outcomes (Fig. 5f), yet it is a core parameter in the strong-mutation regime; this suggests either the parameter range is too narrow or the model is insensitive to a key biological variable, neither of which is discussed.
- The assumption that virus and DIP contribute equally to carrying capacity is acknowledged as a simplification but not tested; DIPs with shorter genomes likely consume fewer resources per particle, potentially biasing population ratio predictions.
- Therapeutic implications are aspirational; the model makes no predictions about real TIP candidates and does not connect abstract phenotype axes to measurable viral traits, limiting practical relevance.
- The 2023 preprint 'No two without three' on virus-DIP-satellite coevolution is not cited or compared, despite using similar PDE machinery; this omission weakens positioning within recent literature.

## Questions
- Can the authors specify which mathematical components (beyond the biological parameterization) are novel relative to Alfaro et al. [39], or is the contribution primarily in application and interpretation?
- What would distinguish the Chase Red Queen mechanism from a simpler directional-drift-with-occasional-returns model, given that both produce the observed transient resistance pattern and the model underfits the experimental data quantitatively?
- Have the authors tested whether the sensitivity analysis results (Fig. 5) hold under alternative interference kernels and fitness landscapes, or are they specific to the Gaussian and quadratic choices?
