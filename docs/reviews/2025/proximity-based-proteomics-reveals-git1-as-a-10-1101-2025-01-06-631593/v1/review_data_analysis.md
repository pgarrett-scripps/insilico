# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
This is a major-revision manuscript from a data-analysis standpoint. The proteomic discovery pipeline is well-conceived and the TMT normalization strategy is defensible, but nearly every downstream follow-up comparison—where the paper's central claims about Git1 function rest—lacks the statistical reporting needed to evaluate them. Parametric tests (t-test, ANOVA) are used throughout without any stated check of normality or variance homogeneity; n values are given for some quantifications but the unit of replication (cilium, cell, field, independent culture) is often ambiguous; multiple comparisons across time points, clones and antibodies are not corrected; and a key 'no difference' claim (cilium length) is supported by a non-significant result in an unpowered sample. The proteomic data-analysis pipeline itself is better documented but still has gaps: the TMT ratio cutoff (>1.5) is stated without an accompanying FDR or permutation-based threshold; volcano plot labels lack a multiple-testing annotation; and the Git1 interaction evidence from the proteomic data is marginal (slight increase after Shh). The rescue experiment (Fig. 6G-H) is promising but the quantification relies on a single comparison without a formal interaction test.

## Weaknesses
- The load-bearing claim that Git1 knockout reduces Hh signaling (Figs 6A, 7C) rests on Gli1 qPCR fold-changes compared between WT and Git1-null cells. The test is named (two-way ANOVA followed by Tukey), but no information is given about the number of independent biological replicates per genotype × treatment cell, how normalization was performed (reference gene? geomean of multiple references? stability tested?), or whether the ANOVA assumptions were checked for qPCR data (typically log2-transformed to better approximate normality). The fold-change scale on the y-axis makes the interaction visually obvious but a formal interaction term in the ANOVA should be reported.
- The 'no difference' claim that Git1 knockout does not affect cilium length (Fig. S4E) is supported by a non-significant ANOVA result. With n=60 cells per condition from 3 biological replicates, the study may lack power to detect small differences; a power analysis or an equivalence test (e.g., TOST) is needed to support a claim of no effect. Furthermore, cells from the same culture dish are not independent—pseudoreplication inflates the n.
- Figures 4E, 4G, 5D-E, and 6F quantify immunofluorescence intensity using two-way ANOVA with Tukey's multiple comparison test. Which conditions were compared and what is the family (i.e., how many pairwise tests)? If all pairwise comparisons among time points × genotypes were tested, the number is large and a stricter correction (e.g., Bonferroni or Holm) or a pre-registered subset should be stated. No interaction term from the ANOVA is reported.
- For every immunofluorescence quantification (Figs 4E, 4G, 5D-E, 6F), the text states n = 90-150 cells/condition from 3 biological replicates. The unit of analysis is ambiguous: cells from three independent experiments are not independent units if all were pooled for the ANOVA—the effective n is 3 experiments, not the cell count. The analysis should use a mixed model or average per experiment and test at the experiment level.
- The proteomic data analysis (Fig. 2) uses a >1.5-fold TMT ratio and p<0.05 from an Empirical Bayes model as the hit threshold. No FDR, permutation, or q-value is reported for this multi-thousand-test screen. The >1.5 cutoff is arbitrary without a false-discovery assessment. The heatmap (Fig. 2D) and boxplots (Fig. 2E) show selected proteins without an FDR label — readers cannot tell which hits survive correction.
- The Git1 biotinylation experiment (Fig. 4B) is interpreted as showing Git1 is 'biotinylated before and after Shh stimulation but the intensity is slightly increased after Shh stimulation.' This is a qualitative reading of a western blot with no loading control normalization quantified, no replicates quantified, and no statistical test—it cannot support the claim of a Shh-dependent increase.
- Figures 1C, 4H-I, 6A-B, 7B-C use 'n = 3 biological replicates' or similar. For the western blots (Fig. 6B-D, S3B, S4C), no n is stated for quantifications; in Figure 6C-D the n=4 independent experiments is given but the error bars (SD) are shown only for selected bars. Full transparency requires reporting individual points or a consistent n for all quantified conditions.
- The qPCR data in Figures 6A and 7C do not state the reference gene(s) used for normalization, whether its stability was tested across conditions, or whether the delta-Ct method (or a standard-curve method) was used. Fold-change relative to a single reference without validation can be misleading if the reference itself changes with genotype or treatment
