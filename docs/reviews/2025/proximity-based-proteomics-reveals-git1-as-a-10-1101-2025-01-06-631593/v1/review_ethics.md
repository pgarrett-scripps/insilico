# Ethics & Compliance Reviewer

## Summary
This manuscript uses primary tissue from postnatal day 7 mice (cerebellar granule neuron precursors) and discloses funding but never declares competing interests. Two triggered compliance categories — animal research and competing-interest disclosure — are met with silence. Both are fixable with explicit statements, but as written the manuscript does not document the approvals and declarations required for the work it reports.

## Strengths
- Funding sources are fully and specifically disclosed in the Acknowledgements.
- The work uses established cell lines (NIH3T3, HEK293, MEFs) and standard reagents; no human-subjects, clinical-trial, dual-use, or restricted-consent categories are triggered.

## Weaknesses
- Animal research is reported with no IACUC statement (HARD). The Materials and Methods describe harvesting cerebella from postnatal day 7 C57BL/6J mice for primary GNP culture, and Figure 7 reports results from those cultures. No animal care and use approval, institution, or protocol number is stated anywhere in the manuscript. The claim that this work was conducted under appropriate animal ethics oversight rests on an approval that is never named. What would settle it: a statement identifying the IACUC (or equivalent) that approved the protocol, with the approval or protocol number. This is a required statement, not a formality — the manuscript's own methods section establishes that the category is triggered.
- Competing interests are never declared (HARD). Funding is disclosed, but the manuscript contains no competing-interests statement of any kind — neither a declaration of interests nor an explicit "the authors declare no competing interests." The mandate here is that competing interests be declared or explicitly declared as none. What would settle it: a one-line statement in the manuscript declaring the presence or absence of competing interests for all authors.
- No ethics statement for the PKA-null MEF work. The PKA-deficient MEFs are described as derived from a colleague's lab and further modified by CRISPR; if these cells trace to animal-derived primary material, the same IACUC statement should cover them. This is a SOFT request to confirm the source is covered by the same approval, not a separate failure.

## Questions
- Which IACUC (or equivalent animal ethics committee) approved the use of P7 mice for GNP isolation, and what is the protocol number?
- Do the authors declare any competing interests, financial or otherwise?
