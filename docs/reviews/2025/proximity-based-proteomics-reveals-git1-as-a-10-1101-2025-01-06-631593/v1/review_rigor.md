# Rigor & Overclaiming Reviewer

## Summary
This is a substantial, well-executed study whose central finding — that Git1 promotes Grk2-dependent Smo phosphorylation and thereby Hh signaling — is supported by multiple converging lines (pSmo loss with normal Smo accumulation, reduced ciliary PKA-C, Gli1/Gli3/Gli2 readouts, and a Grk2-ciliary-targeting rescue). The main rigor problems are over-strong causal wording at the abstract level and two places where the evidence does not fully isolate the claim as worded: the rescue lacks a non-ciliary Grk2 control, and the Grk2-transport conclusion rests on a small effect in overexpressed tagged protein. These are fixable with one control and tightened language rather than fatal.

## Strengths
- The causal chain is tested at multiple independent points (pSmo, PKA-C, Gli1/Gli3/Gli2, and a rescue), so the core conclusion does not rest on a single assay.
- The paper is candid about its own limits — e.g., that endogenous ciliary PKA-C and Grk2 are below detection and require the Smo-TurboID line or lentiviral overexpression, and that Git1-Smo interaction is not Co-IP-detectable.
- The Smo-TurboID line is carefully validated against self-activation (no ciliary Smo, no Gli1, near-endogenous expression, normal cilia) before use.

## Weaknesses
- The rescue does not isolate ciliary targeting as the operative variable. 'Selectively targeting Grk2 to the cilium is sufficient to restore Hh signaling' (Fig 6G-H) compares Grk2-V5-DArl13b against a V5-DArl13b backbone control, not against Grk2-V5 without the ciliary tag. As designed, the result shows that Grk2 expression rescues Gli1 in Git1-null cells; it does not show that ciliary localization specifically (rather than Grk2 presence anywhere) is what rescues. This matters because the paper's headline mechanistic conclusion is 'pinpoint the cilium as the primary site where this phosphorylation occurs.' The missing control — Grk2-V5 lacking DArl13b — would distinguish 'Grk2 anywhere restores signaling' from 'ciliary Grk2 is required,' and without it the 'primary site' claim is not established as worded.
- The claim that 'Git1 controls the cilium transport of Grk2' rests entirely on lentivirally overexpressed Grk2-V5 (endogenous ciliary Grk2 is below detection), and the WT effect is only a 'moderate but significant 1.2-fold increase' (Fig 5E). A 1.2-fold change in a tagged, overexpressed protein, contrasted with 'no detectable Grk2' in the KO, is thin support for a strong causal verb ('controls'); the result could reflect altered Grk2-V5 handling or retention rather than endogenous transport. The paper also asserts that a basal-body protein promotes Grk2 entry into the cilium without testing any transport mechanism — the mechanism is inferred, not isolated.
- The abstract's causal chain crosses systems: 'Loss of Git1 impairs Smo phosphorylation by Grk2... leading to attenuated Hh signaling and reduced cell proliferation in granule neuron precursors.' The Smo-phosphorylation and Hh-signaling data are all in NIH3T3 cells; the GNP experiments test only Git1 knockdown → reduced Gli1 and reduced EdU incorporation. Git1 has well-established pleiotropic functions (cell adhesion, migration, ArfGAP activity), so the reduced GNP proliferation is not shown to be Hh-mediated, and no rescue links the proliferation defect to the Smo/Grk2 mechanism. The 'leading to... reduced cell proliferation' wording overstates what the GNP data establish.
- Sweep: 'Git1 is essential for Hh signaling' (Fig 7) is over-strong given Gli1 is reduced to 15–30% of control, not abolished — 'essential' should be 'important' or 'required for full activation.'
- Sweep: The Discussion's 'Git1 interacts with both Smo and Grk2 at the cilium base' overstates the evidence: Git1–Smo proximity is shown only by TurboID biotinylation of overexpressed YFP-Git1, and Git1–Grk2 interaction only by Co-IP of co-overexpressed proteins in 293T; neither demonstrates an endogenous ternary interaction at the cilium base.
- Sweep: The proteomic foundation (Tables S1–S5, the 1070-protein list, the 450/576 candidate sets) is not inspectable from the manuscript text, so I could not verify the dataset that motivates the Git1 candidate; this lowers my confidence in the discovery claim, not the functional characterization.
- Sweep: The 'primary site' conclusion also overreaches on the negative side — the paper shows basal-body Grk2 is retained in Git1-null cells and that ciliary Grk2 is sufficient, but 'the pool of Grk2 at the basal body is less likely contributing' is asserted from absence-of-effect rather than a direct test.

## Questions
- Can you add a rescue arm expressing Grk2-V5 without the DArl13b ciliary tag, and report whether it also restores Gli1 in Git1-null cells — this is the control that isolates ciliary localization as the operative variable?
- For the Grk2-transport claim, can you report the endogenous Grk2 ciliary signal (or a Grk2-V5 titration) and the absolute magnitude of the WT increase, to show the 1.2-fold effect is not an artifact of overexpression?
- In the GNP experiments, is the reduced EdU incorporation attributable to reduced Hh signaling specifically, or could it reflect Git1's known roles in adhesion/migration — is there any Hh-specific readout or rescue linking proliferation to the Smo/Grk2 pathway in GNPs?
