# Methodology Reviewer

## Summary
The design is logical overall and the TurboID time-course is a genuine improvement over co-IP. The central causal claim — Git1 enables Grk2 translocation to the cilium, enabling Smo phosphorylation — is provisionally supported, but there are gaps in the quantitative chain from that translocation deficit to the claimed conclusions, and a few controls are absent or described too loosely to assess fully. The rescue experiment is the strongest link; the Grk2 translocation measurement is the weakest.

## Strengths
- The use of TurboID with a selected near-endogenous-expression clone, and the time-resolved design (15 min, 1 h, 4 h), directly address the limitation of co-IP for transient interactions — the rationale is well-argued and the controls for Smo self-activation are convincing.
- The rescue experiment (Fig. 6G–H) — targeting Grk2 to the cilium in Git1-null cells and measuring Gli1 transcription — is exactly the kind of causal chain the design needs. It links the Git1→Grk2-transport deficit to the pathway output.
- The Git1 Grk2 interaction is confirmed by co-IP and colocalisation, and the two knockout cell clones are verified by sequencing, immunoblot, and their intact ciliogenesis, which eliminates trivial explanations for the signalling defect.

## Weaknesses
- HARD — The claim that Git1 controls Grk2 ‘translocation into the cilium’ rests on quantifying Grk2-V5 signal in lentivirally transduced cells (Fig. 5C–E). The ciliary Grk2 signal in WT cells after 30 min Shh is only ~1.2-fold above background, and the text labels the Git1-null signal as ‘background levels’ — but no formal threshold (e.g., >2 SD above the mean of a no-primary or untransduced control) is stated, and no positive control shows that the imaging system can resolve a signal of 1.2-fold from noise. A 20% increase at the very limit of detection is not a sound foundation for a mechanistic conclusion that the entire paper rests on. A second orthogonal readout — e.g., biochemical enrichment of Grk2 from cilia-enriched fractions — would address this. Without it, the claim is indistinguishable from ‘we cannot measure Grk2 in the cilium under these conditions’.
- HARD — The conclusion that ‘Git1 loss reduces ciliary Grk2 independently of effects at the basal body’ is contradicted by the authors’ own data. Fig. 5D shows that basal-body Grk2 in unstimulated Git1-null cells is already lower than in WT unstimulated cells (the text says ‘slightly lower’ but the figure appears to show a difference comparable in magnitude to the ciliary difference). If Grk2 is reduced at the entry point of the cilium, its failure to accumulate in the cilium could reflect a smaller available pool rather than a specific translocation defect. The design does not distinguish these alternatives, and the claim that ‘Git1 does not mediate Grk2 localization to the basal body’ is not supported.
- SOFT — The proteomic hit list is presented as a discovery resource (450 and 576 candidates), but the filtering pipeline (removal of keratins, endogenously biotinylated proteins, then requiring detection in all six channels across three replicates, then eBayes P<0.05 and FC>1.5) is typical and not described with enough quantitative detail to assess false-discovery stringency. The TMT ratios are normalised but only one normalisation method is named (TMM), with a supplementary table referenced. A reviewer in my role cannot reconstruct what was excluded at each step, and the paper does not report FDR or a multiple-testing correction for the eBayes contrasts. This does not invalidate the resource, but it limits how much weight the reader can assign to any individual candidate.
- SOFT — The Grk2 rescue construct (Grk2-V5-DArl13b) targets Grk2 to the cilium, but the control used is ‘V5-DArl13b backbone’ (Fig. 6H). This does not control for the fact that the rescue cells express additional Grk2 (fused to a cilium-localising domain) while the control expresses only the Arl13b fragment. The improved signalling could therefore reflect increased Grk2 dosage rather than its ciliary location per se. A proper control would be Grk2-V5 fused to a non-ciliary membrane anchor, or Grk2-V5 without the DArl13b domain.
- Minor — The cyclopamine experiment (Fig. 3C–D) is used to argue that ‘inactive Smo cannot recruit PKA-C’. This is a known conclusion from earlier work (cited). The design is sound but the time course extends to 24 h without showing a positive control that the cyclopamine is active and the cells are still responsive — e.g., that Smo remains at the cilium across that interval. Without it, the flat PKA-C signal could equally mean loss of cyclopamine activity or drug toxicity by 24 h.

## Questions
- Fig. 5C–D reports Grk2 intensity at the basal body in Git1-null cells. What is the positive control that confirms the vehicle- and Shh-stimulated WT responses in the same experiment are within the dynamic range of the assay? A line graph at fixed time points cannot distinguish "no increase" from "increase too small to resolve" without that calibration.
- For the TurboID proteomics (Fig. 2), what fraction of the 450 and 576 candidate proteins were identified in ≥2 of 3 replicates within each condition, and what was the overlap between the two time windows (15 min + 1 h + 4 h) after Shh? The current intersection of 112 proteins mixes all three time points; the design would be more informative disambiguated.
- How many independent Git1-null clones (biallelic edits) were used for the key experiments in Figs. 4D–E, 5C–E, and 6A–F? The text says "two cell clones" were selected but does not state whether both were tested in each figure or only one representative clone is shown.
