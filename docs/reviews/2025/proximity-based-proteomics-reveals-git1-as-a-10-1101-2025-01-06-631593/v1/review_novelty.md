# Novelty & Contribution Reviewer

## Summary
The two core contributions — a time-resolved Smo-TurboID proteomic resource and the identification of Git1 as a regulator of Grk2-dependent Smo phosphorylation in the cilium — are genuinely new. My searches (Semantic Scholar, bioRxiv/medRxiv preprints) found no prior work reporting Git1 involvement in Hedgehog signaling, Smo phosphorylation, or Grk2 ciliary translocation. The contribution is incremental rather than transformative: it adds a mechanistic step (Git1 scaffolds Grk2 at the ciliary base to enable its entry into the cilium) to a pathway whose key players (Grk2, Smo, PKA-C) were already known from the same group's earlier work. That is sound, useful science. The resource claim for the proteomic dataset is legitimate but would benefit from clearer differentiation from prior ciliary proximity-labeling datasets and from explicit deposition of the full dataset in a public repository.

## Weaknesses
- The central alternative explanation that Git1 might not directly regulate Smo phosphorylation but instead control a general property of the ciliary compartment is only partially excluded; a specific confound remains: Git1 could affect Grk2 stability, expression, or general kinase activity rather than its spatial localization. Grk2 basal body intensity data partially addresses stability but kinase activity is not assessed.
- The claim that Git1 controls Grk2 transport into the cilium (rather than its turnover/retention) is not fully secured: the 1.2-fold increase in WT is modest, and without time-course measurements at earlier points or a Grk2 photoconversion/live-imaging approach, entry failure cannot be differentiated from accelerated exit.
- The Smo-TurboID proteomic resource claim is weakened because the full dataset is only presented as supplementary tables without mention of deposition in a public repository (ProteomeXchange, MassIVE, etc.), and the closest prior work (May et al. 2021, JCB) performing time-resolved ciliary Hedgehog proteomics is not directly compared.
- Because only one candidate (Git1) from >500 identified proteins was functionally validated, the resource's utility remains prospective rather than demonstrated.
- The rescue experiment targeting Grk2 to the cilium in Git1 KO cells uses a DArl13b fusion that could cause protein overaccumulation — a WT Grk2 control at matching expression level is needed to confirm the rescue is specific.
- The manuscript uses the phrase 'majority of known Smo-associated proteins' without citing which specific known interactors were recovered and which were missed.
