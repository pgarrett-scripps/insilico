## Concessions
- **data_analysis** — Multiple-comparison correction is not disclosed or applied for the RAAS dotplot comparisons in Fig. 4b,c and Fig. 5c, which involve hundreds of substitution-type and domain-level t-tests underlying the paper's specificity claims.
  - Proposed change: We will re-run all RAAS dotplot analyses (Fig. 4b,c, Fig. 5c, Extended Data Fig. 5) with Benjamini-Hochberg correction applied within each analysis family, explicitly report the family size (number of tests) in the Methods, and update all dot-plot significance cutoffs and p-value thresholds in the text and figure legends to reflect corrected q-values.
- **methodology** — The claim that protein stability causes high RAAS is stated with more causal confidence than the correlational SILAC data supports, given plausible detection/selection-bias and reverse-causality alternatives.
  - Proposed change: We will revise the Abstract, Results heading, and Discussion to describe stability as 'associated with' rather than 'contributing to' or 'driving' high RAAS, add an explicit paragraph in the Discussion enumerating selection-bias and reverse-causality alternatives, and note that establishing causation requires future perturbation experiments (e.g., synonymous codon engineering or targeted mutagenesis) that are beyond the scope of this dataset.
- **clarity** — The paper's central hypothesis (alternate translation produces abundant, stable proteoforms) is not explicitly stated before the Results section.
  - Proposed change: We will add a concluding paragraph to the Introduction that explicitly states the two central hypotheses being tested: (1) alternate translation generates proteins of unexpectedly high relative abundance in mammalian tissues, and (2) this abundance is partly explained by proteoform-specific differences in degradation rate.
- **clarity** — The term 'alternate translation' is used throughout without a single formal definition unifying the underlying mechanisms.
  - Proposed change: We will add one sentence early in the Introduction explicitly defining 'alternate translation' as 'incorporation of an amino acid at a codon position that deviates from the standard genetic code, arising from mechanisms including codon-anticodon mismatch, tRNA mischarging, or mRNA/tRNA modification-driven recoding' and cross-reference this definition where the term first appears in Results.
- **data_analysis** — The XGBoost model (Fig. 5i) does not report confidence intervals across retrainings, whether hyperparameters were tuned on the test set, or a null-model baseline for the reported r=0.44.
  - Proposed change: We will report the full distribution (median, IQR, 95% CI) of test-set Pearson correlations across the 1000 retrainings, confirm and describe that hyperparameter tuning used only training-fold cross-validation (not the held-out test set), and add a null baseline (predicting the global median RAAS) for comparison in the Fig. 5i legend and Methods.
- **data_analysis** — Effect sizes (e.g., η² for ANOVA, Spearman ρ for the gnomAD constraint analysis, sample sizes for degradation-rate correlations) are not reported alongside p-values.
  - Proposed change: We will add η² with 95% CI for each ANOVA factor in Fig. 4f and Fig. 5m, report Spearman ρ with 95% CI and codon counts per RAAS quartile for Fig. 5k, and state n (number of SAAP/proteins) for each degradation-rate correlation in Fig. 3d and Extended Data Fig. 3m,n.
- **literature** — The assertion that 'mutations in the genomes of LSCC patients did not correspond to any of the identified AAS' is unsupported by shown data or methodological detail.
  - Proposed change: We will add a supplemental table reporting the variant-calling pipeline, sample size, and matching procedure used for the LSCC genomic comparison, and cite it explicitly at the point this claim is made in Results.
- **literature** — Pataskar et al. (2022) on stress-induced Trp-to-Phe substitutions is cited only in passing rather than engaged with as a competing mechanistic explanation for high-abundance substitutions.
  - Proposed change: We will add a paragraph to the Discussion explicitly comparing our stability-based mechanism to the starvation/stress-induced substitution mechanism reported by Pataskar et al. and Yang et al., noting where our degradation-rate data can or cannot distinguish between these mechanisms.
- **methodology** — The relationship between the 8,801 total substitution sites, the 2,000 high-confidence sites, and the 360 proteins with RAAS>1 is not clearly explained, and it is unclear how many proteins retain median RAAS>1 using only high-confidence sites.
  - Proposed change: We will add a table (or panel) explicitly reporting the number of proteins with median RAAS>1, RAAS>0.5, and RAAS>0.1, separately computed for the full 8,801-site set and the 2,000 high-confidence subset, so readers can assess sensitivity of the headline count to confidence threshold.
- **reproducibility** — The histone-ruler assumption of 30x10^6 histone copies per cell is used without justification or sensitivity analysis.
  - Proposed change: We will cite the original justification for this value from Wiśniewski et al. and add a sensitivity analysis in Extended Data showing how estimated SAAP copy numbers scale under a plausible range of histone copy-number assumptions (e.g., ±50%).

## Disagreements
- **data_analysis** — The tissue-enrichment analysis (Extended Data Fig. 5h,i) applies FDR correction without disclosing family size, suggesting the correction may be inadequate given ~1,000 implicit tests.
  - Response: Correction was in fact applied to this specific analysis, contrary to the suggestion that the reported thresholds are 'uncorrected-looking'; the family size is well defined (255 substitution types x tissues) and we will disclose it explicitly, but the analytical step itself is not missing.
  - Manuscript reference: Methods, 'Substitution enrichment in tissue types': 'p-values were adjusted with a Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction to get q-values... Substitution types were considered significantly enriched with high or low RAAS values in a given tissue if the log2(fold change) relative to all other tissues was >1 or <-1, respectively, with a q-value of ≤0.01.'
- **novelty** — The absence of a variant in gnomAD does not prove a substitution is not genetic, and the LSCC check covers only one cohort, so the non-genetic origin of substitutions is not established.
  - Response: We agree gnomAD absence alone cannot prove non-genetic origin for any single site, which is why we did not rely on gnomAD alone: the non-genetic-origin argument rests on the convergence of five independent lines of evidence (patient-matched transcriptome-derived protein databases already incorporating each sample's own RNA-level SNVs/indels via customProDB, blast filtering against six-frame translations, low mass errors, retention-time concordance, and the LSCC genomic check), not on population allele frequency alone. We will still add the LSCC methodological detail as conceded above, but we do not agree the underlying inference is unsupported.
  - Manuscript reference: Results, 'Systematic identification and validation of amino acid substitutions': patient-specific databases were generated by 'SNV and indel calling' with freebayes and customProDB and merged with the de novo transcriptome database prior to MS search, so any genetically encoded substitution present in a given patient's RNA would already appear as a standard (non-substituted) database match rather than a SAAP.
- **rigor** — Fifty-five shared SAAP between human and mouse out of 8,801 human sites (~0.6% overlap) is too small a fraction to support the claim of cross-species conservation.
  - Response: We do not claim broad conservation of all 8,801 sites; we explicitly frame the 55-site overlap as a lower bound limited by peptide detectability and unaccounted sequence-homology differences, and the conservation claim rests specifically on the significant correlation of RAAS values within this matched subset, not on the raw overlap fraction.
  - Manuscript reference: Results, 'Conservation across species': 'This intersection demonstrates conservation, though it likely underestimates the degree of conservation since (1) we did not account for protein sequence homology differences and (2) technical factors affect peptide detectability... Their significantly high correlation (p<10^-8) across human and mouse strongly support species conservation.'
- **methodology** — The shared-peptide degradation-rate comparison (Fig. 3e-f) does not escape the selection-bias critique because only SAAP that already passed detection and stringent filtering enter the analysis.
  - Response: We agree the shared-peptide design does not eliminate upstream detectability selection at the level of which SAAP-BP pairs enter our dataset at all, and we will state this limitation explicitly in the Discussion; however, we disagree this makes the comparison uninformative: conditional on a SAAP being detected, the shared-peptide analysis tests an independent prediction (that shared-peptide turnover tracks SAAP turnover specifically at high RAAS and BP turnover at low RAAS) that is not guaranteed by the detection threshold alone and could have come out either way.
  - Manuscript reference: Results, 'Increased protein stability contributes to substitution abundance': 'On average shared peptide degradation rates for proteins with lowly abundant substituted proteoforms are more similar to the degradation rate of the encoded peptide... For proteins with highly abundant substituted proteoforms, the shared peptides degradation rates are more similar to the degradation rate for the substituted proteoform... The degradation rate ratio difference between the two RAAS bins is statistically significant for both substituted (p=0.00015) and base peptides (p=0.0098).'
