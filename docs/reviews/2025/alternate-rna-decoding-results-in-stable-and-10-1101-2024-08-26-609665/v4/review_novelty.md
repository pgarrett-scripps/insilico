# Novelty & Contribution Reviewer

## Summary
This is solid, well-executed work that makes a real contribution to understanding alternate translation in mammals. The core finding—that alternate translation produces abundant, stable, conserved proteoforms in healthy and diseased tissues—is novel and significant relative to prior work in microorganisms. The validation pipeline is rigorous and the scope is impressive. However, three load-bearing claims rest on inferences that are consistent with the data but not uniquely established by it: (1) that high-RAAS substitutions are the dominant proteoform in cells (not just abundant in a subset), (2) that observed substitutions are non-genetic (not fully ruled out for individual samples), and (3) that codon frequency and tRNA pairing are the primary mechanisms (not definitively distinguished from sense codon recoding). These are not fatal flaws—the evidence is strong enough to support the conclusions as stated—but they define the limits of what the data actually show. The manuscript would be strengthened by addressing these gaps, particularly through single-cell data or functional perturbations, but the work is publishable as is with appropriate framing of uncertainty. The contribution is incremental in the sense that it extends known phenomena (translational errors, sense codon recoding) to a new context (abundant mammalian proteoforms), but it is significant because it demonstrates that alternate translation is not merely noise but a source of functional protein diversity.

## Strengths
- Rigorous validation pipeline: The authors apply multiple independent lines of evidence to establish confidence in SAAP identification—database validation, fragment ion localization, deep learning rescoring, multi-digest reproducibility, IP-MS confirmation, and PepQuery validation—substantially reducing false positive risk compared to prior work on substitutions in microorganisms.
- Direct measurement of protein stability: Use of metabolic pulse-labeling with stable isotopes to measure degradation rates of substituted versus canonical peptides (Fig. 3c–f) provides mechanistic evidence that high RAAS correlates with reduced proteoform degradation, moving beyond abundance ratios alone to address causation.
- Comprehensive scope and reproducibility: Analysis of >1,000 human samples across 6 cancer types and 26 healthy tissues, plus mouse validation, with publicly available code and data, enables verification and extension; the finding of conserved SAAP between human and mouse (Fig. 5l–m) is a non-trivial cross-species check on biological relevance.

## Weaknesses
- The shared peptide test (Fig. 2d–e) does not definitively exclude the possibility that high-RAAS sites reflect a mixture of two proteoforms in which the substituted form is simply more abundant in a subset of cells or tissues, rather than the dominant form in most cells, and the histone ruler estimate (Fig. 2i) does not resolve whether high-RAAS proteoforms are the majority form in a single cell or a minority form present in many cells.
- The absence of a variant in gnomAD does not prove a substitution is not genetic, only that it is rare or absent in the sequenced population, and the LSCC genome check covers only one cancer type and one patient cohort rather than a genome-wide check across all 1,000+ samples.
- The manuscript does not report whether identified SAAP correspond to patient-matched genomic variants called from the same RNA-seq or WGS data, which would be a more direct test of genetic origin than gnomAD comparisons.
- Codon frequency and tRNA pairing associations remain correlative; an alternative explanation is that sense codon recoding (via tRNA or mRNA modifications) preferentially occurs at rare codons, and the overlap between U modifications and substitution sites is modest (180 of 4,250 U in AAS codons) with unclear causal direction.
- The manuscript would be stronger if it provided direct evidence that blocking or altering tRNA modifications reduces RAAS at specific sites, or that introducing modifications increases RAAS, rather than relying on correlative evidence.
- The biological significance of most identified substitutions remains unclear—many have low RAAS and may be inconsequential, and the 360 proteins with RAAS > 1 represent a small fraction of the proteome with no estimate of what fraction of total protein mass or function is affected.
- Functional claims are associative, not causal: enrichment of high-RAAS substitutions in signaling, proteolysis, and transcription (Fig. 5c) is suggestive but does not show that these substitutions alter function.
- Tissue specificity is demonstrated but not mechanistically explained—the manuscript does not explain why some substitutions are tissue-specific (Extended Data Fig. 5h–i), whether due to differential tRNA expression, tissue-specific modifications, or cell-type-specific translation rates.
- Cancer associations are weak: while some substitutions show higher RAAS in tumors (Fig. 5a–b), the overall signal is not strong (Extended Data Fig. 6a–b), and no causal link to tumorigenesis is demonstrated.
- Cross-species conservation is limited in scope: only 55 of 1,102 mouse substitutions match human sequences exactly, and quantitative conservation analysis (e.g., homology-based matching) is not provided.
- The KRAQ motif and other sequence patterns lack functional validation, and the authors note they 'cannot confidently rule out the possibility of multiple modifications' for these sites, which substantially weakens the claim.
- Missing data bias is acknowledged but not fully addressed: low-RAAS substitutions on low-abundance proteins are less likely to be detected, potentially inflating median RAAS estimates for such proteins.

## Questions
- Did you check whether any identified SAAP correspond to patient-matched genomic variants (SNVs/indels) called from the same RNA-seq data? If so, what fraction matched, and if not, why was this check not performed?
- For the 360 proteins with RAAS > 1, what is the estimated fraction of total protein copies that are substituted versus canonical in a typical cell? The histone ruler estimate gives absolute copy numbers but not the ratio within each protein.
- Can you provide quantitative evidence that sense codon recoding (via tRNA or mRNA modifications) contributes more to high-RAAS substitutions than codon misreading? The current evidence is correlative; a functional perturbation (e.g., blocking pseudouridylation) would be more convincing.
