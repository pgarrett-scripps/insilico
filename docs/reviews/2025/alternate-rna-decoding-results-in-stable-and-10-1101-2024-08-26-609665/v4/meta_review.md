# Meta-Review

**Draft recommendation:** major

## Synthesis
The panel largely converges on this being a substantial, well-validated proteomics resource: the six-pronged peptide-identity validation strategy (ionization efficiency, detectability, multi-digest reproducibility, shared-peptide triangulation, retention-time prediction, degradation rates) is independently praised by clarity, methodology, novelty, reproducibility, and rigor reviewers as substantially reducing false-positive risk. Ethics is a clean pass (5/5). The genuine tension is between accepting the peptide-identity validation as sound (which the skeptic conceded) and whether the paper's headline mechanistic/specificity claims are adequately supported. Three independent reviewers (methodology, reproducibility, rigor) converge on the same unresolved confound: the correlation between low degradation rate and high RAAS (Fig. 3d-f) is consistent with stability causing abundance, but equally consistent with a detection/selection artifact (only stable-enough substitutions are observable at all), and the advocate's rebuttal citing the shared-peptide comparison only 'partially neutralizes' this, as conceded in debate round 2 — the upstream filtering-based selection bias is not addressed by that design. Separately, data_analysis and reproducibility independently identify that Fig. 4b/c and Fig. 5c RAAS dotplots appear to use raw uncorrected t-tests despite implying hundreds of comparisons, which the debate established is distinct from the (correctly BH-corrected) tissue-enrichment analysis — this bears directly on the Abstract's claim of 'strong tissue-type and cancer specificity,' a claim central to the paper's positioning. Literature flags an unverifiable assertion (LSCC genome check) and thin engagement with a directly competing mechanistic paper (Pataskar et al.). Clarity and novelty reviewers, while flagging real gaps (undefined central claim placement, protein-level RAAS>1 count ambiguity), still rate the work favorably and describe it as essentially sound with framing fixes.

## Decisive Factors
No reviewer recommended rejection, and the confidence-weighted average (3.56/5, mode 'major') supports a major-revision verdict rather than anything harsher; I am not diverging downward from the panel. I set the recommendation at 'major' rather than 'minor' because the debate sharpened two problems into revision-item status rather than dissolving them: (1) the stability-causes-abundance claim rests on a confound flagged independently by three specialists and only partially addressed by the advocate, so the causal language in the Abstract/Discussion needs to be substantively softened, not just clarified; (2) the uncorrected/underdisclosed multiple-comparison issue in Fig. 4b,c and Fig. 5c is not the same finding as the (properly corrected) tissue-enrichment analysis, and the debate established the advocate's rebuttal did not actually resolve it — this threatens the tissue/cancer-specificity claims marketed in the Abstract and requires either re-running with correction or narrowing the claim to descriptive/exploratory status. These are consistent with the panel's own median verdict of 'major' and are revision-tractable (redo/report corrected statistics, add effect sizes and CIs, soften causal framing, verify LSCC claim with data, clarify the 360-protein/protein-level RAAS calculation), not grounds for rejection given the strength of the underlying peptide-identification validation that survived debate scrutiny.
