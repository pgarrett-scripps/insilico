# Ethics & Compliance Reviewer

## Summary
This manuscript presents a comprehensive proteomics study identifying thousands of amino acid substitutions arising from alternate translation in human and mouse tissues. The ethics and compliance framework is sound: human tissue samples come from established public repositories (CPTAC, label-free datasets) with appropriate institutional oversight; no new human subjects research, clinical trials, or animal experiments were conducted; funding sources and competing interests are fully disclosed. The work is purely computational analysis of existing public proteomics and transcriptomics data, placing it squarely in the exempt category.

## Strengths
- Funding sources explicitly listed (NIH grants R01GM144967, R35GM148218, R01AG092460, UG3CA268117, UH3CA268117, and Paul G. Allen Frontiers Group award) and competing interests declared (N.S. is founding director/CEO of non-profit research institute).
