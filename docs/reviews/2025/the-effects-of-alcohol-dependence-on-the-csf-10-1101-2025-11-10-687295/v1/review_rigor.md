# Rigor & Overclaiming Reviewer

## Summary
This is a hypothesis-generating discovery study with an honest limitations section and generally well-hedged body text, but the headline claims outrun the evidence in three specific ways. The central answer to the paper's own question — that the two states are "dichotomous" — is not licensed by a presence/absence analysis performed at the detection limit, which cannot distinguish qualitative from quantitative differences. The temporal language ("transition," "sustained," "beginnings") is not supported by a single-time-point, cross-sectional design. And the title's BBB dysfunction claim, while defensible, rests on protein-presence inferences with unexcluded alternatives. The dataset is potentially valuable; the framing needs substantial requalification.

## Weaknesses
- HARD — The "dichotomous" claim is not licensed by detection-threshold analysis. Abstract: "alcohol exposure and alcohol dependence are dichotomous." The evidence is 140 proteins detected in ≥2/4 Dep and ≤1/5 Non-dep animals (and 67 the reverse), plus PCA clustering. But presence/absence calls are made at the limit of detection: a protein moderately more abundant in Dep mice but present in both groups is scored "Dep-specific" if its Non-dep levels fall below the threshold. The authors acknowledge this ("missing values are likely to represent protein levels below the lower limit of detection") and that the lists are "minimum estimates," then build the dichotomy narrative on exactly these calls. The alternative — a quantitative continuum (e.g., graded BBB permeability) producing apparent qualitative differences through detection thresholds — is not excluded. The authors hold quantitative data (diann_maxlfq) and could test whether the "group-specific" proteins are genuinely bimodal in abundance; they do not. The PCA also shows one Non-dep mouse clustering within the Dep group, acknowledged but never reconciled with the dichotomy claim. To license "dichotomous," the authors need to show the quantitative distributions are bimodal, not continuous, and account for the overlapping animal.
- HARD — Temporal claims outrun the cross-sectional design. Abstract: "a clear molecular transition from maintained protective mechanisms to vascular damage, BBB breakdown, and sustained neuroinflammation"; Conclusions: "during the transition to dependence"; abstract: "the beginnings of endothelial damage." CSF was collected once per animal. A two-group, single-time-point comparison cannot establish a "transition" (which requires observing change over time) or a "sustained" state (which requires at least two time points). The design supports "differences between dependent and non-dependent mice consistent with…" — not "transition" or "sustained." This is a wording fix, but it must be applied to the abstract and conclusions, where the temporal language currently carries the paper's interpretive weight.
- SOFT — BBB dysfunction and complement activation interpretations have unexcluded alternatives. Title: "Evidence for Blood-Brain Barrier Dysfunction." The Dep-specific detection of MMP2, VE-cadherin, VCAM1, ACTA, LAMB2, C1QA and C6 is interpreted as BBB breakdown and complement activation. Two alternatives are not excluded: (a) serum leakage — C6 is primarily liver-derived and abundant in serum; its presence in CSF is more parsimoniously explained by barrier leakage than by local complement activation, which would undercut the "complement system activation" signature; (b) local production — MMP2 and GFAP are produced by brain-resident cells, so their presence in CSF does not require barrier failure. The body hedges ("suggestive of vascular compromise"), but the title asserts the dysfunction claim without the hedge. A panel of abundant serum proteins not expected in CSF, measured across both groups, would help distinguish barrier leakage from local production.
- SOFT — "Loss of protective mechanisms" is inferred from non-detection. Conclusions: "protective mechanisms… are lost or diminished during the transition to dependence." Non-detection in Dep mice means below the detection limit, not absent; the same threshold artifact that makes the Dep list a minimum estimate applies symmetrically to the Non-dep list. Hedge to "consistent with reduced abundance of proteins associated with…"
- "Unique to alcohol dependence" (Introduction) overstates: there is no alcohol-naïve control group, and the CIE vapor/withdrawal procedure is confounded with the dependent phenotype; the data support "unique to the dependent group in this model."
- The anti-IL-6R antibody (administered to all animals) is disclosed in Limitations but absent from the title/abstract framing; if the antibody acts group-specifically, the headline "effects of Alcohol Dependence" could be partly antibody-dependent — flag for the methodology reviewer.
- The interpretation of Non-dep-specific immunoglobulin variable regions as preserved "immune surveillance" sits awkwardly with the BBB narrative: a leakier barrier in Dep mice would predict more, not fewer, Ig-derived peptides in Dep CSF; the authors do not address whether these peptides track the contamination markers (keratins, hemoglobin) they discuss elsewhere.
- The "novel" claim in the abstract is a priority claim outside my remit — flag for the novelty reviewer.
