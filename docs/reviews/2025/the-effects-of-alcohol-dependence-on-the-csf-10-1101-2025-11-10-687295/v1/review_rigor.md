# Rigor & Overclaiming Reviewer

## Summary
This is an honestly framed preliminary discovery study whose detection-based proteomic comparison of CSF from dependent vs. non-dependent mice is a reasonable starting point, but whose interpretive scaffolding — "dichotomous" states, BBB disruption, loss of immune surveillance — consistently outruns what detection patterns in n=4–5 mice can establish. The two most serious problems are a design confound that prevents causal attribution to "dependence" specifically, and an immunoglobulin interpretation that is contradicted by the authors' own finding of the injected anti-IL-6R antibody in every sample. The core descriptive result (different protein detection patterns between groups) survives; the mechanistic narrative built on it needs substantial requalification.

## Strengths
- 1. The study is candid about its limits: power analysis, the "minimum estimates" caveat, and the keratin/hemoglobin contamination question are all addressed directly rather than buried.
- 2. Anchoring the neuroinflammation interpretation to clinically validated biomarkers (GFAP, UCHL1, CHI3L1) gives the reader a concrete reference point.
- 3. The DIA-MS choice with an explicit rationale for detection-based analysis is methodologically considered, and the data appear deposited.

## Weaknesses
- 1. HARD — The causal attribution to "dependence" is confounded by the vapor procedure. The two groups differ in more than dependence status: Dep mice received ethanol vapor, ethanol+pyrazole injections, and repeated withdrawal cycles; Non-dep received only pyrazole and 2BC. The abstract's "dichotomous" framing and the conclusion's "transition to alcohol dependence" attribute the proteomic differences to the dependence state, but a design comparing vapor-exposed vs. non-vapor-exposed mice cannot isolate dependence from the vapor/withdrawal procedure itself. The alternative — that the differences reflect repeated ethanol exposure, withdrawal stress, or the injection regimen — is equally consistent with the data. This is partly methodology's call, but it bears directly on claim wording: "dependence-associated" is licensed; "dependence causes" or "transition to dependence" is not.
- 2. HARD — The immunoglobulin variable regions interpreted as "maintained adaptive immune surveillance" are confounded by the injected antibody. The authors themselves detected the rat anti-IL-6R antibody (IGG2B, peptide DILLISQNAK) in all CSF samples. The presence of immunoglobulin-derived peptides in CSF is therefore not a clean readout of endogenous adaptive immunity — it is entangled with the administered antibody and with BBB permeability state. Interpreting KV5AB/LV1C/HVM02/03 detection in Non-dep as evidence of intact immune surveillance, and their absence in Dep as "loss" of it, is not licensed. Differential antibody passage, clearance, or binding across groups would produce the same pattern. The authors flag the antibody's presence in a limitation but do not connect it to the immune-surveillance claim they make in the Abstract and Discussion.
- 3. SOFT — BBB disruption is inferred from protein identity, not measured. MMP2, BIP, VE-cadherin, and VCAM1 in CSF are consistent with barrier compromise, but no BBB permeability assay, tight junction quantification, or functional test is performed. MMP2 also participates in normal ECM turnover, and VE-cadherin could reflect endothelial shedding without frank barrier failure. The Abstract's "signatures of" is defensible; the Discussion's "BBB compromise" and "BBB dysfunction" are not. A functional assay (Evans blue or dextran permeability) or tight junction protein measurement would distinguish barrier breakdown from other release mechanisms.

## Questions
- 1. Was there any cohort that did not receive anti-IL-6R whose CSF could be compared, to test whether the immunoglobulin signal and any group differences depend on the antibody?
- 2. Can the authors report the detection analysis restricted to proteins whose absence in the comparison group is not attributable to the injected antibody or to detection sensitivity limits?
- 3. What functional evidence (BBB permeability assay, tight junction staining) could be added to support the BBB disruption interpretation, and would the authors consider softening the Discussion's "BBB breakdown" language until such evidence exists?
