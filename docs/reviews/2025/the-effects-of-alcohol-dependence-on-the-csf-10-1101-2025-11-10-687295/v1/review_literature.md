# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The citation record is largely sound — I verified several load-bearing attributions (Prystupa 2015 for serum MMP-2 in alcoholic liver disease; Carrino 2021 for the RBE4/BIP in-vitro BBB study; Lopez & Becker 2014 for the dependence model) and found no fabricated references. The paper's central interpretation, however, rests on one citation-based defense I could not verify, and one attribution appears mismatched to its source. These are confirmable rather than fatal, so minor revision.

## Strengths
- The key human-correlate claims (MMP-2 in alcoholic liver disease, BIP in the RBE4 BBB model) resolve to real, on-point primary sources that I verified.
- The authors cite the methodological comparator (Lourbopoulos 2025) transparently and give a depository identifier for the contested keratin/Hb identifications, which is exactly the candour an overlay review should reward.
- Self-citation (Patel, Warden, Roberts, Borgonetti, Turner, Hashimoto) is proportionate to the model-development lineage and not padding.

## Weaknesses
- The contamination defense rests on a citation I could not verify. The Limitations section rebuts the blood-contamination alternative to the Dep-specific protein list by citing Lourbopoulos 2025 and its ProteomeXchange dataset PXD053568 as containing Hb and keratin identifications despite undetectable Hb by ELISA. This is the entire citation-based answer to the alternative that the Dep-specific proteins (MMP2, complement C1QA, immunoglobulins, GFAP, hemoglobin — all abundant blood proteins) reflect blood contamination rather than BBB breakdown. I could not confirm the dataset identifier or that it contains those identifications. If the attribution is wrong, the central interpretation loses its only external support. The authors should confirm the identifier and the specific claim.
- Ref 32 (Gaither 2025) appears mismatched to the claim it supports. The text states "increased BIP levels were found to be positively correlated with alcohol consumption grade and reflected the degree of alcohol-induced damage in human liver biopsy samples 32," but the cited paper's title concerns the ensemble of drug-metabolizing enzymes and transporters in human liver. Nothing in the title or metadata indicates it reports BIP/GRP78 correlation with consumption grade. Either the claim or the citation is wrong; the authors should confirm which.
- The central claim — that Dep-specific proteins indicate BBB breakdown and neuroinflammation — is not distinguished from the contamination alternative by the evidence presented, and the only citation-based rebuttal is the unverifiable Lourbopoulos claim above. The design aspects (sample-purity controls, blood-marker quantitation) belong to the methods reviewer; from the citation record alone, the rebuttal is a single unverified source.
