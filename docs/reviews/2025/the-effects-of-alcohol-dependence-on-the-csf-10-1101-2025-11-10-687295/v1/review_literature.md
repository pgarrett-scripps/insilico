# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The citation record is largely sound and current, with the CIE-2BC model, the CSF-biomarker literature, and the authors' own prior work properly anchored, but two load-bearing attributions need attention: the BIP-in-liver claim appears to rest on a source whose scope does not obviously support it, and the MMP2-cirrhosis claim silently ignores a directly contradicting published finding. The contamination defense via the Lourbopoulos citation is used in a way that does not actually exclude the blood-contamination alternative. These are fixable in revision and do not undermine the honest, preliminary framing.

## Weaknesses
- The central claim that Dep-specific proteins reflect CNS pathology rather than blood contamination is not established by the evidence offered. The manuscript acknowledges hemoglobin and multiple keratins in the Dep group and defends their presence via Lourbopoulos, arguing that these appear even in clean CSF. But the alternative explanation — that the Dep CSF samples were more blood-contaminated than the Non-dep samples, whether from a genuinely leakier BBB or from collection — predicts exactly the same protein pattern, since MMP2, BIP, complement components, and immunoglobulins are all blood-abundant. The Lourbopoulos citation shows these proteins are not reliable contamination indicators, but it does not establish that the Dep-specific proteins are CNS-derived. What would distinguish the two accounts: report a per-sample blood-contamination marker (e.g., quantitative hemoglobin or a blood/CNS abundance ratio) and show the Dep-specific proteins do not track it. This is a HARD gap in the evidence chain for the paper's headline claim.
- The BIP-in-liver attribution is questionable. The manuscript states "increased BIP levels were found to be positively correlated with alcohol consumption grade and reflected the degree of alcohol-induced damage in human liver biopsy samples," citing Gaither et al. 2025 (J Xenobiot). The cited paper's stated scope — "Effects of Chronic Alcohol Intake on the Composition of the Ensemble of Drug-Metabolizing Enzymes and Transporters in the Human Liver" — does not obviously support a claim about BIP/GRP78 correlation with consumption grade. I could not verify this from the abstract/metadata. Please confirm the specific claim is actually made in that source, or cite the primary source that established it.
- A directly contradicting prior finding on MMP2 and cirrhosis is silently omitted. The manuscript cites Prystupa 2015 (verified: PMID 26094532) for "elevated levels of MMP2... correlate with the development of liver cirrhosis," but the literature also contains Madro et al. 2012, "The decrease of serum MMP-2 activity corresponds to alcoholic cirrhosis stage" (PMID 21908153), which reports the opposite direction of association. A contradicting finding bearing directly on the translational bridge claim must be acknowledged, not ignored. This is a HARD completeness issue.
- The reference list as parsed contains several entries that run together (entries 10–15, 16–17, 31–32, 46–47, 61–62) — likely a PDF-conversion artifact, but please confirm the final bibliography is clean and resolvable.
