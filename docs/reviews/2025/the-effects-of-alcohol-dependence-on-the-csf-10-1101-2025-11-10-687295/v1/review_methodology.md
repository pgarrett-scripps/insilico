# Methodology Reviewer

## Summary
The design is a standard CIE-2BC comparison, and the proteomics are honestly reported with acknowledged power limitations. However, the central claims — that the proteomic differences reflect dependence specifically (not higher ethanol exposure), and that vascular protein detection in CSF indicates BBB breakdown — are not fully earned by the design. The presence/absence analysis with n=4–5, the absence of a BBB-integrity control, and the interpretive leap from detection to "protective mechanisms" all outrun the evidence. The manuscript's own hedging in the abstract does not carry through to the discussion, where 140 "Dep-specific" proteins are interpreted mechanistically.

## Strengths
- The power analysis is candid and correctly identifies the study as underpowered for moderate effect sizes, with the protein lists appropriately framed as minimum estimates.
- The CIE-2BC model is the field-standard dependence paradigm, and the behavioral escalation criterion grounds the Dep/Non-dep distinction.
- The search for the IL-6R antibody in CSF is a thoughtful internal check on the MS pipeline's sensitivity.

## Weaknesses
- Dose and dependence are confounded; the qualitative-transition claim is not testable with this design (HARD). The Dep group differs from Non-dep in three variables simultaneously: total ethanol dose, route of administration (vapor + 1.75 g/kg i.p. injection vs oral 2BC), and the dependence phenotype. The manuscript frames its central question as whether dependence is a 'quantitative progression' or involves 'qualitatively distinct pathophysiological mechanisms,' but this design cannot separate those — the Dep group has both higher exposure and dependence. Any proteomic difference could reflect cumulative ethanol dose rather than the dependent state. A matched-exposure control (e.g., vapor-exposed mice that do not escalate drinking, or a group yoked for blood ethanol levels) would be required to attribute the signature to dependence per se. As written, the conclusion that the proteome reflects 'the transition to alcohol dependence' is not earned.
- The BBB-disruption interpretation lacks a positive control for barrier integrity (HARD). Detection of MMP2, VE-cadherin, ACTA, and LAMB2 in Dep CSF is interpreted as BBB breakdown. Two alternative explanations are not excluded. First, blood contamination during cisterna magna collection: the manuscript's own data show hemoglobin and multiple keratins in the Dep-specific list, and the argument that 'MS detects trace amounts below ELISA limits' cuts both ways — trace blood contamination would produce exactly this pattern. Second, the anti-IL-6R antibody (150 kDa, administered i.p. to all mice) could itself alter BBB permeability or CSF composition; the finding that the antibody is present in CSF is evidence of permeability to large molecules that is not integrated into the BBB interpretation. A blood-borne tracer excluded from CSF in intact-barrier conditions (e.g., Evans blue, or a quantitative hemoglobin/albumin ratio) would distinguish true barrier breakdown from collection artefact or treatment effect. Without it, 'BBB disruption' is an interpretation, not a finding.
- The 'protective mechanisms' interpretation of Non-dep-specific proteins outruns the evidence (SOFT–HARD). CALB1, SUMO2/3, and TAGL3 detected in Non-dep CSF are interpreted as 'maintained protective mechanisms' lost in dependence. These are presence/absence differences with no functional validation; the alternative — that these proteins are simply below the detection limit in Dep CSF, or present at different abundance for reasons unrelated to protection — is not addressed. The functional labels (calcium homeostasis, neuroprotection) are imported from the literature and applied to detection patterns. This is a reasonable hypothesis-generating observation, but the discussion presents it as a mechanism.
- The analysis is entirely presence/absence; no quantitative comparison of shared proteins is reported, which would substantially strengthen (or weaken) every claim.
- Sex is a potential confound that cannot be examined at n=4–5; the PCA shows Non-dep mice segregating by sex along PC2, which is acknowledged but not addressed.
- All mice received anti-IL-6R antibody; the study cannot separate dependence effects from dependence-plus-IL6R-blockade effects, and this is underweighted relative to its importance.
- The 'preferred' threshold (≥2/4 vs ≤1/5) is permissive, and no statistical test or multiple-testing correction is applied to detection-rate differences between groups.
- The detection of the IL-6R antibody in CSF is presented as a methodological validation but is equally evidence relevant to BBB permeability; this internal inconsistency is not reconciled.

## Questions
- Was any independent measure of BBB integrity collected (e.g., Evans blue extravasation, quantitative hemoglobin or albumin ratio in CSF vs serum)?
- Were CSF samples screened for blood contamination (e.g., hemoglobin ELISA) prior to MS, and if so, what were the results?
- Does the cohort include any vapor-exposed animals that did not escalate drinking, which could serve as a matched-exposure control?
