# Methodology Reviewer

## Summary
This is a preliminary discovery study using DIA-MS to compare CSF proteomes of alcohol-dependent vs non-dependent mice, with the authors transparently acknowledging the small sample size and limited power. The core approach is sound and the data deposition is a strength. However, three design gaps undermine the strongest claims: all mice received an IL-6R antibody with no untreated control reported; blood contamination is not ruled out as a source of the Dep-specific protein pattern; and the binary detection analysis cannot support the 'dichotomous' claim of qualitatively distinct mechanisms. The findings are plausible and potentially valuable, but the conclusions need to be either re-scaled to what the design can support or backed by additional analyses.

## Strengths
- The DIA-MS approach with explicit detection thresholds is appropriate for a discovery study, and the authors' power analysis is honest about what n=4–5 can and cannot detect.
- The detection of the IL-6R antibody in CSF is a thoughtful control that demonstrates the antibody crosses the BBB and confirms the search pipeline can find it.
- The authors acknowledge the small sample size and limited power in the limitations section rather than overstating the certainty of their findings.

## Weaknesses
- The IL-6R antibody treatment is a confound for the generalizable claim. All mice in this study received the anti-IL-6R antibody (200 μg/day for 7 days); no untreated or control-antibody group is reported. The stated conclusion — 'the effects of alcohol dependence on the CSF proteome' — is therefore conditional on IL-6R blockade.
- Blood contamination is not ruled out as a source of the Dep-specific protein pattern; no hemoglobin or other blood-marker quantification is reported to exclude this alternative explanation.
- The binary detection analysis cannot support the 'dichotomous' claim of qualitatively distinct mechanisms; presence/absence calls at n=4–5 cannot distinguish a true qualitative difference from a quantitative one that fell below the detection threshold.

## Questions
- Was any untreated or control-antibody group run, and if so why is it not reported?
- Can the authors quantify hemoglobin or other blood markers to rule out blood contamination as the source of the Dep-specific pattern?
- How would the conclusions change if the binary detection analysis were replaced with quantitative abundance comparisons?
