# Novelty & Contribution Reviewer

## Summary
The core contribution — a CSF proteomic comparison between alcohol-dependent and non-dependent mice in the CIE-2BC model — is genuinely new: my searches (find_related_work and search_preprints across several query phrasings) found no prior report of CSF proteomics in this dependence model, and no preprint preempting it. The work is honestly framed as a preliminary discovery study, which is to its credit. However, the central interpretive claim — that dependence represents a qualitatively distinct molecular state rather than a quantitative progression — is a strong inference resting on a small-n, presence/absence design, and the contribution's significance is somewhat inflated relative to what the evidence can carry. The novelty is real but incremental; the framing needs to be pulled back to match the data.

## Strengths
- The core contribution — a CSF proteomic comparison between alcohol-dependent and non-dependent mice in the CIE-2BC model — is genuinely new: my searches (find_related_work and search_preprints across several query phrasings) found no prior report of CSF proteomics in this dependence model, and no preprint preempting it.
- The work is honestly framed as a preliminary discovery study, which is to its credit.
- The manuscript makes no explicit "first/novel" claim, which is honest.
- The authors' own related astrocyte transcriptomics work (Hashimoto et al. 2025) is cited and used to corroborate the neuroimmune finding — good practice, and the strongest part of the novelty positioning.

## Weaknesses
- The "dichotomous" transition claim (dependence = qualitatively distinct proteome): the manuscript's central framing — "alcohol exposure and alcohol dependence are dichotomous" — rests on the 140 Dep-specific vs 67 Non-dep-specific proteins. But with n=4–5 per group and a detection-based (present/absent) analysis, "not detected" is an abundance threshold, not an absence. A protein present in all 4 Dep mice and 0 of 5 Non-dep mice could be a true qualitative difference or simply a protein whose level in Non-dep CSF sits just below the DIA detection limit. The authors acknowledge this ("missing values are likely to represent protein levels below the lower limit of detection") but then build the entire dichotomous narrative on it. What would distinguish the two accounts: quantitative comparison of the shared 404 proteins between groups, and a demonstration that the "Dep-specific" proteins are genuinely absent rather than low-abundance in Non-dep CSF. As written, the qualitative/dichotomous claim outruns the evidence; the data support a graded difference with a high-abundance tail in the Dep group.
- The claim that BBB disruption and neuroinflammation are specific to dependence: the evidence is MMP2, GFAP, CHI3L1, CX3CL1 detected in Dep but not Non-dep. The alternative account: the Dep group received ethanol vapor exposure plus escalated drinking, while Non-dep received only moderate voluntary drinking — so the difference could be driven by total ethanol dose/exposure magnitude rather than by "dependence" as a distinct state. The two are confounded in this design. The manuscript does not offer any analysis separating dependence status from cumulative exposure (e.g., correlation of protein detection with blood ethanol levels or total intake). The claim that these signatures are dependence-specific rather than high-exposure-specific is therefore not established by the data presented. This is the differentiation issue at the heart of the contribution.
- The framing ("a fundamental question remains unanswered") implies priority that should be stated explicitly and checked against the human CSF-AUD literature (e.g., Azuar et al. 2021) and the recent mouse CSF method paper (Lourbopoulos et al. 2025), neither of which is cited for differentiation.
- The nearest competing work — human CSF biomarker studies in AUD — is not named or positioned against; a one-paragraph head-to-head would sharpen the contribution.
- The translational "so what" (MMP2/BIP as peripheral biomarkers, linking to human serum/liver studies) is asserted as a downstream use case the present study does not demonstrate; this is a SOFT inflation, not a HARD failure.
- The IL-6R antibody treatment is a confound for interpreting immune-related proteins (LAG3, immunoglobulin variable regions); the manuscript addresses it for the antibody itself but not for the possibility that IL-6R blockade attenuated the very neuroimmune signature being interpreted.

## Questions
- Can you state explicitly whether this is the first CSF proteomic comparison of dependent vs non-dependent mice in the CIE-2BC model, and cite the closest human CSF-AUD and mouse CSF-method papers for differentiation?
- What analysis would you offer to separate dependence status from cumulative ethanol exposure (e.g., correlating protein detection with BELs or total intake)?
- Is the IL-6R antibody treatment expected to attenuate the neuroimmune proteins you interpret (GFAP, CHI3L1, CX3CL1), and if so, does that strengthen or weaken your dependence-specificity claim?
