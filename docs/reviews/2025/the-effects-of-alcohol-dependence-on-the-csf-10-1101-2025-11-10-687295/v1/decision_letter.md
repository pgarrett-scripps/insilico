# Decision Letter

**Decision:** major

## Summary of Evaluation
The panel signal is clear: 6 major, 1 minor, 1 reject, with a confidence-weighted score of 3.03/5. The reviewers converge on a consistent diagnosis: this is a genuinely novel, honestly framed preliminary discovery study with an unusually well-specified MS pipeline and deposited data, but the headline claims outrun the evidence as submitted. The central 'dichotomous' claim rests on descriptive presence/absence detection counts (140 vs 67 proteins) with no stated error rate, no multiple-testing correction across ~600 proteins, and the authors' own post-hoc power analysis concedes only 38% power for the moderate effect sizes that dominate their lists. The 'protective mechanisms lost' and 'BBB dysfunction' claims are inferred from non-detection and protein presence respectively, with the blood-contamination alternative (which predicts exactly the Dep-specific pattern) not excluded. Temporal language ('transition', 'sustained', 'beginnings') is not licensed by a single-time-point cross-sectional design. The IL-6R antibody administered to all animals is a confound for the generalizable claim. I do not adopt the reject position: every load-bearing criticism has a concrete, in-scope remedy that the authors' own deposited quantitative data (diann_maxlfq, the 404 shared proteins) can supply, and the novelty is confirmed real. This is a major revision, not a rejection — the fixes require reanalysis and new analyses whose outcome could change a conclusion, which is the definition of major.

## Required Revisions
1. Replace the qualitative presence/absence differential analysis with a quantitative comparison of the shared proteins between groups (e.g., MaxLFQ intensities from diann_maxlfq), reporting a named statistical test, effect sizes, and multiple-testing correction across the ~600 proteins. Report the expected number of proteins that would pass the ≥2/4 vs ≤1/5 detection threshold by chance alone, and state the false-discovery rate of that threshold.
2. Test whether the 'group-specific' proteins are genuinely bimodal in abundance (quantitative distributions) rather than a continuous gradient that falls below the detection limit in one group; reconcile the one Non-dep animal that clusters within the Dep group in Figure 1c (state whether it was excluded and whether exclusion was pre-specified).
3. Rule out blood contamination as the source of the Dep-specific pattern: report a per-sample blood-marker quantification (e.g., quantitative hemoglobin or a blood/CNS abundance ratio) and show that the Dep-specific proteins (MMP2, BIP, complement components, immunoglobulins) do not track it. The Lourbopoulos citation alone does not exclude the contamination alternative.
4. Re-scale the temporal and dichotomous claims to what the cross-sectional design supports. Remove or requalify 'transition', 'sustained', 'beginnings', and 'dichotomous' from the abstract and conclusions; replace with 'differences between dependent and non-dependent mice consistent with...'. Hedge 'protective mechanisms lost' to 'consistent with reduced abundance of proteins associated with...' given non-detection is an abundance threshold, not absence.
5. Address the IL-6R antibody confound: state the hypothesis the antibody was testing (one sentence in Introduction or Methods), and discuss whether IL-6R blockade is expected to attenuate the neuroimmune proteins interpreted (GFAP, CHI3L1, CX3CL1, LAG3, immunoglobulin variable regions), and how that affects the dependence-specificity claim. State whether any untreated or control-antibody group was run.
6. Separate dependence status from cumulative ethanol exposure: provide an analysis (e.g., correlation of protein detection with blood ethanol levels or total intake) or explicitly acknowledge that dependence and exposure magnitude are confounded in this design and requalify the 'dependence-specific' claims accordingly.
7. Resolve the BIP-in-liver attribution: confirm the specific BIP/GRP78 claim is actually made in Gaither et al. 2025, or cite the primary source that established it. Acknowledge the directly contradicting MMP2 finding (Madro et al. 2012, PMID 21908153) reporting the opposite direction of association with cirrhosis stage.
8. Make the analysis code and data accessible: make the GitHub repository public or deposit an archived version (e.g., Zenodo DOI) with a stated commit/tag at the time of preprint posting, and confirm the MassIVE accession C5GX4573B is public and resolvable with stated access conditions.
9. Specify the power-analysis method (which test, what effect-size model, what assumptions produced 38% for 75% vs 20% detection) so it can be recomputed.
10. Add the missing methods details: animal age, randomization/blinding statement, injection vehicle for the i.p. treatments, mass-tolerance values for the DIA-NN search, and fix the broken reference numbering for the CIE-2BC and FASP protocol-provenance citations so the delegated protocols are resolvable.

## Minor Suggestions
- Fix the inconsistent labeling of the 140/67 counts: state in one sentence the exact threshold that produced them (unique vs preferred), and reconcile with the Venn diagram and Tables 1-2.
- State the sex counts per group (how many males/females in Non-dep vs Dep) so the PCA sex-segregation claim is checkable.
- Fix the garbled Table 1/Table 2 column header ('Evidence Detection Detection Strength') and the Figure 1 legend ambiguity.
- Add an explicit competing-interests statement (or state that none exist).
- Report quantitative comparison of the IGG2B/IL-6R antibody peptide across groups rather than presence alone, and temper the 'crosses the BBB' claim to what the abundance data support.
- State the STRING web-tool version and provide an environment capture (lockfile/conda env/container) for the R analysis.
- Confirm the final bibliography is clean and resolvable (several entries appear to run together in the parsed reference list).
- Position the contribution against the nearest human CSF-AUD biomarker studies (e.g., Azuar et al. 2021) and the mouse CSF method paper (Lourbopoulos et al. 2025) in one head-to-head paragraph.
