# Novelty & Contribution Reviewer

## Summary
This manuscript makes a novel and well-supported contribution by establishing fatty acid double-bond geometry as a previously unrecognized determinant of ferroptosis sensitivity. Through an unbiased screen and mechanistic follow-up, the authors demonstrate that trans-unsaturated fatty acids (linoelaidic acid and petroselaidic acid) are potent ferroptosis sensitizers that act through distinct metabolic pathways converging on phospholipid remodeling. The discovery that the trans-MUFA petroselaidic acid requires SCD-dependent desaturation to a trans-PUFA — while its cis-stereoisomer suppresses ferroptosis — is a striking and original finding. The work is rigorous, multi-faceted, and significantly advances understanding of how lipid stereochemistry governs membrane composition and cell fate.

## Strengths
- The unbiased FALCON screen of 62 structurally diverse fatty acids provides a systematic, hypothesis-free entry point that credibly identifies trans-fatty acids as a distinct class of ferroptosis sensitizers.
- Mechanistic depth is exceptional: complementary loss- and gain-of-function experiments (SCD inhibition, overexpression, isotope tracing) establish that petroselaidic acid's activity requires enzymatic conversion, directly proving double-bond geometry redirects metabolic fate.
- Lipidomic analyses reveal that two structurally distinct trans-fatty acids remodel the phospholipid landscape through different routes (diPUFA enrichment vs. arachidonate enrichment) yet converge on expanding ferroptosis-susceptible phospholipid pools, a sophisticated and biologically important insight.

## Weaknesses
- The targeted oxidized phospholipid analysis (Figure 5) captures only a defined subset of phosphatidylcholine hydroperoxides for which standards existed; the authors acknowledge that petroselaidic acid's robust ferroptosis sensitization is not fully reflected in these data, leaving the specific oxidized species driving its activity incompletely defined. This gap means the mechanistic link between petroselaidic acid's metabolic conversion and the actual peroxidation substrates remains partially inferential.
- Cell-line variability in the DGAT inhibition experiments (Figure 4, Supplementary Figure S5) is noted but not mechanistically explored; U-2 OS cells show qualitatively different responses to DGAT inhibition under petroselaidic acid treatment compared to H460 cells, and the manuscript does not explain whether this reflects differential lipid handling, baseline ferroptosis sensitivity, or off-target effects of the inhibitors.
- The claim that trans-fatty acids do not promote ferroptosis through intrinsic oxidizability (Discussion) relies on literature showing trans-PUFAs oxidize slower than cis-PUFAs in vitro, but the manuscript does not directly measure oxidation kinetics of the relevant phospholipid species in cells, leaving a small but non-trivial gap between the cited biophysical data and the cellular context.

## Questions
- Could the authors clarify whether the trans-18:2 metabolite generated from petroselaidic acid (putatively linopetroselaidic acid, 6E,9Z-18:2) was definitively identified by mass spectrometry or retention time matching, or is its structure inferred from the SCD-dependence and known SCD regioselectivity?
- In the lipidomics data (Figure 6H,I), several phospholipids increased by petroselaidic acid are consistent with 18:1/20:4 species; were any of these confirmed to contain the trans-18:1 (petroselaidic) versus the SCD-generated trans/cis-18:2 at the sn-1 position, and does this distinction affect interpretation of which metabolite drives phospholipid remodeling?
- The manuscript states that FADS2 inhibition had no effect on ferroptosis sensitization by any fatty acid (Supplementary Figure S4C); given that FADS2 is the Δ6-desaturase and petroselaidic acid already has a Δ6-trans bond, this is expected, but was FADS1 (Δ5-desaturase) or elongation activity assessed to rule out further metabolism of the SCD-generated trans/cis-18:2 to longer-chain trans-PUFAs?
