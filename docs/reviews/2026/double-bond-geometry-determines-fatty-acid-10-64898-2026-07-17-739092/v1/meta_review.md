# Meta-Review

**Draft recommendation:** major

## Synthesis
The manuscript reports a genuine and important phenotypic discovery: trans-unsaturated fatty acids (linoelaidic acid, petroselaidic acid) are potent ferroptosis sensitizers, contradicting the dogma that MUFAs suppress ferroptosis. This finding is robustly validated across multiple inducers (RSL3, ML162, ML210, erastin-2, IKE, BSO), inhibitors (Fer-1, DFO, idebenone), and cell lines (H460, U-2 OS, HT1080, NSCLC panel). The unbiased FALCON screen provides a credible, hypothesis-free entry point.

However, the three mechanistic pillars that the paper builds on this phenotype — and that feature prominently in the title, abstract, and model figure — are not adequately supported by the evidence presented:

1. **SCD-dependent conversion of petroselaidic acid**: The claim that petroselaidic acid requires SCD-dependent Δ9-desaturation to 6E,9Z-linopetroselaidic acid (trans/cis-18:2) rests on pharmacological inhibition (CAY10566) and inducible overexpression. The inhibitor alone increases RSL3 killing (Suppl Fig S4G,H); the combination returning to BSA levels is equally consistent with cancellation of opposing effects as with specific blockade of a metabolite. The gain-of-function experiment (Fig 3E) lacks a WT+dox control, so doxycycline toxicity or off-target effects cannot be excluded. No direct biochemical validation (purified SCD assay, MS/MS or NMR of the metabolite) is provided. The abstract's claim of "directly demonstrating that double-bond geometry can redirect fatty acid metabolic fate through altered recognition by lipid metabolic enzymes" overreaches.

2. **Structural identity of the trans-18:2 metabolite**: The "trans 18:2" peak is identified only by GC-FID retention time and SCD dependence. It could be 6E,12E-18:2 (elongation/desaturation) or other trans-18:2 isomers. The manuscript presents the 6E,9Z assignment as fact in Fig 7 and the abstract without structural confirmation.

3. **Phospholipid remodeling as causal convergence**: Figure 6 shows correlative lipidomic changes — linoelaidic acid enriches diPUFA-PLs and sn-1 18:2-PLs; petroselaidic acid enriches arachidonate-PLs. The sn-1 positional assignments are inferred from shotgun lipidomics (Lipidyzer) without positional standards or phospholipase digestion, yet are presented as established molecular architectures in Fig 7 and the abstract. No perturbation of ACSL4, LPCAT3, MBOAT1/2, or other remodeling enzymes tests whether these changes are necessary for ferroptosis sensitization. The claim that the two trans-FAs "converge on phospholipid remodeling that expanded pools of ferroptosis-susceptible phospholipids" (Abstract) presents correlation as mechanism.

Statistical reporting is pervasively absent for load-bearing claims: FALCON screen hits lack replicate numbers, statistical thresholds, and multiple-testing correction; Fig 3D equivalence claim has no n, test, or powered equivalence analysis; Fig 6 lipidomics has no stats or FDR for dozens of species compared. Reproducibility is blocked by zero data deposition (lipidomics raw files, GC-FID chromatograms, FALCON screen raw images/code).

These are not minor clarity issues; they are gaps between the causal claims made and the evidence provided. The phenotypic discovery is strong and novel (novelty score 4/5 @ conf 5), but the mechanistic superstructure requires substantial reinforcement.

## Decisive Factors
Three fatal mechanistic gaps that the advocate did not rebut: (1) SCD mechanism confounded by inhibitor's independent pro-ferroptotic effect and missing WT+dox control in gain-of-function; (2) central metabolite (6E,9Z-linopetroselaidic acid) lacks structural confirmation beyond GC-FID retention time; (3) phospholipid remodeling convergence presented as causal mechanism with sn-1 assignments inferred without positional standards. These are explicitly called out by the methodology, rigor, and data-analysis reviewers and persist across both debate rounds. The confidence-weighted score (3.68) and verdict distribution (4 major, 3 minor, 1 accept) align with a major-revision recommendation — the work is important but the central claims outrun the evidence. Per In Silico's calibration: 'Reserve the top of the scale for work you would actively recommend a colleague read, and the bottom for work whose central claim does not survive the evidence given.' The phenotypic claim survives; the mechanistic claims do not yet. The recommendation diverges from 'minor revision' because the gaps are in the load-bearing mechanistic core, not peripheral presentation; it diverges from 'reject' because the unbiased phenotypic discovery is solid, reproducible across orthogonal assays, and the gaps are addressable with additional experiments (structural ID, unconfounded epistasis, causal remodeling tests, statistical rigor, data deposition). The venue's standards ('Are the conclusions supported by the evidence?') require that the mechanistic claims be either substantiated or qualified in revision before acceptance. The panel did not miss a reject-worthy flaw — the phenotypic core is sound — but the mechanistic overreach requires major revision to bring claims into alignment with evidence. The advocate's concession that statistical/reproducibility gaps are 'fixable in revision' is correct but incomplete: the three mechanistic gaps require new experiments, not just textual fixes. The skeptic's 'FATAL' assessment of the mechanistic core is sustained. Therefore: major revision with explicit conditions addressing the three mechanistic pillars, statistical rigor, and data deposition.
