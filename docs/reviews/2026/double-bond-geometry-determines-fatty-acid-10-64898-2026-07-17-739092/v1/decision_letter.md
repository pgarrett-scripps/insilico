# Decision Letter

**Decision:** major

## Summary of Evaluation
The manuscript reports a genuine and important phenotypic discovery: trans-unsaturated fatty acids (linoelaidic acid, petroselaidic acid) are potent ferroptosis sensitizers, contradicting the dogma that MUFAs suppress ferroptosis. This finding is robustly validated across multiple inducers, inhibitors, and cell lines, and the unbiased FALCON screen provides a credible hypothesis-free entry point. The phenotypic core is sound, reproducible, and novel.

However, the three mechanistic pillars featured in the title, abstract, and model figure are not adequately supported by the evidence presented:

1. **SCD-dependent conversion of petroselaidic acid**: The claim that petroselaidic acid requires SCD-dependent Δ9-desaturation to 6E,9Z-linopetroselaidic acid rests on pharmacological inhibition (CAY10566) and inducible overexpression. The inhibitor alone increases RSL3 killing; the combination returning to BSA levels is equally consistent with cancellation of opposing effects. The gain-of-function experiment lacks a WT+dox control. No direct biochemical validation (purified SCD assay, MS/MS or NMR of the metabolite) is provided.

2. **Structural identity of the trans-18:2 metabolite**: The "trans 18:2" peak is identified only by GC-FID retention time and SCD dependence. It could be 6E,12E-18:2 or other isomers. The manuscript presents the 6E,9Z assignment as fact without structural confirmation.

3. **Phospholipid remodeling as causal convergence**: Figure 6 shows correlative lipidomic changes. The sn-1 positional assignments are inferred from shotgun lipidomics without positional standards or phospholipase digestion, yet are presented as established molecular architectures. No perturbation of ACSL4, LPCAT3, MBOAT1/2 tests whether these changes are necessary for ferroptosis sensitization.

Statistical reporting is pervasively absent for load-bearing claims (FALCON screen hit-calling, Fig 3D equivalence, Fig 6 lipidomics FDR). Reproducibility is blocked by zero data deposition.

The authors' rebuttal concedes nearly all methodological, statistical, and reproducibility gaps and proposes concrete corrective actions (additional controls, structural validation, qualification of causal language, statistical rigor, data deposition). They credibly disagree on the FALCON screen's generalizability (orthogonal validation across 6 inducers, 3 inhibitors, 12 cell lines exceeds typical follow-up) and acknowledge limitations in oxidized PL analysis and isotope tracing as future directions. The load-bearing critiques are acknowledged and mapped to specific revision plans.

Editorial compliance audits reveal six HARD gaps in methods completeness (sample sizes, antibody RRIDs, cell line authentication, plasmid details, MS repository accession, microscopy/flow details) and six unverifiable citation items (future-year references, claim-support verification). These are folded into required revisions.

Per In Silico's calibration, the phenotypic claim survives but the mechanistic claims do not yet. The verdict is major revision because the gaps are in the load-bearing mechanistic core, not peripheral presentation, and are addressable with additional experiments and textual qualifications. The decision aligns with the confidence-weighted score (3.68), the verdict distribution (4 major), and the meta-review's decisive factors.

## Required Revisions
1. Mechanistic: SCD-dependent conversion of petroselaidic acid — Add WT + doxycycline control to Figure 3E; perform SCD CRISPR knockout + rescue with catalytically dead SCD to distinguish catalytic vs. off-target effects; include these data in revised Figure 3 or new supplementary figure.
2. Mechanistic: Structural identity of trans-18:2 metabolite — Perform LC-MS/MS with authentic 6E,9Z-linopetroselaidic acid standard (synthesized or purchased) to confirm metabolite identity by retention time and MS/MS fragmentation; if standard unavailable, use ozone-induced dissociation (OzID) or NMR on purified metabolite to assign double-bond geometry/position; include structural validation data in revised manuscript.
3. Mechanistic: Phospholipid remodeling convergence — Qualify all remodeling claims as 'correlative' in Abstract, Results, Discussion, Figure 7, and title; add perturbation experiments (e.g., ACSL4 KO, LPCAT3 KD, MBOAT1/2 KD) testing necessity of diPUFA/sn-1 incorporation (linoelaidic) and arachidonate enrichment (petroselaidic) for ferroptosis sensitization; if not feasible in revision, explicitly state these are future directions and temper causal language throughout.
4. Statistical rigor: Add biological replicate numbers (n) and replicate type (biological vs technical) to all figure legends; specify exact statistical test for each comparison (e.g., 'two-way ANOVA with Dunnett's post-hoc test, n=4'); define error bars as SEM or SD in each legend; add FDR correction for lipidomics species comparisons (Benjamini-Hochberg, q<0.05); include equivalence test or confidence intervals for Figure 3D 'rescue to control levels' claim; report FALCON screen hit-calling criteria, replicate numbers, statistical thresholds, and multiple-testing correction.
5. Data deposition: Deposit raw and processed lipidomics data (LC-MS/MS MRM files, shotgun lipidomics peak tables) to MetaboLights; deposit GC-FID chromatograms and OmegaQuant protocol details to Figshare; deposit FALCON screen raw Incucyte images and normalization script to GitHub/Zenodo; add data availability statement with accessions.
6. Methods completeness (HARD gaps from audit): Report sample size (n) and replicate type for every experiment (FALCON screen, dose-response, GC-FID, immunoblots, flow cytometry, LC-MS/MS, targeted lipidomics); provide RRIDs for all primary and secondary antibodies, explicitly state host species and clonality; provide RRID/CVCL identifiers and STR authentication records for all cell lines (including elephant seal primary endothelial cells); provide plasmid source (Addgene #), construct description (promoter, tag, selection marker), and validation details for mCherry-NLS and Tet-on SCD overexpression lines; deposit mass spectrometry data to public repository (MassIVE/PRIDE) and provide accession; report objective NA for confocal, detector settings for Incucyte and confocal, software versions for Incucyte and Harmony, and full gating strategy for flow cytometry.
7. Citation integrity: Verify accessibility of future-year references (Refs 14, 27, 42) — replace with accessible preprints or published versions if available; complete missing page/article numbers for Refs 26, 32, 36, 46, 48, 49, 50, 53, 55; verify that Refs 47–51 directly support the cited claims and adjust citations if needed.
8. Clarity fixes per author concessions: Correct Figure 3A caption to accurately reflect time-course data (0, 4, 8, 24 hr) and fix duplicated lipid species label to 'trans 18:1, cis 18:2, and trans 18:2'; revise Abstract petroselinic acid statement to reflect Figure 2G data (protection only with FSP1 inhibition); qualify all sn-1 positional claims as 'inferred from acyl chain composition' in Results, Figure 6 legend, Figure 7, and Discussion; add explicit Methods statement that Lipidyzer shotgun lipidomics does not resolve sn-positional isomers.

## Minor Suggestions
- Expand targeted oxidized phospholipid analysis to PE and PI classes in future work to comprehensively assess stereochemical oxidation signatures.
- Consider isotope tracing (e.g., 13C-labeled fatty acids) for flux quantification of metabolic conversion pathways in follow-up studies.
- Disclose the approximate self-citation count (9 of 55 references from Olzmann group/collaborators) in a transparency statement.
- If perturbation experiments for phospholipid remodeling are not feasible in this revision, clearly label the correlative lipidomics data as hypothesis-generating and specify the exact experiments needed for causal testing in the Discussion.
