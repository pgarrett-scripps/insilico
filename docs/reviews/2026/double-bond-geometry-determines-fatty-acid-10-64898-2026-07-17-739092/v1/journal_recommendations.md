# Journal Recommendations

## As-is (current quality)
- **bioRxiv**
  - Fit: The manuscript reports a novel phenotypic discovery (trans fatty acids as ferroptosis sensitizers) that is robustly validated across multiple inducers, inhibitors, and cell lines. As a preprint server, bioRxiv imposes no mechanistic completeness threshold and would make the phenotypic data immediately citable while revisions are underway. The FALCON screen and orthogonal validation (6 inducers, 3 inhibitors, 12 cell lines) constitute a solid, standalone resource for the field.
  - Realism: Certain acceptance (preprint server, no peer review). Use to establish priority and solicit feedback during revision.

## After required revisions
- **Nature Metabolism**
  - Fit: Direct thematic fit: the paper establishes fatty acid double-bond geometry as a determinant of metabolic fate (SCD-dependent desaturation of trans-MUFA to trans-PUFA) and membrane phospholipid remodeling (diPUFA enrichment, sn-1 positional incorporation, arachidonate enrichment) that converges on ferroptosis sensitivity. Nature Metabolism regularly publishes work at the intersection of lipid metabolism, enzyme substrate specificity, and cell fate. The unbiased FALCON screen + mechanistic depth (if gaps are filled) matches their scope.
  - Realism: High if all three mechanistic pillars are experimentally substantiated (structural ID of 6E,9Z-linopetroselaidic acid, unconfounded SCD epistasis, causal remodeling perturbations). The phenotypic novelty is strong; the risk is whether revised mechanistic data reach the journal's standard for 'mechanistic insight' vs. correlation.
- **Nature Cell Biology**
  - Fit: Strong fit for a study that redefines a structural determinant (cis/trans geometry) of membrane composition and links it to a regulated cell death pathway via phospholipid remodeling. NCB prioritizes cell biological mechanism with physiological relevance. The multi-cell-line validation (including primary seal endothelial cells) and the convergence of two distinct trans-FAs on ferroptosis-susceptible phospholipid pools align with their emphasis on generalizable cell biology.
  - Realism: Moderate-high if revisions deliver direct structural validation of the SCD metabolite and at least one causal perturbation of the remodeling pathways (e.g., ACSL4/LPCAT3/MBOAT KD). The journal expects mechanistic claims to be rigorously tested; correlative lipidomics alone would not suffice.
- **Cell Metabolism**
  - Fit: Excellent topical match: fatty acid metabolic fate (SCD substrate redirect), phospholipid remodeling (Lands cycle enzymes), and ferroptosis as a metabolic cell death readout. Cell Metabolism favors studies that integrate metabolic flux with functional outcomes. The SCD-dependent conversion of a trans-MUFA to a trans-PUFA is a metabolic rerouting story central to their scope.
  - Realism: Moderate-high if isotope tracing or direct flux measurements are added (editors often request this for 'metabolic fate' claims) and the SCD mechanism is genetically unconfounded. The phenotypic robustness across inducers/cell lines is a strength.

## Alternative outlets
- **Molecular Cell**
  - Fit: Mechanistic cell biology venue that accepts deep dives into lipid-mediated cell death. The paper's focus on enzyme substrate specificity (SCD, ACSL4, remodeling enzymes) and structural determinants of membrane composition fits well. Broader than metabolism-specific journals.
  - Realism: Moderate. Would require the same mechanistic upgrades as above. Accepts correlative lipidomics more readily if framed as 'hypothesis-generating' with clear causal tests proposed.
- **eLife**
  - Fit: Publishes rigorous cell biology and metabolism work with transparent review. The major-revision verdict aligns with eLife's model (they often invite revision on mechanistic gaps). The phenotypic novelty and multi-system validation meet their 'important advance' bar if claims are qualified to evidence.
  - Realism: Moderate-high. eLife is more tolerant of 'correlative but deeply characterized' lipidomics if the Discussion honestly frames limitations. The required revisions (structural ID, genetic epistasis) are feasible for a revision round.
- **Journal of Lipid Research**
  - Fit: Specialized home for lipid metabolism, enzyme specificity, and phospholipid remodeling. The GC-FID/LC-MS/MS lipidomics depth, SCD substrate redirect story, and positional isomer analysis (even if inferred) are core JLR content. Audience is exactly the lipid biochemistry community that will scrutinize and build on the structural claims.
  - Realism: High. JLR routinely accepts papers with strong phenotypic data and mechanistic hypotheses that need further validation, provided lipid analytics are rigorous. The current lipidomics methods (Lipidyzer, targeted MRM) meet their technical standards. A 'major revision' here is often a 'minor revision' at JLR.

## Notes
The manuscript's current 'major revision' verdict reflects three load-bearing mechanistic gaps: (1) SCD-dependent conversion lacks unconfounded genetic evidence and direct structural validation of the 6E,9Z-linopetroselaidic acid metabolite; (2) phospholipid remodeling claims are correlative with sn-1 assignments inferred without positional standards or causal perturbations; (3) statistical rigor and data deposition are absent. No top-tier venue will accept until these are addressed with new experiments (not just text changes). After revision, Nature Metabolism / Nature Cell Biology / Cell Metabolism are realistic headline targets if the metabolite is structurally confirmed (OzID/NMR/synthetic standard), SCD epistasis is genetically clean (CRISPR KO + catalytically dead rescue), and at least one remodeling enzyme perturbation tests necessity. If revisions are partial (e.g., only textual qualification of correlative claims), Journal of Lipid Research, eLife, or Molecular Cell become the appropriate tier. bioRxiv deposition now is strongly advised to establish priority on the phenotypic discovery.
