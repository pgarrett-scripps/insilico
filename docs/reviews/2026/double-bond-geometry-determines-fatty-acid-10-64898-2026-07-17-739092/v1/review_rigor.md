# Rigor & Overclaiming Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript convincingly identifies trans-unsaturated fatty acids as ferroptosis sensitizers through an unbiased screen and demonstrates that double-bond geometry alters metabolic fate—most rigorously for petroselaidic acid's SCD-dependent conversion to a PUFA. The lipidomic evidence for distinct phospholipid remodeling by linoelaidic vs. petroselaidic acid is strong, though sn-position assignments remain inferential. Claims that geometry 'determines' metabolic fate and ferroptosis sensitivity are somewhat overgeneralized from two C18 fatty acid pairs in limited cell lines. The work is sound and important but requires qualification of mechanistic scope and structural certainty.

## Strengths
- Unbiased FALCON screen with orthogonal validation across multiple ferroptosis inducers and cell lines provides robust phenotypic evidence.
- Complementary loss- and gain-of-function experiments (SCD inhibition/overexpression) establish a clear causal mechanism for petroselaidic acid's metabolic conversion and ferroptosis sensitization.
- Comprehensive lipidomics and oxidized phospholipid profiling reveal distinct, geometry-dependent remodeling pathways that converge on ferroptosis-susceptible membrane compositions.

## Weaknesses
- The claim that double-bond geometry 'determines' metabolic fate is mechanistically complete only for petroselaidic acid (SCD-dependent Δ9 desaturation); for linoelaidic acid, the paper shows preferential incorporation into diPUFA phospholipids and sn-1 18:2 species but does not identify the enzymatic machinery responsible, leaving 'determines' as a descriptive correlation rather than a demonstrated mechanism.
- sn-position assignments and exact molecular species (e.g., petroselaidic acid at sn-1 paired with arachidonate at sn-2) are inferred from shotgun lipidomics without chiral or positional standards; the authors acknowledge this limitation, but the abstract and model figure present these architectures as established facts.
- The generalization that 'fatty acid double-bond geometry determines ferroptosis sensitivity' rests on two C18 cis/trans pairs and three mixed isomers tested in a 62-compound screen across a handful of cancer lines; the title and abstract present this as a broad principle without qualifying the structural and cellular scope actually tested.

## Questions
- For linoelaidic acid, which acyltransferases or remodeling enzymes mediate the preferential sn-1 incorporation and diPUFA phospholipid formation?
- Can the trans-18:2 metabolite from petroselaidic acid be structurally confirmed as 6E,9Z-linopetroselaidic acid (vs. other trans-18:2 isomers) using authentic standards?
- Do the ferroptosis-sensitizing effects of trans fatty acids persist at physiologically relevant concentrations (e.g., ≤10 μM) rather than the 100–200 μM used here?
