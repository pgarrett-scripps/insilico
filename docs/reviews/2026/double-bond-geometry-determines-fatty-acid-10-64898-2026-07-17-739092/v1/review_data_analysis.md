# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript makes strong mechanistic claims supported by extensive experimental data, but the statistical reporting has significant gaps: n values are often missing or ambiguous, multiple comparisons corrections are not consistently described, error bars mix SEM and SD without clear labeling in figures, and several key quantitative claims lack explicit statistical tests. These issues undermine confidence in the reported effect sizes and significance levels.

## Strengths
- The experimental design includes appropriate controls (BSA vehicle, ferroptosis inhibitors, genetic knockouts) and orthogonal validation across multiple cell lines.
- Lipidomics and oxidation product quantification use internal standards and external calibration, providing quantitative rigor for the metabolic profiling.
- The FALCON screen employs a normalized AUC metric with DMSO subtraction, a reasonable approach for high-throughput lethality data.

## Weaknesses
- Load-bearing claim 1 (trans fatty acids are potent ferroptosis sensitizers): The FALCON screen (Fig 1B) ranks hits by normalized AUC but does not state the number of biological replicates, the statistical test used to call 'hits', or any multiple-testing correction across 62 fatty acids × 2 cell lines × 2 conditions (±RSL3). Without this, the false-discovery rate for the top hits (linoelaidic, petroselaidic) is unknown. The text says 'reproducibly enhanced' but no replication number or variance is given for the screen itself.
- Load-bearing claim 2 (petroselaidic acid requires SCD for ferroptosis sensitization): Figure 3D shows lethal fraction over time with and without CAY10566, but the figure legend and methods do not state n, the test comparing curves, or whether the comparison is at a single time point or AUC. The claim that SCD inhibition 'abolished the ability... reducing cell death to levels observed in control cells' is a quantitative equivalence claim that requires a powered equivalence test or at least a confidence interval for the difference; none is provided.
- Load-bearing claim 3 (trans fatty acids remodel phospholipids to generate distinct ferroptosis-susceptible species): Figures 6C–I present lipidomics data with log2 fold-changes but no statistical test, n, or correction for the dozens of phospholipid species compared. The statement 'significantly increased' (e.g., diPUFA phospholipids in 6E) has no associated p-value or FDR. The methods mention 'normalized to cell count' but not how variability across replicates was handled.
- Sweep: Figure 2A–D dose-response curves lack n and error-bar definitions in the legends; the methods say 'error bars in dose-response plots represent SEM' but do not state n for each curve. Figure 2E uses 'strongly suppressed' without a statistical comparison across five inhibitors. Figure 4B, C quantify lipid droplets and flow cytometry but do not report the test used for the multiple comparisons (Dunnett's is mentioned in methods for lipid droplets but not for flow cytometry). Figure 5A, B, D, E show median ratios or intensities with error bars but no n or test for the pairwise comparisons implied by the bars. The methods state 'ordinary one-way ANOVA and Dunnett's multiple comparisons test or Brown-Forsythe and Welch ANOVA tests with Dunnett's T3' but do not specify which test was used for which figure, and n is absent from all figure legends.
- Sweep: The GC-FID fatty acid profiling (Fig 3A, 6A, Supp Fig 4A, B, E, F) reports absolute quantification but never states the number of biological replicates; 'normalized to cell count' is described but the unit of replication (independent culture dishes? independent passages?) is not defined.
- Sweep: The SCD overexpression experiment (Fig 3E, Supp Fig 4I) shows lethal fraction over time for WT vs +dox but provides no n, no test for curve separation, and no verification that doxycycline alone has no effect (the WT + dox condition is missing).
- Sweep: Several 'no difference' claims are made from non-significant results without power justification (e.g., 'FADS2 inhibition had no effect on RSL3 dose response' in Supp Fig 4C; 'DGAT1/2 inhibition failed to block ferroptosis sensitization' in Fig 4D–H and Supp Fig 5E–H).
- Sweep: The Liperfluo and BODIPY C11 flow cytometry data (Fig 5A, B) present median fluorescence ratios but the gating strategy, number of cells analyzed per replicate, and number of replicates are not stated.

## Questions
- For the FALCON screen (Fig 1B): How many biological replicates were performed per fatty acid per cell line? What statistical threshold (e.g., FDR, p-value) was used to define 'hits'?
- For Figure 3D: What is n for each condition? Was the comparison between petroselaidic acid ± CAY10566 done at a single time point or on AUC? What test was used?
- For Figures 6C–I: What is n for the lipidomics? What statistical test and multiple-testing correction were applied to the phospholipid species comparisons?
- For Figure 2E: What test was used to compare cell death across the five inhibitors? What is n?
- For the GC-FID data (Fig 3A, 6A, Supp Fig 4): What is the unit of replication and n for each fatty acid treatment?
- For Figure 3E: What is n? Was a WT + doxycycline control included? What test compared the curves?
- For the flow cytometry lipid peroxidation assays (Fig 5A, B): How many cells were analyzed per replicate? How many biological replicates? What test compared median ratios?
