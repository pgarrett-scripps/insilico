# Clarity & Presentation Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript suffers from severe and pervasive clarity and presentation defects that substantially impede comprehension. The central claim—that Mtb follows a 'universal strategy' of preferring mild mutations in non-conserved regions—is stated only in the abstract and conclusion, not clearly in the main narrative. Critical terms ('lethal,' 'moderate,' 'mild,' 'neutral') are defined only after extensive results are presented, forcing readers to backtrack. The methods section is fragmented across multiple subsections with unclear sequencing, and the results section reads as a list of disconnected findings rather than a coherent narrative building toward the hypothesis. Figures lack consistent labeling, legends are incomplete, and quantitative claims frequently omit comparison baselines. The paper's length, density, and organizational structure make it difficult for a competent reader to extract the core argument without substantial effort.

## Strengths
- The abstract clearly states the four-part hypothesis and main finding (Mtb favours less harmful mutations), providing an entry point for readers.
- Supplementary tables (S1, S3, S5–S11) are referenced and appear to contain detailed supporting data, which aids reproducibility.
- The paper attempts a systematic four-phase framework (data collection, screening, analysis, ranking), which is conceptually clear in Figure 3a.
- Geographic distribution of mutations is presented with WHO region aggregation, which is a reasonable approach given data limitations.

## Weaknesses
- **HARD: Central claim is not clearly stated in the main narrative.** The hypothesis that 'Mtb favours mutations that protect them from a drug while making the drug ineffective' appears in the abstract and conclusion but is never explicitly restated in the Introduction or Methods as the guiding research question. Readers must infer the through-line from scattered statements like 'drug resistance is an internal optimisation or balancing act' (page 5) without a clear focal claim.
- **HARD: Core ranking categories ('lethal,' 'moderate,' 'mild,' 'neutral') are defined only after extensive results.** Section 3.6.2.5 (page 25) states: 'Functionally deleterious mutations that compromise residue conservation with reduced structural stability and binding affinity were categorised "lethal."' This definition appears ~15 pages after these terms are first used in results (e.g., 'We identified 340 mutations as "lethal"' on page 1 abstract). A reader cannot understand the results section without backtracking to the conclusion.
- **HARD: Methods section is fragmented and lacks clear operational sequencing.** The comprehensive analysis (Section 4, Materials and Methods) lists tools and concepts but does not clearly specify the order in which analyses were performed or which mutations were subjected to which analyses. For example: Were all 821 mutations modeled structurally? The text states 'A total of 603 mutant protein models were generated' (page 17), implying ~218 mutations were not modeled, but the criteria for inclusion/exclusion are never stated. This is critical for reproducibility.
- **HARD: Quantitative claims lack comparison baselines.** Examples: 'The most frequently occurring mutation was observed at katG315 position harbouring 3690 mutations in 5667 phenotypically INH resistant Mtb isolates' (page 13)—this does not convey whether 3690 is high or low relative to other positions without reading further. 'Out of 821 mutations, 66.5% were in HC, 14.13% were in C and 19.37% were in V' (page 19)—the reader cannot assess whether 66.5% in highly conserved regions contradicts the claim that 'frequent mutations were at variable sites' without explicit comparison.
- **HARD: Figure 9b is referenced but its meaning is unclear from caption and labels alone.** The figure shows distribution of mutated residues by conservation in 'three different sites: site-1, site-2, site-3' but the legend does not define what these sites are. The text (page 20) defines them retroactively: 'site-1 was defined as the ligand-binding region; site-2 directly interacts with site-1 and site-3 was defined for mutations occurring elsewhere.' Without this definition in the figure legend, the panel is incomprehensible.
- **HARD: The relationship between 'overall mutation count' and 'non-synonymous mutation count' is presented confusingly.** Section 3.2 states katG and rpoB have 'much higher mutation count' (4589 and 5191 total mutations), but Section 3.5 states 'the nonsynonymous mutation count does not follow the same trend' and that katG and rpoB have 'lower non-synonymous mutation count than pncA and emb' (202 and 120 vs. 273 and 226). This apparent contradiction is explained only in the text ('most are synonymous mutations'), but the figure or table presenting both counts side-by-side is missing, forcing readers to reconstruct the comparison manually.
- **HARD: Table 1 legend is incomplete.** The legend states 'In terms of impact on the target, sequence conservation predicted by ConSurf is shown as HC (Highly conserved) and V (Variable)' but does not explain what 'C' (conserved) means, which appears in the table. The legend also does not define 'site-1,' 'site-2,' 'site-3' or explain what 'Presence in domain region' means (Yes/No).
- **SOFT: Inconsistent terminology for drug targets.** The paper uses 'katG,' 'rpoB,' 'pncA,' 'embCAB,' 'embB,' 'embA,' 'embC' interchangeably without always clarifying whether a statement applies to the gene, the protein, or a specific subunit. For example: 'embCAB mutations' (page 10) vs. 'embB' (page 13) vs. 'arabinosyltransferase' (page 17) all refer to the same drug target but are not explicitly linked.
- **SOFT: Results section reads as a disconnected list rather than a narrative.** Sections 3.4–3.6 present findings (hotspot sites, amino acid substitutions, mutation atlas, conservation patterns, binding affinity) without explicitly connecting each to the central hypothesis. A reader cannot easily answer: 'How does the finding that S315 is in a variable region support the claim that Mtb prefers mild mutations?' The logical thread is present but implicit.
- **SOFT: Figure 7 caption does not specify what 'mutation count' represents.** Is this the total number of times a position was mutated across all isolates, or the number of distinct mutations at that position? The y-axis label 'Mutation count' is ambiguous. The text clarifies this (page 13: 'The mutation count for katG ranged from 1 to 3690'), but the figure should be self-contained.
- **SOFT: The paper uses 'Mtb' and 'Mycobacterium tuberculosis' inconsistently.** Both are used throughout without a clear rule (e.g., 'Mtb' in some sections, full name in others). While not a HARD issue, it creates minor friction.
- **SOFT: Section 3.6.2.1 states 'We were not able to construct all the mutant structures for arabinosyltransferase (emb) because to date, only structural information on the C-terminal domain of embC is available' (page 17).** This is a critical limitation but is buried in a methods subsection rather than highlighted in the results or discussion. Readers may not realize that conclusions about embB are based on incomplete structural data.
- **SOFT: The claim 'all or most residues in the ligand binding region are mutated' (page 20, Section 3.6.2.3) is not quantified.** How many residues are in the binding region? How many were mutated? Figure 9c shows red (mutated) and green (non-mutated) residues but does not provide counts or percentages.

## Questions
- In Section 3.6.2.5, the ranking of mutations into 'lethal,' 'moderate,' 'mild,' and 'neutral' is described as based on 'concordance analysis' integrating results from multiple tools. Can you clarify the exact algorithm or decision tree used? For example, if a mutation is predicted 'deleterious' by SIFT but 'benign' by PolyPhen-2, how is it classified?
- The paper states that 603 mutant protein models were generated out of 821 mutations (page 17). Which 218 mutations were not modeled, and why? Were they excluded because they could not be modeled, or because they were not subjected to structural analysis?
- In Figure 9b, the distribution of mutations across site-1, site-2, and site-3 is shown for each drug target, but the figure does not indicate how many total mutations are represented. Are these the 821 non-synonymous mutations, or a subset? If a subset, which subset and why?
- Section 3.5 states that 'the nonsynonymous mutation count does not follow the same trend as the overall mutation count' and that katG and rpoB have lower non-synonymous counts than pncA and embCAB. Can you provide a table or figure showing both counts side-by-side for all four targets to make this comparison explicit?
- The paper claims that 'frequently mutated positions also have a larger number of amino acid substitutions' (Section 3.4 heading). Can you quantify this relationship? For example, what is the correlation between mutation frequency and number of distinct amino acids at a position?
- In Section 3.6.2.2, you state that 'mutations occurring more frequently were not present in the highly conserved regions in the first-line drug targets but in the highly variable regions.' However, Table 1 shows that S315T (the most frequent mutation in katG) is in a variable region, but Q10P (the most frequent in pncA) is in a highly conserved region. How do you reconcile this apparent contradiction?
- The paper uses the term 'hotspot site' to mean 'a position with three or more amino acid substitutions' (page 12). Is this definition standard in the field, or is it specific to this study? If specific, how was the threshold of three chosen?
- Section 3.6.2.4 states that 'all or most residues in the ligand binding region are mutated' but does not provide counts. Can you specify: (1) how many residues are in the binding region for each drug target, and (2) how many of these were mutated in your dataset?
- The paper claims that 'Mtb follows some guided evolutionary pathway to balance the impact caused by drug resistant mutations on the fitness of Mtb' (page 28). What evidence in this study directly supports 'guided' evolution, as opposed to random mutation followed by selection? Is this claim speculative?
- In the Materials and Methods section, you state that 'single mutation frequency is calculated using the following formula' but the formula shown has a denominator that is ambiguous: 'Total number of resistant isolates in a drug target having that mutation.' Does this mean isolates with that specific mutation, or isolates resistant to that drug? If the former, how is this different from the numerator?
