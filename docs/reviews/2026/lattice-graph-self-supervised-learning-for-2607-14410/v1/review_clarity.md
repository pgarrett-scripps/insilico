# Clarity & Presentation Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript is generally well-structured and readable, with clear motivation, methods, and results sections. The technical content is presented with sufficient detail for reproduction, and figures support the narrative. However, there are several presentation issues that create friction: the modality ladder (M1–M5) is introduced informally and used inconsistently; the relationship between ReCAST and SARSIM preprocessing is underspecified in the main text; and some quantitative claims lack precision. The abstract is intelligible but could be more explicit about the central claim. These are mostly soft issues that slow comprehension rather than block it, but they should be addressed for clarity.

## Strengths
- The paper clearly motivates the problem: existing spatial omics methods treat modalities separately, and LATTICE addresses this by learning unified spot-level representations from multimodal features.
- The modality ladder (M1–M5) is a well-designed pedagogical device that allows readers to track the contribution of each modality incrementally, and it is used consistently throughout Tables 2 and Figure 2.
- Methods section (Section 3) is mathematically precise: equations are numbered, symbols are defined (e.g., X for concatenated features, Z for embeddings, Ω for reconstruction mask), and the three loss components are clearly separated (Eqs. 6, 8, 9, 10).
- Algorithm 1 provides a clear step-by-step training procedure that is easy to follow and reproducible.
- Figures 1, 4, 5, 6 are well-captioned and directly support claims about spatial domains, TF programs, and accessibility–gene links; axes and legends are clear.
- Appendix H provides comprehensive implementation details (architecture, hyperparameters, optimization settings, hardware, runtime) that enable reproduction.
- The paper honestly reports limitations: ARI/NMI decrease with additional modalities (M4–M5), and the authors explicitly state this reflects capture of chromatin structure beyond transcriptomics, not a failure of the method.

## Weaknesses
- The abstract uses 'LATTICE' and 'multimodal spatial omics integration' without defining them in the abstract itself, making it less self-contained. The abstract should state upfront that LATTICE is a graph neural network method, not just name it.
- The relationship between ReCAST and SARSIM is mentioned in the introduction (Section 1) but their roles are not clearly delineated until Section 4.1 and Appendices F–G. A reader cannot determine from the main text alone which preprocessing steps are performed by which tool, creating ambiguity about the method's scope.
- The term 'modality blocks' is used before it is formally defined. Section 3.1 introduces X^(b) as 'modality block b' but does not explain what constitutes a 'block' (is it a set of features? a matrix?) until Section 4.1 lists the five blocks explicitly.
- Section 4.1 states 'ReCAST performs preprocessing and harmonization' and 'SARSIM performs spatially anchored regulatory projection,' but the main text does not specify which steps are internal to LATTICE and which are upstream. This makes it unclear whether LATTICE's contribution includes the harmonization or only the graph SSL training.
- Table 1 caption and text do not explain what 'Multiome cells' represents (is it the number of cells profiled? cells mapped to spots?). The column 'Final intersected genes' varies from 129–322 across samples, but the text does not explain why this variation occurs or whether it affects comparability.
- Figure 2 shows trends across M1–M5 but the y-axis ranges differ across panels (e.g., ARI and NMI both 0–1.0, but spatial contiguity also 0–1.0 despite different semantics). The figure caption does not explain what 'cohort mean ± std' means operationally (is it mean across 11 samples? across runs within samples?).
- The Multimodal Utility Score (MUS) is introduced in Section 4.1 (Eq. 11) as averaging four normalized quantities, but the normalization procedure is deferred to Appendix A.2. A reader cannot assess whether MUS is a reasonable metric without reading the appendix, and the main text does not justify why these four components were chosen or weighted equally.
- Section 4.2 states 'Adding projected scMultiome RNA to Visium RNA improves ARI, NMI, spatial contiguity, silhouette, and MUS' but does not provide the actual numbers in the main text—readers must consult Table 2. The claim would be stronger if the main text stated the magnitude (e.g., 'ARI improved from 0.269 to 0.426, a gain of +0.157').
- The ablation study (Table 3) removes components from the full M5 model, but the table caption does not explain what 'Spatial reg.' and 'Masking' mean operationally (are these loss terms? architectural components?). A reader unfamiliar with the method cannot interpret the rows without re-reading Section 3.3.
- Section 4.3 claims 'Paired pre/post-treatment samples show patient-specific shifts' and 'global shift alignment is near zero,' but these quantities are not defined or quantified in the main text. What is 'global shift alignment'? How is it computed? The reader must infer this from Figure 3 alone, which is insufficient.
- The paper uses 'Space Ranger clusters' as a reference label in Section 4.1 but does not explain whether Space Ranger is a tool, a method, or a dataset. The text should clarify that Space Ranger is 10x Genomics' standard RNA-only clustering pipeline (this is stated in a footnote but should be in the main text).
- Appendix I (Theoretical Analysis) provides lemmas and a theorem but these are not referenced in the main text and their relevance to the empirical results is unclear. If the theory is meant to justify the method, the main text should cite it; if it is supplementary, the appendix should be clearer about its role.

## Questions
- In Section 1, the paper states that LATTICE 'constructs a spatial neighborhood graph and trains a TransformerConv encoder using masked reconstruction, cross-modal alignment, and spatial smoothness objectives.' Are all three objectives always used, or are they optional? Table 3 ablates them, but the main text does not state whether the default configuration uses all three.
- Section 4.1 defines the modality ladder M1–M5 and states 'Levels M2 to M3 align with the SARSIM-scope inputs, and levels M4 to M5 add ReCAST-derived in situ epigenomics.' Does this mean SARSIM produces M2–M3 and ReCAST produces M4–M5? Or do both tools contribute to all levels?
- Table 1 shows 'Final intersected genes' ranging from 129–322. The text states (Appendix H) that 'the final intersected gene set is sample-specific' but does not explain why. Is this due to quality filtering? Availability of features in each modality? This affects the comparability of embeddings across samples.
- Figure 2 shows that ARI and NMI decrease from M2 to M5, while spatial contiguity increases. The text interprets this as the embeddings capturing 'chromatin and regulatory structure beyond transcriptomic similarity alone.' But how do you distinguish between (a) the embeddings capturing real biological signal that RNA-only methods miss, and (b) the embeddings diverging from the RNA reference due to noise or misalignment in the chromatin modalities?
- Section 4.3 states 'leave-one-patient-out prediction is mixed' but does not define what is being predicted or what 'mixed' means quantitatively. Can you provide the actual prediction accuracy or AUC?
- Appendix I provides theoretical analysis (Lemmas I.1–I.4 and Theorem I.4) but these results are standard (spatial smoothness from graph Laplacian, reconstruction consistency from autoencoders, alignment from NCE). Are these meant to be novel theoretical contributions, or are they provided for completeness? The main text should clarify their role.
- The paper states that LATTICE is 'modular with respect to multimodal feature construction' and 'compatible with a range of upstream integration or mapping strategies' (Section 3.4). But all experiments use ReCAST and SARSIM. Has the method been tested with alternative upstream pipelines, or is this a claim about potential rather than demonstrated compatibility?
