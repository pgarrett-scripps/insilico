# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
LATTICE presents a multimodal spatial omics integration framework evaluated on an 11-sample melanoma cohort. The statistical reporting has significant gaps: key comparisons lack explicit test names, sample sizes, and definitions of variability; the multimodal utility score (MUS) is a composite metric without validation or justification of its weighting scheme; baseline comparisons in Table 2 report standard deviations across samples without clarifying whether these reflect biological variation, run-to-run noise, or both; and the ablation study (Table 3) lacks statistical testing to determine whether observed metric changes are meaningful. The modality-ladder results show interesting trends (M1→M2 gains, M4–M5 trade-offs), but the interpretation that chromatin channels 'capture structure beyond transcriptomic similarity' rests on a decrease in RNA-reference agreement without a formal test of whether this decrease is significant or clinically meaningful. The paired pre/post-treatment analysis explicitly disclaims cross-patient generalization, which is appropriate but limits the scope of claims. For a computational methods paper, the work is technically sound in design, but the statistical presentation falls short of standards expected for quantitative claims in a peer-reviewed venue.

## Strengths
- The modality-ladder design (M1–M5) is a clear, incremental framework for isolating the contribution of each data source, and the progression from Visium RNA alone to full multimodal integration is methodologically sensible.
- Ablation study (Table 3) directly tests the contribution of spatial regularization and masking by removing each component, supporting the claim that spatial loss drives contiguity gains.
- The authors transparently acknowledge limitations: paired pre/post-treatment samples do not show consistent cross-patient signatures (Section 4.3), and they explicitly state 'we do not claim a consistent cross-patient treatment signature,' which is candid and appropriate.
- Reproducibility artifacts are detailed in Appendices G and H: pinned dependencies, Slurm logs, wall-clock times, random seeds, and early-stopping epoch ranges are provided, supporting replication.
- The manuscript clearly separates upstream engineering (ReCAST) from scientific contribution (LATTICE), and notes that ReCAST is not presented as a methodological novelty.

## Weaknesses
- Table 2 reports cohort-level means and standard deviations (e.g., 'ARI 0.426±0.075' for LATTICE M2) but does not state whether these are computed across the 11 samples, across multiple runs per sample, or both. The text states 'joint multisample analysis uses seed 7 and reports stability summaries over 11 analysis seeds' (Appendix H), but it is unclear whether the ±values in Table 2 reflect inter-sample biological variation, inter-seed algorithmic variation, or a combination. This is a HARD issue because the denominator for the standard error is undefined.
- The multimodal utility score (MUS, Eq. 11) is a weighted average of four normalized metrics (spatial contiguity, silhouette, same-cluster spatial-neighbor fraction, and embedding k-NN/spatial k-NN Jaccard overlap). No justification is given for equal weighting (1/4 each), no sensitivity analysis shows how MUS changes if weights are perturbed, and no external validation demonstrates that this composite metric correlates with downstream biological utility. This is a SOFT issue (a better approach would include weight sensitivity or biological validation), but it undermines the claim that M5 has 'highest MUS' without knowing whether the weighting is arbitrary.
- Figure 2 and Table 2 show that ARI and NMI decrease from M2 to M5 (e.g., ARI drops from 0.426 to 0.329), and the authors interpret this as evidence that 'the learned embeddings capture regulatory and chromatin structure beyond transcriptomic similarity alone.' However, no statistical test is provided to determine whether this decrease is significant or whether it could reflect noise or overfitting. A paired t-test or Wilcoxon signed-rank test across the 11 samples comparing M2 vs M5 ARI values would clarify whether the drop is real. This is a HARD issue because the interpretation hinges on a quantitative claim without a test.
- Table 3 (ablation) reports metric changes when spatial regularization or masking are removed (e.g., spatial contiguity drops from 0.850 to 0.783 when spatial regularization is removed), but no p-values or confidence intervals are provided. It is unclear whether these differences are statistically significant or merely noise. A paired test across the 11 samples would be appropriate here. This is a HARD issue.
- The baseline comparisons in Table 2 include methods (GraphST, STAGATE, SpaGCN, SIMO, MaxFuse) applied to the same cohort. However, the text does not state whether hyperparameters for these baselines were tuned on the same data, whether cross-validation was used to select hyperparameters, or whether the reported numbers represent the best configuration found. If hyperparameter tuning was done on the evaluation data, this introduces leakage. This is a HARD issue if leakage occurred; a SOFT issue if tuning was done on a held-out set or pre-specified.
- Section 4.3 reports 'leave-one-patient-out prediction is mixed' for pre/post-treatment shifts but provides no quantitative results, no test statistics, and no definition of 'mixed.' This is a HARD issue: a claim about cross-patient generalization requires explicit numbers (e.g., mean absolute error, correlation, or classification accuracy with a test and confidence interval).
- The marker gene enrichment score (Appendix A.2) is defined but never reported in the main results or tables. It is unclear whether this metric was computed, what the values are, or how they compare across modality levels. If this metric was computed and supports the biological validity of the embeddings, it should appear in the main results; if not, its definition is orphaned.
- The text states 'reproducible embeddings across analysis seeds' (Abstract) but does not quantify reproducibility. Appendix H mentions 11 analysis seeds {7, 11, 19, 23, 29, 31, 37, 41, 43, 47, 53}, but no metric (e.g., Spearman correlation of embeddings, stability of cluster assignments) is reported to measure reproducibility across seeds. This is a HARD issue because the claim is unquantified.
- Spatial contiguity is defined as 'whether neighboring spots receive coherent labels' but the exact formula is not given in the main text or Appendix A.2. The definition 'SpotCut_v' appears in the MUS formula but is not expanded. Without a precise definition, the metric cannot be verified or reproduced. This is a HARD issue.
- The cohort size is small (11 samples, 54,912 spots total, ~4,992 per sample). No power analysis is provided to justify this sample size for detecting the reported effect sizes (e.g., ARI improvement of +0.157 from M1 to M2). For a clinical cohort, sample size justification is important. This is a SOFT issue (power analysis would strengthen the work) but becomes HARD if any claim of 'no difference' is made from a non-significant result in this small cohort.
- Figure 3 shows PCA of pre/post-treatment samples with the caption 'Shift magnitude varies by patient,' but no quantitative measure of shift magnitude is provided. The figure is qualitative; a table with shift magnitudes (e.g., Euclidean distance between pre and post centroids) and a test for whether shifts differ significantly across patients would be more rigorous. This is a SOFT issue.
- The text states 'Adding scMultiome RNA to Visium RNA alone substantially improved concordance' (Abstract), quantifying this as 'ARI +0.157, NMI +0.143, spatial contiguity +0.174.' However, no test is provided to determine whether these improvements are statistically significant. A paired t-test or Wilcoxon test across the 11 samples would be appropriate. This is a HARD issue because 'substantially improved' is a quantitative claim without a p-value.

## Questions
- In Table 2, are the reported standard deviations (e.g., '0.426±0.075' for LATTICE M2 ARI) computed across the 11 samples, across the 11 analysis seeds, or both? Please clarify the denominator and whether these reflect inter-sample biological variation, inter-seed algorithmic variation, or a combination.
- For the multimodal utility score (MUS, Eq. 11), why are the four components weighted equally (1/4 each)? Have you performed a sensitivity analysis to show how MUS rankings change if weights are perturbed? Does MUS correlate with downstream biological utility (e.g., marker gene enrichment, disease outcome prediction)?
- In Figure 2 and Table 2, ARI and NMI decrease from M2 to M5 (e.g., ARI 0.426→0.329). You interpret this as evidence that embeddings 'capture regulatory and chromatin structure beyond transcriptomic similarity.' Have you performed a paired statistical test (e.g., paired t-test or Wilcoxon signed-rank test across the 11 samples) to determine whether this decrease is statistically significant?
- In Table 3, when spatial regularization is removed, spatial contiguity drops from 0.850 to 0.783. Is this difference statistically significant? Please provide a paired test (e.g., paired t-test) across the 11 samples with a p-value and 95% confidence interval.
- For the baseline methods in Table 2 (GraphST, STAGATE, SpaGCN, SIMO, MaxFuse), how were hyperparameters selected? Were they tuned on the same cohort used for evaluation, or on a held-out validation set? If tuned on the evaluation data, this introduces leakage.
- In Section 4.3, you state 'leave-one-patient-out prediction is mixed.' Please provide quantitative results: what metric was used (e.g., mean absolute error, correlation, classification accuracy)? What are the numerical values and their 95% confidence intervals?
- You define a marker gene enrichment score in Appendix A.2 but never report its values. Was this metric computed? If so, what are the cohort-level values for each modality level (M1–M5), and do they support the biological validity of the embeddings?
- You claim 'reproducible embeddings across analysis seeds' (Abstract) but do not quantify reproducibility. How do embeddings differ across the 11 analysis seeds? Please report a reproducibility metric (e.g., Spearman correlation of embeddings, adjusted Rand index of cluster assignments) with mean and 95% CI.
- Please provide the exact formula for spatial contiguity (SpotCut_v in Eq. 11). The current definition 'whether neighboring spots receive coherent labels' is qualitative and cannot be verified without the formula.
- For the claim 'substantially improved concordance' with ARI +0.157 from M1 to M2 (Abstract), please provide a paired statistical test (e.g., paired t-test or Wilcoxon signed-rank test) across the 11 samples with a p-value and 95% confidence interval. Is this improvement statistically significant at α=0.05?
- In Figure 3, you show that pre/post-treatment samples separate by patient but note 'shift magnitude varies by patient.' Please quantify shift magnitude (e.g., Euclidean distance between pre and post centroids) for each patient and test whether shifts differ significantly across patients (e.g., one-way ANOVA or Kruskal–Wallis test).
- What is the sample size justification for the 11-sample cohort? Have you performed a post-hoc power analysis to determine the power to detect the reported effect sizes (e.g., ARI improvement of +0.157)?
