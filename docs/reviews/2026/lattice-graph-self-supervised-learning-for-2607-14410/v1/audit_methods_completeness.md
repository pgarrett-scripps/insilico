# Methods Completeness & Reagent Traceability Auditor

## Summary
This manuscript presents LATTICE, a graph-based self-supervised learning framework for multimodal spatial omics integration. Triggers detected include: computational/ML methods (primary), human subjects/clinical data (melanoma cohort), genomics/sequencing/omics (five modality blocks including Visium RNA, scMultiome RNA/ATAC, spatial ATAC, spatial CUT&Tag), and protocol provenance (upstream pipelines ReCAST and SARSIM). The work is evaluated on a private 11-sample melanoma cohort (54,912 spots). Major gaps include: (1) the raw cohort data cannot be released due to institutional restrictions, making external validation impossible; (2) upstream pipelines ReCAST and SARSIM are incompletely documented—ReCAST is described as internal engineering with no public code or detailed protocol, and SARSIM is cited as a preprint (arXiv:2026) with unverifiable contents; (3) critical hyperparameters and ablation details are present but some design choices lack justification; (4) no external public benchmark dataset is provided for independent reproduction. The authors acknowledge data unavailability and position results as preliminary, but this severely limits reproducibility and external validation.

## HARD gaps — reproduction blockers
- **[Genomics/sequencing/omics] Repository accession (GEO/SRA/ENA)** — Section 4.1 and Appendix G.1 state: 'The cohort tensors are de-identified clinical biospecimen-derived profiles under a collaborator institution's proprietary agreement, cannot be redistributed publicly, and have no public five-modality substitute at this lattice resolution.' No GEO, SRA, or ENA accession is provided. Raw sequencing data are not deposited.
- **[Human subjects/clinical] Inclusion/exclusion criteria** — No inclusion or exclusion criteria are stated for the melanoma cohort. Table 1 lists 11 retained samples from 14 source samples but does not explain why 3 samples were excluded or what criteria were used for sample selection.
- **[Human subjects/clinical] Participant demographics (age, sex, disease stage, treatment history)** — Table 1 identifies samples by patient number and pre/post-treatment status (e.g., '1-pre', '1-post') but provides no demographic information: age, sex, disease stage, histology, prior treatments, or clinical outcomes. The cohort is described only as 'melanoma' from 'an anonymized clinical collaborator.'

- **[Genomics/sequencing/omics] Batch handling (SOFT)** — The manuscript does not discuss batch effects or batch correction. Appendix A.1 mentions 'per-modality log1p_then_zscore before concatenation' but does not address whether batch effects across samples or modalities were corrected.

## Documented (for the record)
- **[Computational/ML/modeling] Dataset version and exact train/val/test split** — Section 4.1 and Table 1 describe the cohort: 11 retained samples from 14 source samples, 54,912 total Visium spots, each with 4,992 spots. Appendix H states validation fraction = 0.1 with train/validation masks generated at node level. However, the exact train/val/test split percentages are not explicitly stated numerically (e.g., 80/10/10), only that validation is 0.1 of nodes.
- **[Computational/ML/modeling] Architecture and algorithm specification** — Section 3.3 and Appendix H provide full architecture: TransformerConv encoder with 3 graph layers, 4 attention heads, hidden dimension 128, dropout 0.1, ReLU activation, LayerNorm. Decoder is one hidden layer (256 units) + linear output. Modality-specific linear adapters and projection heads (dimension 64, two-layer MLP) are specified.
- **[Computational/ML/modeling] Hyperparameters (loss weights, learning rate, masking ratio, temperature, graph k, etc.)** — Appendix H lists: λ₁=1.0, λ₂=0.5, λ₃=0.1; learning rate 1×10⁻³; weight decay 1×10⁻⁴; masking ratio ρ=0.15; temperature τ=0.1; k=6 for kNN graph; Huber loss δ=1.0; projection head output dimension dₓ=64; hidden dimension dₕ=128; embedding dimension d=128.
- **[Computational/ML/modeling] Training procedure (optimizer, schedule, early stopping, batch size)** — Appendix H: AdamW optimizer with learning rate 1×10⁻³, weight decay 1×10⁻⁴, batch size full graph (no neighbor sampling), up to 100 epochs, early stopping patience 20, gradient clipping norm 1.0. Validation fraction 0.1. No learning rate schedule mentioned.
- **[Computational/ML/modeling] Library versions and hardware** — Appendix H states PyTorch and PyTorch Geometric used; Appendix G.2 specifies CPU-only execution (device=cpu), 16 CPU cores, 128 GB RAM per node. Appendix G.2 notes 'pinned dependency manifests' and 'environment.yml and/or requirements.txt' included as supplementary material, but specific version numbers for PyTorch, PyTorch Geometric, or other dependencies are not listed in the main text or appendices.
- **[Computational/ML/modeling] Random seeds (or seed-averaging statement)** — Appendix H: 'Single-sample runs use global_seed=42' and 'Joint multisample analysis uses seed 7 and reports stability summaries over 11 analysis seeds {7, 11, 19, 23, 29, 31, 37, 41, 43, 47, 53}.' Deterministic cuDNN flags enabled when CUDA used.
- **[Protocol provenance] Space Ranger RNA clustering (reference for ARI/NMI comparison)** — Space Ranger [17] is the 10x Genomics analysis pipeline for Visium data. Reference [17] is 'Space Ranger, 10x Genomics, 2026, https://www.10xgenomics.com/support/software/space-ranger/latest.' This is a resolvable vendor tool, though the version is not specified and the URL points to the latest version (which may change).
- **[Protocol provenance] Leiden clustering (downstream analysis)** — Leiden clustering [19] is used for spatial domain identification. Reference [19] is 'From Louvain to Leiden: Guaranteeing Well-Connected Communities, Scientific Reports, 9(1):5233, 2019.' This is a resolvable publication. Appendix H states 'Leiden with target cluster count K imported from SARSIM clustering metadata and achieved via a resolution sweep' but the resolution sweep details are not provided.
- **[Protocol provenance] Silhouette metric (cluster separation evaluation)** — Silhouette [18] is referenced as 'Silhouettes: A Graphical Aid to the Interpretation and Validation of Cluster Analysis, Journal of Computational and Applied Mathematics, 20:53–65, 1987.' This is a standard, resolvable metric. Appendix H specifies 'mean cosine silhouette' but the exact implementation is not detailed.
- **[Protocol provenance] ARI and NMI metrics (cluster agreement with reference)** — ARI and NMI [16] are referenced as 'Cluster Ensembles—A Knowledge Reuse Framework for Combining Multiple Partitions, Journal of Machine Learning Research, 3(Dec):583–617, 2002.' This is a resolvable publication. The metrics are standard and well-defined.
- **[Computational/ML/modeling] Metric definitions (MUS, marker gene enrichment, spatial contiguity, BioKNN, BioJaccard)** — Appendix A.2 defines MUS as averaging four normalized quantities: SpotCut (spatial contiguity), Silhouette, BioKNN (same-cluster neighbor consistency), and BioJaccard (Jaccard overlap between embedding kNNs and spatial kNNs). Appendix A.2 also defines marker gene enrichment score. However, 'spatial contiguity' (SpotCut) is not formally defined; it is referenced as 'graph-edge spatial contiguity on the spot kNN graph' but the exact formula is not given.
- **[Computational/ML/modeling] Ablation studies and component justification** — Section 4.4 and Table 3 provide ablations: removing spatial regularization, masking, or chromatin channels at test time. Results show spatial regularization is important for spatial contiguity, and masking shapes embedding geometry. However, the ablation does not systematically vary loss weights (λ₁, λ₂, λ₃) or other hyperparameters, and no sensitivity analysis is provided (Appendix D acknowledges 'We do not include quantitative one-dimensional sensitivity curves in this submission').
- **[Computational/ML/modeling] Environment file or dependency manifest** — Appendix G.1 states 'Supplementary bundle ships pinned dependency files (environment.yml and/or requirements.txt)' and Appendix H mentions 'pinned dependency files' but the actual files are not shown in the manuscript. They are stated to be included as supplementary material.
- **[Computational/ML/modeling] Compute budget and runtime** — Appendix G.2 provides: per-sample LATTICE job used one cluster node with 16 CPU cores and 128 GB RAM; ReCAST used 8 cores and 64 GB RAM; wall-clock time ~8 minutes per sample under light queue contention; early stopping spans 36–63 epochs (mean 45.2). Peak resident set size stayed below RAM ceilings.
- **[Computational/ML/modeling] Sample size n with definition (biological vs technical replicates)** — Section 4.1 and Table 1: 11 retained samples (biological replicates, each a distinct patient or timepoint), 54,912 total Visium spots (technical replicates within samples), 4,992 spots per sample. Three patients have paired pre/post-treatment samples. However, the manuscript does not explicitly state whether n=11 refers to samples or patients, and the relationship between samples and patients is not fully clarified (e.g., patient 1 has 2 samples, patient 2 has 1 sample, etc.).
- **[Computational/ML/modeling] Statistical test and error bars (SD/SEM/CI)** — Tables 2 and 3 report metrics as 'mean ± std' (e.g., 'ARI 0.426±0.075'). Figure 2 shows 'cohort mean ± std, N=11 samples.' The standard deviation is reported across the 11 samples. However, the manuscript does not state whether error bars in figures represent SD, SEM, or CI, and no statistical significance tests (e.g., t-tests, ANOVA) are performed to compare methods or modality levels.
- **[Computational/ML/modeling] Data availability statement** — Appendix G.1 states: 'The cohort tensors are de-identified clinical biospecimen-derived profiles under a collaborator institution's proprietary agreement, cannot be redistributed publicly, and have no public five-modality substitute at this lattice resolution.'
