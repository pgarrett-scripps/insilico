## Disagreements
- **novelty** — LATTICE is an incremental extension of GraphST/STAGATE and does not articulate what it does that those methods cannot.
  - Response: GraphST and STAGATE are single-block encoders that take expression (plus optionally histology) as node features; neither defines a per-modality masked reconstruction target, a cross-modal alignment loss between distinct assay views of the same spot, nor an architecture with modality-specific input adapters feeding a shared TransformerConv encoder. The contribution is not 'apply SSL to concatenated features' but a specific joint objective (Eq. 10) designed so that (i) each modality's signal is separately recoverable (masked reconstruction per block), (ii) modality views of the same spot are pulled together in latent space (contrastive alignment), and (iii) spatial coherence is enforced independently of either. We agree the framing should not overstate this as a paradigm shift, and will soften 'key limitation' language in the introduction, but the architectural and objective differences from GraphST/STAGATE are real and specified in Section 3.3, not merely feature concatenation.
- **rigor** — Introducing MUS after ARI/NMI decline at M4-M5 is post-hoc metric selection to rescue a narrative of improvement.
  - Response: MUS was not constructed in response to the M4-M5 result; it was designed prospectively to capture properties (spatial contiguity, embedding-space separation, local neighborhood consistency, and multimodal kNN concordance) that ARI/NMI cannot measure by construction, since ARI/NMI presuppose an RNA-derived reference and cannot credit any signal that departs from that reference. We agree the manuscript does not currently make this design timeline clear, and will add a statement to that effect, together with the sensitivity analysis already conceded above.
  - Manuscript reference: Section 4.1: 'Because ARI and NMI reward agreement with an RNA-only reference, they can undervalue embeddings that capture chromatin or histone structure beyond transcriptomic similarity. We therefore define a multimodal utility score (MUS) to summarize complementary aspects of spatial and multimodal organization.'
- **methodology** — There is no positive control or biological validation ruling out that spatial ATAC/CUT&Tag signals are simply noisier rather than biologically informative.
  - Response: We partially agree a dedicated positive control (e.g., simulated ground-truth chromatin signal) is missing and will not claim to have ruled out the noise explanation definitively. However, the manuscript does provide biological plausibility checks beyond the aggregate metrics: TF program activity varies coherently across Leiden clusters (Fig. 5) and accessibility-gene link distributions are non-degenerate and cluster-structured (Fig. 6), which would not be expected if the added channels were pure noise randomly permuted across spots. We will strengthen this by adding a marker-gene enrichment comparison across M1-M5 (the metric already defined in Appendix A.2 but not reported) as partial biological validation, while explicitly softening the causal language ('capture...beyond transcriptomic similarity') to 'consistent with' pending a full positive-control study.
  - Manuscript reference: Figures 5-6 and Appendix A.2 'Marker Gene Enrichment Score'
- **literature** — Recent 2025-2026 multimodal spatial omics papers (arXiv:2601.12381, 2508.00969, 2511.11730) appear to be missing from the related work.
  - Response: We were unable to verify these specific identifiers correspond to directly competing multimodal spatial omics methods at the time of writing; if the reviewer can confirm these are accessible preprints, we will review and cite them. In the interim, our related-work section already distinguishes LATTICE from the closest existing lines (GraphST/STAGATE/SpaGCN for spatial graph SSL, SIMO/SSpMosaic/MaxFuse for single-cell-to-spot mapping, and SARSIM for regulatory projection), which we believe covers the relevant methodological neighbors currently verifiable to us.
  - Manuscript reference: Section 2, 'Spatial omics, multimodal integration, and graph self-supervision'
