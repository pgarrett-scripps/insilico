# Meta-Review

**Draft recommendation:** major

## Synthesis
The panel converges on a consistent picture: LATTICE is a competently engineered, honestly reported graph-SSL framework for multimodal spatial omics, but several of its headline claims outrun the evidence as presented. Six of eight reviewers recommend major revision; the two more favourable reports (clarity 4@4, ethics 4@5) are favourable on dimensions that do not touch the central inferential problem — and the ethics report, despite its 4, contains an explicit hard blocker.

What the panel agrees is genuinely good: the modality-ladder design (M1\isolating device praised even by the harshest reviewers — methodology, novelty, data_analysis all call it principled), the mathematical precision of Section 3 and Algorithm 1, the extensive implementation disclosure in Appendix H (hyperparameters, seeds, wall-clock, CPU-only feasibility), and — repeatedly noted — the authors' candour in reporting that ARI/NMI *decline* at M4–M5 and that cross-patient treatment signatures do not replicate. That candour should be credited, not penalised.

The genuine tension is over whether the remaining problems are text-fixable or require new work. The advocate frames matched-input baselines, paired significance tests and MUS justification as ordinary revision requests. The skeptic's counter survived contact: the methodology and rigor reviewers independently identify the same confound — every baseline in Table 2 runs on M1 only while LATTICE is credited across M1–M5, so the comparative claim cannot separate method contribution from input richness. Fixing that means re-running GraphST/STAGATE/SpaGCN/SIMO/MaxFuse on the M2–M5 feature matrices, i.e. new experiments. The second surviving critique concerns MUS: rigor, methodology, novelty and data_analysis all flag that a composite metric with unjustified equal weighting, no sensitivity analysis and no external validation is introduced at precisely the point where standard metrics decline, and is then used to reinterpret that decline as a virtue. Without a positive control or biological validation, 'captures chromatin structure beyond transcriptomics' and 'fits noisier epigenomic signal' are observationally equivalent under the evidence given.

The statistical reporting gap (data_analysis, conf 4) is a related, independently sufficient reason for major revision: the ± values in Table 2 have an undefined denominator (samples vs. seeds), no paired test supports M1→M2 'substantial improvement' or the M2→M5 decline, the ablation deltas in Table 3 carry no confidence intervals, 'leave-one-patient-out prediction is mixed' is unquantified, and the abstract's 'reproducible embeddings across analysis seeds' is never measured. Most of these are analyses on data the authors already hold, so they are tractable.

The ethics reviewer (highest confidence on the panel, 5) identifies a compliance gate rather than a scientific flaw: IRB committee name and protocol number are deferred to camera-ready, and no informed-consent statement (obtained, waived, or biobank-covered) appears anywhere. This must be supplied at revision, not after acceptance. It is curable by disclosure and does not by itself sink the paper.

Secondary but real: the literature reviewer (conf 3, appropriately lower weight) flags that SARSIM is cited as a 2026 preprint with no verifiable identifier while being load-bearing for M2–M3, and ReCAST is an undocumented internal pipeline generating M4–M5 — a reproducibility chain that reviewers cannot inspect. Several references carry impossible future access dates. The reproducibility reviewer adds that code is promised as supplementary material with no versioned URL, DOI or commit hash, and that procedural details ('log1p_then_zscore', 'union symmetrization', the Leiden resolution sweep, masking granularity, whether ablations are retrained) are named but not defined. Private clinical data is an acceptable constraint that reviewers correctly did not treat as a defect in itself; the absence of a citable code artifact is not.

Novelty is judged incremental over GraphST/STAGATE — the panel treats this as a positioning issue, not a disqualifier, but asks the authors to state plainly what LATTICE does that GraphST with concatenated multimodal input cannot.

## Decisive Factors
The recommendation matches the confidence-weighted signal (3.28/5, 6 of 8 for major) rather than diverging from it. Two things drive it. First, the confound identified concurrently by the methodology and rigor reviewers — baselines evaluated only on M1 while LATTICE is credited across M1–M5 — means the paper's central comparative claim is not currently attributable to the method, and correcting it requires new runs rather than rewriting. Second, MUS is doing load-bearing interpretive work at exactly the point where pre-existing metrics decline, and no positive control distinguishes signal from noise in the chromatin channels; several reviewers reached this independently and the advocate did not dislodge it. These are not grounds for rejection: the underlying architecture is sound, the modality-ladder experiment is well-designed, the reporting is unusually candid about its own negative results, and the required fixes (matched-input baselines, paired tests across the 11 samples, MUS weight sensitivity, a defined spatial-contiguity formula, quantified seed stability, a versioned code DOI) are all achievable with data and infrastructure the authors already have. The ethics gap (IRB identifiers, consent statement, funding disclosure) is a mandatory condition of any revised submission but is curable by disclosure and does not on its own determine the verdict. I did not let the two 4-scores pull this toward 'minor': the clarity 4 concerns presentation only, and the ethics 4 is accompanied by an explicit statement that the manuscript cannot proceed to acceptance as written. For a venue that publishes the referee record next to the preprint, the honest outcome is a public major-revision report naming exactly which claims currently outrun the evidence and what would settle them.
