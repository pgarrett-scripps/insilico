# Rigor & Overclaiming Reviewer

## Summary
LATTICE presents a graph-based self-supervised framework for multimodal spatial omics integration with a clear methodological contribution. However, the manuscript contains several load-bearing claims that are not fully supported by the evidence presented, particularly regarding the generalizability and biological utility of the approach. The abstract claims 'complete multimodal integration across all samples' and 'stable optimization behavior' without defining what these mean operationally or providing quantitative evidence. The core claim that adding chromatin/histone modalities improves spatial organization is contradicted by the authors' own finding that ARI and NMI decrease with M4–M5, and the proposed explanation (that embeddings 'capture chromatin and regulatory structure beyond transcriptomic similarity') is post-hoc rationalization rather than mechanistic evidence. The evaluation is limited to a single private 11-sample melanoma cohort with no external validation, yet claims about 'practical and empirically grounded framework' and positioning as a general paradigm are stated without acknowledging this narrow scope. The multimodal utility score (MUS) is introduced to rescue the narrative when standard metrics decline, raising concerns about metric selection and cherry-picking. Ablation studies are incomplete, and the theoretical analysis in Appendix I makes standard claims without novel insights.

## Strengths
- The method is technically sound: masked reconstruction, cross-modal alignment, and spatial regularization are well-motivated objectives with clear mathematical formulation (Eqs. 6–10).
- Honest reporting of metric trade-offs: the authors transparently show that ARI/NMI decrease with additional modalities (Table 2, Figure 2), rather than hiding this result.
- Modular design: LATTICE is explicitly positioned as compatible with different upstream integration strategies (SARSIM, ReCAST alternatives), which is a genuine practical advantage.
- Comprehensive implementation details: Appendix H provides sufficient architectural, hyperparameter, and optimization specifics to enable reproduction, including CPU-only execution details and wall-clock times.
- Appropriate use of hedging in places: the authors state 'likely because' when explaining the ARI/NMI decline (Abstract), and acknowledge in Section 4.3 that treatment signatures are 'patient-specific rather than universal.'

## Weaknesses
- HARD: Abstract claims 'complete multimodal integration across all samples' without defining what 'complete' means or providing quantitative evidence. No metric explicitly measures integration completeness. This is vague and unsupported as stated.
- HARD: Abstract states 'LATTICE demonstrated stable optimization behavior' without defining stability or citing evidence. Section 4.2 shows early stopping spans 36–63 epochs (Appendix G.2), but no formal stability metric (e.g., variance across seeds, convergence diagnostics) is reported. The claim is unsubstantiated.
- HARD: The core narrative claim—that adding chromatin and histone modalities 'improves spatial organization'—is contradicted by the authors' own data. Table 2 shows M4 and M5 have lower ARI (0.343, 0.329) and NMI (0.462, 0.450) than M2 (ARI 0.426, NMI 0.507). The authors then introduce MUS to show M4–M5 are 'better' on spatial contiguity and MUS, but this is post-hoc metric selection to rescue a declining result, not evidence that the modalities improve the embedding. The explanation that embeddings 'capture chromatin and regulatory structure beyond transcriptomic similarity alone' (Abstract) is speculation, not mechanistic evidence. This is a HARD overclaim.
- HARD: Generalization scope is not acknowledged. The entire evaluation is on a single private 11-sample melanoma cohort. The abstract and conclusion claim LATTICE is 'practical and empirically grounded' and 'positions LATTICE as a general paradigm for multimodal spatial omics integration' (Conclusion), but no external validation, no other tissue types, no other cancer types, and no public datasets are used. This violates the cross-cutting rule that broad claims ('in general', 'practical framework') must acknowledge their narrow evidence base. The authors do acknowledge 'the need for stronger supervision and broader external benchmarking' (Abstract), which is good, but the framing of LATTICE as a 'practical' and 'general' framework is premature.
- SOFT: The multimodal utility score (MUS, Eq. 11) is introduced only after standard metrics (ARI, NMI) show declining performance with additional modalities. While MUS is mathematically defined and transparent, its introduction appears motivated by the need to show M4–M5 are beneficial. The choice to average four normalized quantities without biological justification or prior validation is defensible but ad hoc. Why these four components? Why equal weighting? Why not use a single established metric?
- SOFT: Ablation study is incomplete. Table 3 removes spatial regularization, masking, and tests modality dropout at inference, but does not ablate the cross-modal alignment loss (Lalign). The authors state 'we do not report an additional appendix grid beyond these cohort summaries' (Appendix B.1), but this is a load-bearing component of the objective and its contribution is not isolated.
- SOFT: The claim that 'adding scMultiome RNA to Visium RNA alone substantially improved concordance' (Abstract) is supported by Table 2 (ARI +0.157, NMI +0.143), but the magnitude of improvement is not contextualized. Is ARI 0.426 considered 'good' clustering? How does this compare to typical spatial transcriptomics benchmarks? The term 'substantially' is interpretive without context.
- SOFT: Theoretical analysis (Appendix I) makes only standard claims (spatial smoothness via graph Laplacian, reconstruction via autoencoders, alignment via NCE). Lemmas I.1–I.3 are textbook results with 'proof sketches' that do not add novel insight. Theorem I.4 is a trivial consequence of the additive objective structure. This appendix does not strengthen the contribution.
- SOFT: Patient-level analysis (Section 4.3) reports that 'leave-one-patient-out prediction is mixed' and treatment signatures are patient-specific, but no quantitative results are shown. What does 'mixed' mean? What were the LOPO accuracies? This finding is important (it limits clinical utility) but is under-reported.
- SOFT: The role of ReCAST and SARSIM in producing the input tensors is acknowledged, but the contribution of LATTICE itself is unclear. How much of the improvement from M1 to M2 comes from SARSIM's regulatory projection versus LATTICE's graph SSL? This is not disentangled. The authors state 'LATTICE is modular with respect to multimodal feature construction' (Section 3.4), but the dependence on upstream pipelines is not quantified.

## Questions
- What is the operational definition of 'complete multimodal integration' in the abstract? Which metric or threshold establishes that all 11 samples achieved it?
- How is 'stable optimization behavior' measured? Are there convergence diagnostics, variance across random seeds, or other quantitative stability metrics beyond the reported early-stopping epoch range?
- The abstract and conclusion frame LATTICE as a 'general paradigm' for multimodal spatial omics, but evaluation is limited to melanoma. Do the authors plan external validation on other tissues, cancer types, or public datasets? If not, should the framing be narrowed to 'melanoma' or 'this cohort'?
- Why was MUS introduced? Was it pre-specified in a protocol, or was it designed post-hoc after observing that ARI/NMI declined with M4–M5? If post-hoc, this is metric selection and should be disclosed.
- In Table 3, why is the cross-modal alignment loss (Lalign) not ablated? This is a core component of the objective, and its isolated contribution is not shown.
- What are the quantitative results for leave-one-patient-out prediction (Section 4.3)? The text states results are 'mixed,' but no accuracies or confusion matrices are provided.
- How much of the M1→M2 improvement (ARI +0.157) is attributable to SARSIM's regulatory projection versus LATTICE's graph SSL? Can you disentangle these contributions?
- The spatial contiguity metric is not formally defined in the main text. Is it the fraction of same-cluster neighbors in the spatial kNN graph? Please clarify in the main text, not just Appendix A.2.
- Figure 3 shows pre/post-treatment separation by patient, but no statistical test is reported. Are the shifts significant? How were the PCA axes chosen?
- Figures 4–6 show results for Patient 1 only. Are these representative? How do Figures 4–6 compare to the other two paired patients?
