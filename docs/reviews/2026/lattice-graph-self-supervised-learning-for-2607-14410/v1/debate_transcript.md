# Debate Transcript

## Advocate — round 1

LATTICE makes a genuine, well-executed contribution: a unified graph-SSL encoder that ingests five harmonized spot-level blocks (Visium RNA, scMultiome RNA/ATAC, spatial ATAC, CUT&Tag) and jointly optimizes masked reconstruction, cross-modal alignment, and spatial smoothness (Eqs. 6,8,9). The clarity reviewer confirms the methods section is 'mathematically precise' with 'numbered equations' and Algorithm 1 'easy to follow and reproducible,' and even the harshest reviewers (methodology, novelty) call the modality-ladder design 'a principled way to isolate the contribution of successive modalities' with 'clear trends.' The M1→M2 result (ARI +0.157, NMI +0.143, spatial contiguity +0.174 across 11 samples) is a real, consistent, cohort-wide signal, not an isolated anecdote. Reviewers also credit the paper's honesty: it explicitly reports that ARI/NMI decline at M4–M5 rather than hiding it, and Section 4.3 candidly disclaims a universal treatment signature—rare candor that should count in favor of trustworthiness, not against acceptance. The reproducibility reviewer notes extensive artifacts: pinned dependencies, seeds, Slurm logs, and Appendix H hyperparameters sufficient 'to enable configuration replication for anyone with the input data.' Concerns about missing significance tests, MUS weighting justification, IRB identifiers, and baseline matching (same-input comparisons) are legitimate but are precisely the kind of well-scoped revision requests—add paired tests, clarify MUS appendix material in-text, disclose IRB protocol, run baselines on M2–M5—that a revision cycle is designed to fix, rather than fatal flaws invalidating the core contribution. The underlying architecture, objective design, and empirical ladder findings stand as a solid, reproducible foundation.

## Skeptic — round 1

The advocate concedes many issues are 'revision-fixable,' but two are not cosmetic — they undercut the paper's central claims right now. First, ethics: the ethics reviewer (conf 5) states plainly that IRB protocol/committee identifiers and an informed-consent statement are 'required disclosure before publication, not deferred to camera-ready' and that the manuscript 'cannot proceed to acceptance' without them. This isn't a clarity nit like renaming a table column — it's a compliance gate that blocks publication of human-subjects work as submitted. FATAL as currently written, though curable by disclosure. Second, and more damaging to the science: the methodology and rigor reviewers independently identify that the comparative claim — that LATTICE 'substantially improved concordance' and that chromatin modalities capture real biology — is confounded. Every baseline in Table 2 runs on M1 only, while LATTICE is credited across M1–M5; so we cannot attribute the ARI/NMI/contiguity gains to the *method* versus simply to *richer input features*. Worse, when the RNA-reference metrics decline at M4–M5, the paper introduces MUS — a metric with no external validation, arbitrary equal weighting, never sensitivity-tested — to declare the result a win rather than noise. As the methodology reviewer notes, there's no positive control ruling out that spatial ATAC/CUT&Tag are 'noisier, lower-resolution... and the method is fitting noise.' The advocate calls this 'well-scoped revision,' but fixing it requires new experiments (matched-input baselines, a validated ground-truth metric), not just added text — that's a substantive re-evaluation, not a rewrite. I concede the reproducibility artifacts and honest reporting are genuine strengths, but they don't rescue an unfalsifiable central claim.

**Key points:**
- IRB/consent disclosure gap is a hard compliance blocker per the ethics reviewer, not deferrable to camera-ready
- Baseline comparisons confound method contribution with input richness (all baselines run on M1 only, LATTICE credited across M1-M5)
- MUS is introduced post-hoc, unvalidated, equally-weighted, and used to reframe a decline in ARI/NMI as a virtue without ruling out noise as the explanation
- No positive control or biological validation distinguishes 'captures real chromatin structure' from 'fits noisier epigenomic signal'
- These require new experiments/disclosures, not just textual revision, distinguishing them from the paper's genuine clarity and reproducibility strengths
