# Novelty & Contribution Reviewer

## Summary
LATTICE applies established self-supervised learning objectives (masked reconstruction, cross-modal contrastive alignment, spatial regularization) to multimodal spatial omics data via graph neural networks. The work is technically sound and well-executed on a private 11-sample melanoma cohort, with honest reporting of modality-ladder trade-offs. However, the core novelty is incremental: it extends existing spatial graph SSL methods (GraphST, STAGATE) by concatenating multimodal features and adding cross-modal alignment, which are straightforward combinations of known techniques. The evaluation is limited to a single private cohort with no external validation, no public benchmarks, and no head-to-head comparison to other multimodal spatial methods on identical inputs. Upstream dependencies (SARSIM, ReCAST) are not published, limiting reproducibility. The positioning overstates the novelty relative to existing spatial graph methods that already integrate multiple signals.

## Strengths
- Honest and transparent reporting: the manuscript does not overstate gains and explicitly acknowledges that chromatin modalities reduce RNA-reference agreement (ARI/NMI) while improving spatial contiguity.
- Systematic modality-ladder evaluation (M1–M5) provides useful empirical guidance for practitioners on incremental value of adding modalities.
- Reproducibility artifacts: detailed hyperparameters (Appendix H), code, logs, and pinned dependencies provided; CPU-only training enables independent reproduction.
- Sensible combination of three SSL objectives: masked reconstruction preserves per-modality signal, cross-modal alignment ties views of the same spot, spatial regularization enforces local coherence.
- Theoretical appendix (Appendix I) provides intuition for why the combined objective works, even if the individual components are standard.

## Weaknesses
- Core novelty is incremental over GraphST/STAGATE: applying masked reconstruction, contrastive alignment, and spatial smoothness to concatenated multimodal features is a straightforward extension of existing spatial graph SSL methods, not a fundamental methodological advance. The manuscript does not clearly articulate what LATTICE does that GraphST cannot do with multimodal input.
- No external validation or public benchmarks: evaluation is limited to a single private 11-sample melanoma cohort (54,912 spots). No cross-dataset generalization, no public multimodal spatial omics benchmarks, no comparison to other multimodal methods on identical inputs.
- Incomplete baseline comparisons: Table 2 compares LATTICE M1–M5 to GraphST, STAGATE, SpaGCN, SIMO, and MaxFuse, but all baselines use M1 (Visium RNA only) or M1 + histology. No baseline is evaluated on the full M5 multimodal input, making it impossible to assess whether LATTICE's gains are due to the method or simply to having more input modalities.
- Upstream pipeline not published: SARSIM (reference [4], 'under review') and ReCAST (internal, Appendix F) are not available. LATTICE's contribution is isolated from these; independent reproduction requires reimplementing or obtaining these pipelines.
- Cross-modal alignment loss uses only two modalities: Equation 8 and Section H state that alignment is computed only between 'modality indices zero and one' (Visium RNA and spatial ATAC). Why not align all modality pairs? This design choice is not justified.
- Limited biological validation: Figures 4–6 show spatial domains and TF programs for one patient but do not validate whether learned embeddings capture regulatory structure beyond RNA. The divergence between ARI/NMI (which decrease with chromatin) and spatial contiguity/MUS (which increase) is noted but not explained—is the embedding learning true regulatory structure or just spatial smoothness?
- MUS metric introduced without prior validation: Equation 11 combines four normalized quantities (spatial contiguity, silhouette, same-cluster neighbor fraction, embedding–spatial kNN overlap). This composite metric is reasonable but not standard; no ablation shows which components drive improvements.
- Positioning overstates novelty: Abstract and introduction claim LATTICE addresses a 'key limitation' of lack of frameworks for multimodal spatial integration, but GraphST and STAGATE already integrate multiple signals (expression + spatial proximity). LATTICE's delta is the specific combination of objectives and multimodal concatenation, which is incremental.

## Questions
- How does LATTICE perform when GraphST, STAGATE, or SpaGCN are given the same M5 multimodal input? Table 2 compares baselines on M1 only; a fair comparison requires identical inputs.
- Why does the cross-modal alignment loss (Eq. 8) use only Visium RNA and spatial ATAC (modality indices 0 and 1)? Why not align all five modality pairs? Is this a design choice or a limitation?
- Can you provide ablations showing the isolated contribution of each objective (reconstruction alone, alignment alone, spatial regularization alone) on the full M5 input?
- What is the biological interpretation of the divergence between ARI/NMI (which decrease from M2 to M5) and spatial contiguity/MUS (which increase)? Does the embedding capture true regulatory structure, or is it learning spatial smoothness that happens to disagree with RNA-derived clusters?
- How sensitive is LATTICE to the hyperparameter choices (λ₁, λ₂, λ₃, masking ratio ρ, temperature τ)? Appendix D states no sensitivity curves are included; can these be provided?
- Can LATTICE be evaluated on public multimodal spatial omics datasets (e.g., 10x Genomics Visium + ATAC, or other published cohorts) to assess generalization beyond the private melanoma cohort?
- SARSIM (reference [4]) is cited as 'under review' and ReCAST is internal. When will these be available? Can LATTICE be evaluated independently of these pipelines, or are they essential dependencies?
