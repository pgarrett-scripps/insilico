# Decision Letter

**Decision:** major

## Summary of Evaluation
Eight referee reports converge on a consistent assessment (confidence-weighted 3.28/5; 6 of 8 recommending major revision): LATTICE is a carefully engineered, unusually candidly reported graph-SSL framework for multimodal spatial omics, whose architecture and modality-ladder design are praised even by the harshest reviewers, but whose headline comparative and interpretive claims currently outrun the evidence presented.

Two critiques are load-bearing and neither is resolvable by rewriting alone. First, the matched-input confound: every baseline in Table 2 is evaluated on M1 inputs while LATTICE is credited across M1-M5, so the comparative claim cannot separate method contribution from input richness. The authors concede this squarely and commit to re-running GraphST and STAGATE on M2/M5 concatenated features with the same spatial kNN graph — the right fix, but it is new computation, which places this beyond minor revision. The authors also state plainly that if this experiment cannot be completed within the window, the 'LATTICE outperforms baselines' framing cannot be upheld; I take that commitment seriously and it is written into the requirements below as a conditional.

Second, MUS. I weighed the rebuttal here and it partially succeeds. The authors quote Section 4.1 showing an a priori rationale (ARI/NMI presuppose an RNA-derived reference and cannot credit non-transcriptomic signal) and state that MUS was designed prospectively rather than in response to the M4-M5 decline. That is a reasonable defence against the strongest framing of 'post-hoc metric selection', and I am not asking the authors to remove MUS. What survives is narrower but still decisive: equal weighting is unjustified, no sensitivity analysis exists, SpotCut is never formally defined, and no positive control distinguishes 'captures chromatin structure' from 'fits noisier signal'. The authors concede the missing positive control and offer to soften causal language to 'consistent with' and add marker-gene enrichment across M1-M5. That is an acceptable route, and the softened claim is one the evidence can carry.

The statistical reporting gap (undefined or ununquantified 'mixed' LOPO results, unmeasured seed reproducibility despite an abstract claim) is independently sufficient for major revision, but is tractable on data already in hand; the authors' proposed paired Wilcoxon tests over n=11 with CIs address it directly.

On novelty, the rebuttal is credible: the reviewer's framing as 'SSL on concatenated features' is answered by the per-block masked reconstruction, cross-modal alignment and spatial term of Eq. 10, and the authors volunteer to soften overclaiming language. I do not treat incrementality as a barrier here, only positioning. On the missing 2025-2026 citations the literature reviewer flagged, the authors reasonably note they could not verify those identifiers; I am not requiring citation of works whose existence is not established.

The ethics report carries the panel's highest confidence and contains a compliance gate: IRB committee and protocol identifiers, and any informed-consent or waiver statement, are deferred to camera-ready. This is curable by disclosure but must be resolved at revision, not after acceptance. The compliance audits add three HARD methods gaps (no sequencing repository accession, no inclusion/exclusion criteria for the 11-of-14 retained samples, no cohort demographics) plus a set of soft/unverifiable items around code deposition, genome build, tool versions and the undocumented ReCAST pipeline. The data-restriction itself is an acceptable constraint honestly stated; the absence of a citable, versioned code artifact and of basic cohort descriptors is not, and both are fixable.

The verdict tracks the panel signal rather than departing from it. The two 4-scores do not pull this toward minor: clarity concerns presentation only, and the ethics 4 is accompanied by an explicit statement that the manuscript cannot proceed as written. Rejection is not warranted — the architecture is sound, the ladder experiment well designed, the negative results reported honestly, and every required fix is achievable with data and infrastructure the authors already hold.

## Required Revisions
1. Matched-input baselines. Re-run at least GraphST and STAGATE on the M2 and M5 concatenated feature matrices using the same spatial kNN graph and preprocessing as LATTICE, and report them in an expanded Table 2 with an explicit method-vs-input decomposition. If any baseline cannot be run on the richer inputs, say so and explain why. If these runs cannot be completed, the abstract, introduction and discussion must be rewritten so that no claim of superiority over baselines is made — the comparison should then be presented explicitly as method-plus-input versus method-on-M1.

## Minor Suggestions
- State plainly, in one or two sentences in the introduction or related work, what LATTICE does that GraphST or STAGATE with concatenated multimodal input cannot — the rebuttal's answer (per-block masked reconstruction targets, cross-modal alignment between assay views of the same spot, modality-specific adapters into a shared encoder) is convincing and belongs in the paper.
- Soften 'key limitation' framing of prior work in the introduction, per the authors' own offer, so positioning matches the incremental-but-real nature of the contribution.
- Move the compact 'modality block' definition and a ReCAST-vs-SARSIM division-of-labour table to Section 1, immediately after the modality ladder is introduced.
- Add foundational masked-autoencoder and multimodal contrastive citations (MAE, BERT, SimCLR/CLIP) alongside the NCE reference, and attribute the masked-reconstruction objective correctly — the audit notes NCE is currently the only cited source and it supports the alignment loss, not reconstruction.
- Provide a data-access mechanism (contact point, DTA template, or governance route) alongside the existing statement that the cohort cannot be redistributed, so that the restriction is documented rather than absolute.
- Report sequencing-level metadata where available (platform, read configuration, depth) and the reference genome build, or state clearly which of these were fixed upstream by the collaborating institution and are unknown to the authors.
- Consider noting whether any batch correction across samples or modality blocks was applied; the audit found this unaddressed.
