# Methodology Reviewer

## Summary
LATTICE presents a graph-based self-supervised learning framework for multimodal spatial omics integration with a reasonable methodological foundation, but the design has significant limitations in supporting its main claims. The core issue is that the evaluation relies on a single private cohort (11 samples, 54,912 spots) with no external validation, making it impossible to assess generalizability. More critically, the comparison to baselines is not conducted under matched conditions: baselines use only M1 (Visium RNA) while LATTICE is evaluated across M1–M5, conflating the contribution of additional modalities with the contribution of the method itself. The modality-ladder design (M1–M5) is clever but does not isolate the method's contribution from the data contribution. The ablation study (Table 3) addresses training components but not the core methodological claim. The introduction of a custom metric (MUS) without external validation, combined with the observation that adding chromatin modalities reduces agreement with RNA-derived reference labels, raises questions about whether the learned embeddings capture meaningful biological structure or noise. The manuscript is honest about limitations but the design does not adequately support the claim that LATTICE is a 'practical and empirically grounded framework' for multimodal integration.

## Strengths
- The modality-ladder design (M1–M5) is a principled way to isolate the contribution of successive modalities and shows clear trends (Table 2, Figure 2).
- The ablation study (Table 3) systematically removes training components (spatial regularization, masking) and tests robustness to missing modalities at inference time, supporting claims about which objectives matter.
- The manuscript is transparent about limitations: it acknowledges that ARI/NMI decrease with additional modalities (Section 4.2), that cross-patient treatment signatures are not consistent (Section 4.3), and that external benchmarking is needed (Conclusion).
- Reproducibility artifacts are well documented: fixed hyperparameters (Appendix H), compute resources (Appendix G.2), and code/logs are provided as supplementary material.
- The theoretical analysis (Appendix I) provides formal justification for why the three loss components (reconstruction, alignment, spatial smoothness) should work together, grounding the design in spectral graph theory.

## Weaknesses
- HARD: Baseline comparison is not conducted under matched conditions. Table 2 compares LATTICE M1 against GraphST, STAGATE, SpaGCN, SIMO, and MaxFuse, all using M1 (Visium RNA only), but then reports LATTICE M2–M5 with progressively richer inputs. This conflates two variables: (i) the LATTICE method itself, and (ii) the addition of scMultiome RNA, ATAC, spatial ATAC, and CUT&Tag. To isolate the method's contribution, baselines should be re-run on M2–M5 inputs using the same feature matrix and graph construction. Without this, the claim that 'LATTICE demonstrated stable optimization behavior' and 'substantially improved concordance' (Abstract) cannot be attributed to the method rather than to the additional modalities. The authors acknowledge that M1 to M2 shows 'the largest change' (Section 4.2), but do not test whether other methods would also improve with M2 inputs.
- HARD: No external validation cohort. The entire evaluation rests on a single private 11-sample melanoma cohort. The authors state 'the raw data cannot be publicly released because of institutional data-use and privacy restrictions' (Section 4.1) and 'there is no public five-modality substitute at this lattice resolution' (Appendix G.1). This makes it impossible for readers to independently verify results or assess whether findings generalize beyond melanoma or this specific data-generation pipeline (ReCAST + SARSIM). For a method paper claiming practical utility, at least one external cohort or a synthetic benchmark with known ground truth would strengthen the claim.
- HARD: The custom metric MUS is not validated against independent ground truth or external benchmarks. MUS (Eq. 11) averages four normalized quantities (spatial contiguity, silhouette, same-cluster spatial-neighbor fraction, embedding k-NN overlap). While the components are reasonable, the metric is introduced without justification for why these four terms should be equally weighted, why this particular combination captures 'multimodal utility,' or how it relates to downstream biological discovery. The fact that MUS increases with M4–M5 while ARI/NMI decrease suggests MUS may be rewarding embeddings that diverge from transcriptomic organization without evidence that the divergence is biologically meaningful. No comparison to other multimodal integration quality metrics (e.g., from the single-cell integration literature) is provided.
- HARD: The claim that chromatin and histone features improve spatial organization is confounded with the possibility that they introduce noise or capture non-biological variation. Section 4.2 states: 'ARI and NMI decrease relative to M2 because the embedding no longer follows the RNA reference alone, while spatial contiguity and MUS increase.' This is presented as evidence that M4–M5 capture 'chromatin and regulatory structure beyond transcriptomic similarity alone.' However, an equally plausible explanation is that spatial ATAC and CUT&Tag signals are noisier, lower-resolution, or less biologically informative than RNA, and the method is fitting noise. The design does not include a positive control (e.g., simulated data where the ground-truth chromatin signal is known to be informative) or a biological validation (e.g., enrichment of known regulatory elements in the learned embeddings) to rule out this alternative.
- SOFT: The cross-modal alignment loss (Eq. 8) is applied only to 'selected modality pairs' (Appendix H: 'modality indices zero and one correspond to Visium RNA and spatial ATAC'). The manuscript does not justify why only these two modalities are aligned, or whether aligning other pairs (e.g., scMultiome RNA with spatial ATAC, or CUT&Tag with RNA) would improve results. This is a design choice that is not ablated.
- SOFT: Reproducibility across analysis seeds is reported as 'reproducible embeddings across analysis seeds' (Abstract) but the quantitative evidence is limited. Section 4.3 mentions 'leave-one-patient-out prediction is mixed' and Appendix H states that 'joint multisample analysis uses seed 7 and reports stability summaries over 11 analysis seeds {7, 11, 19, 23, 29, 31, 37, 41, 43, 47, 53},' but no table or figure shows the variance in embeddings or downstream metrics across these seeds. The standard deviations in Table 2 may reflect seed variation, but this is not explicitly stated.
- SOFT: The spatial graph construction uses k=6 nearest neighbors (Appendix H) without justification or sensitivity analysis. The choice of k affects the graph structure and thus the spatial regularization loss. A brief ablation or justification (e.g., 'k=6 was chosen to match typical Visium spot density') would strengthen the design.

## Questions
- Why were baselines not re-run on M2–M5 inputs to isolate the contribution of the LATTICE method from the contribution of additional modalities? Can the authors provide a comparison where all methods (GraphST, STAGATE, SpaGCN, SIMO, MaxFuse) are applied to the same M2–M5 feature matrix and graph as LATTICE?
- Can the authors validate MUS against an independent ground truth or external benchmark? For example, do embeddings with higher MUS scores better predict downstream biological outcomes (e.g., treatment response, cell type, disease subtype) in a held-out validation set?
- The decrease in ARI/NMI with M4–M5 is attributed to the embeddings capturing 'chromatin and regulatory structure beyond transcriptomic similarity.' What evidence supports this interpretation over the alternative that spatial ATAC and CUT&Tag signals are noisier or less informative? Can the authors provide a positive control (e.g., simulated data or enrichment analysis) to validate that the learned embeddings capture known regulatory relationships?
- Why is cross-modal alignment applied only to Visium RNA and spatial ATAC (Appendix H)? Have the authors tested aligning other modality pairs, and if so, what were the results?
- Can the authors provide quantitative evidence for 'reproducible embeddings across analysis seeds' (Abstract)? Table 2 reports standard deviations, but are these across seeds, samples, or both? A figure showing embedding stability across the 11 analysis seeds would clarify this claim.
- Is there a public or synthetic benchmark dataset with known ground truth that could be used to validate LATTICE on external data? If not, can the authors discuss plans to release a subset of the melanoma cohort or create a synthetic multimodal spatial dataset for community benchmarking?
- How sensitive are the results to the choice of k in the k-NN graph construction? Was k=6 chosen based on prior work, Visium spot density, or empirical tuning? An ablation across k ∈ {3, 6, 10, 15} would strengthen the robustness claims.
- The manuscript states that SARSIM and ReCAST are 'internal engineering pipelines' (Section 1). How much of the improvement from M1 to M2–M5 is attributable to SARSIM/ReCAST preprocessing versus the LATTICE method itself? Can the authors quantify the contribution of each stage?
