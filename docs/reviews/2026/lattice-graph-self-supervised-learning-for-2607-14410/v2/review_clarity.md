# Clarity & Presentation Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript's central contribution—a framework for multimodal spatial omics integration—is ambitious and potentially valuable. However, its presentation is critically undermined by ambiguous, circular definitions and a failure to clearly distinguish between the authors' novel method (LATTICE) and the pre-existing upstream pipelines (ReCAST, SARSIM) that supply its data. This creates a HARD clarity issue where a reader cannot determine what LATTICE itself *does* versus what it *consumes*. The narrative flow is further disrupted by extensive, premature detail about the evaluation cohort and modality ladder before the method is fully introduced. These structural flaws make it impossible for a competent reader to reliably follow the argument or understand exactly what was done and claimed.

## Strengths
- The manuscript explicitly defines a "modality ladder" (M1-M5), providing a concrete framework for evaluating incremental integration.
- The abstract and introduction clearly state the goal of learning unified spot-level representations from harmonized multimodal features.
- The authors attempt to position their work relative to existing methods like GraphST and SARSIM.

## Weaknesses
- Circular Definition of LATTICE's Inputs and Scope. The manuscript repeatedly defines LATTICE's inputs in terms of outputs from other frameworks (ReCAST, SARSIM), but never clarifies what distinguishes LATTICE's *learning* from the *integration* already performed upstream. For example, the abstract states LATTICE "integrates five aligned modality blocks," but Section 4.1 clarifies these blocks are *already harmonized and aligned* by ReCAST and SARSIM. The text then says LATTICE "assembles" these blocks, which is ambiguous: does it perform a new integration, or simply concatenate pre-integrated features? The central claim that LATTICE is a framework for "multimodal spatial omics integration" is therefore unclear. A reader cannot tell if LATTICE's contribution is the *learning algorithm* applied to already-integrated data, or if it includes the integration pipeline itself. This is a HARD ambiguity.
- Unclear Narrative and Premature Results. The manuscript's structure violates the principle that the reader should understand the question before the results arrive. Figure 1 and the modality ladder (M1-M5) are introduced in the abstract and Section 1, and detailed cohort statistics (Table 1) appear in Section 4.1, all *before* the LATTICE method is described in Section 3. This forces the reader to evaluate results (e.g., "adding scMultiome RNA... improved concordance") without yet knowing how those results were generated by the novel method. This disrupts the motivation→methods→results flow, creating a SOFT but significant friction.
- Figure 1 caption references "multimodal feature construction" and a "fusion step," but the main text does not explicitly describe this step, leaving its mechanics ambiguous.
- The acronym "MUS" (Multimodal Utility Score) is defined in Section 4.1 but used in the abstract and Figure 2 caption before its full definition, causing a minor SOFT hiccup.
- Section 3.1 states "In our cohort B = 5," but the reader must infer this from the earlier modality ladder description; a direct cross-reference would improve clarity.
- The phrase "ReCAST-backed M4 through M5" in the Figure 2 caption assumes the reader has internalized the upstream pipeline roles, which is a SOFT burden given the core ambiguity.
- The methods readability suffers because the order of operations for LATTICE proper (Algorithm 1) depends on the undescribed earlier steps of "assembling" the five modality blocks, a HARD issue.
- The abstract claims LATTICE demonstrated "complete multimodal integration across all samples," a strong quantitative claim that is not directly evidenced by the presented metrics (ARI, NMI, MUS), which show trade-offs, not completeness.

## Questions
- Please clarify the specific, novel operation performed by LATTICE that occurs *after* the five modality blocks have been harmonized and aligned by ReCAST/SARSIM and *before* the graph SSL training begins. Is it solely the concatenation and graph construction described in Sections 3.1-3.2?
- Can you provide a succinct, standalone description of the LATTICE framework that does not rely on the names or outputs of ReCAST or SARSIM, to clearly delineate its scope?
- For the sake of narrative clarity, could the manuscript be restructured so that the LATTICE method (Section 3) is introduced *before* the detailed evaluation setup and cohort description (Section 4.1)?
