# Methodology Reviewer

## Summary
The methodological design is ambitious in its multimodal integration but contains several critical confounds that prevent its central comparative claims from being fully earned. The primary issue is that the "modality ladder" comparison does not isolate the effect of adding new data from the effect of changing the underlying integration model's suitability. Furthermore, the evaluation against an RNA-only reference creates a fundamental tension when assessing chromatin-informed embeddings, and the chosen baselines are not tuned or applied in a manner that ensures a fair comparison. The core idea is sound, but the experimental design needs significant restructuring to support the stated conclusions.

## Strengths
- The framework elegantly combines established self-supervised objectives (masked reconstruction, contrastive alignment, spatial smoothing) into a unified graph learning approach for a novel data structure.
- The ablation study (Table 3) cleanly isolates the contribution of individual training components (spatial reg, masking) when the input is fixed.
- The manuscript is transparent about the patient-specific nature of treatment responses, avoiding an overgeneralized claim.

## Weaknesses
- Load-bearing: The "modality ladder" (M1-M5) confounds input data with model suitability. The central claim is that adding modalities (e.g., moving from M1 to M2) improves performance. However, LATTICE is a multimodal model. Its architecture and loss functions (especially cross-modal alignment) are designed for and likely *require* multiple modalities to function optimally. When evaluated on M1 (Visium RNA only), it is being asked to perform a task it was not designed for, using a loss (`L_align`) that has no meaningful signal. Its poor performance on M1 (Table 2) is therefore an expected artefact of the model design, not evidence that M1 is a weak baseline. The observed "gain" from M1 to M2 is thus conflated with the model simply moving into its intended operating regime. A fair test would require comparing LATTICE (trained on M5) against a model *specifically designed and tuned for unimodal data* (like GraphST) on the M1 task, and against other multimodal models on the M5 task. The current design cannot support the claim that "adding projected scMultiome RNA to Visium RNA alone substantially improved" outcomes; the improvement may simply be the model working as intended once given multimodal data.
- Load-bearing: Evaluation against an RNA-only reference (Space Ranger) creates a circular tension for assessing chromatin integration. The manuscript correctly notes that ARI/NMI "can undervalue embeddings that capture chromatin or histone structure beyond transcriptomic similarity" and thus introduces the MUS. However, it then repeatedly uses drops in ARI/NMI when adding chromatin data (M4, M5) as a negative outcome to be explained away, rather than treating it as a predictable and perhaps even desirable result of successful multimodal integration. If the goal is to learn a representation that harmonizes transcriptomic and epigenomic signals, perfect agreement with a transcriptome-only clustering is not the target. The design lacks a "ground truth" or orthogonal validation for what a *good* integrated multimodal embedding should look like, making it impossible to determine whether a drop in ARI is a failure of integration or a success in capturing new, relevant biological signal. The MUS, while a step forward, is a composite of internal metrics and does not resolve this fundamental construct validity issue.
- Baseline comparisons are not equivalently tuned or applied. The baselines (GraphST, STAGATE, etc.) are run on M1 only. It is unclear if they were given the same preprocessed feature matrix (log1p_then_zscore on the intersected genes) as LATTICE-M1, or if they used their own standard pipelines. More critically, these models have hyperparameters (e.g., graph construction parameters, clustering resolutions) that significantly affect outcomes. The manuscript states "External methods use the same graph construction and K-matching procedure as LATTICE with a tractable Leiden resolution sweep," but this is insufficient. For a fair benchmark, each baseline must be *optimized* for the task on this specific dataset (e.g., a hyperparameter sweep for each baseline to maximize its ARI or silhouette), not just have a single configuration applied. The reported performance differences may therefore reflect suboptimal tuning of baselines rather than LATTICE's superiority.
- The claim of "complete multimodal integration across all samples" is not tested by the design. The metrics show the model runs and produces embeddings, but "integration" implies a meaningful fusion where each modality informs the other. The ablation (Table 3) shows that removing chromatin channels at test time doesn't catastrophically fail, but this tests robustness, not integration quality.
- The "joint PCA" in Figure 3 is presented as evidence of capturing patient-specific treatment shifts, but PCA is a descriptive, post-hoc visualization. The design lacks a quantitative, controlled analysis to test whether the observed separation is greater than that expected by chance or batch effect.
- The replication of the key modality-ladder result across 11 samples is a strength for robustness, but the internal replication (11 analysis seeds) is on the same underlying data partitions and does not address external validity.
- The use of projected single-cell data (scMultiome RNA/ATAC) introduces a potential confound: the quality of these features is entirely dependent on the upstream SARSIM mapping. Variability or errors in this projection become a nuisance variable affecting LATTICE's performance, but this is not controlled for or measured in the design.

## Questions
- To address weakness #1, could you provide a controlled experiment: Train a *unimodal* variant of LATTICE (e.g., by removing the cross-modal alignment loss and adapting the architecture for a single feature block) on M1, and compare its performance to LATTICE-M1 and GraphST? This would isolate the effect of model architecture from the effect of added data.
- For weakness #2, besides the MUS, is there any orthogonal biological validation that the embeddings from M4/M5 capture meaningful, spatially coherent chromatin states that are *not* simply redundant with RNA? For instance, do the Leiden clusters from M5 embeddings show enrichment for distinct chromatin accessibility peaks or histone marks, independent of gene expression?
- Regarding weakness #3, please provide a supplementary table showing the performance of each baseline (GraphST, STAGATE, etc.) after a hyperparameter optimization sweep (e.g., over graph k, clustering resolution) on the M1 data, to confirm that the reported differences are not due to suboptimal baseline configuration.
