# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
This manuscript presents a single-cell top-down proteomics (SC-TDP) method applied to 13 individual cardiomyocytes, reporting identification of 57 proteins and 165 proteoforms with claimed cell-to-cell heterogeneity. From a statistical and data-analysis perspective, the work is largely descriptive and exploratory rather than hypothesis-driven, which is appropriate for a methods paper. However, several quantitative claims lack sufficient statistical rigor: the heterogeneity claim rests on visual inspection of overlap heatmaps without formal statistical testing; reproducibility is assessed via overlap percentages without defining the metric or accounting for sampling variability; and no power analysis or sample-size justification is provided for the n=13 cells. The bulk-sample validation (n=3 replicates) shows reasonable reproducibility, but the single-cell claims about heterogeneity are not formally tested. The work is technically sound and the descriptive findings are valuable, but the statistical support for the central claim of "substantial heterogeneity" is weaker than the language suggests.

## Strengths
- Bulk-sample validation (n=3 replicates) demonstrates reproducible detection of core proteoforms (MLC-2, ATP5ME) with consistent retention times across runs, supporting method robustness.
- Overlap analysis (Figures 2A–B) is presented transparently: proteins show high overlap (conserved core proteome), while proteoforms show lower overlap, visually supporting the heterogeneity claim.
- Technical reproducibility is addressed: Figure S4 quantifies overlap across bulk replicates, and the authors acknowledge stochastic sampling and undersampling as sources of discrepancy rather than claiming perfect reproducibility.
- Sample size (n=13 cells) is reasonable for a proof-of-concept single-cell method paper, and cell-size control (61.04–67.92 µm) reduces a potential confound.
- Data availability stated (figshare) and raw MS data processing pipeline clearly described (ProSight PD 4.5, three-node search strategy, 1% FDR threshold).

## Weaknesses
- The central claim of 'substantial heterogeneity' (Abstract, Results) is supported only by visual inspection of overlap heatmaps (Figures 2A–B) and descriptive statistics (e.g., 'highest number of proteins (36) and proteoforms (70)' in one cell vs. 'lowest (14 proteins and 18 proteoforms)' in another). No formal statistical test (e.g., variance comparison, clustering analysis, or permutation test) is applied to quantify heterogeneity or test whether observed variation exceeds technical noise. The claim 'proteoform composition varied substantially among cells' (Abstract) is not statistically tested.
- Reproducibility metrics are reported as overlap percentages (Figure 2, Figure S4) but the denominator and calculation method are not explicitly defined. For example, Figure 2B states 'proteoforms were compared based solely on protein descriptions and associated modifications, so the identification numbers may not represent the total number of proteoforms listed in Table S2'—this caveat undermines the quantitative claim. How is overlap calculated when the same proteoform may be detected in some cells but not others due to stochastic sampling?
- No power analysis or sample-size justification is provided. With n=13 cells, the study is underpowered to detect modest effect sizes or to claim 'no difference' in proteoform composition between cells. The authors do not discuss whether n=13 is sufficient to characterize the population-level heterogeneity or whether additional cells would change conclusions.
- The bulk-sample validation uses n=3 technical replicates (not biological replicates), which is appropriate for assessing instrument/method reproducibility but does not validate the biological relevance of the single-cell findings. No biological replicates (e.g., cells from different animals or different heart regions) are analyzed.
- Multiple comparisons issue: The authors report 'expanding the delta search to 200 Da revealed many previously unknown structural deviations' and identify 165 proteoforms across 13 cells. No multiple-testing correction (e.g., FDR control) is mentioned for the proteoform search or for claims about novel modifications (e.g., 'first evidence of MLC-2 trimethylation'). The 1% FDR threshold applies to the database search, but it is unclear whether this controls the family-wise error rate across all 165 proteoforms or only within each cell.
- The claim that 'the highest number of proteins (36) and proteoforms (70) were observed in the cardiomyocyte measuring 67.92 µm while the lowest numbers (14 proteins and 18 proteoforms) were detected in the cell 64.26 µm' (Results) is presented without statistical analysis. Is this variation consistent with random sampling, or does it suggest a true biological or technical effect of cell size? A correlation analysis or regression model would strengthen this observation.
- Figure 1 (Total Ion Chromatograms) shows qualitative reproducibility across 13 cells but does not quantify peak intensity, retention-time precision, or signal-to-noise ratios. No error bars, confidence intervals, or statistical summary is provided.
- The paper does not address whether the observed proteoform heterogeneity could be explained by technical factors (e.g., cell lysis efficiency, protein extraction bias, stochastic sampling in MS/MS) versus true biological heterogeneity. A comparison with a negative control (e.g., repeated analysis of the same cell lysate) would help disentangle these sources of variation.

## Questions
- How is 'overlap' quantified in Figures 2A–B? Specifically, what is the denominator (union or intersection of detected proteoforms across cells), and how are proteoforms matched when they differ in PTM localization or truncation position?
- The authors state that 'proteoform identifications exhibit greater overlap than proteoform identifications' (Results, discussing Figure S4). Did you mean 'protein identifications exhibit greater overlap than proteoform identifications'? Please clarify.
- Was a formal statistical test (e.g., variance ratio test, permutation test, or clustering analysis) applied to test whether the observed proteoform heterogeneity is statistically significant, or is the claim based solely on visual inspection of the heatmaps?
- How was n=13 cells chosen? Was a power analysis performed to determine the sample size needed to detect a given effect size or to characterize population-level heterogeneity with a specified precision?
- In Table S2, why do cells 67.92 µm and 64.26 µm differ so dramatically in protein and proteoform counts (36 vs. 14 proteins; 70 vs. 18 proteoforms)? Is this variation consistent with random sampling, or does it suggest a biological or technical effect? A sensitivity analysis or correlation with cell size would clarify this.
- The 1% FDR threshold is applied to the database search, but does this control the false discovery rate across all 165 proteoforms identified in the study, or only within each individual cell? If the latter, what is the family-wise error rate across all cells and all proteoforms?
- The authors report 'first evidence of MLC-2 trimethylation' and 'first evidence of Qcr7 succinylation' in single cardiomyocytes. How many cells were required to observe each of these modifications, and is the frequency consistent with random sampling or biological variation?
- Were any exclusion criteria applied to the 13 cells (e.g., based on cell viability, morphology, or proteoform count) before or after analysis? If post-hoc exclusions were made, please disclose the original sample size and the exclusion criteria.
- The bulk-sample validation uses n=3 technical replicates from a single animal. Were biological replicates (e.g., cells from different animals or different heart regions) analyzed to assess inter-individual variation?
