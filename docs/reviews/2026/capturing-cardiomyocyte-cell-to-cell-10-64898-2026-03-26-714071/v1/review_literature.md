# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript presents a novel single-cell top-down proteomics method applied to cardiomyocytes with generally sound citation practices and accurate historical framing of the field. Key methodological references (EThcD fragmentation, ProSight PD) are appropriately cited, and the reference list is complete with resolvable DOIs. However, there are significant gaps in coverage of directly relevant recent work on single-cell cardiac proteomics, particularly Marín-Vicente et al. (2026), Hu et al. (2026), and Gao et al. (2025), which bear directly on the novelty claims. Additionally, claims about "first evidence" of MLC-2 trimethylation and MYL3 trimethylation lack proper literature support to establish novelty scope. These omissions and unsupported claims constitute hard issues requiring substantive revision to properly contextualize the contribution within the current literature.

## Strengths
- Accurate historical framing of the field's progression from Valaskovic et al. (1996) erythrocyte work through scPiMS (Su et al. 2025) and recent CE-MS platforms (Johnson et al. 2022) with proper citations.
- Key methodological references present and appropriate, including EThcD fragmentation strategy (Gomes et al. 2019, 2020) and ProSight PD software citations that support technical claims.
- Cardiac-relevant citations verified, such as Ali et al. (2020) on succinylation in failing cardiac myofibrils (PMID:31836543), which directly supports discussion of succinylation as a potential biomarker in heart failure.
- Complete reference list with all cited works resolvable via DOIs; no fabricated, garbled, or obviously false citations detected.
- Proportionate self-citation of authors' prior work (Gomes et al. 2019, 2020, 2025) to establish EThcD methodology without excessive padding.

## Weaknesses
- Incomplete coverage of directly competing single-cell cardiac proteomics work: the manuscript omits Marín-Vicente et al. (2026, Genome Biology, PMID:42174693) on pro-regenerative fingerprints in cardiomyocytes, Hu et al. (2026, Proteomics, PMID:42148543) on cardiac fibrosis proteomics, and Gao et al. (2025, Circulation: Heart Failure, PMID:41277423) on proteoform alterations in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, all of which directly address cardiomyocyte heterogeneity and proteoform analysis.
- Claim about "first evidence" of MLC-2 trimethylation in single cardiomyocytes lacks literature support establishing whether this modification has been observed in bulk samples or other cell types, and the scope of novelty (first in single cells vs. first ever) is not clarified.
- Claim about "first identification of MLC-2 concurrently harboring trimethylation and phosphorylation" is unsupported by citations establishing whether either modification alone has been previously reported in MLC-2.
- Missing citation or literature search for MYL3 trimethylation novelty claim; Figure S10 shows detection but no comparison to prior literature establishes novelty.
- Recent succinylation literature in cardiac disease (Jiang et al. 2026 on ATP5A1 succinylation in heart failure; Grzeczka et al. 2026 on SIRT5 and cardiac health) is not cited, suggesting the field is rapidly evolving beyond the statement that succinylation's role in heart failure is "poorly understood."
- Limited engagement with proteoform biomarker literature; discussion cites only Ali et al. (2020) and Tiambeng et al. (2019) on cardiac troponin proteoforms, missing recent work like Gao et al. (2025) on hypertrophic cardiomyopathy proteoforms.
- Incomplete discussion of how top-down proteomics fully resolves the protein inference problem; no detailed citation of protein inference literature (e.g., Nesvizhskii & Aebersold reviews) to support claims about this advantage.

## Questions
- Please provide a literature search or statement confirming whether MLC-2 trimethylation, MLC-2 T51 phosphorylation, and MYL3 trimethylation have been reported in any prior study (bulk or single-cell), and clarify the scope of novelty (first in single cells vs. first ever reported).
- How does your SC-TDP approach compare to the bottom-up single-cell proteomics methods used in Marín-Vicente et al. (2026) and Hu et al. (2026) for detecting cardiomyocyte heterogeneity, and does SC-TDP reveal proteoform-level heterogeneity that bottom-up methods miss?
- The manuscript identifies 57 proteins and 165 proteoforms across 13 cells; how does this compare to the proteoform coverage reported in Gao et al. (2025) on cardiomyopathy proteoforms, and does your method detect a similar or distinct set of cardiac-relevant proteoforms?
- Given recent literature on succinylation in cardiac disease (Jiang et al. 2026, Grzeczka et al. 2026), can you provide preliminary functional insight into the Qcr7 K11 succinylation you detected, or is this purely descriptive at this stage?
- Does your three-node search strategy (complete sequences, known proteoforms, truncated proteoforms) fully eliminate ambiguity in proteoform assignment, or are there edge cases where multiple proteoforms could explain the same MS/MS spectrum?
