# Methodology Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript presents a single-cell top-down proteomics (SC-TDP) workflow applied to 13 individual cardiomyocytes and claims to reveal cell-to-cell proteoform heterogeneity. The methodological design is generally sound in its technical execution—the LC-MS workflow is well-optimized, controls are present (protein standards, bulk tissue replicates), and the data acquisition strategy (EThcD + HCD) is justified. However, the design has a critical limitation: the claim of 'cell-to-cell heterogeneity' rests on a small, non-random sample of 13 cells from a single mouse, with no independent biological replicates (different animals), no randomization, and no statistical framework to distinguish true heterogeneity from technical sampling variance or stochastic MS effects. The authors acknowledge that 'stochastic sampling and undersampling effects' explain proteoform overlap discrepancies (Figure S4), yet do not formally separate this noise from genuine biological variation. The comparison to bulk tissue (41 proteins, 83 proteoforms) versus single cells (57 proteins, 165 proteoforms across 13 cells) is descriptive, not controlled—no paired design isolates the contribution of cell-level biology from detection sensitivity differences. These are SOFT-to-HARD design gaps that prevent strong causal claims about heterogeneity but do not invalidate the technical feasibility demonstration.

## Strengths
- Robust technical optimization: the lysis buffer (TFE + DMSO) is justified with protein standards (Figure S1) and validated on bulk tissue in triplicate (Figure S2, Table S1), demonstrating reproducible chromatography and proteoform detection.
- Appropriate positive controls: detection of MLC-2 trimethylation and ATP5ME consistently across all 13 cells and bulk tissue (Figures 1, S2) provides internal validation that the workflow reliably captures expected proteoforms.
- High-resolution fragmentation strategy: the combined EThcD + HCD approach is well-motivated (EThcD superior per Figure S6, HCD added to reduce duty cycle), and diagnostic ions (e.g., b₅₃, c₅₉ for MLC-2 T51 phosphorylation, Figure 3D) are validated with TDValidator, supporting precise PTM localization.
- Comprehensive data reporting: raw spectra available via figshare, detailed methods (nano-LC conditions, MS parameters, data analysis pipeline with 1% FDR filtering), and supplementary tables (S1, S2) enable reproducibility of the technical workflow.
- Biological relevance: identified proteoforms (MLC-2, ATP5ME, COX6c, Qcr7) are cardiac-relevant, and novel modifications (MLC-2 trimethylation + phosphorylation co-occurrence, MYL3 trimethylation, Qcr7 succinylation) are plausible biomarkers.

## Weaknesses
- HARD: No independent biological replicates (different animals). All 13 cells derive from a single mouse heart. This violates the basic design principle for claiming cell-to-cell heterogeneity as a biological phenomenon—a single animal cannot establish whether variation is intrinsic to cardiomyocytes or idiosyncratic to that individual. The authors state 'we limited our cells to be approximately the same size to reduce any impact due to differences in starting proteoform quantities' (Methods), but this is a confound control, not a biological replicate.
- HARD: No statistical framework to distinguish stochastic MS sampling noise from true biological heterogeneity. The authors acknowledge 'stochastic sampling and undersampling effects inherent to MS/MS fragmentation' explain proteoform overlap discrepancies (Figure S4 caption), yet present proteoform variation (Figure 2B: high proportion of unique proteoforms per cell) as evidence of heterogeneity without quantifying the signal-to-noise ratio. A cell with 70 proteoforms vs. one with 18 (Table S2: 67.92 µm vs. 64.26 µm) could reflect biological difference or sampling variance; the design does not separate these.
- SOFT: Bulk tissue comparison is not a matched control. The bulk sample (10 ng total protein from homogenized left ventricle, 41 proteins, 83 proteoforms) and single cells (57 proteins, 165 proteoforms total across 13 cells) are compared descriptively, but the bulk sample is not a 'ground truth' for what a single cell should contain. A paired design—analyzing the same cell lysate in bulk vs. single-cell mode—would isolate the effect of sample amount and detection sensitivity from biological heterogeneity.
- SOFT: Cell size selection as a confound control is incomplete. The authors selected cells 61.04–67.92 µm to 'reduce any impact due to differences in starting proteoform quantities,' but do not measure or report total protein per cell, nor do they show that proteoform count correlates with cell size within this range. The largest cell (67.92 µm) yielded 70 proteoforms; the smallest (64.26 µm) yielded 18—a 3.9-fold difference. Is this biological or a size-dependent sampling artifact? The design does not answer this.
- SOFT: No orthogonal validation of key heterogeneity claims. The novel modifications (MLC-2 trimethylation, MYL3 trimethylation, Qcr7 succinylation) are identified by MS alone. A second method (e.g., immunofluorescence, Western blot with PTM-specific antibodies) on a subset of cells would corroborate that these proteoforms are real and variable across cells, not MS artifacts.
- SOFT: Limited sample size for drawing general conclusions. Thirteen cells from one mouse is a proof-of-concept, not a population-level study. The authors do not discuss whether findings generalize to other mice, ages, sexes, or disease states. (The methods mention 'male and female mice' for isolation but do not report sex of the 13 analyzed cells or stratify results by sex.)
- SOFT: Incomplete reporting of negative/null results. The manuscript does not state how many cells were isolated, how many were analyzed, or whether any were excluded and why. This limits assessment of selection bias and reproducibility.

## Questions
- Can the authors provide data from at least 2–3 independent mice to establish that proteoform heterogeneity is a biological property of cardiomyocytes, not an artifact of the single animal analyzed?
- How do the authors quantitatively distinguish stochastic MS sampling variance from true biological heterogeneity in proteoform composition? For example, can they model the expected overlap in proteoforms under a null hypothesis of identical cells with stochastic detection, and test whether observed overlap is lower than expected?
- Did the authors measure total protein content per cell (e.g., by BCA assay on individual cell lysates before MS)? If so, does proteoform count correlate with total protein, and if so, does this confound the heterogeneity claim?
- What is the sex of each of the 13 analyzed cells? Do proteoform profiles differ between male and female cardiomyocytes, and if so, is this controlled or reported?
- Why was the bulk tissue sample (10 ng total protein) not run in a paired design with a single-cell lysate from the same heart to directly compare detection sensitivity and proteoform overlap under matched conditions?
- Can the authors provide a sensitivity analysis: if they subsample the 13 cells to n=3 or n=5, how stable are the conclusions about heterogeneity? This would address whether the small sample size drives the high variance in proteoform counts (18–70 per cell).
