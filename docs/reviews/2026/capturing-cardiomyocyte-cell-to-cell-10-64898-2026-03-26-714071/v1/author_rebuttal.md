## Concessions
- **clarity** — 'Proteoform' is never formally defined, leaving ambiguity about scope (PTMs, truncations, sequence variants).
  - Proposed change: Add a one-sentence operational definition of 'proteoform' in the introduction (consistent with the Consortium for Top-Down Proteomics/HUPO usage: distinct molecular forms of a protein arising from PTMs, sequence variation, and/or proteolytic processing) and note explicitly which categories (PTM, truncation) are covered by our proteoform counts.
- **clarity** — Figure 2 heatmap methodology (overlap metric, color scale) is undefined.
  - Proposed change: Revise Figure 2 legend to state explicitly that overlap = number (and %) of shared protein/proteoform identifications between each cell pair divided by the union of identifications for that pair, and rescale the color bar with numerical tick labels (0-100%).
- **clarity, novelty, rigor** — The relationship between 57 proteins/165 proteoforms and the proportion of unique vs. shared proteoforms is never quantified.
  - Proposed change: Add a supplementary table/summary statistic reporting: proteins with 1 vs >1 proteoform, and the number/percentage of the 165 proteoforms that are singleton (1 cell), shared by 2-5 cells, or shared by all 13 cells.
- **data_analysis, methodology, rigor** — The 4-fold spread in proteoform counts (18 vs 70) per cell is presented without correlation to cell size or total protein yield.
  - Proposed change: Add a scatterplot/regression of protein and proteoform counts against cell length (and, if available, total protein estimate) for the 13 cells, and report the correlation coefficient in the revised Results/Supplement.
- **reproducibility, ethics** — Raw data availability statement lacks a resolvable DOI/accession, and computational/database provenance (UniProt release, .psdb conversion, software versions) is unspecified.
  - Proposed change: Deposit the raw spectra on figshare with a public DOI prior to publication and add a Methods paragraph specifying the UniProt release date/version used, the script/procedure for .psdb conversion, and the software versions for Xtract, UniDec, ProSight PD, and TDValidator.
- **reproducibility** — It is unclear which of the three ProSight PD search nodes applied the 200 Da delta-search tolerance and how FDR was aggregated across nodes.
  - Proposed change: Add explicit text stating that the 200 Da tolerance was applied only in node 1 (unexpected-modification search) across all 13 cells, and clarify whether the 1% FDR was computed per-node or across the combined result set.
- **ethics** — IACUC protocol number is not given and the scope of K.R.D.'s ProSight PD commercial interest is unclear.
  - Proposed change: Add the IACUC protocol number to the Methods and expand the Notes/COI statement to specify the nature of K.R.D.'s interest (e.g., consulting, equity, or royalty) in ProSight PD.
- **rigor** — Terms like 'unbiased,' 'functional heterogeneity,' and 'previously unrecognized' are not licensed by the data shown.
  - Proposed change: Revise the abstract and discussion to replace 'unbiased' with 'untargeted,' replace 'functional heterogeneity' with 'molecular/compositional heterogeneity' throughout, and remove or qualify 'previously unrecognized' to 'not previously characterized at the proteoform level in cardiomyocytes.'
- **literature** — Recent single-cell cardiac proteomics/proteoform literature (e.g., Marín-Vicente 2026, Hu 2026, Gao 2025) is not cited or discussed.
  - Proposed change: Add a paragraph in the Discussion citing and contrasting these recent studies with the present shotgun SC-TDP approach, and explicitly state the literature-search scope supporting each 'first evidence' claim (or rephrase as 'we report' where a systematic search was not performed).

## Disagreements
- **novelty** — The core proteoform-heterogeneity finding is preempted by Melby et al. (2023), making the contribution incremental.
  - Response: Melby et al. used a targeted imaging/microfluidic platform to characterize large proteoforms in isolated single muscle fibers, a fundamentally different cell type, size scale, and acquisition strategy. We already explicitly cite and differentiate this work in the introduction rather than ignoring it. Our contribution is a shotgun (untargeted, LC-MS/MS-based) SC-TDP workflow applied to cardiomyocytes that resolves combinatorial PTM co-occurrence (e.g., simultaneous trimethylation and phosphorylation on a single MLC-2 backbone) at the single-cell level, which has not been previously demonstrated. We will reframe the novelty claim to 'extends proteoform-level single-cell heterogeneity analysis to cardiomyocytes via a distinct shotgun LC-MS platform' rather than claiming to be first to observe muscle-cell proteoform heterogeneity in general.
  - Manuscript reference: 'Additionally, TDP was applied to investigate heterogeneity of large proteoforms in single muscle fibers. Notably, using a Bradford protein assay, the authors estimated the total protein content of individual fibers (~100 ng)...' (Main Text, paragraph on SC-TDP evolution)
- **methodology, data_analysis, rigor** — Because all 13 cells are from a single animal with no negative control (repeat measurement of the same lysate) or null model, the observed proteoform variability cannot be statistically distinguished from stochastic MS/MS sampling noise, so the heterogeneity claim is unsupported.
  - Response: We agree the design cannot fully partition biological from technical variance and will soften language accordingly. However, the data are not silent on this question: at the protein level, where detection depends on the same LC-MS acquisition and stochastic precursor sampling, cardiomyocytes show extensive overlap (Figure 2A), while proteoform-level overlap is markedly lower (Figure 2B) despite proteins and their proteoforms being subject to the same instrument duty-cycle and stochastic MS/MS selection. If the variability were driven purely by stochastic sampling noise common to both levels, we would expect comparable overlap patterns at the protein and proteoform level; the differential pattern is more consistent with genuine proteoform-specific variation layered on a conserved core proteome, though we acknowledge this is not a formal statistical test.
  - Manuscript reference: 'At the protein level (Figure 2A), cardiomyocytes exhibit extensive overlap, reflecting a highly conserved core proteome. In contrast, the high proportion of unique proteoforms in each cell (Figure 2B) reveals substantial heterogeneity among individual cardiomyocytes.' (Results)

## Load-bearing critiques
- A mandatory requirement for new multi-animal biological replicates or a dedicated technical-noise negative control (repeated MS analysis of a single pooled lysate) to formally decompose biological vs. stochastic variance would be fatal this cycle, as it requires new animal cohorts and instrument time beyond the scope of a text/data revision.
- A mandatory requirement for orthogonal (non-MS) functional or antibody-based validation of the novel PTMs (MLC-2 trimethylation, MYL3 trimethylation, Qcr7 succinylation) before acceptance would be fatal this cycle, as no validated antibodies exist for several of these modifications and such assays cannot be developed within a standard revision window.
