# Debate Transcript

## Advocate — round 1

This manuscript should be accepted because it delivers a genuine, well-validated technical advance: the first demonstration of shotgun top-down proteomics on single cardiomyocytes, with rigorous multi-level validation. Every reviewer's methodology and rigor sections agree the core engineering is sound—protein standards (Figure S1), triplicate bulk validation showing reproducible retention times and consistent detection of MLC-2 and ATP5ME (Figure S2, S4), and 13 single cells showing the same core proteoforms (Figure 1). The rigor reviewer explicitly calls PTM localization 'well-supported by fragmentation data,' citing the 42 Da trimethylation shift with diagnostic ion-loss controls (Fig. 3B-C) and T51 phosphorylation confirmed by b53/c59 ions validated in TDValidator (Fig. 3D)—this is not speculative chemistry, it is textbook top-down evidence. The novelty reviewer, while pushing back on 'first' framing, still concedes 'genuine methodological refinement' in the EThcD+HCD hybrid strategy and 'rigorous technical validation.' Methodology reviewer calls the lysis buffer optimization and fragmentation strategy 'robust technical optimization... well-motivated.'  Critically, most objections raised—undefined heatmap metrics, missing quantification of proteoform overlap percentages, absent figshare DOI, unclear delta-search node assignment, single-animal scope—are revision-fixable: they require adding numbers already computable from Table S2, depositing an accession number, and softening specific adjectives ('unbiased,' 'functional heterogeneity'), not new experiments. These are presentation and hedging issues, not evidence that the underlying proteoform identifications are wrong. Even the harshest reviewers (data_analysis, methodology) rate the paper 3-4/5 and confirm the technical feasibility demonstration stands regardless of whether heterogeneity is fully disentangled from stochastic sampling. A proof-of-concept single-cell method paper establishing that 165 proteoforms with combinatorial PTMs can be resolved from a single mammalian cardiomyocyte is a substantive contribution meriting publication with revision.

**Key points:**
- Technical validation is thorough and independently confirmed by multiple reviewers (standards, bulk triplicates, 13-cell reproducibility)
- PTM localization (MLC-2 trimethylation, T51 phosphorylation, Qcr7 succinylation) is supported by rigorous fragmentation evidence, not speculation
- Novelty and methodology reviewers concede genuine methodological refinement in EThcD+HCD hybrid strategy
- Most flagged weaknesses (missing DOI, undefined heatmap metrics, unquantified overlap) are revision-fixable clarifications, not fatal flaws in the underlying data
- Proof-of-concept feasibility of SC-TDP in cardiomyocytes stands even if the 'heterogeneity vs. stochastic noise' question needs further statistical treatment

## Skeptic — round 1

The load-bearing problem is not phrasing—it's that the paper's central deliverable, cell-to-cell proteoform 'heterogeneity,' is empirically indistinguishable from technical noise in the design as run. Methodology, data_analysis, and rigor reviewers converge independently on this: all 13 cells come from one animal, there is no statistical test separating biological variance from 'stochastic sampling and undersampling effects' that the authors themselves invoke to explain replicate discrepancies (Figure S4 caption), and the 4-fold spread in proteoforms per cell (18 vs. 70, Table S2) is presented with zero correlation analysis against cell size or protein yield. Advocate calls this 'revision-fixable by adding numbers already in Table S2'—but you cannot compute a biological-vs-technical variance decomposition from data that was never designed to allow it. That requires new experiments: biological replicates across animals, repeated measurement of the same cell lysate as a negative control, or a formal null model of stochastic MS/MS sampling. This is FATAL to the manuscript's title and abstract claim as currently stated ('previously unrecognized level of molecular heterogeneity').

I'll concede what's genuinely established: fragmentation-based PTM localization (MLC-2 trimethylation, T51 phosphorylation, Qcr7 succinylation) is chemically rigorous and not in dispute—the b/c-ion ladders in Figure 3D are solid evidence for those specific proteoforms existing somewhere in the sampled cells.

But the novelty reviewer also shows Melby et al. (2023) already established single-muscle-cell proteoform heterogeneity, undercutting the 'first' framing that the abstract leans on. Combined with unvalidated 'unbiased' and 'functional heterogeneity' language (rigor reviewer), the paper's headline claim is overstated in a way that isn't just adjective-softening—it's the paper's reason for being.

**Key points:**
- FATAL: heterogeneity claim (title/abstract) cannot be distinguished from stochastic MS sampling noise—no biological replicates, no null model, single animal only
- This gap requires NEW data (repeat measurement of same lysate, multi-animal replicates) not text revision, contradicting advocate's 'just add numbers' framing
- Unexplained 4-fold proteoform range (18 vs 70) per cell lacks correlation analysis against cell size/protein yield, a basic confound check
- CONCEDE: PTM site-localization via EThcD fragmentation (trimethylation, T51 phosphorylation) is rigorous and technically sound
- Novelty of the central heterogeneity claim is undercut by Melby et al. (2023) precedent, weakening the 'previously unrecognized' framing beyond wording fixes
