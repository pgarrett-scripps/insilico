# Rigor & Overclaiming Reviewer

## Summary
This manuscript presents a single-cell top-down proteomics (SC-TDP) method applied to 13 cardiomyocytes and reports detection of 57 proteins and 165 proteoforms with cell-to-cell heterogeneity. The technical approach is sound and the method development is rigorous. However, the manuscript contains several load-bearing claims that are either unsupported by the evidence presented, over-generalized beyond the tested scope, or imprecisely hedged. Most critically, claims about 'previously unrecognized' heterogeneity, functional significance of modifications, and the transformative potential of the platform exceed what 13 cells from a single mouse can establish. The work is a solid methodological contribution but requires substantial requalification of its biological and translational claims.

## Strengths
- The technical validation is thorough: protein standards tested, bulk cardiomyocyte sample analyzed in triplicate with reproducible retention times and 83 proteoforms identified, and single-cell workflow clearly described with optimization details (lysis buffer composition, chromatography, EThcD/HCD strategy).
- Proteoform identifications are well-supported by fragmentation data: MLC-2 trimethylation at A1 confirmed by high-resolution mass measurement (42 Da shift) and diagnostic ion loss patterns (Figures 3B–C); MLC-2 T51 phosphorylation localized by diagnostic ions (b53, c59) validated with TDValidator (Figure 3D); COX6c N-terminal acetylation and Qcr7 K11 succinylation supported by EThcD backbone fragmentation.
- The observation of proteoform heterogeneity across 13 cells is clearly demonstrated: Figure 2B shows high proportion of unique proteoforms per cell despite conserved core proteome (Figure 2A), with quantitative overlap data provided (Table S2: range 14–36 proteins, 18–70 proteoforms per cell).
- Raw data availability via figshare and detailed methods enable reproducibility of the technical workflow.

## Weaknesses
- HARD: Title and abstract claim 'previously unrecognized level of molecular heterogeneity among cardiomyocytes' without evidence that this heterogeneity is novel or that prior work did not observe proteoform variation. The manuscript cites no prior single-cell proteoform studies of cardiomyocytes to establish what was or was not 'previously recognized.' This is a priority/novelty claim that belongs to the novelty reviewer, but as worded it is an unsupported factual assertion about the state of prior knowledge.
- HARD: Abstract states the method 'enables direct, unbiased proteoform profiling from single cardiomyocytes' but provides no evidence of unbiasedness. No comparison to alternative methods, no assessment of detection bias by protein size/charge/abundance, no validation that the organic solvent lysis buffer does not preferentially extract or preserve certain proteoforms. The claim 'unbiased' is not licensed by the data shown.
- HARD: The manuscript claims 'we provide the first evidence of MLC-2 concurrently harboring trimethylation and phosphorylation on a single protein backbone' and 'first evidence of Qcr7 succinylation in single cardiomyocytes.' These are 'first to' claims that require literature review to establish novelty—a task for the novelty reviewer—but the phrasing 'first evidence' is a factual claim about prior literature that should not appear without explicit literature search or citation of a negative prior result. As written, these are unsupported.
- HARD: The manuscript states 'proteoform composition varied substantially among cells' and uses this to argue for 'functional heterogeneity' (Abstract, Main Text), but provides no functional data. Proteoform variation is observed; functional consequence is inferred. The claim 'functional heterogeneity' is not established—only compositional heterogeneity. The word 'functional' should be removed or replaced with 'compositional' or 'molecular.'
- HARD: The conclusion states 'the resulting proteoforms, may serve as potential biomarkers for cardiac disease' and 'proteoforms have been shown to play a critical role in numerous human diseases, including heart failure.' The first is speculative (no disease data presented); the second cites prior work but does not establish that the specific proteoforms identified here are relevant to disease. This is a causal/mechanistic overreach: observation of a modification in healthy mouse cardiomyocytes does not establish its role in disease without disease-state comparison.
- SOFT: The claim that the method is 'simple, robust, fast, ultrasensitive, and high-throughput' is not quantitatively supported. No comparison of sensitivity to other SC proteomics methods, no throughput metrics (cells/day, cost per cell), no definition of 'fast' (analysis time per cell is not stated). These are importance-laden adjectives without calibration.
- SOFT: The manuscript states 'minimizing losses during sample handling and preparation is critically important' and claims the direct lysis approach 'eliminates sample transfer/handling,' but does not quantify recovery or compare to alternative preparation methods. The claim is reasonable but not evidenced.
- SOFT: The observation that the largest protein standard (~68 kDa) was not detected is attributed to 'limitations of the mass spectrometer in analyzing intact proteins of this size,' but the manuscript does not establish whether this is a fundamental limitation of the instrument or the method (e.g., ionization efficiency, fragmentation, or charge state issues). The explanation is speculative.
- SOFT: The paper identifies 57 proteins and 165 proteoforms across 13 cells but does not report the total number of unique proteoforms across all cells or the degree of overlap at the proteoform level quantitatively (Figure 2B is qualitative). A summary statistic (e.g., median proteoforms per cell, range, total unique) would clarify the heterogeneity claim.
- SOFT: The claim that 'individual cardiomyocytes exist in distinct molecular configurations, which contribute to specialized roles in cardiac physiology' (Main Text) is not supported by the data. The cells are from the same tissue (left ventricle), same animal, same size range, and no functional or spatial information is provided. The inference of 'specialized roles' from proteoform composition alone is over-interpreted.

## Questions
- The abstract and title claim 'previously unrecognized' heterogeneity—what prior single-cell proteoform studies of cardiomyocytes are you comparing to, and how do your results differ in scope or magnitude?
- How do you establish that your lysis buffer and LC-MS workflow are 'unbiased'? Have you tested whether the organic solvent composition preferentially extracts or preserves certain protein classes (e.g., hydrophobic vs. hydrophilic, large vs. small)?
- You identify 57 proteins and 165 proteoforms across 13 cells. What is the total number of unique proteoforms across all 13 cells, and what is the median overlap between any two cells? This would quantify the heterogeneity claim more precisely.
- For the 'first evidence' claims (MLC-2 trimethylation + phosphorylation, Qcr7 succinylation)—have you performed a systematic literature search to confirm these modifications have not been reported in cardiomyocytes or other cell types? If not, these claims should be reworded as 'we report' rather than 'first evidence.'
- You state that 'functional consequences of this modification remain to be defined' for MLC-2 trimethylation and other PTMs. Given this, how do you justify the claim that proteoform heterogeneity reflects 'functional heterogeneity' or that these proteoforms 'may serve as potential biomarkers for cardiac disease'? These are mechanistic inferences without functional data.
- The manuscript compares bulk (10 ng, 41 proteins, 83 proteoforms) to single cells (14–36 proteins, 18–70 proteoforms per cell). Why does the bulk sample detect more proteins overall but fewer proteoforms per unit protein? Is this expected, or does it suggest sampling or detection bias in the single-cell workflow?
- You limit cells to 61–68 µm to 'reduce any impact due to differences in starting proteoform quantities,' but you still observe a 4-fold range in proteoforms detected (18–70). How much of this variation is attributable to cell size vs. biological heterogeneity vs. stochastic sampling in MS?
- The manuscript uses a 200 Da delta search window to capture 'up to two phosphorylation events.' How many identifications fall into this extended window, and how confident are you in the assignment of these modifications without targeted validation (e.g., phosphatase treatment)?
