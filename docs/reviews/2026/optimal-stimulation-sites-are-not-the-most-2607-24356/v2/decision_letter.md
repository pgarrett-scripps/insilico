# Decision Letter

**Decision:** major

## Summary of Evaluation
The manuscript presents a sophisticated computational framework using personalized reservoir models to explore stimulation targeting in Alzheimer's disease. The central methodological approach—using identifiable generative models as counterfactual testbeds—is innovative and addresses an important question in network neuroscience. However, the panel identified several substantive issues that must be addressed before the work can be properly evaluated.

Key concerns include: (1) A category error in comparing "pathology" (theoretical read-out interpolation) and "therapy" (physical resonant drive) interventions, which undermines the core dissociation claim; (2) Overstated language regarding "reversion," "cure," and "correctability" that misrepresents the model-internal, high-amplitude overwrite as therapeutic restoration; (3) Insufficient validation that ΔW (model parameter deviation) is a meaningful proxy for biological pathology; (4) Statistical concerns about potential circularity in personalized target selection despite leave-one-out attempts; (5) Omission of foundational literature on model-based personalized stimulation targeting (network control theory); and (6) Reproducibility gaps in specifying exact data subsets and random seeds.

The work has conceptual merit and the methodological framework is promising, but these issues collectively require major revision to ensure the claims are properly supported and accurately framed.
