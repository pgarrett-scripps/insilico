# Meta-Review

**Draft recommendation:** major

## Synthesis
The panel agrees on the substance of the contribution: subject-specific, individually identifiable reservoir models fit to resting-state fMRI, used as a counterfactual testbed for in-silico stimulation. Ethics is clean (5/5, high confidence): ADNI secondary-analysis exemption is now explicit, DUA respected, cohorts documented in Table S1. Novelty (4/5) credits the LOPO analysis in Fig. S9 with resolving the main circularity concern, and reproducibility, clarity, data_analysis and literature all sit at 4/5 with revision-scale asks. Five of eight reports read as 'minor'.

The split is between that majority and the two 3/5 reports (methodology and rigor, both at confidence 4) — and the debate did not dissolve it. The advocate's framing that everything outstanding is 'statistical polish' did not survive contact with the skeptic on two specific items. First, rigor-8: Fig. 6's flagship 'largely disjoint' claim rests on 11 shared sites where a random partition of 121 sites into sets of 35 and 33 would yield ~9.5 by chance. No permutation or hypergeometric test is offered, yet this dissociation is the title's premise and the abstract's headline. This is not phrasing; it is a headline claim with no supporting test, and the data_analysis reviewer independently flags the same gap (data_analysis-5, no formal overlap metric). Second, methodology-1/rigor-11: the abstract's 'complete, individualised reclassification' comes from the transductive, open-loop, supra-physiological analysis, while the only leakage-free estimate (~78% net cure for the population-average target, Fig. S9B) appears nowhere in the abstract or Results narrative. The advocate correctly notes the honest analysis already exists in the manuscript; the skeptic correctly notes that possessing the honest number and headlining the leaky one are different things.

Secondary but converging: methodology-4 (no ablation of fixed reservoir hyperparameters), methodology-5 (tangent-space benchmark run on mismatched preprocessing and CV, so the 'lower end of the benchmark' comparison is not interpretable as stated), rigor-5 (conflation of 'the model's read-out is not reducible to focal nodes' with 'the disease is distributed'), and reproducibility's nine unaddressed procedural gaps (transductive SVD fold structure, reservoir initialisation, oscillation injection method, no versioned code snapshot). The reproducibility reviewer scored 4 but wrote that the authors 'have not addressed any of the nine reproducibility points substantively' and that these are 'load-bearing' — that text argues below its own score. Clarity's new finding that the abstract calls 97.5% vs 100% 'comparable' is small but symptomatic of the same directional drift in the abstract.

Strengths the panel does not dispute and that the authors should retain credit for: honest LOPO validation, the S10 physiological-amplitude diagnostics, transparent reporting of modest AUROC (0.66–0.70 against a 0.84 structural benchmark), the closed-loop dose-reduction result, and an unusually candid Discussion.

## Decisive Factors
I land one step below the confidence-weighted average (3.91, which rounds to 'minor'). The reason is not the count of reviewers but the location of the defects. Two independent reviewers at confidence 4 identify that the paper's two flagship claims — the pathology/therapy dissociation in Fig. 6 and 'complete individualised reclassification' in the abstract — are respectively untested against a chance null and drawn from the leakage-exposed analysis while the leakage-free number is materially weaker and absent from the narrative. Under this venue's first criterion (are the conclusions supported by the evidence presented?), a title-level claim with no significance test and an abstract anchored to the leaky arm are exactly the failure mode 'major' is for, even when everything else is in good order.

I weight the 4/5 cluster carefully: literature is confidence 3 and self-flags that it could not verify the two allegedly-competing 2026 works, so it should not carry much weight either way. Reproducibility's narrative is harsher than its score. Ethics at 5/5@5 is decisive only for scope, not for the central claim.

Crucially, the advocate is right that no new experiments are needed. The route forward is bounded and concrete: (1) run a permutation or hypergeometric test on the Fig. 6C overlap and, if it is not significant, retitle to 'limited overlap' and rewrite the abstract accordingly; (2) re-anchor the Results and abstract to the LOPO numbers, reporting ~78% population-average net cure as the primary generalizable estimate with the ~100% personalised figure explicitly labelled an upper bound; (3) state in the abstract that 'distributed' is a property of the fitted model's representational requirement, not an established property of AD, and that the reclassifications are in-silico at supra-physiological amplitude; (4) add the reservoir-hyperparameter ablation or state plainly that robustness to those choices is untested; (5) either re-run the tangent-space benchmark on matched data/splits or withdraw the 'lower end of the benchmark' comparison; (6) close the reproducibility gaps (fold structure of the transductive SVD, reservoir initialisation, oscillation injection, versioned code DOI, script-to-figure map); (7) define 'physiological amplitude' numerically. Items 1–3 are what separate 'major' from 'minor' here; the rest are the accumulated 4/5 asks. This is a manuscript I expect to accept once the claims are re-anchored to the analyses the authors have already run.
