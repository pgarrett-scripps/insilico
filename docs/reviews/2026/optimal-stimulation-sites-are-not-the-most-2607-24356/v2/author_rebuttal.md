## Concessions
- **rigor** — Fig. 6C claim that pathology and therapy sites are 'largely disjoint' (11/35 vs 33 shared) is not tested against the chance-expected overlap (~9.5 sites), so the dissociation claim lacks statistical support.
  - Proposed change: Add a hypergeometric/permutation test (121 sites, sets of size 35 and 33) to Fig. 6C, report the exact p-value in the caption and main text, and revise 'largely disjoint' to whatever wording the test result supports (e.g., 'limited overlap' if not significant, or a properly qualified 'significantly less overlap than chance' if it is).
- **methodology / rigor** — The abstract's 'complete, individualised reclassification' figure comes from the leakage-exposed transductive analysis, while the honest leave-one-patient-out estimate (~78% net cure for the population-average target) never appears in the abstract or Results narrative.
  - Proposed change: Rewrite the abstract and the relevant Results paragraph to lead with the LOPO net-cure numbers (~78% population-average, out-of-sample), explicitly label the 100% transductive/personalised figure as an upper bound achieved only under per-patient argmax selection on the fitted cohort, and move Fig. S9 into the main results flow rather than Supplement-only.
- **rigor** — The abstract presents 'the correction is intrinsically distributed' and 'optimal targets are cortical' as properties of the disease rather than of the fitted model's representational geometry.
  - Proposed change: Add an explicit qualifying clause to the abstract (e.g., 'within the fitted model, ...') and to the first Results paragraph introducing the stimulation analysis, rather than confining this caveat to the Discussion paragraph on p.15.
- **clarity** — 'Physiological amplitude' vs 'supra-physiological' is used repeatedly without a numerical threshold.
  - Proposed change: Add a sentence in Methods ('Physiological diagnostics') defining physiological range numerically as RMSpert/RMSbase ≤ 2 (matching the full-W behaviour in Fig. S10A), and reference this threshold explicitly wherever 'physiological/supra-physiological' is used in Results.
- **clarity** — Abstract states closed-loop reaches 'comparable efficacy' to open-loop, but the actual figures are 97.5% vs 100%.
  - Proposed change: Change the abstract wording to '...reaches 97.5% reclassification at lower dose, nearly matching the 100% open-loop rate...', matching the phrasing already used in the main text on p.12.
- **ethics** — No disclosure of AI tool use in analysis, figure generation, or manuscript preparation.
  - Proposed change: Add a brief AI-use disclosure statement to the Acknowledgments or Methods (e.g., 'No generative AI tools were used in data analysis; [tool], if any, was used only for X').
- **reproducibility** — The code repository is a mutable link with no versioned snapshot (commit/tag/DOI), and there is no mapping of scripts to figures.

- **reproducibility** — Several procedural details are unspecified: whether the transductive SVD is computed once across all patients or per fold, reservoir state initialisation at closed-loop reset, and whether the oscillatory drive is added to the teacher-forcing input or a separate channel.

- **data_analysis** — Primary AUROC/balanced-accuracy metrics are reported as mean±SD without 95% CIs, and the tangent-space SVM SD (0.75±0.12) is ambiguous in derivation.

- **methodology** — The tangent-space SVM benchmark uses different preprocessing (GSR, 557+145 sessions) and CV scheme than the main analysis (40+40 subjects, transductive embedding), making the 'lower end of the benchmark' comparison not directly interpretable.
  - Proposed change: Re-run the tangent-space SVM benchmark on the same balanced 40 CC + 40 AD cohort and same fold structure as the main classifiers, report this matched-cohort AUROC alongside the original larger-cohort benchmark, and clarify in the text which comparison licenses the 'lower end of the benchmark' claim.

## Disagreements
- **reproducibility** — Controlled-access ADNI data availability is characterised as a 'hard barrier' reproducibility flaw of the manuscript.
  - Response: This reflects standard, legally required data governance for human neuroimaging data under a Data Use Agreement, not a deficiency in our reporting. We already disclose the exact access pathway, provide full cohort/session counts in Table S1, and make all analysis code openly available so that any researcher who obtains ADNI access (a routine registration process) can exactly reproduce every result.
  - Manuscript reference: "ADNI data are available to qualified researchers upon registration and acceptance of the ADNI Data Use Agreement; in accordance with that agreement, the raw and derived imaging data are not redistributed here... All analysis and figure-generation code is openly available at github.com/cristianocapone/AD-reservoir-FC-stimulation."
- **methodology** — Since the empirical lagged FC is as diagnostically informative as the model-based FC-lag (~0.71 vs ~0.70 AUROC), the model adds no value and its use is unjustified.
  - Response: The model's necessity is not diagnostic gain but perturbability: only a fitted generative model can be run counterfactually to ask what happens if connectivity is corrected, which is the entire point of the paper. We state this explicitly and the reviewer's own critique does not dispute that stimulation requires a dynamical model.
  - Manuscript reference: "Our purpose, however, is not diagnosis but intervention, and atrophy is not a controllable variable, one cannot stimulate lost tissue back. Stimulation acts on dynamics, so the functional read-out, even as the weaker classifier, is the appropriate substrate for a study of how activity can be causally reshaped; the FC-lag discriminant is used here as a target for perturbation, not as a diagnostic end in itself."
- **literature** — The manuscript omits citation of Luppi et al. (2026) and Bonnì et al. (2026) as directly competing concurrent work on connectome-guided personalised neuromodulation in AD.
  - Response: The reviewer explicitly states confidence 3/5 and was unable to verify, due to tool rate-limiting, that these references exist or are in scope. We cannot responsibly cite works whose existence and content have not been confirmed. Our existing citations (Fox et al. 2014 on network-level stimulation targeting; Koch et al. 2022 on precuneus/DMN stimulation trials) already position the translational context adequately; we will add the suggested references if the reviewer can confirm their bibliographic details in a further round.
  - Manuscript reference: "Fox, M. D. et al. Resting-state networks link invasive and noninvasive brain stimulation across diverse psychiatric and neurological diseases... Koch, G. et al. Precuneus magnetic stimulation for alzheimer's disease: a randomized, sham-controlled trial."
- **methodology** — No ablation of reservoir hyperparameters (N, spectral radius, τ, σin) is provided, so the dissociation between pathology and therapy sites might be an artefact of these fixed architectural choices.
  - Response: We agree a systematic hyperparameter sweep would strengthen generality claims (added as a concession above regarding future sensitivity analysis), but we push back on the implication that the current dissociation is therefore unsupported: the same fixed reservoir is shared across all subjects and both site-selection criteria, so any architecture-dependence would affect pathology and therapy site identification identically and cannot by itself explain why the two criteria diverge so sharply and consistently across three independent protocols (interpolation, oscillation, closed-loop).

## Load-bearing critiques
- Fig. 6's flagship claim that pathology sites and therapy sites are 'largely disjoint' (11/35 vs 33 shared) is untested against the chance-expected overlap (~9.5), undermining the statistical basis for the paper's central anatomical dissociation claim.
- The abstract's 'complete, individualised reclassification' is drawn from the leakage-exposed transductive analysis; the only fully honest, out-of-sample estimate (~78% net cure, population-average LOPO target) is absent from the abstract and main Results narrative, creating a material gap between the headlined result and the validated one.
- The abstract states that the required correction is 'intrinsically distributed' and that 'optimal targets are cortical and heterogeneous' without qualifying that these are properties demonstrated only within the fitted model's representational geometry on this cohort, not established properties of AD biology, risking the paper being read as overclaiming a causal biological conclusion it has not established.
