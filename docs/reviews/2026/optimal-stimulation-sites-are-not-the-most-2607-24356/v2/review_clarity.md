# Clarity & Presentation Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript's central argument—that optimal stimulation sites are not the most pathologically altered—is compelling and clearly stated. However, the presentation of the evidence supporting this claim is often ambiguous and lacks critical clarity. Key terms are inconsistently defined, figures are referenced unclearly, and the narrative flow between methods, results, and interpretation is frequently disrupted by jargon and dense, overly long sentences. These issues make it difficult for a reader to follow the logic and verify the claims without significant guesswork. The core contribution is valuable, but the manuscript requires substantial revision to meet a standard of clarity suitable for a broad, interdisciplinary audience.

## Strengths
- The central claim is explicitly stated and is conceptually clear and important.
- The manuscript attempts to integrate multiple complex analyses (model fitting, classification, in-silico perturbation) into a single narrative arc.
- The figures are generally well-constructed and labeled.

## Weaknesses
- Load-bearing: The evidence for "complete reclassification" from a single, personalised site is ambiguous and potentially overstated. The manuscript claims a "single-site drive... achieves complete, individualised reclassification" (Abstract) and "reverts essentially all of them" (Results). The cited evidence is Figure 5A, showing "100%" reclassification for the "LDA-resonant site" curve. However, the y-axis is labeled "AD reclassified as CC (%)," and the curve reaches 100% at a relative dose of ~0.8. The text states this intervention uses amplitude `A ∈ [0,20]`. A reader cannot determine: a) What absolute amplitude `A` corresponds to the 100% reclassification point? b) Is this amplitude within a physiologically plausible range, or is it the "supra-physiological" amplitude later discussed? c) Does "complete reclassification" mean 100% of the 40 AD patients cross the decision boundary, or 100% of those who were misclassified at baseline? The distinction is critical for assessing the claim's strength. An alternative explanation is that the effect is achieved only at an implausibly high drive amplitude, which would undermine the practical significance of the finding. The manuscript must explicitly state the amplitude at the 100% point and justify its physiological plausibility.
- Load-bearing: The operational definition of "most-affected sites" is unclear and its interpretation is potentially misleading. The manuscript states "most-affected sites are those with the largest per-site read-out deviation from the control template, i.e. the largest column norm ||ΔW||" (Results). However, `ΔW = W̄_CC - W_AD` is a matrix of differences between group-mean and individual read-outs. The "column norm" of this difference matrix for a given region is therefore a measure of how much that region's *model parameter* differs from the control average, not necessarily a direct measure of pathological "affection" in the biological sense. The text later cautions that this ranking "should thus be read as indicating where the correction kernel carries its largest weights, not as an independently validated map of Alzheimer pathology" (Results). This crucial caveat is buried and contradicts the earlier, more definitive language (e.g., "targeting the most-deviant sites... fails"). This conflation of a model parameter deviation with a biological pathology marker is a HARD clarity issue, as it leaves the reader unsure what the "most-affected site" intervention is actually testing. The authors must consistently and explicitly define "most-affected" in terms of the model (`ΔW` norm) and avoid implying it is a direct biomarker.
- The abbreviation "FC" is used throughout for "functional connectivity," but "FC-lag" is introduced without definition (first use: "the lagged FC of the model's reconstruction"). A reader must infer that "FC-lag" refers to a feature vector constructed from lagged correlation matrices.
- Figure references are often imprecise, making it hard to locate specific evidence. For example, "The model captured individual connectivity well: group-mean reconstructed FC matrices (Fig. 2C,D) reproduced the corresponding empirical matrices (Fig. 2A,B)" – it is unclear which subpanels (C vs D, A vs B) correspond to which group (CC vs AD).
- The narrative jumps between analysis stages without clear signposting. For instance, the transition from the classifier results (Fig. 3) to the in-silico stimulation begins with "Finally, we used the model to ask..." but does not restate the specific question being addressed (can the AD signature be reverted, and is it focally reducible?).
- The description of the "LDA-resonant" site selection criterion is scattered and unclear. It is first mentioned in the Results as "selecting, for each patient, the site whose resonant drive most efficiently shifts the FC-lag score toward the control distribution," but the precise algorithm (e.g., scanning all 121 sites with a resonant drive and choosing the one that minimizes the LDA score) is not concisely stated in one place.
- The term "G-space" is introduced ("the geometry of the read-out across patients") but its construction (projection onto reservoir state singular vectors, SVD across patients) is described in a later Methods subsection, breaking the narrative flow.
- The Methods section states the reservoir uses "a short membrane time constant τ (effectively instantaneous update)," but the value of τ is not provided, making the simulation step unclear.

## Questions
- For the "complete reclassification" claim in Fig 5A (LDA-resonant site), please specify the absolute stimulation amplitude `A` at the 100% reclassification point and provide a justification for its physiological plausibility relative to the endogenous drive amplitude.
- Please clarify in the main text (e.g., in the first paragraph of "In-silico stimulation: pathology site versus therapeutic target") that "most-affected sites" refers specifically to sites with the largest `||ΔW||` (model parameter deviation), and explicitly distinguish this from a claim about the locus of biological pathology.
- In the caption for Figure 2, please explicitly state which subpanels (A, B, C, D) correspond to the CC group and which to the AD group for both the data and model matrices.
