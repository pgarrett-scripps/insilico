**Score:** 4/5 → 4/5 (confidence 4/5)

## Summary
The revision addresses several clarity issues raised in round 1. The abstract has been substantially rewritten to remove undefined terms ('lagged FC', 'connectivity kernel') and to distinguish the two intervention protocols more explicitly. The main text now explicitly names 'theoretical read-out correction' and 'physical resonant drive' (p. 11) and moves the leakage-free analysis into the main narrative (p. 12) rather than relegating it to Methods. Quantitative comparisons are now more explicit (AUROC ranges stated in main text, not just supplementary). However, three significant clarity issues remain unresolved: (1) 'physiological amplitude' is still not formally defined, leaving ambiguity about what amplitude range constitutes 'physiological' vs. 'supra-physiological'; (2) the transductive vs. leave-one-out distinction is named but not explained, so readers unfamiliar with transductive learning will not understand why the main results (Figs. 3–5) require validation in Fig. S9; (3) the term 'read-out' is still used in multiple senses without consistent distinction. Additionally, a new issue has emerged: the abstract claims the closed-loop controller reaches 'comparable efficacy' to open-loop, but the text shows 97.5% vs. 100%, which is a meaningful difference that should be stated explicitly.

## Questions
- What is the formal threshold for 'physiological amplitude'? Is it RMSpert/RMSbase ≤ 1, ≤ 2, or some other value? The Methods mention 'beyond the RMS of the endogenous teacher-forcing drive' but do not state a numerical boundary.
- In the transductive embedding (Figs. 3–5), the SVD basis and LDA discriminant are fit on all patients, then classifiers are evaluated on held-out folds. Why is this not considered leakage? A brief explanation in the main text (e.g., 'the embedding is transductive, meaning the basis is fit on all patients but the classifier is trained and tested on disjoint folds') would clarify why Fig. S9 is necessary.
- Figure 2I caption states 'median ± IQR' but the error bars appear symmetric. Should this be 'median ± SD' or 'median ± SEM'?
- The abstract claims the closed-loop controller reaches 'comparable efficacy' to open-loop, but Fig. 5D–F shows 97.5% vs. 100%. Should the abstract state '97.5%, nearly matching the 100% open-loop rate' (as the main text does on p. 12) rather than 'comparable'?
- In Results (p. 8), the term 'connectivity kernel' is used without definition. Does this refer to the read-out weights W, the recurrent weights J, or the effective input-to-output transfer function? A formal definition would strengthen the mechanistic claim.
