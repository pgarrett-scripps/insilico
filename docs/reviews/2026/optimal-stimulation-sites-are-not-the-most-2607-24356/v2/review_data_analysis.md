# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer — Revision Review

**Score:** 4/5 → 4/5 (confidence 4/5)

## Summary
The revision has made minor clarifications to the Methods section, particularly regarding the leakage-free target selection and the closed-loop dynamics justifying α>1 over-correction. However, most of the statistical concerns raised in round 1 remain unresolved. The primary classification metrics (AUROC, balanced accuracy) are still reported as mean±SD without 95% CIs. The tangent-space SVM benchmark's SD remains ambiguous. The selection of reference K and σ is still not explicitly stated as training-set-only. The learning-curve fit lacks R² and sensitivity analysis. The dissociation between pathology and therapy sites is still reported qualitatively without a formal overlap metric. The amplitude×frequency landscape lacks summary statistics. The frequency sweep range is not justified. The net cure comparison between transductive and LOPO is clearer but still lacks a side-by-side absolute reclassification table. The per-site therapeutic effect (120/121 sites) is not corrected for multiple comparisons. The personalised targeting bound is not quantified. The α>1 over-correction lacks sensitivity analysis. Overall, the revision addresses the conceptual clarity but does not substantially strengthen the statistical rigor or quantitative reporting.

## Score rationale
The manuscript remains at score 4 (minor revision). The revision has improved clarity in the Methods and Discussion but has not resolved the core statistical concerns: missing confidence intervals on primary metrics, ambiguous SD definitions, lack of formal effect-size quantification, absence of multiple-comparison corrections, and incomplete sensitivity analyses. These are not fatal flaws—the core findings are sound and the analysis is generally honest—but they prevent the work from reaching a score of 5 (accept). The authors have had the opportunity to address these points and have chosen not to do so substantively, suggesting either that they do not view them as important or that they are constrained by space or other factors. A score of 4 reflects that the work is suitable for publication with minor revisions, but the statistical reporting should be strengthened in a final version.

## Points from the previous round
- **[data_analysis-1] outstanding** — The manuscript still reports AUROC and balanced accuracy as mean±SD over folds (e.g., Fig. 3A–D: 'mean over folds; bands, ±1SD'). No 95% confidence intervals are provided on the primary classification metrics. The text states 'mean±SD' but does not give the actual CI bounds, which would quantify uncertainty on the modest ~0.70 AUROC claims more precisely than a band width.
- **[data_analysis-2] outstanding** — The tangent-space SVM benchmark is still reported as 'AUROC 0.75±0.12 (out-of-fold 0.71; Fig. 3E)' in the Results section. The Methods clarify that this is 'mean±SD over a nested 5×5 patient-level CV,' but it remains ambiguous whether the ±0.12 is the SD of the 25 fold-level AUROCs or a pooled estimate. The discrepancy between the reported mean (0.75) and the out-of-fold ROC (0.71) is not explained. A clearer statement of how the SD was computed (e.g., 'SD of 25 fold-level AUROCs') would resolve this.
- **[data_analysis-3] partial** — The Methods section now states: 'Reference slices were chosen by the marginal-mean AUROC across classifiers and the complementary axis.' However, it does not explicitly state whether this selection was made on the training set only or involved the test set. The text says 'The SVD embeddings were built transductively (across all patients) while the classifiers were trained and tested on disjoint folds,' which clarifies that the embedding is transductive but does not clarify whether the K and σ selection involved test-set inspection. A statement such as 'K and σ were selected on the training set only' or 'K and σ were chosen post-hoc from the full cross-validation grid' would fully resolve this.
- **[data_analysis-4] partial** — Fig. S1 now includes a 'saturating power-law fit' with a '68% band from the fit covariance' and projects AUROC≈0.76 at N=100 and ≈0.80 at N=200. However, no R² or residual plot is provided to assess fit quality, and the extrapolation range is large (from N=40 to N=200). The caption states 'saturating power law m(N) = A − B·N^(−C) is fitted to each metric' but does not report the fitted parameters (A, B, C) or their uncertainties, limiting reproducibility and sensitivity analysis.
- **[data_analysis-5] outstanding** — Fig. 6C reports 'of the 35 distinct pathology sites (top-5 union across patients) and 33 distinct per-patient therapy sites (personalised LDA-resonant targets) identified across the 40 AD patients share only 11 sites, the great majority of each set is exclusive to its own criterion.' This is stated qualitatively as ~31% and ~33% overlap. No formal overlap metric (Jaccard index, Sørensen–Dice, or other) is provided to quantify the dissociation magnitude or allow comparison to other datasets.
- **[data_analysis-6] outstanding** — Fig. S8 shows per-patient (A, B) and population-level (C, D) amplitude×frequency heatmaps, but no summary statistic (median, IQR, or range) is provided for the amplitude needed to cross the decision boundary at resonance across the 40 patients. The caption describes the landscape but does not report, e.g., 'median amplitude at f_eig = X ± Y (IQR),' limiting reproducibility and generalisability assessment.
- **[data_analysis-7] outstanding** — The Methods section ('Oscillatory stimulation') states 'Frequencies were swept over f ∈ [0, 0.5] cycles/step' but does not justify this range or state whether it was chosen a priori or post-hoc. The text notes that f_eig ≈ 0.077 cycles/step is the dominant eigenmode, which falls well within [0, 0.5], but does not explain why the upper bound of 0.5 was selected or whether a wider range was tested. A statement such as 'the range [0, 0.5] was chosen a priori to cover the infraslow and low-frequency bands' or 'a sensitivity analysis over [0, 1.0] yielded consistent results' would resolve this.
- **[data_analysis-8] partial** — Fig. S9 now reports 'net cure' as 'the fraction of patients on the AD side at baseline that stimulation moves to the CC side' under both transductive and LOPO schemes. The caption states 'Honest cross-validation raises the baseline misclassification of AD as CC from 17.5% (transductive) to 42.5% (LOPO), so that only 23 of 40 AD patients lie on the AD side of the boundary unstimulated.' This clarifies the difference in operating points, but a side-by-side table comparing absolute reclassification rates (not just net cure) under both schemes would make the comparison more transparent and easier to interpret.
- **[data_analysis-9] outstanding** — Fig. S6A reports 'almost every site is therapeutic on average at f_1 (A=4)' and states '120/121 sites move the score toward CC; only site 81 net-harmful.' The caption notes 'a safety argument for personalised targeting' but does not apply a multiple-comparison correction (Bonferroni, FDR) to determine which sites are significantly therapeutic versus which are indistinguishable from noise. The analysis is exploratory, but the claim '120/121 sites are therapeutic' should be qualified as 'move the mean score toward CC' without implying statistical significance, or a correction should be applied.
- **[data_analysis-10] outstanding** — The Methods section ('Leakage-free target selection') states that the personalised per-patient LDA-resonant site is 'an upper bound on achievable personalisation' but does not quantify this bound. Fig. S9B shows that the personalised LOPO target reaches ~100% net cure, whereas the population-average LOPO target reaches ~78%. However, the text does not state how much of the 100% is due to the per-patient argmax over 121 sites versus how much would be achieved by a fixed global site, or provide a sensitivity analysis (e.g., top-3, top-5 sites per patient) to quantify the cost of personalisation.
- **[data_analysis-11] outstanding** — The Methods section ('In-silico stimulation') states 'α > 1 is over-correction' and notes 'the exact α=1 replacement can be insufficient: the reconstruction is closed-loop, so the reservoir state driven by the AD input keeps the FC partly AD-like even under the CC read-out, and crossing the decision boundary can require pushing past W_CC.' However, no sensitivity analysis is provided showing the effect of capping α at 1.0 or demonstrating that α=1 is empirically insufficient. A figure showing reclassification rate vs α with and without the α>1 range would clarify whether the over-correction is necessary or merely convenient.
- **[data_analysis-12] outstanding** — The Methods section ('Read-out fitting') states that the read-out W is obtained by 'ridge-like regularised least squares with additive Gaussian noise of scale σ in the regressor,' with σ=0.025 as the default. However, the justification for this choice is not provided, and it is unclear whether σ was selected a priori or tuned to maximise performance. The text sweeps σ in Fig. 3C–D but does not state whether the sweep was performed on the training set only or involved the test set.

## Strengths
- The core methodological contribution—fitting identifiable, subject-specific reservoir models and using them as a counterfactual testbed for stimulation—is novel and well-executed. The dissociation between pathology sites (subcortical/limbic) and therapeutic sites (cortical) is striking and well-demonstrated across multiple protocols (read-out interpolation, oscillatory stimulation, closed-loop control).
- The leakage-free validation (leave-one-AD-patient-out cross-validation in Fig. S9) is a significant strength, demonstrating that the therapeutic effect is not an artefact of classifier overfitting. The net cure metric (fraction of AD-side patients moved to CC side) is a sensible and honest measure.
- The closed-loop control analysis (Fig. 5D–F) is methodologically sophisticated, showing that real-time amplitude titration reduces stimulation dose by ~44% while maintaining efficacy. This is a genuine contribution to adaptive neuromodulation design.
- The manuscript is transparent about limitations: modest classifier accuracy, cross-sectional cohort, small sample size, phenomenological (not biophysical) model, and the gap between in-silico amplitude and deliverable dose. The authors do not overclaim.
- The supplementary analyses are thorough: physiological diagnostics (Fig. S10), frequency tuning (Fig. S7), per-patient amplitude×frequency landscapes (Fig. S8), and comparison of site-selection criteria (Fig. S4, S6). The code is made available.

## Questions
- Can you provide 95% confidence intervals (not just ±1 SD bands) on the primary AUROC and balanced-accuracy metrics in Fig. 3A–D? This would quantify uncertainty more precisely and allow readers to assess whether the modest ~0.70 AUROC is consistent with the tangent-space benchmark (0.75±0.12).
- For the tangent-space SVM benchmark (AUROC 0.75±0.12), please clarify: is the ±0.12 the standard deviation of the 25 fold-level AUROCs, or a pooled estimate? How does the reported mean (0.75) relate to the out-of-fold ROC (0.71 in Fig. 3E)? A table showing the 25 fold-level AUROCs would resolve this.
- In Fig. 3A–D, were the reference K and σ selected on the training set only, or did the selection involve inspection of test-set performance? If the latter, this could introduce selection bias. Please state explicitly: 'K and σ were chosen by marginal-mean AUROC on the training set only' or provide a sensitivity analysis showing that the results are robust to different K and σ choices.
- For Fig. S1, please report the fitted parameters (A, B, C) of the power-law model m(N) = A − B·N^(−C), the R² of the fit, and a residual plot. Also, perform a sensitivity analysis: how do the projected AUROC values at N=100 and N=200 change if you fit a different saturating model (e.g., exponential, logistic)?
- In Fig. 6C, please quantify the dissociation between pathology and therapy sites using a formal overlap metric (e.g., Jaccard index = |A ∩ B| / |A ∪ B|). This would allow comparison to other datasets and make the magnitude of the dissociation more concrete.
- In Fig. S8, please report summary statistics (median, IQR, range) for the amplitude A needed to cross the decision boundary at resonance f_eig across the 40 patients. This would strengthen the reproducibility and generalisability claims.
- For the oscillatory stimulation analysis, please justify the frequency sweep range [0, 0.5] cycles/step: was it chosen a priori, or based on preliminary inspection? If the latter, please perform a sensitivity analysis over a wider range (e.g., [0, 1.0]) to confirm that f_eig is the true optimum.
- In Fig. S9, please provide a side-by-side table comparing absolute reclassification rates (not just net cure) under transductive and LOPO schemes, to make the comparison more transparent. For example: 'Transductive: X% of 40 AD patients reclassified; LOPO: Y% of 23 AD-side patients reclassified.'
- In Fig. S6A, the claim that '120/121 sites are therapeutic on average' should be qualified: are these sites significantly therapeutic (after multiple-comparison correction), or do they simply move the mean score toward CC? If the latter, please apply a Bonferroni or FDR correction to determine which sites are significantly different from zero effect.
- In the Methods ('Leakage-free target selection'), you state that the personalised per-patient LDA-resonant site is 'an upper bound on achievable personalisation.' Can you quantify this bound? For example, what is the net cure rate if you restrict each patient to their top-3 or top-5 sites, rather than the top-1? This would show how much personalisation is necessary.
- For the read-out interpolation (Figs. 4, S10), please provide a sensitivity analysis showing reclassification rate vs α with α capped at 1.0. Does capping α at 1.0 substantially reduce efficacy, or is the α>1 range merely a convenience? This would clarify whether over-correction is necessary or empirical.
