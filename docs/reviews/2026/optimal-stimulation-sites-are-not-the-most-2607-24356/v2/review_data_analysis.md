# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript presents a sophisticated computational modeling and intervention analysis, but its central quantitative claims are insufficiently supported by the statistical evidence provided. The diagnostic performance of the model-based classifiers is presented without confidence intervals or a clear demonstration of statistical significance over a robust null baseline. Crucially, the headline finding—that discriminant-aligned stimulation achieves "complete reclassification"—rests on an analysis that appears circular: the same patients used to define the discriminant and select the optimal stimulation site are then used to measure the success of that intervention. Without an honest, leakage-free evaluation, this claim is not established. The work is conceptually interesting but requires rigorous statistical validation to support its conclusions.

## Strengths
- The use of a generative, subject-specific model to ask causal, counterfactual questions about network intervention is a powerful methodological approach.
- The authors thoughtfully compare multiple intervention strategies (full-W correction, pathology-targeted, discriminant-targeted) on a common ground.
- The attempt to implement a closed-loop controller and evaluate its efficiency is a commendable step towards translational relevance.

## Weaknesses
- Load-bearing Claim 1: The superior efficacy of discriminant-aligned stimulation. The claim that "personalising [the target] per patient reverts essentially all of them" (Fig 5A, 100% reclassification) is the paper's central result. However, the evaluation protocol is suspect. The FC-lag discriminant (LDA) and the site-selection criterion ("the site whose resonant drive moves the FC-lag score most toward CC") are both defined using the *entire set of AD patients*. Selecting the optimal site for each patient by maximizing score reduction on that patient's own data, and then scoring that same patient's post-stimulation data against the same discriminant, constitutes a severe form of overfitting or data leakage. The result in Fig. S9 suggests the authors attempted a leave-one-out correction, but the reported "net cure" of ~100% for the personalised target under LOPO is still alarmingly high and requires explicit, step-by-step documentation of the cross-validation procedure for the site-selection step to be credible. As presented, the dramatic efficacy of the personalised intervention is not statistically distinguishable from a circular analysis.
- Load-bearing Claim 2: Diagnostic utility of the model-based classifiers. The reported AUROC values (~0.66-0.70) are presented without confidence intervals (e.g., via bootstrap). More importantly, the comparison to the "strong, leakage-free FC baseline" (tangent-space SVM, AUROC 0.75 ± 0.12) is described as "comparable" and "within its wide spread," but no statistical test is provided to support this equivalence claim. Given the modest sample sizes (n=36 CC, n=40 AD), the overlap in standard deviations does not constitute evidence of comparable performance. A proper comparison (e.g., a DeLong test for paired ROC curves) is needed.
- Sweep Items: - Figure 3 reports classifier performance (accuracy, AUROC) vs. parameters (K, σ) as mean ± SD over CV folds. SD over folds measures variability due to fold composition, not the precision of the performance estimate itself; reporting mean ± 95% CI (via bootstrap over repetitions) would be more informative. - The statement "two-sided Mann–Whitney 𝑝 = 8.7×10^{-8}" (Fig 4A) is presented without the corresponding effect size (e.g., rank-biserial correlation). - In the dose-response analyses (e.g., Fig 5A-C, S7), the "dose" axis is a "normalised dose," but the normalization method is not defined, making the x-axis uninterpretable. - The claim that accuracy "had not saturated at the present sample size" (Fig S1) is supported by an extrapolation based on a saturating power-law fit. The goodness-of-fit of this model to the empirical learning curve is not reported (e.g., R²), and the extrapolation to N=200 is a substantial leap beyond the observed data (N≤40). - For the physiological diagnostics (Fig S10), the thresholds for "plausible amplitude" and "supra-physiological" are not justified or referenced. - The analysis in Fig. S6E states "24% of all (site,patient) drives push toward AD." It is unclear how this proportion was calculated (is it over 121 sites × 40 patients?) and whether it accounts for the non-independence of testing multiple sites per patient.

## Questions
- Please provide a complete, step-by-step pseudocode description of the evaluation protocol for the personalised LDA-resonant stimulation, explicitly detailing how data leakage is prevented between the steps of (a) constructing the FC-lag embedding/LDA, (b) selecting the optimal stimulation site for each patient, and (c) evaluating the post-stimulation classification score for that patient. The description in the Methods ("leave-one-AD-patient-out cross-validation") and Fig. S9 is insufficiently detailed to judge its rigor.
- Please report 95% confidence intervals (e.g., via 1000 bootstrap samples stratified by patient) for the AUROC of the G-space and FC-lag classifiers, and perform a statistical comparison (e.g., DeLong test) between the best-performing model-based classifier and the tangent-space SVM benchmark to formally test the hypothesis of equivalent diagnostic performance.
- Please clarify how the "normalised dose" is calculated in Figures 5A-C and S7C-D. What is the denominator (e.g., maximum amplitude tested, baseline RMS)?
