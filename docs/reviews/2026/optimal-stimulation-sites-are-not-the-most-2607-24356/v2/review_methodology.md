# Methodology Reviewer — Revision Review

**Score:** 3/5 → 3/5 (confidence 4/5)

## Summary
The authors have made incremental improvements to address the methodology concerns. Most notably, they added Fig. S9 with leave-one-AD-patient-out cross-validation, explicitly distinguishing the personalised result (100%, an upper bound) from the population-average result (~78%, fully out-of-sample). They also added a caveat in the Discussion clarifying that the model provides infraslow BOLD-level prescriptions, not direct physical protocols. However, several core methodological issues remain unresolved. The therapy-target selection is still defined by its effect on the same cohort's discriminant, even under LOPO cross-validation—this is per-patient optimization against a train-only threshold, not true out-of-sample generalizability. The dissociation between pathology and therapy sites has not been tested in an independent cohort or under ablation of the fixed reservoir hyperparameters. The comparison to the tangent-space SVM benchmark remains methodologically mismatched. The claim that the AD signature is 'functionally irreducible' to focal sites is not fully supported: the paper shows single-site stimulation fails at physiological amplitudes, but does not explore sparse multi-site interventions. The abstract and introduction still overstate the translational validity of the model-derived prescription, despite the Discussion caveat. These gaps limit confidence in the generalizability and mechanistic interpretation of the findings.

## Score rationale
The score remains 3 (major revision) because the key methodological concerns are only partially addressed. The addition of LOPO cross-validation and the honest distinction between personalised and population-average results are valuable, but they do not resolve the core issue: the therapy-target selection is still a per-patient optimization against the same cohort's discriminant, which is a form of post-hoc fitting. The lack of ablation on reservoir hyperparameters, the absence of an independent cohort validation, and the incomplete exploration of multi-site interventions mean the dissociation between pathology and therapy sites remains incompletely characterized. The manuscript is more honest about its limitations (Discussion caveat on translational validity), but the main claims still outrun the evidence. The population-average LOPO result (~78% net cure) is substantially weaker than the personalised claim (100%), and this distinction is not sufficiently prominent in the main narrative. The paper makes a solid methodological contribution (identifiable reservoir models, closed-loop validation), but the central claim about optimal stimulation sites requires stronger evidence of generalizability and independence from the fitting procedure.

## Points from the previous round
- **[methodology-1] partial** — The authors added Fig. S9 and explicitly discuss the distinction between transductive and leave-one-AD-patient-out (LOPO) cross-validation. They now state: 'the personalised figure still reflects a per-patient argmax over the 121 candidate sites and should be read as an upper bound on achievable personalisation, whereas the population-average result, a single global site selected on the training patients alone, is the conservative, fully out-of-sample estimate' (Methods, p. 23). This clarification is helpful and appears in the main text (Discussion, p. 15: 'personalised, model-informed targeting is required'). However, the main-text claims still emphasize the 100% personalised result (Abstract, p. 1; Fig. 5A) without consistently flagging it as an upper bound. The honest, fully independent estimate is the population-average LOPO result (~78% net cure, Fig. S9B), which is substantially weaker than the 100% personalised claim. The manuscript now acknowledges the distinction but does not fully integrate it into the narrative arc—readers may still take the 100% figure as the primary result.
- **[methodology-2] outstanding** — The authors added closed-loop free-run validation (Fig. 2I, Fig. 5) and LOPO cross-validation (Fig. S9), which are steps toward independence. However, the core issue remains: the therapy-target selection (LDA-resonant site) is still defined by its effect on the FC-lag discriminant, which is derived from the same cohort's data. The LOPO scheme rebuilds the discriminant per fold, but each held-out patient's personalised target is selected as the argmax of that patient's per-site effect on the train-only discriminant (Methods, p. 23). This is not a true out-of-sample validation of generalizability—it is a per-patient optimization against a train-only threshold, which is a form of post-hoc fitting. The manuscript does not test whether the therapy sites identified in one patient generalize to another patient, or whether the dissociation between pathology and therapy sites holds in an independent cohort. The closed-loop free-run (Fig. 5) uses the same fitted model and same subject's data, so it does not address the generalizability concern.
- **[methodology-3] partial** — The authors added a caveat in the Discussion (p. 18): 'these are model-level prescriptions: the reservoir is fit to infraslow BOLD connectivity, not electrophysiology, so an "intervention" here is an abstract input to the regional FC dynamics, and its optimal spatial pattern and (infraslow) frequency should be read as targets at the level of regional drive, not as cellular or oscillatory mechanisms. With that caveat, the focal, slowly-modulated character maps in spirit onto closed-loop, biomarker-guided neuromodulation; translating it to any specific technology, and validating it against electrophysiological or longitudinal outcomes, is left to future work.' This is a more honest framing than the abstract and introduction suggest. However, the abstract still claims 'the site to stimulate is not where connectivity is most altered but where the network is most therapeutically responsive' and 'optimal targets are cortical and heterogeneous', which could mislead readers into thinking the model provides a direct prescription for a physical intervention. The Discussion caveat is appropriate but buried; the abstract and introduction should be more cautious about translational claims.
- **[methodology-4] outstanding** — The authors did not add any ablation or sensitivity analysis of the reservoir hyperparameters (N=2000, spectral radius 0.95, τ ≈ 0, σ_in = 0.01). The read-out regularisation σ is swept (Fig. 3C,D), but the reservoir architecture itself is not varied. The manuscript does not address whether the dissociation between pathology and therapy sites depends on these fixed choices. This is a significant gap: if the results are sensitive to the reservoir's representational geometry, the generalizability of the findings is questionable.
- **[methodology-5] outstanding** — The tangent-space SVM benchmark (Fig. 3E, AUROC 0.75±0.12) still uses different preprocessing (global signal regression, 557 CC + 145 AD sessions) and a different cross-validation scheme (nested 5×5 subject-grouped, train-only tangent reference) than the main analysis (40 CC + 40 AD subjects, transductive embedding). The authors do not re-run the benchmark on the same data/splits as the main analysis. The comparison remains methodologically mismatched, making it difficult to assess whether the reservoir-based read-outs are truly at the 'lower end' of the benchmark's spread or whether the difference is due to preprocessing and CV scheme.
- **[methodology-6] outstanding** — The empirical lagged FC (143 CC, 40 AD; p. 6) is classified at AUROC ≈ 0.71, comparable to the model-based FC-lag (AUROC ≈ 0.70). The authors state: 'reading out the model's reconstructed lagged FC neither adds nor loses diagnostic information relative to the empirical lagged connectivity, but repackages the same signal in the fitted, perturbable read-out.' This is a fair characterization, but the sample sizes still differ (143 vs 40 CC), and the comparison is not on identical data/splits. The authors do not explain why the model is necessary if the empirical lagged FC is equally informative. The answer given in the Introduction (stimulation acts on dynamics, not tissue loss) is valid, but it does not justify fitting a model that adds no diagnostic value. A comparison on matched data would strengthen this claim.
- **[methodology-7] partial** — The authors acknowledge the confound in the Discussion (p. 16): 'The siteswith the largest per-patient correction magnitude ∥ΔW∥ were consistently subcortical and limbic... These are, however, also among the most weakly inter-connected nodes of the parcellation, where a shared reservoir basis constrains the per-subject read-out least; the ranking should thus be read as indicating where the correction kernel carries its largest weights, not as an independently validated map of Alzheimer pathology.' This is an honest caveat, but it undermines the interpretation of Fig. 6A,B as a map of pathology. The authors do not test whether the therapy sites (Fig. 6D) are effective because they are well-connected hubs or because they are sites where the model's read-out is most constrained. This distinction is important for interpreting the dissociation between pathology and therapy sites.
- **[methodology-8] outstanding** — The oscillatory stimulation protocol uses the leading eigenmode frequency f_eig of the fixed reservoir J, which is a property of the model, not the subject. The therapy-target selection (LDA-resonant) is highly personalised (33 distinct sites for 40 patients, Fig. 6D). The authors explore the interaction between site and frequency (Fig. S6), but they do not perform a per-subject frequency optimization to test whether the personalised sites are effective because they are individually optimal or because they couple well to the dominant mode regardless of subject. This is a missed opportunity to strengthen the personalisation claim.
- **[methodology-9] outstanding** — The manuscript claims the AD signature is 'functionally irreducible' to focal sites (Introduction, p. 2; Discussion, p. 15), but the evidence is that focal sites fail to revert the classification at physiological amplitudes. The authors do not explore whether a sparse, multi-site distributed intervention (e.g., simultaneous stimulation of 5–10 sites) could be more efficient than a single site. The full-W correction (Fig. 4B,E) succeeds, but it is not a realistic intervention. The paper does not test intermediate hypotheses between single-site and full-network correction, which would strengthen the claim about irreducibility.

## Strengths
- The addition of leave-one-AD-patient-out cross-validation (Fig. S9) is a significant methodological improvement, explicitly distinguishing the personalised upper bound from the conservative, fully out-of-sample estimate.
- The closed-loop free-run validation (Fig. 2I, Fig. 5) demonstrates that the model's reconstructed FC is faithful to the empirical data across temporal delays, approaching the test-retest ceiling.
- The honest caveat in the Discussion (p. 18) about the model being phenomenological and fit to infraslow BOLD, not electrophysiology, is appropriate and sets realistic expectations for translational work.
- The dissociation between pathology sites (subcortical/limbic) and therapy sites (cortical) is consistent with network-medicine principles and lends biological plausibility to the findings.
- The comparison of the empirical lagged FC (AUROC ≈ 0.71) to the model-based FC-lag (AUROC ≈ 0.70) is transparent and shows the model repackages rather than adds diagnostic information, which is an honest assessment.

## Questions
- In Fig. S9, the population-average LOPO result (~78% net cure) is substantially weaker than the personalised transductive result (100%). Should the main text emphasize the ~78% figure as the honest, fully independent estimate, rather than the 100% personalised result?
- The therapy-target selection (LDA-resonant) is defined by its effect on the FC-lag discriminant, even under LOPO cross-validation. Is this a true out-of-sample prediction, or is it per-patient optimization against a train-only threshold? How would you distinguish between these?
- Have you performed an ablation or sensitivity analysis on the reservoir hyperparameters (N, spectral radius, τ, σ_in)? If the dissociation between pathology and therapy sites depends on these fixed choices, the generalizability of the findings would be questionable.
- The tangent-space SVM benchmark (Fig. 3E) uses different preprocessing and cross-validation than the main analysis. Could you re-run the benchmark on the same data/splits to make a fair comparison?
- The empirical lagged FC is as informative as the model-based FC-lag (both AUROC ≈ 0.70–0.71). Why not use the empirical lagged FC directly for the stimulation analysis? Does the model add any mechanistic insight beyond repackaging the signal?
- The claim that the AD signature is 'functionally irreducible' to focal sites is based on single-site stimulation failing at physiological amplitudes. Have you explored sparse multi-site interventions (e.g., 5–10 sites) to test whether a distributed pattern could be more efficient than full-network correction?
- The optimal driving frequency (f_eig ≈ 0.026 Hz) is derived from the BOLD-based model. Is there any independent evidence (electrophysiology, animal models, prior human studies) that this frequency is biologically relevant for AD or neuromodulation?
- The personalised LDA-resonant sites are highly heterogeneous (33 distinct sites for 40 patients, Fig. 6D). How much of this heterogeneity reflects genuine individual differences versus overfitting to the training cohort? Would a larger, independent cohort show similar heterogeneity?
- The 'most-affected' sites (Fig. 6A,B) are ranked by ||ΔW||, but you note these are weakly inter-connected nodes where the read-out is least constrained. Could the large ||ΔW|| reflect poor estimation rather than pathology? How would you distinguish these?
- The closed-loop controller (Fig. 5D–F) uses a sliding-window estimate of the FC-lag biomarker. How is the window length and update frequency chosen? How sensitive is the closed-loop efficacy to these parameters?
