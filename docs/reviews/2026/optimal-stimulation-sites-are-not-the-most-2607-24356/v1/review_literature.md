# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript's citations are substantially accurate and grounded in appropriate primary sources for foundational work on reservoir computing, AD connectivity, and whole-brain modeling. However, there are two hard issues requiring attention: (1) clarification of Braak staging attribution for specific subcortical/limbic structures, and (2) omission of directly competing recent work on personalized neuromodulation targeting in AD (Luppi et al. 2026, Bonnì et al. 2026), which weakens novelty positioning and translational plausibility claims. Several soft issues—outdated citations (Koch et al. 2022 vs. 2025), missing recent in-silico perturbation work, and uncited Riemannian classifier methods—should be addressed to strengthen literature positioning. The omissions do not invalidate the work but require acknowledgment of concurrent efforts and clarification of specific contribution relative to emerging personalized neuromodulation literature.

## Strengths
- Accurate attribution of foundational methods: Echo-state networks (Jaeger 2001, ref. 12; Maass et al. 2002, ref. 13) and reservoir computing principles are correctly cited as primary sources.
- Appropriate grounding in AD connectivity literature: Canonical references on default-mode network disruption in AD (Greicius et al. 2004, ref. 3; Sheline & Raichle 2013, ref. 4) and AD as disconnection syndrome (Delbeuck et al. 2003, ref. 2; Seeley et al. 2009, ref. 1) are properly cited.
- Whole-brain modeling precedent well-documented: References to Honey et al. (2009, ref. 7), Jirsa et al. (2017, ref. 10), and Wang et al. (2019, ref. 11) establish the personalized modeling framework appropriately.
- Gamma stimulation claims supported: Cited work on 40 Hz stimulation (Iaccarino et al. 2016, ref. 31; Martorell et al. 2019, ref. 32) is accurate and current.
- Network medicine principle cited: Fox et al. (2014, ref. 6) on resting-state networks and neuromodulation is correctly invoked for the principle that optimal targets are network-coupled, not lesion-localized.

## Weaknesses
- Braak staging attribution unclear: The manuscript states regions 'overlap with regions harbouring early neurofibrillary pathology in Braak staging' (Discussion, p. 16) citing refs. 27–28, but does not specify which regions in the current parcellation correspond to Braak stages I–II or explicitly map subcortical/limbic structures (pallidum, accumbens, brainstem, amygdala) to early Braak stages; the claim is plausible but attribution should be more precise.
- Missing directly competing recent work on personalized neuromodulation targeting in AD: The manuscript does not cite Luppi et al. (2026, eNeuro) on connectome-guided personalization of optimal tDCS intervention selection in Alzheimer's disease, which is a directly parallel study using connectome-based models to identify personalized stimulation targets in AD.
- Missing Bonnì et al. (2026, Alzheimer's Research & Therapy) on personalized combined magnetic/electrical stimulation of the DMN in mild AD, which is a clinical trial protocol directly relevant to the translational claims in the Discussion; this omission weakens the novelty claim and positioning against concurrent work.
- Koch et al. (2022) citation may be outdated: Reference 22 cites the 2022 Brain publication, but a 2025 follow-up exists (Koch et al., Alzheimer's Research & Therapy, 2025) reporting longer-term effects of 52 weeks of precuneus rTMS; clarification is needed on whether 2022 is the initial trial and 2025 is follow-up, or if 2025 supersedes the 2022 report.
- Limited coverage of recent in-silico perturbation work: The manuscript cites Spiegler et al. (2016), Muldoon et al. (2016), Kunze et al. (2016), and Deco et al. (2019) on connectome-based stimulation modeling, but more recent work on digital twin brain models and in-silico perturbation (e.g., 2025 publications on personalized long-term recovery and precision non-invasive brain stimulation) exists and should be cited.
- Infraslow oscillation literature attribution unclear: The citation of DiAntonio et al. (2026, ref. 20) as a reference for infraslow connectivity is unusual—that paper is about 'Linearizing and forecasting: A reservoir computing route to digital twins of the brain' and may not be the primary source for infraslow oscillation evidence; Fox & Raichle (2007) or Cordes et al. (2001) may be more direct.
- Riemannian/tangent-space FC classifier benchmark uncited: The manuscript uses a tangent-space SVM classifier (Fig. 3E, Methods) as a leakage-free baseline but does not cite prior work on Riemannian geometry for FC classification, which is a standard method that should be cited for reproducibility and positioning.
- Transductive vs. inductive embedding not discussed in related work: The manuscript acknowledges (Discussion, p. 18) that the embedding is 'transductive' (built across all patients), but the related-work section does not discuss trade-offs between transductive and inductive embeddings in personalized models, which would strengthen the framing.

## Questions
- Can you cite the specific Braak stage mapping for the subcortical/limbic structures (pallidum, accumbens, brainstem, amygdala) identified as most-affected in Fig. 6A? The current citation (refs. 27–28) does not explicitly link these parcels to early Braak stages in the text.
- Are you aware of the concurrent connectome-guided personalization work by Luppi et al. (eNeuro, 2026) and the clinical trial protocol by Bonnì et al. (Alzheimer's Research & Therapy, 2026)? If so, how does your work differ in scope or method? If not, these should be cited in revision as directly relevant concurrent work.
- Is the 2022 Koch et al. citation the initial trial report, with the 2025 follow-up being a longer-term outcome? Please clarify the relationship and consider citing the 2025 publication if it is the more complete report.
- The tangent-space SVM benchmark (Fig. 3E) is a standard method but lacks a citation. Can you provide a reference for the Riemannian geometry approach to FC classification, or confirm that this is a standard application of existing methods?
- Is DiAntonio et al. (ref. 20) the appropriate citation for the claim that resting-state connectivity fluctuates in the infraslow band, or would Fox & Raichle (2007) or Cordes et al. (2001) be more direct? The DiAntonio paper appears to be about reservoir computing and digital twins rather than infraslow oscillations per se.
