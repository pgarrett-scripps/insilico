## Concessions
- **methodology** — Under leave-one-AD-patient-out validation, the personalised LDA-resonant site is still chosen by a 121-way argmax evaluated on the held-out patient's own simulated response, so only the classifier/threshold is truly out-of-sample, not the site-selection search itself.
  - Proposed change: In the revised Methods and Results we will relabel the per-patient LOPO result explicitly as an 'upper bound on achievable personalisation' everywhere it is quoted, including the abstract, and will promote the single population-average LDA-resonant site result (~78% net cure, chosen entirely from training patients with no per-test-patient search) to the primary leakage-free headline number, with the per-patient figure reported alongside as a ceiling estimate rather than the flagship claim.
- **rigor** — Causal/therapeutic language ('stimulation', 'cure', 'prescribes how to stimulate') is used in the abstract, title and Results to describe read-out interpolations and sinusoidal inputs to a fixed random linear reservoir fit to infraslow BOLD, without the caveat that this is a model-level perturbation, not a physical intervention.
  - Proposed change: We will rewrite the abstract, title framing, and the opening paragraphs of the Results/Discussion to consistently use 'in-silico perturbation' or 'model-prescribed correction' in place of unqualified 'stimulation'/'cure', and will move the existing Discussion caveat ('the reservoir is fit to infraslow BOLD connectivity...an intervention here is an abstract input to the regional FC dynamics') into the Results section immediately before Fig. 4, rather than leaving it only in the Discussion.
- **rigor** — The claimed disjointness between pathology sites (ΔW-ranked) and therapy sites (LDA-resonant) in Fig. 6 (11/35 and 11/33 shared, ~31–33%) is asserted qualitatively but never tested against the overlap expected from two random subsets of the 121-parcel atlas.
  - Proposed change: We will add a permutation test (10,000 draws of two random subsets of sizes 35 and 33 from the 121 parcels) to Fig. 6's caption/Methods, reporting the empirical p-value for the observed overlap being smaller than chance, and will soften the Fig. 6 title and Discussion sentence accordingly if the test does not reach significance at the parcel level.
- **clarity** — The distinction between read-out interpolation (α) and oscillatory stimulation (amplitude A) is introduced only in Methods, after being used throughout Figs. 4–5, causing confusion about which protocol is theoretical vs. physical.
  - Proposed change: We will add a short paragraph at the start of the 'In-silico stimulation' Results subsection explicitly naming and contrasting the two protocols (read-out interpolation α vs. input oscillation amplitude A) before Fig. 4 is introduced, cross-referencing the Methods definitions.
- **clarity** — The transductive vs. leave-one-patient-out cross-validation distinction, central to the leakage-free claim, is only explained in Methods/Fig. S9 and referenced opaquely in the main text ('honest cross-validation').
  - Proposed change: We will add 2–3 sentences in the Results, before the paragraph beginning 'A natural concern is that this reclassification could be inflated...', defining transductive embedding versus leave-one-patient-out reconstruction in plain language, and will state the baseline misclassification shift (17.5%→42.5%) and its cause explicitly at first mention.
- **clarity** — 'Physiological amplitude' is used repeatedly to justify that focal stimulation fails 'at any amplitude' but is never given a numerical threshold.
  - Proposed change: We will define a concrete threshold (RMS_pert/RMS_base ≤ 2, matching the full-W envelope in Fig. S10A) in Methods 'Physiological diagnostics' and explicitly state this threshold in the Results wherever 'physiological/supra-physiological amplitude' is invoked.
- **literature** — The manuscript omits directly competing/concurrent work on personalised neuromodulation targeting in AD (Luppi et al. 2026; Bonnì et al. 2026) and lacks a citation for the tangent-space/Riemannian FC classification method used as a benchmark.
  - Proposed change: We will add citations to Luppi et al. (2026, eNeuro) and Bonnì et al. (2026, Alzheimer's Research & Therapy) in the Discussion where translational plausibility is discussed, explicitly contrasting scope/method, and will add a standard Riemannian-geometry FC classification citation (e.g., Barachant et al.) in the Methods description of the tangent-space SVM benchmark.
- **reproducibility** — Software library versions, the exact order of operations for injecting the oscillatory drive relative to teacher-forced input, and the reservoir state initialisation at closed-loop reset are not specified.
  - Proposed change: We will add a reproducibility subsection listing exact package versions (Python, NumPy, scikit-learn, nilearn, Matplotlib), state explicitly that the closed-loop reservoir state is inherited from the teacher-forced warmup (not reset to zero) before the free-run, and clarify that the sinusoid is added to, not substituted for, the teacher-forced input channel u(t)=f·ŝ(t) during oscillatory stimulation trials.
- **data_analysis** — No confidence intervals are reported on primary classification metrics (AUROC, balanced accuracy) across folds, only mean±SD.
  - Proposed change: We will add bootstrap 95% confidence intervals (patient-level resampling, 2000 iterations) for AUROC and balanced accuracy in Fig. 3A–D and the tangent-space benchmark, reported in the figure legends and a new supplementary table.
- **methodology** — Reservoir hyperparameters (N, spectral radius, τ, σ_in) are fixed without ablation, leaving open whether the pathology/therapy dissociation depends on this specific reservoir's representational geometry rather than being a general network property.
  - Proposed change: We will add a supplementary sensitivity analysis re-running the pathology-site ranking and LDA-resonant site selection for 2–3 alternative reservoir configurations (varying N∈{1000,2000,4000} and spectral radius∈{0.85,0.95,0.99}) on a subset of patients, reporting whether the cortical/subcortical anatomical dissociation and the qualitative focal-vs-distributed result are preserved.

## Disagreements
- **methodology** — The transductive embedding used for classification (Figs. 3–5) constitutes leakage that undermines the main classification and stimulation results, not just the personalised site-selection step.
  - Response: The transductive step builds only the shared SVD/Gram basis across all patients; the discriminant (LDA/RF) is always trained and evaluated on disjoint cross-validation folds within that fixed basis, which is standard practice for population-level feature spaces (e.g., PCA before nested CV) and does not let fold-test labels or discriminant parameters leak into training. This is explicitly distinct from, and less severe than, the site-search leakage we concede above, which we agree needs a dedicated fix. We will keep the transductive results as the main analysis (as is conventional) but foreground the fully independent LOPO reconstruction (Fig. S9) as the confirmatory check, per our concession.
  - Manuscript reference: Methods, 'Classification': 'The SVD embeddings were built transductively (across all patients) while the classifiers were trained and tested on disjoint folds.'
- **novelty** — Reservoir computing for brain dynamics is an established technique, so the contribution is largely incremental application rather than a new method.
  - Response: We agree reservoir computing itself is not new, but the contribution is not the reservoir architecture — it is the demonstration that a per-subject, cross-subject-identifiable read-out (validated in Fig. 2F–H, within- vs cross-subject r=0.72 vs 0.34) can serve simultaneously as a diagnostic feature and as a perturbable counterfactual testbed, letting us ask and answer a causal question (focal vs. distributed correction; pathology vs. therapy site dissociation) that purely observational FC studies cannot address. That causal-testbed use, and the resulting site/frequency prescription, is the claimed contribution, not the reservoir formalism per se.
  - Manuscript reference: Results, 'A subject-specific, identifiable reservoir model of FC': 'the correlation between a subject's simulated and empirical FC was far higher within- than across-subjects (Fig. 2F,G,H), establishing that the fitted models are individually identifiable.'
- **rigor** — The claim that the AD signature is 'distributed' conflates a property of the disease with a property of the model's representational geometry, since the evidence only shows the model's read-out requires distributed correction.
  - Response: We accept this distinction should be made more explicit (see concession on causal language), but we do not agree the finding is merely a model artifact: the negative result — that the most-deviant ΔW sites (a data-driven, not model-architecture-driven, quantity) fail to revert classification even far beyond physiological amplitude, while a fully distributed correction succeeds near baseline amplitude — is a property of how AD-vs-CC differences are organized in the fitted per-subject read-outs, which are themselves validated as faithful, identifiable reconstructions of each subject's empirical connectivity (Fig. 2). The model is the necessary instrument for the causal test, but the asymmetry between focal and distributed correction is an empirical finding about the data through that instrument, not an assumption built into the reservoir.
  - Manuscript reference: Results, 'In-silico stimulation': 'applying the same correction to focal site sets...failed to revert the classification within physiological amplitude limits...In sharp contrast...full-W...moved both classifiers' scores into the CC range at small interpolation strength.'
