# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
The statistical and data-analytic framework is generally sound and well-documented, with appropriate cross-validation, honest reporting of modest classification accuracy, and careful handling of multiple comparisons in the stimulation analysis. The main strengths are transparent disclosure of sample-size limitations, leakage-free validation of the therapeutic effect, and proper separation of training and test sets. However, there are several soft issues around effect-size reporting, confidence intervals on key metrics, and some ambiguities in how certain statistical quantities are defined or combined across analyses. The work is suitable for publication with minor revisions to clarify statistical reporting.

## Strengths
- Proper nested cross-validation (5×5 patient-level) for the tangent-space SVM benchmark with a train-only tangent reference, explicitly avoiding leakage into feature construction (Methods, Fig. 3E).
- Honest leave-one-AD-patient-out (LOPO) validation of the therapeutic reclassification effect (Fig. S9, Methods), rebuilding the embedding, LDA, threshold, and site-selection criterion from training patients only, with transparent reporting of the higher baseline misclassification rate under this leakage-free scheme.
- Clear disclosure that classification accuracy is modest (AUROC ~0.66–0.70) and sample-size limited, with a learning-curve analysis (Fig. S1) projecting improvement to ~0.76–0.80 AUROC at N=100–200, explicitly stating that present accuracy reflects cohort size rather than a representational ceiling.
- Balanced accuracy and AUROC reported alongside each other, appropriate for the imbalanced cohort (36 CC vs 40 AD in the main analysis), with repeated stratified 5-fold CV (10 repeats) and mean±SD reported over folds.
- Proper two-sided Mann–Whitney U tests for group comparisons (e.g., Fig. 1C, Fig. 4A,D) with exact p-values stated and n clearly identified.
- Transparent reporting of hyperparameter sweeps (K ∈ [2,50], σ ∈ [0.003,0.5]) with reference slices chosen by marginal-mean AUROC, and sensitivity analysis showing robustness to regularisation noise (Fig. 3C,D).
- Physiological diagnostics (Fig. S10) quantifying signal-amplitude ratios (RMS_pert/RMS_base) to verify that reclassification is achieved within plausible bounds, a thoughtful check on biological realism.
- Explicit statement of the transductive embedding (SVD built across all patients) while classifiers are trained/tested on disjoint folds, clarifying the design choice and its implications.

## Weaknesses
- No confidence intervals reported on the primary classification metrics (AUROC, balanced accuracy) across folds; only mean±SD is given. A 95% CI on AUROC for each classifier would strengthen claims about the modest but above-chance performance and its comparison to the tangent-space benchmark.
- The tangent-space SVM benchmark (AUROC 0.75±0.12) is reported as mean±SD over nested CV, but the definition of 'SD' is ambiguous—is this the SD of fold-level AUROCs, or a pooled estimate? The out-of-fold ROC (Fig. 3E) shows AUC≈0.71, which sits at the lower end of the reported range, raising a question about how the mean and SD are computed.
- In Fig. 3A–D, the 'reference noise σ' and 'reference K' are chosen by 'marginal-mean AUROC across classifiers,' but it is unclear whether this selection was made on the training set only or involved the test set in any way. If the reference slices were chosen post-hoc to maximise performance, this could introduce a soft bias.
- The learning-curve extrapolation (Fig. S1) fits a saturating power law m(N) = A − B·N^(−C) to leave-one-patient-out data, but no confidence band or uncertainty quantification is provided for the projected AUROC at N=100 and N=200. The 68% band shown is from the fit covariance, but the extrapolation range is large and the fit quality is not reported (R², residual plot).
- Effect sizes (e.g., the dissociation between pathology sites and therapy sites) are reported qualitatively (e.g., 'only 11 sites shared, ~31% and ~33% of each set'; Fig. 6C) but not with a formal overlap metric or Jaccard index, making the magnitude of the dissociation harder to quantify.
- The per-patient amplitude×frequency stimulation landscape (Fig. S8) shows individual and population-level heatmaps, but no summary statistic (e.g., median or IQR of the amplitude needed to cross the decision boundary at resonance) is provided across the 40 patients, limiting reproducibility and generalisability assessment.
- In the oscillatory stimulation analysis (Methods, 'Oscillatory stimulation'), the choice of frequency sweep range [0, 0.5] cycles/step is not justified, and it is unclear whether this range was chosen a priori or post-hoc based on preliminary results.
- The 'net cure' metric in Fig. S9 (fraction of AD-side patients moved to CC side by stimulation) is a sensible leakage-free measure, but the baseline misclassification rate (42.5% under LOPO) is substantially higher than the transductive baseline (17.5%), making the comparison between transductive and LOPO results harder to interpret without a clearer statement of what is being held constant.
- No multiple-comparison correction is applied across the 121 sites tested in the per-site stimulation analysis (Fig. S6A, where 'almost every site is therapeutic on average'). Although the authors note that 120/121 sites move the score toward CC, no Bonferroni or FDR correction is mentioned, and it is unclear whether this is a single exploratory analysis or a hypothesis test.

## Questions
- In Fig. 3, the 'reference slices' for K and σ are chosen by 'marginal-mean AUROC across classifiers'—was this selection made on the training set only, or did it involve any inspection of test-set performance? If the latter, this could introduce selection bias.
- The tangent-space SVM benchmark reports AUROC 0.75±0.12 as 'mean±SD over a nested 5×5 patient-level CV.' Can you clarify whether this SD is the standard deviation of the 25 fold-level AUROCs, or a pooled estimate? The out-of-fold ROC (Fig. 3E) shows AUC≈0.71, which is at the lower end of the range; how do these relate?
- In Fig. S1, the learning-curve fit m(N) = A − B·N^(−C) is extrapolated to N=100 and N=200. What is the R² of this fit, and how sensitive are the projected AUROC values to the choice of functional form (e.g., would a different saturating model yield substantially different projections)?
- For the oscillatory stimulation analysis, was the frequency sweep range [0, 0.5] cycles/step chosen a priori, or was it selected based on preliminary inspection of the data? If the latter, a sensitivity analysis over a wider range would strengthen the claim that f_eig is the true optimum.
- In Fig. S6A, you report that 120/121 sites are therapeutic on average at f_1 (A=4). Given that 121 sites are tested, should a multiple-comparison correction (e.g., Bonferroni) be applied to determine which sites are significantly therapeutic? Or is this an exploratory, non-inferential analysis?
- The 'net cure' metric in Fig. S9 compares transductive (baseline 17.5% misclassification) and LOPO (baseline 42.5% misclassification) schemes. Can you provide a side-by-side comparison of the stimulation effect (absolute reclassification rate) under both schemes, to clarify whether the LOPO result is genuinely comparable or reflects a different operating point?
- In the Methods section ('Leakage-free target selection'), you state that the personalised per-patient LDA-resonant site is 'an upper bound on achievable personalisation.' Can you quantify this bound—e.g., how much of the 100% net cure under LOPO personalisation is due to the per-patient argmax over 121 sites, versus how much would be achieved by a fixed global site?
- For the read-out interpolation analysis (Figs. 4, S10), the interpolation strength α is allowed to exceed 1 (α > 1 = over-correction). What is the justification for exploring α > 1, and does the closed-loop dynamics guarantee that α=1 (full replacement by the CC-mean read-out) is insufficient, or is this an empirical observation? A sensitivity analysis showing the effect of capping α at 1 would clarify this.
