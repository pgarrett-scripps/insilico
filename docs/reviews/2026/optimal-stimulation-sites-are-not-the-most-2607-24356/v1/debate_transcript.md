# Debate Transcript

## Advocate — round 1

This manuscript makes a genuine, well-executed contribution: it moves AD connectivity research from observational correlation to a causal, counterfactual testbed. The identifiability validation (Fig. 2F–H, within-subject r≈0.72 vs cross-subject r≈0.34) is rigorous and establishes that the reservoir models are not generic fits but individualised generative surrogates—a real methodological achievement the methodology reviewer credits ('well-motivated and addresses a genuine gap'). The core finding—that ΔW-ranked focal targeting fails even at supra-physiological amplitude (Fig. 4C,F, S10) while discriminant-aligned single-site targeting succeeds (Fig. 5A)—is large, consistent across two independent read-outs (G-space, FC-lag) and two classifiers, and survives leave-one-AD-patient-out reconstruction of the entire pipeline (Fig. S9), which the data_analysis and methodology reviewers both acknowledge as a substantive leakage check, not window dressing. The authors are also unusually transparent: they report structural atrophy outperforms their own FC classifiers (Note S1), disclose modest AUROC with learning-curve evidence it's sample-limited (Fig. S1), and flag the ΔW-anatomy caveat themselves (Discussion, 'weakly inter-connected nodes... where the read-out is least constrained').

The rigor reviewer's overclaiming concerns are legitimate but are almost entirely about *framing*—abstract wording, hedging placement, missing overlap statistics—not about the underlying evidence, which the same reviewer calls 'a genuine dissociation.' These are revision-level fixes: tempering causal language, adding a permutation test for Fig. 6 overlap, moving Fig. S9 numbers into the main text. None require new experiments or invalidate the pathology/therapy dissociation itself. Similarly, clarity issues (α vs A, transductive vs LOPO exposition) are presentational, explicitly flagged by the clarity reviewer as 'fixable.' The paper's substance—an identifiable model turning 'where to stimulate' into a computable, testable prescription—merits publication with these revisions.

**Key points:**
- Rigorous identifiability validation (within- vs cross-subject FC reconstruction, Fig. 2F–H) establishes genuine subject-specific generative models, not generic fits.
- The pathology-vs-therapy dissociation is large, robust across two read-outs/classifiers, and survives leave-one-AD-patient-out reconstruction of the full pipeline (Fig. S9), addressed by multiple reviewers as a substantive check.
- Authors demonstrate unusual transparency: honest disclosure of modest AUROC, structural-atrophy comparison, sample-size-limited learning curve (Fig. S1), and self-flagged ΔW anatomical caveat.
- Rigor reviewer's overclaiming critiques target abstract framing and hedging, not the underlying evidence, and are fixable via wording/statistics additions rather than new experiments.
- Clarity issues (α vs A distinction, transductive/LOPO exposition) are explicitly characterized as presentational and fixable by the clarity reviewer.

## Skeptic — round 1

The advocate's strongest concession undercuts their own case: Fig. S9 rebuilds the *classifier* out-of-sample, but it does not resolve the deeper problem the methodology reviewer flags as HARD — 'the therapy-target selection still uses a per-patient argmax over 121 sites, each scored against the train-only threshold... This is not a fully independent validation.' Selecting a site by trying all 121 candidates on that same patient's own simulated response and keeping whichever one moves the score furthest is a 121-way search for a favorable result; only the *threshold* is honest, not the site-discovery process. That is a different, unaddressed leakage channel from classifier leakage, and it sits directly under the abstract's central claim of 'complete, individualised reclassification.' This is FIXABLE only if the authors can show personalised targets generalize under a procedure where the site itself, not just the LDA boundary, is chosen without touching the test patient — otherwise the headline result is partly an artifact of exhaustive per-patient search.//Second, the rigor reviewer's HARD point stands unrebutted: 'stimulation,' 'cure,' and 'therapeutic' describe read-out interpolations and sinusoidal inputs to a fixed random linear reservoir fit to BOLD, not tissue. The Discussion buries this caveat while the abstract asserts the model tells us 'where and how to stimulate.' This is FIXABLE by rewording, but as written it overclaims translational meaning the phenomenological model cannot support.//These are real, reviewer-corroborated defects in the paper's most load-bearing claims, not stylistic nitpicks.

**Key points:**
- The per-patient LDA-resonant site is chosen via a 121-way argmax evaluated on the test patient's own simulated data; only the classifier threshold is leave-one-out, not the site-search itself — methodology reviewer flags this explicitly as HARD and unresolved by Fig. S9.
- This distinct leakage channel (intervention-selection overfitting) directly undermines the abstract's flagship claim of 'complete, individualised reclassification from one site.'
- Rigor reviewer's HARD objection that causal/therapeutic language ('cure', 'stimulation') is applied to abstract read-out edits on a phenomenological BOLD-fit reservoir remains unanswered by the advocate.
- Both issues are fixable via revision (independent site-selection validation, toned-down language) but are currently load-bearing overclaims, not mere presentation nits.
