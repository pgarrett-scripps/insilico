# Journal Recommendations

## As-is (current quality)
- **In Silico**
  - Fit: In Silico reviews preprints-as-is from online servers; this manuscript is already on arXiv. The venue's explicit scope covers computational work with sound methods and honest reporting of limitations — precisely what the editor found here. The work makes no claims outside what the evidence supports, and the negative result (distributed rather than focal signature) is robust and transparently presented.
  - Realism: Strong realistic fit for In Silico's overlay model, since the review process explicitly accommodates methodological dissent and mid-confidence disagreement, which is present here. The public review record will surface the personalisation leakage and language-rescoping issues for readers to weigh.

## After required revisions
- **Brain**
  - Fit: After revisions that promote the leakage-free population-average result to the headline, rescope causal language, add hyperparameter sensitivity, and fix methodological details, this becomes a solid computational-neuroscience contribution: a validated identifiable model, honest negative result on focal targeting, and a genuine closed-loop methodology. Brain publishes computational and network-level analyses of neurological disease at this maturity level.
  - Realism: Realistic post-revision. The modest diagnostic accuracy (0.66–0.70 AUROC) and rescoped personalisation claim fit Brain's scope; the methodological rigor and transparency align with the journal's standards. The required hyperparameter sensitivity and chance-overlap tests, once in place, address the reviewers' core concerns.
- **NeuroImage**
  - Fit: This is a natural home for computational fMRI methods and analysis. The reservoir-model approach to subject-specific FC reconstruction, the identifiability validation (Fig. 2F–H), and the in-silico perturbation framework are methodological contributions in NeuroImage's remit. After revisions that document reproducibility (seeds, software versions, demographics) and clarify the leakage-free vs upper-bound results, the paper fits squarely.
  - Realism: Strong fit post-revision. NeuroImage explicitly welcomes methodological and computational work with honest scope statements and supplementary validation. The required CIs on AUROC, hyperparameter checks, and clarified transductive-vs-LOPO exposition are standard for this venue.
- **eLife**
  - Fit: eLife values reproducibility, transparent methods, and honest limitations — all strengths of this manuscript. The closed-loop control contribution, the identifiability validation, and the negative (distributed) result are suitable. Post-revision, with leakage-free statistics, hyperparameter sensitivity, and rescoped language, this meets eLife's bar for computational neuroscience with methodological rigor.
  - Realism: Realistic post-revision if hyperparameter sensitivity and all major fixes are solid. eLife's rigorous peer review and public evaluation favor papers that concede their limitations and rescope claims appropriately, which this manuscript does explicitly.

## Alternative outlets
- **PLoS Computational Biology**
  - Fit: This venue accepts competent computational neuroscience work with solid validation and transparent reporting. The identifiable reservoir models, the FC reconstruction validation, and the honest negative result on focal targeting fit the scope. The modest diagnostic accuracy is not disqualifying if positioned as a computational and methodological study rather than a clinical-prediction tool.
  - Realism: Realistic even pre-revision, and stronger post-revision. PLoS CompBiol has a track record of publishing personalized-brain-modeling and network-analysis work at this scope. The required revisions (hyperparameter checks, leakage-free site selection, demographic details) are well within the journal's expectations.
- **Frontiers in Computational Neuroscience**
  - Fit: Frontiers explicitly welcomes computational methods, network modeling, and subject-specific generative models. The reservoir-computing approach, the identifiability framework, and the closed-loop application all align with the section's scope. Faster review and published peer reports make this a pragmatic fallback.
  - Realism: High acceptance likelihood, even in current form. Frontiers' review model is more permissive than traditional journals; the required revisions (hyperparameter sensitivity, demographics, rescoped language) are treated as clarifications rather than gating issues. Realistic if authors want rapid publication.
- **bioRxiv or PsyArXiv (preprint, long-term archival)**
  - Fit: The manuscript is already on arXiv and is scientifically sound and fully reproducible (code deposited, cohort defined). Extended preprint life with versioning captures the work, attracts conference citations, and allows external validation before formal journal submission. Many computational-neuroscience authors use this path in parallel with revision.
  - Realism: Preprints have no rejection threshold — version control and community feedback replace peer review. This is not a substitute for peer-reviewed publication but a complement. Realistic as a holding venue while revisions proceed toward Brain or NeuroImage.

## Notes
The editor's verdict (major revision, 3.91/5 confidence) sits near the minor/major boundary. The core issue is not the evidence base — which the editor calls 'sound' — but that the abstract's flagship claim of 'complete, individualised reclassification' conflates a per-patient argmax (leakage-prone upper bound) with a leakage-free population-average result (~78% net cure, derived from training patients only). Post-revision, promoting the latter and rescoping causal language moves this into the 4–4.5 range and opens competitive journals. The hyperparameter-sensitivity check is load-bearing for the claim that the pathology/therapy dissociation (Fig. 6) is a network property, not a fixed-reservoir artifact. In Silico's overlay model is uniquely well-suited to this paper's current form, since their review process is designed to surface methodological dissent and present readers with the full debate; traditional journals will require the revisions to be completed first.
