# Meta-Review

**Draft recommendation:** major

## Synthesis
The panel agrees on the substance: this is a competently executed, transparent study that fits subject-specific reservoir models to ADNI resting-state fMRI, validates their individual identifiability (within- vs cross-subject FC reconstruction, Fig. 2F–H), and uses them as counterfactual testbeds to ask whether the AD FC signature is focal or distributed. Ethics is clean and confidently scored (5@5). Clarity, data_analysis, literature, novelty and reproducibility all sit at 4@4 with issues that are genuinely revision-level: definitional ambiguities (physiological amplitude, connectivity kernel, read-out senses), missing CIs and fit diagnostics on the learning-curve extrapolation, uncited concurrent personalized-neuromodulation work (Luppi 2026, Bonni 2026) and an uncited Riemannian/tangent-space baseline, and unspecified software versions and embedding/order-of-operations details.

The split is between that majority and the two 3@4 reviewers (methodology, rigor), and the debate sharpened rather than dissolved it. The advocate's framing — that the rigor complaints are about wording and the leakage question is settled by Fig. S9 — did not survive the skeptic's reply on one specific point. Methodology's HARD critique is that the leave-one-AD-patient-out analysis rebuilds the embedding, LDA and threshold out-of-sample, but the personalised therapy site is still chosen by a 121-way argmax evaluated on the held-out patient's own simulated response. That is an intervention-selection leakage channel distinct from classifier leakage, and it sits directly beneath the abstract's flagship claim of 'complete, individualised reclassification from one site.' The authors themselves call this an 'upper bound' in Methods, which is candid but is not carried into the abstract. Novelty independently flags the same circularity: the LDA-resonant site succeeds partly by construction, because it is selected to move the very discriminant used to score success.

A second surviving critique is rigor's: the paper's most quotable claims ('where and how to stimulate', 'cure', 'therapeutic') describe read-out interpolations and sinusoidal inputs to a fixed random linear reservoir fit to infraslow BOLD. The Discussion states this caveat plainly; the abstract and title do not. Related, rigor notes the headline dissociation in Fig. 6 (11 shared sites, ~31–33% of each set) is never tested against chance overlap — a quantitative claim resting on a visual comparison. Methodology adds that the reservoir hyperparameters (N, spectral radius, tau, sigma_in) are never ablated, so it is untested whether the pathology/therapy dissociation is a network property or a property of the fixed reservoir geometry; and that the resonant frequency f_eig derives from J, not from the patients, which makes the 'prescription' partly a model artifact. The authors' own caveat that the largest-||dW|| sites are the weakly connected nodes where the read-out is least constrained further weakens Fig. 6A/B as a pathology map.

What is not in dispute: the negative result (focal ΔW-ranked targeting fails even at supra-physiological amplitude) is robust and honestly reported, the closed-loop controller is a real methodological contribution, and the reporting standard is above average for the field.

## Decisive Factors
I land slightly below the confidence-weighted average (3.91, majority 'minor') and call for major revision, on the following reasoning. The 'minor' votes are concentrated in dimensions — clarity, citations, reproducibility detail, statistical reporting — where the fixes are additive and do not touch the conclusions. The two 'major' votes are both at confidence 4 and both land on the same load-bearing claim, from independent angles (methodology: intervention-selection leakage; rigor: causal/translational overclaim), with novelty's circularity point corroborating from a third. When two mid-confidence dissenters converge on the paper's headline sentence rather than on peripheral matters, that outweighs a broad but diffuse majority of 4s.

Concretely, three things must change before the abstract's claims are supportable, and two of them are more than rewording. (1) Report an intervention-selection-honest estimate of the therapy effect: the per-patient site must be chosen without evaluating candidates on the held-out patient (e.g. site chosen from training patients' consensus, or a nested search), with the current per-patient argmax result relabelled explicitly as an upper bound wherever it appears, including the abstract. (2) Provide a chance-overlap test (permutation over the 121 parcels) for the Fig. 6 pathology/therapy disjointness, which is currently asserted quantitatively but never tested. (3) Rescope causal and translational language in title, abstract and Results so that 'stimulation', 'cure' and 'prescribes how to stimulate' are qualified as abstract perturbations of a phenomenological BOLD-fit model — the Discussion caveat moved forward, not deleted. A reservoir-hyperparameter sensitivity check (even a coarse one over spectral radius and N) would substantially strengthen the claim that the dissociation is a network property rather than a property of J; I regard this as strongly desirable rather than strictly required. The remaining items — CIs on AUROC, learning-curve fit quality, the missing concurrent-work citations, software versions, and the transductive-vs-LOPO exposition — should be handled alongside but would not by themselves have moved the verdict.

None of this threatens the paper's survival. The negative result and the identifiability validation stand on their own, and the authors' own candour makes the required corrections small in substance if large in framing. Major rather than reject, because the evidence base is sound and the problem is that the claims are pinned to the least-defended part of it.
