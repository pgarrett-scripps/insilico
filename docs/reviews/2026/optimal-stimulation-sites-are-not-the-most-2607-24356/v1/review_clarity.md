# Clarity & Presentation Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript is generally well-written and logically structured, with clear motivation, methods, and results flow. The central claim—that AD's FC signature is distributed rather than focal, and that therapeutic targets differ from pathology sites—is stated explicitly and supported throughout. Figures are informative and mostly well-captioned. However, several HARD clarity issues impede full understanding: the distinction between 'read-out interpolation' (α parameter) and 'oscillatory stimulation' (amplitude A) is introduced late and remains confusing; the transductive vs. leave-one-out cross-validation distinction is buried in Methods; and some quantitative comparisons lack explicit baselines. These are fixable but currently create friction for readers unfamiliar with reservoir computing.

## Strengths
- The abstract and introduction clearly frame the central question: whether AD's FC signature is focal or distributed, and why this matters for intervention.
- The narrative arc is explicit and well-signposted: from reading out disease (classification) to intervening on it (stimulation), with a clear transition at 'In-silico stimulation' section.
- Methods are presented in logical order (data → reservoir fit → read-out fitting → closed-loop reconstruction → classification → stimulation), with each step's purpose clear.
- Figures are generally well-captioned with legends defining symbols and abbreviations; panel references in text are accurate and comprehensive.
- Quantitative claims consistently state actual values: e.g., 'AUROC 0.66–0.70' (Fig. 3), '44% reduction in dose' (Fig. 5D), '33 distinct sites for 40 patients' (Fig. 6E).
- The distinction between 'pathology sites' (largest ∥ΔW∥) and 'therapy sites' (LDA-resonant) is made explicit and visually clear in Fig. 6C–E.
- Supplementary figures (S1–S10) are comprehensive and well-integrated; cross-references are accurate.

## Weaknesses
- {HARD} The two intervention protocols—read-out interpolation (α parameter, Figs. 4–5) and oscillatory stimulation (amplitude A, Figs. 5–7)—are introduced without clear upfront distinction. The text states 'α is distinct from the oscillation amplitude A below' (Methods, p. 22) but this appears only after results are presented. A reader encountering Fig. 4 does not immediately know whether the perturbation is a theoretical read-out edit or a physical drive. Suggest: clarify in the main-text Results section before Fig. 4 that two protocols are being compared and what each represents.
- {HARD} The transductive vs. leave-one-out cross-validation distinction is critical to the leakage-free claim but is relegated to Methods (p. 23, 'Leakage-free target selection') and Fig. S9. The main text (p. 12) states 'Honest cross-validation does raise the baseline...' without first explaining what 'honest' means or why the transductive embedding used throughout Figs. 3–5 is not honest. A reader unfamiliar with transductive learning may not grasp why Fig. S9 is necessary or what it changes. Suggest: briefly define transductive vs. leave-one-out in Results before presenting the main stimulation findings, or move the leakage-free analysis to the main text.
- {HARD} The term 'physiological amplitude' is used repeatedly (Figs. 4C, 5, Methods p. 22) but is never formally defined in the main text. The Methods state 'supra-physiological amplitude limits' and reference 'fig. S10' for 'physiological diagnostics', but S10 defines it only as 'RMSpert/RMSbase > 1'. What is the threshold for 'physiological'? Is it RMSpert/RMSbase ≤ 2, ≤ 1, or something else? This ambiguity undermines the claim that focal strategies fail 'at any amplitude' (p. 9). Suggest: define the physiological amplitude threshold explicitly in Results or Methods.
- {SOFT} The term 'read-out' is used in three distinct senses: (1) the linear decoder W, (2) the output y = W⊤x, and (3) the feature space derived from W (G-space). While context usually disambiguates, the first mention of 'read-out geometry' (p. 5) could be clearer. Suggest: use 'read-out weights' for W, 'read-out output' for y, and 'read-out geometry' for the feature space, or define each sense at first use.
- {SOFT} The abstract claims the model 'reconstruct[s] each individual's lagged FC' but does not define 'lagged FC' until Methods (p. 21). For readers unfamiliar with this term, the abstract's claim is opaque. Suggest: either define 'lagged FC' in the abstract or replace with 'temporal structure of connectivity'.
- {SOFT} Figure 2I caption states 'median ± IQR' but the plot shows error bars that appear symmetric; IQR is typically asymmetric around the median. Are these actually SD or SEM? The caption should match the plot. Suggest: clarify whether the bands represent IQR or SD/SEM.
- {SOFT} The phrase 'a distributed change in the model's connectivity kernel' (abstract, p. 1) is never formally defined. What is the 'connectivity kernel'? Is it the read-out W, the recurrent weights J, or something else? The term appears again in Results (p. 9) but without definition. Suggest: define 'connectivity kernel' at first use or replace with a more standard term.
- {SOFT} Quantitative comparisons sometimes lack explicit baselines: e.g., 'modest accuracy, below that of structural atrophy' (abstract) is vague—how much below? The text later states structural AUROC ≈ 0.84 vs. functional ≈ 0.66–0.70 (Supplementary Note S1), but this comparison is relegated to supplementary material. Suggest: state the comparison explicitly in the main text or abstract.

## Questions
- What is the formal definition of 'physiological amplitude' used to evaluate whether focal stimulation fails? Is the threshold RMSpert/RMSbase ≤ 2 (as suggested by Fig. S10A), and does this apply uniformly across all focal protocols?
- In the transductive embedding (Figs. 3–5), the SVD basis and LDA discriminant are fit on all patients, then classifiers are evaluated on held-out folds. Does this mean the embedding itself is not held-out, only the classifier? If so, how does this differ from a standard train-test split, and why is it not considered a form of leakage?
- The term 'connectivity kernel' in the abstract and Results—does this refer to the read-out weights W, the recurrent weights J, or the effective input-to-output transfer function? A formal definition would clarify the mechanistic claim.
- Figure 2I shows agreement between empirical and simulated lagged FC across delays. Does the model's lagged-FC structure emerge from the closed-loop dynamics, or is it inherited from the teacher-forced training? How sensitive is this to the choice of feedback gain f = 0.1?
- In Fig. 5A, the theoretical W correction (top-1 column) is 'essentially inert' at α = 50, yet Fig. S5B shows it reaches ~90% reclassification at α = 2. Why does the main text emphasize the inertness at α = 50 rather than the efficacy at α = 2? Is α = 50 chosen as a fair upper bound, or is there a reason to dismiss over-correction?
- The LDA-resonant site is selected per patient as the argmax of FC-lag score reduction over 121 sites. In the leave-one-out cross-validation (Fig. S9), is this argmax computed on the training patients only, or is each held-out patient's best site chosen from the training set? The Methods state 'each patient's personalised site chosen as the argmax of the train-only score reduction over the 121 sites'—does this mean the argmax is over 121 sites in the training set, or 121 sites in the full atlas?
