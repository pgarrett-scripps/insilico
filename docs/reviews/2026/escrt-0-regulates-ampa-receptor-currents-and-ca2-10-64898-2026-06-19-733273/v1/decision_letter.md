# Decision Letter

**Decision:** major

## Summary of Evaluation
The panel's numerical signal (3.50/5, 5 minor, 2 major, 1 reject) points toward major revision. The central claims — that Hrs localises to the post-synapse, is bidirectionally regulated by activity, and modulates AMPAR function — are plausible and the manuscript is clearly a useful contribution. However, the load-bearing electrophysiological claim that Hrs alters single-synapse AMPAR kinetics cannot be distinguished from a change in polysynaptic recruitment, and the phosphoproteomics discovery screen reports 16 hits at unadjusted p<0.05 from 924 tests — a number indistinguishable from the expected false-positive rate under the global null. Both the Methods and Data-Analysis reviewers flagged these as HARD issues that prevent the conclusions from being evaluated as written. Additionally, the constitutive knockout (Syn1-Cre, E13 onset) used for the CaMKII and scaffold findings is conflated with the acute shRNA knockdown used for electrophysiology, leaving the mechanistic specificity uncertain. The advocate correctly notes that orthogonal validation partially mitigates the phosphoproteomics concern, but the screen should be reported with FDR correction, and the developmental confound must be acknowledged and addressed with either acute data or explicit hedging. The compliance audits identified 5 HARD gaps (IACUC protocol number, blinding/randomisation statement, RRIDs, shRNA sequences, biotinylation reagent inconsistency). Because the manuscript sits at the border of major and minor revision, I assign major revision: the required changes are concrete and specific, and if addressed convincingly the paper would meet the In Silico bar.
