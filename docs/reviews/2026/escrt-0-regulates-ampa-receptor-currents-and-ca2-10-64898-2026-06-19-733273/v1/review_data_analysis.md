# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript reports that the ESCRT-0 protein Hrs is bidirectionally regulated by neuronal activity and modulates AMPA receptor function, surface trafficking, and Ca2+-dependent signaling. The experimental design is multi-level and the central hypothesis is plausible, but several load-bearing quantitative results are not supported by the statistics as presented: the phosphoproteomics analysis uses uncorrected p<0.05 across 924 tests, the surface biotinylation claim rests on a direct comparison rather than an interaction test with unstated n, and the electrophysiology classification is subjective and unblinded. These issues require revision before the conclusions can be accepted.

## Weaknesses
- Phosphoproteomics multiple comparisons (HARD). The manuscript reports 924 unique phosphopeptides with 16 "differentially abundant" at p<0.05 uncorrected. At α=0.05 with 924 tests, the expected number of false positives is ~46 — the 16 hits are *below* the expected false-positive count and are indistinguishable from noise. The GO/KEGG enrichment analyses (Figure 4C) are built on these 16 hits and inherit the problem. The authors should apply Benjamini-Hochberg correction and report how many hits survive at FDR=0.05, or justify why uncorrected p-values are appropriate.
- Surface biotinylation interaction claim (HARD). The claim "Hrs impacts activity-dependent surface trafficking of GluA1" rests on comparing Cre+Bic (50±7%) vs YFP+Bic (90±24%) directly. The proper test for an activity-dependent effect is the interaction between Cre and Bic in the 2×2 design — basal data exist and show no difference, so the interaction is testable. Without it, the result is consistent with a main effect of Cre that only becomes detectable under Bic, not a specific activity-dependent effect. The n for this experiment is not stated, and the control SEM of 24% is large relative to the reported 40% effect.
- Electrophysiology classification and kinetics (HARD). The slow/fast AMPAR EPSC classification uses a subjective "multiple peaks" criterion, and the experimenter is not blinded (GFP identifies KD neurons). The p=0.002 (Fisher's exact) could reflect classification bias. Additionally, the rise time and decay slope analyses (Figure 3I–L) are performed on currents that include multi-peaked "slow" responses — rise time and decay slope of a multi-peaked current are not well-defined. The authors should report the kinetic analysis restricted to fast, single-peaked currents, and state whether classification was done blind.
- AMPA/NMDA ratio ambiguity. The ratio uses NMDA current at 100 ms post-stimulation. If Hrs depletion alters NMDA receptor composition or kinetics (e.g., slower-decaying GluN2B-containing receptors), the NMDA current at 100 ms increases, reducing the ratio without any change in AMPA. The authors acknowledge the ambiguity but do not report NMDA kinetics to exclude this alternative. Reporting the NMDA decay time constant for both groups would settle it.
- CaMKII ratio not reported. The authors report pCaMKII (0.39) and total CaMKII (1.56) separately normalized to loading control. The meaningful quantity is the phosphorylation fraction pCaMKII/totalCaMKII (~0.25, a 75% reduction), which is a stronger claim than the stated 60% reduction. Reporting the ratio would clarify the interpretation and the claim of "reduced activity."
- Multiple comparisons across the many western blots (CaMKII, PKA, PKC, PP2B, gephyrin, PSD-95, GluA1, GluA2, GluN2A/B, etc.) with no correction — some significant results could be chance.
- Pseudo-replication: dendrite-level n's (Figure 1E: n=30–36 dendrites from 2–3 experiments; Figure 2F: n=34–41 dendrites from 3 experiments) treat dendrites as independent units; the effective n is the number of cultures.
- "No difference" claims (PKA, PKC, PP2B unchanged; basal surface GluA1 unchanged) rest on non-significant results in samples of n=7 or unstated n, without equivalence testing or power analysis.
- Exact p-values are not given anywhere; the tight control SEM of 0.03 with N=8 in Figure 2B (vs treated SEMs of 0.17 and 0.12) suggests unstable normalization and warrants reporting raw normalized values.
- The phosphoproteomics normalization (phospho intensity / unmodified protein intensity) means a change in total protein abundance masquerades as a phosphorylation change; the authors should report whether the 16 hits' parent proteins changed in total abundance.
