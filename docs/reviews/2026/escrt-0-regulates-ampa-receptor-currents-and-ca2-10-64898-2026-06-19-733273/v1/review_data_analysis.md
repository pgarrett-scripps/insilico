# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript reports that ESCRT-0 protein Hrs localizes to synapses, is bidirectionally regulated by neuronal activity, and modulates AMPAR function and Ca²⁺-dependent signaling. The central claims are plausible, but several key quantitative results are not supported by the statistics as presented: the phosphoproteomics screen applies no multiple-comparison correction and yields fewer hits than expected by chance; the electrophysiological kinetic differences may be confounded by the different proportions of fast/slow currents between groups; and multiple "no difference" claims rest on unpowered samples. The manuscript needs substantial statistical revision before the conclusions are supportable.

## Weaknesses
- 1. Phosphoproteomics: no multiple-comparison correction; hits indistinguishable from noise (HARD). The screen identified 924 unique phosphopeptides, with 16 deemed differentially abundant at p < 0.05. At α = 0.05, ~46 false positives are expected by chance alone; 16 hits is *fewer* than the expected false-positive count. The hits cannot be distinguished from noise, and the GO/KEGG enrichment analyses built on these 16 hits inherit this problem. The authors must apply an FDR correction (e.g., Benjamini–Hochberg) and report the hit list against the corrected threshold. If no hits survive correction, the phosphoproteomics claim as presented is unsupported.
- 2. Electrophysiological kinetic differences may be an artifact of the fast/slow current mix (HARD). The rise time and decay slope differences (Figure 3I–L) are measured on averaged currents that include different proportions of slow multi-peak currents between groups (21/41 KD vs 38/46 control slow). A multi-peak current has a slower rise and shallower decay slope by construction — the kinetic differences may reflect the different mix, not a change in AMPAR properties. The authors should report kinetics separately for fast and slow currents, or restrict the kinetic analysis to the fast subset. Additionally, the fast/slow classification threshold ("more than 10% of sweeps... decay times greater than 25 ms") is applied by an experimenter who can see GFP indicating KD neurons; blinding of the classification should be stated.
- 3. Activity-dependent Hrs regulation: multiple comparisons against the same control, variance heterogeneity (HARD). Bic and TTX are both compared to the same untreated control (1.15 ± 0.03), with no correction for two comparisons. The Bic SEM (0.17) is ~6× the untreated SEM (0.03), indicating variance heterogeneity that a Welch or non-parametric test should address. The test used is not named in the text. Report the test, exact p-values, and effect sizes with CIs.
- 4. "No difference" claims are unpowered (HARD). Multiple claims of no change — NMDARs, synaptophysin, PKA substrates, PP2B, total GluA1, basal surface GluA1, and the DPI comparison in Figure 3H ("showed the same AMPA/NMDA ratio, indicating that efficient knockdown was achieved by 5 DPI") — rest on non-significant results in samples of n = 6–7 with no equivalence testing or power analysis. These should be reworded as "not detected" or supported by equivalence tests. The DPI claim is also circular: knockdown efficiency is inferred from the constancy of the ratio rather than measured directly.
- 5. pCaMKII normalization is unclear (HARD). The 60% decrease in pCaMKII and 56% increase in total CaMKII are reported separately. If pCaMKII is normalized to total CaMKII, the effect compounds; if to a loading control, the pCaMKII/total CaMKII ratio should be reported. The large SEM on pCaMKII (0.39 ± 0.10) warrants reporting the distribution.
- 6. AMPA/NMDA ratio reduction is ambiguous without absolute amplitudes (SOFT). The ratio is AMPA peak / NMDA at 100 ms. A reduced ratio could reflect reduced AMPA, increased NMDA, or both. The text acknowledges this ("may reflect") but the discussion interprets it as reduced AMPAR abundance. Report the absolute AMPA and NMDA amplitudes separately.
- 7. Pearson coefficient comparison across different marker pairs does not support "more abundant at the pre-synapse" (SOFT). Comparing Pearson coefficients between Hrs/Bassoon (0.40) and Hrs/PSD-95 (0.29) is not a valid comparison of abundance — Pearson correlation depends on each channel's dynamic range and noise. Also, dendrites from the same culture are not independent units (n = 30 and 36 dendrites from 2–3 experiments); report the number of independent cultures.
- 8. Surface biotinylation: normalization and variance (SOFT). The YFP-Bic control has SEM of 24% on a mean of 90% — the comparison may be driven by one outlier. Surface GluA1 normalized to input GluA1 changes if Hrs depletion alters total GluA1; total GluA1 should be reported for the depletion condition.
