# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript's central quantitative claims — that Hrs depletion alters AMPAR kinetics and that Hrs levels track neuronal activity — are supported only by statistics that are under-specified, under-powered, or computed against denominators that themselves respond to the treatment. The most load-bearing numbers (the AMPA/NMDA ratio, the 'fast vs slow' current classification, the activity-dependent Hrs fold-changes, the surface-GluA1 normalization) are each uninterpretable as reported, and the paper's own design provides plausible alternative explanations for the observed patterns. The phosphoproteomics analysis is the one part that is reasonably well executed, but it is under-powered and its hits are not corrected for multiple comparisons.

## Strengths
- The phosphoproteomics workflow is described in unusual detail — TMT labeling, SPS-MS3, FDR filtering, normalization of phosphopeptides to unmodified protein intensities — and the raw data are deposited (PXD073715), which makes the analysis checkable.
- The authors state the independent replication unit for the imaging experiments (dendrites from multiple cultures) and report exact p-values in several places rather than only 'p<0.05'.
- The surface-biotinylation experiment includes a treatment-matched control (Bic-treated YFP) rather than comparing only to vehicle, which partially addresses the concern that Bic itself changes total GluA1.

## Weaknesses
- The AMPA/NMDA ratio is the paper's central functional claim, but it is computed as peak AMPAR amplitude divided by NMDAR amplitude measured at 100 ms post-stimulation, and the two denominators are not measured on the same scale or at the same time point; a change in the ratio could be driven entirely by a change in the NMDAR component, and the paper reports no NMDAR amplitude data to exclude this. Report the raw AMPAR and NMDAR amplitudes separately, and the ratio computed with both components measured at matched time points.
- The 'fast vs slow' AMPAR EPSC classification is the basis for the claim that Hrs depletion changes AMPAR kinetics, but the classification rule is defined only by a threshold on the fraction of sweeps with multiple peaks and decay time (>25 ms), with no stated inter-rater reliability, no blinded classification, and no report of how many cells fell into each category per group beyond the fraction. The 20-80% rise-time and decay-slope differences are then computed on the averaged traces after alignment at the point of maximal rise, which can itself manufacture a rise-time difference; report the raw per-sweep distributions and the classification done blind.
- The activity-dependent Hrs fold-changes (Bic +34%, TTX −31%) are reported as ratios to α-tubulin, and the TTX/Bic comparison is made against a single 'untreated' group with no stated n per condition beyond '8 independent experiments'; the SEMs (e.g. 1.15 ± 0.03 vs 0.80 ± 0.12) suggest the untreated group has far less variance than the treated groups, which is not addressed. Report the per-condition n, the test used, and the raw Hrs/tubulin values.
- The surface-GluA1 result is normalized to input GluA1, but input GluA1 itself responds to Bic treatment; the reported Bic-induced reduction in surface GluA1 in the Cre group (50 ± 7% vs 90 ± 24%) could be an artefact of the denominator rising. Report the absolute surface signal and the input values separately.
- The phosphoproteomics analysis identifies 16 differentially abundant phosphopeptides out of 924 at p<0.05 with no multiple-comparison correction; at that threshold ~46 false positives are expected by chance alone, so the hit list is not distinguishable from noise. Report the FDR-adjusted q-values and the number of hits surviving correction.
- The 'no difference' claims (e.g. 'PKCα and γ, PP2B, PKA substrates unchanged') are made from non-significant results in samples of n=6-7 per group with no power analysis and no equivalence testing; a null result in an under-powered sample cannot support a claim of no effect.
- The electrophysiology comparisons (rise time, decay slope, AMPA/NMDA ratio) are reported with no stated n per group beyond the total cell counts (41 vs 46), no effect sizes or CIs, and no statement of whether the comparisons are paired or unpaired; the recording exclusion criteria (series resistance >30 MΩ, variation >30%) are stated but it is not disclosed whether these were applied before or after outcomes were seen.
- The imaging colocalization analyses (Pearson coefficients, Manders coefficients) are reported as means with SEM but no stated test (parametric vs non-parametric) and no correction for the multiple comparisons across the many dendrites and conditions; the n for the Hrs/Bassoon comparison (30 dendrites from 2 experiments) is smaller than for Hrs/PSD-95 (36 from 3), which is not discussed.

## Questions
- Can you report the raw AMPAR and NMDAR amplitudes separately, and the AMPA/NMDA ratio computed with both components measured at matched time points, to exclude the possibility that the ratio change is driven by the NMDAR component?
- For the 'fast vs slow' classification, was the classification performed blind to condition, and can you report the per-sweep distribution of decay times and the number of cells in each category per group?
- For the activity-dependent Hrs experiments, what is the n per condition (untreated, Bic, TTX) and which test was used for the three-group comparison?
- Can you report the absolute surface GluA1 signal and the input GluA1 values separately for the biotinylation experiment, so the normalization denominator can be inspected?
- What is the FDR-adjusted q-value threshold for the phosphoproteomics hits, and how many of the 16 differentially abundant phosphopeptides survive it?
- For the 'no difference' claims (PKC, PP2B, PKA substrates), what power analysis or equivalence test supports the conclusion of no effect?
