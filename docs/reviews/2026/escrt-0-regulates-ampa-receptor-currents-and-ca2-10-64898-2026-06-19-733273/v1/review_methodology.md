# Methodology Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript claims that ESCRT-0 (Hrs) localizes to synapses, is bidirectionally regulated by neuronal activity, and modulates AMPAR function and Ca2+-dependent signaling. The activity-dependent regulation of Hrs (Bic up, TTX down) is well-supported by a clean manipulation. However, the two most consequential claims — that Hrs depletion alters AMPAR currents, and that Hrs loss causes LTD-like synaptic depression — rest on designs with unresolved confounds: a slow/fast EPSC classification that may reflect technical artifact rather than biology, and a constitutive developmental knockout that cannot separate acute Hrs function from developmental compensation. The phosphoproteomics additionally uses a lenient threshold that may be dominated by noise. The core idea is interesting and the activity-regulation finding is solid, but the causal claims need stronger designs before they are earned.

## Weaknesses
- 1. HARD — The slow/fast AMPAR EPSC classification may reflect a technical confound, not an Hrs effect. The scrambled control shows slow, multi-peak AMPAR EPSCs in 38/46 neurons. Slow multi-peak EPSCs are classically a sign of polysynaptic contamination, incomplete CA3 cut, or poor space clamp — not a biological property of AMPARs. If the control group's slow currents reflect technical factors (stimulation intensity, slice health, recording quality), then the difference between groups could be a technical confound rather than an Hrs effect. The authors need to demonstrate that stimulation parameters were matched between groups, that the slow currents are not polysynaptic (e.g., by showing they persist with low-intensity stimulation or paired-pulse protocols), and that recording quality metrics (series resistance, input resistance) did not differ between groups. The fact that most control neurons show this atypical phenotype is a red flag that the baseline itself is contaminated.
- 2. HARD — The developmental knockout confound undermines the causal claim about CaMKII and synaptic scaffolds. The Hrsf/f Syn1-Cre model deletes Hrs from E13. The observed reductions in pCaMKII-α/β, PSD-95, and gephyrin, and the increase in total CaMKII, could reflect developmental compensation or chronic adaptation to lifelong Hrs loss rather than a direct role for Hrs in synaptic signaling. The claim "Hrs depletion leads to reduced CaMKII phosphorylation" as a statement about Hrs function at the synapse is not fully earned by a constitutive knockout. An inducible knockout (e.g., AAV-Cre delivered to adult Hrsf/f mice) or acute shRNA knockdown would separate developmental from acute effects. Without this, the LTD-like interpretation is one of several possible accounts of the data.
- 3. HARD — Phosphoproteomics without multiple testing correction is likely dominated by noise. With 924 phosphopeptides tested at p < 0.05, roughly 46 false positives are expected by chance; the authors report 16 differentially abundant phosphopeptides, which is below the chance expectation. The GO/KEGG enrichment analyses are therefore built on a set that may be largely noise. The authors should apply multiple testing correction (e.g., Benjamini-Hochberg) and report how many of the 16 survive; if none survive, the phosphoproteomics claim should be substantially softened or reframed as hypothesis-generating.
- 4. SOFT — Surface biotinylation arms are not matched for transduction efficiency. The Cre and YFP AAV arms are compared for surface GluA1 after Bic treatment, but differences in viral titer, transduction efficiency, or neuronal health between arms could confound the comparison. Reporting transduction efficiency (e.g., % GFP-positive neurons) and total GluA1 per arm would help rule this out.
- 5. SOFT — AMPA/NMDA ratio reduction is not resolved to a component. A reduced ratio is consistent with either reduced AMPA or increased NMDA; the authors acknowledge this but do not resolve it. Reporting the absolute amplitudes of each component (not just the ratio) would distinguish these alternatives.
- 6. SOFT — Single dose for Hrs overexpression. The AAV-Hrs overexpression uses one dose; a dose-response would establish that the PKC/pGluA1-S831 effects are specific to Hrs level rather than a threshold or off-target effect.
- 7. SOFT — No blinding reported for subjective classifications. The slow/fast EPSC classification and the colocalization analyses involve subjective judgment; blinding the analyst to condition would strengthen confidence in these measurements.
- 8. SOFT — STED segmentation reliability not reported. The analysis relies on trained pixel classifiers applied across images; inter-rater or test-retest reliability for the segmentation is not reported, which matters for the quantitative cluster-volume and density claims.
