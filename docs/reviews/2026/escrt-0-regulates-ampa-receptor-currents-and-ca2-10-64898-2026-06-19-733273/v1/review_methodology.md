# Methodology Reviewer

## Summary
The paper proposes that ESCRT-0 (Hrs) localizes to synapses, is bidirectionally regulated by neuronal activity, and modulates AMPAR currents and Ca2+-dependent signaling. The activity-regulation experiment is clean and the loss/gain-of-function strategy is appropriate. However, the central electrophysiological claim — that Hrs depletion accelerates AMPAR kinetics — has a plausible alternative explanation the design does not rule out, and the biochemical claims about CaMKII and scaffolding proteins rest on a constitutive developmental knockout that is conflated with the acute knockdown used elsewhere. The work is substantial and the fixes are specific, but the central claims are not fully earned as presented.

## Strengths
- The bidirectional activity experiment (Bic up, TTX down, 72 h) is a well-controlled design: two opposing treatments on matched cultures, with the direction of the effect matching the direction of activity.
- The surface biotinylation experiment includes a basal comparison showing no difference between Cre and YFP, which isolates the activity-dependent component of the Hrs effect.
- The combination of loss-of-function (shRNA, Cre) and gain-of-function (AAV overexpression) approaches is appropriate for probing Hrs function.

## Weaknesses
- HARD 1 — Polysynaptic contamination confound in the AMPAR kinetics claim. The "slow" AMPAR EPSCs are defined by multiple peaks and decay >25 ms — the classic signature of polysynaptic responses, not slow AMPAR gating. The authors' own data show Hrs knockdown reduces the AMPA/NMDA ratio and activity-dependent surface GluA1, i.e., weaker or fewer synapses. At a fixed stimulation intensity, weaker synapses recruit fewer polysynaptic inputs, which would produce exactly the observed shift toward "fast" currents in the Hrs-KD group. The rise-time and decay-slope differences may therefore reflect the relative proportion of polysynaptic contamination rather than faster AMPAR deactivation/desensitization. The statement that the multi-peak profile is "a reproducible feature of those neurons" addresses within-neuron stability, not the polysynaptic interpretation. To rule this out, the authors must show that the kinetics of the fast currents alone (excluding slow ones) differ between groups, or demonstrate that the slow currents persist at minimal stimulation intensities where polysynaptic recruitment is absent. This is the load-bearing result for the title claim, and as presented it does not distinguish between altered AMPAR gating and altered network recruitment.
- HARD 2 — Constitutive knockout conflated with acute knockdown. The pCaMKII reduction, total CaMKII increase, and PSD-95/gephyrin decreases come from Syn1-Cre mice with Hrs deleted from E13 — a developmental manipulation. The electrophysiology uses acute shRNA (5–12 days). These are different manipulations, and the biochemical changes could reflect developmental compensation (e.g., altered synapse formation) rather than a direct role of Hrs in Ca2+-dependent signaling at mature synapses. The model in Figure 8 and the discussion treat both as evidence for the same mechanism. The authors need to show the pCaMKII reduction with acute knockdown, or explicitly hedge the developmental interpretation.
- SOFT — Surface biotinylation variance. The YFP+Bic control shows 90 ± 24% (SEM), while Cre+Bic shows 50 ± 7%. With this variance, it is unclear whether Bic produces any effect within the YFP control group; the comparison may be driven by the noisy control rather than a genuine Cre-dependent reduction. Report the Bic effect within each group separately.
- SOFT — Activity-dependent Hrs regulation specificity. The bidirectional design is strong, but no control distinguishes a specific effect on Hrs from a general activity-dependent change in protein synthesis or turnover. A time-course or a second protein measured in the same lysates would strengthen the specificity claim.
- SOFT — Hrs overexpression lacks an endosomal-protein control. The AAV-Hrs-HA vs AAV-GFP comparison does not control for the effect of overexpressing a large endosomal protein; a 2.7-fold increase could saturate endosomal sorting machinery non-specifically. An unrelated endosomal protein (e.g., STAM1 or EEA1) at matched expression would isolate the Hrs-specific effect.
- SOFT — STED analysis circularity. The "Pixel Classifier" is trained on a representative image and applied to all images; the 89–91% overlap metric is not defined in the text (overlap of what — voxels, objects, centroids?). The segmentation approach risks circularity if the classifier is trained on the same images it is used to analyze.
- SOFT — Nedd4-1 colocalization. GFP-Nedd4-1 is overexpressed, and the Manders thresholding procedure is not described. The claim is modest and appropriately scaled, but the thresholding detail matters for a colocalization metric.
- One-line flags for other reviewers: Phosphoproteomics: 16 differentially abundant peptides from 924 tested at unadjusted p<0.05 is within the range expected by chance (~46 expected); no multiple-comparison correction is reported, and the GO/KEGG pathway claims rest on very few peptides (data_analysis). The AMPA/NMDA ratio reduction could reflect increased NMDA rather than decreased AMPA; the authors note this but do not distinguish (data_analysis).
