# Methodology Reviewer

## Summary
A well-organized, multi-pronged study of Hrs at synapses, with convergent evidence from biochemistry, imaging, STED, electrophysiology, phosphoproteomics, and two in vivo models. The localization and activity-dependent regulation findings are supported by the design. However, two load-bearing claims — that Hrs depletion alters per-synapse AMPAR kinetics, and that Hrs deletion specifically reduces activity-dependent surface GluA1 — have alternative explanations the current design does not exclude. The bidirectional signaling synthesis also compares across two different model systems. Major revision.

## Weaknesses
- HARD — The AMPAR kinetics claim conflates a population shift with a per-synapse change. EPSCs are classified as "fast" or "slow" (multi-peak, decay >25 ms), and Hrs KD neurons have fewer slow currents (21/41 vs 38/46). The subsequent rise-time and decay-slope differences (Figure 3I–L) are then reported as evidence of faster receptor kinetics. But if the recorded population is a mixture of fast and slow currents, the average rise time and decay slope will change with the proportion shift even if no individual synapse's AMPAR kinetics change at all. The slow currents, defined by multiple peaks, are equally consistent with polysynaptic or asynchronous release — an alternative the extracellular-stimulation design cannot exclude. The classification also leaves a gap (neurons with 5–10% multi-peak sweeps fit neither category), and the thresholds are not justified. To support the kinetics claim, analyze fast and slow currents separately, or use minimal stimulation to isolate unitary events. Note also that the abstract's "reduced amplitude" outruns the evidence, which is an AMPA/NMDA ratio, not an absolute amplitude (overclaiming — rigor's lane).
- HARD — The surface GluA1 experiment lacks a Cre-expression control. The claim that Hrs deletion reduces activity-dependent surface GluA1 rests on comparing AAV-Cre (Hrsf/f) with AAV-YFP (Hrsf/f) after bicuculline. AAV-Cre expression itself — independent of Hrs deletion — can affect neuronal health, protein trafficking, or the response to Bic. The design does not include AAV-Cre on WT (Hrs+/+) neurons, which would isolate the effect of Hrs deletion from the effect of Cre expression. Without this control, the attribution of the surface GluA1 reduction to Hrs loss specifically is not earned.
- SOFT — The activity-regulation experiments lack a specificity control. Bic increases and TTX decreases Hrs, but no other endosomal/ESCRT or synaptic protein is measured in the same lysates. The effect could be a general response of protein turnover to prolonged activity change rather than specific to Hrs. A control protein (e.g., EEA1, another ESCRT component) would establish specificity. Additionally, chronic Bic (72 h) can cause excitotoxicity; cell viability/density is not reported, and the MAP2-normalized immunofluorescence only partially addresses this.
- The "bidirectional" framing (depletion → reduced pCaMKII; overexpression → increased PKC substrates/pGluA1-S831) compares a constitutive, partial developmental KO with adult AAV overexpression — different systems, ages, and routes — so the bidirectional synthesis is not tested under matched conditions.
- The phosphoproteomics hits (gephyrin S264/S268, Cntnap1, Grm3, etc.) are not validated by targeted western blot or phosphosite-specific antibodies; the validated pCaMKII finding was not among the proteomics hits. (The multiple-comparisons issue — 16 hits at unadjusted p<0.05 across 924 peptides — belongs to data_analysis.)
- The STED analysis uses pixel classifiers trained on a representative image and applied to all images, with no reported validation (e.g., comparison with manual segmentation or inter-rater reliability).
- Blinding is not stated for the subjective fast/slow EPSC classification or the colocalization quantifications.
- The Nedd4-1/Hrs colocalization experiment uses overexpressed GFP-Nedd4-1 without a control protein to show the AMPA-induced association is specific to Nedd4-1 rather than reflecting general endosomal accumulation.
