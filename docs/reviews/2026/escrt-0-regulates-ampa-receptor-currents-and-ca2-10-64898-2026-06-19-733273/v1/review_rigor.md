# Rigor & Overclaiming Reviewer

## Summary
This manuscript implicates the ESCRT-0 protein Hrs in post-synaptic AMPA receptor regulation and Ca2+-dependent signaling, reporting bidirectional activity-dependent regulation of Hrs levels and downstream effects on AMPAR kinetics, surface expression, and CaMKII/PKC signaling. The work is timely and addresses a genuine gap in understanding how ubiquitin-mediated sorting interfaces with synaptic plasticity. The evidence is generally sound and supports the main conclusions, though several claims outrun the experiments in their current form — most notably the causal arrow and mechanistic inference from correlational and knockdown data. I recommend minor revision to tighten the alignment between wording and evidence.

## Strengths
- The manuscript provides the first evidence that ESCRT-0's Hrs subunit localizes to the post-synapse, not only the pre-synapse, using both biochemical subcellular fractionation and super-resolution microscopy — a clear and important advance.
- The bidirectional regulation of Hrs protein levels by prolonged increases versus decreases in neuronal activity (bicuculline/TTX, Fig. 2) is a clean observation that establishes dynamic coupling between Hrs and activity state.

## Weaknesses
- LOAD-BEARING CLAIM 1: 'Hrs regulates AMPA receptor currents' (title, abstract, Fig. 3). The evidence is Hrs knockdown in organotypic slices producing faster AMPAR kinetics, a lower AMPA/NMDA ratio, and fewer 'slow' EPSCs. This is a correlational manipulation — Hrs depletion changes many endosomal trafficking events, not just AMPAR sorting. The Hrs-depleted neurons show faster rise times and steeper decay slopes, which is consistent with altered AMPAR subunit composition (e.g., loss of GluA2, switch to TARP-less receptors, or altered editing), but also with changes in dendritic cable properties, electrotonic distance of the stimulated synapse from the soma, or altered release probability. The manuscript does not measure paired-pulse ratio, coefficient of variation, or mEPSC frequency/amplitude to distinguish pre- vs post-synaptic contributions. The claim 'regulates AMPAR currents' is defensible; the implied mechanism — that Hrs sorts AMPARs to lysosomes and loss of this sorting explains the phenotype — is only one of several possibilities, and the text does not acknowledge alternatives. SOFT: requalify 'regulates' as 'modulates' or 'influences' and add a limitation stating that pre-synaptic changes are not excluded.
- LOAD-BEARING CLAIM 2: 'Hrs controls Ca2+-dependent signaling' (abstract, Fig. 5-7). The evidence is that Hrs-depleted mice show reduced pCaMKII-T286/T287 with elevated total CaMKII, while Hrs-overexpressing mice show increased PKC substrate phosphorylation and pGluA1-S831. These are parallel manipulations with distinct outcomes — a classic 'comparison of two different states' design that reveals correlation, not causation. The claim that Hrs 'controls' Ca2+ signaling implies a regulatory relationship (Hrs → pathway X), but the data cannot establish direction: reduced pCaMKII in Hrs-depleted mice could equally result from reduced network activity (which itself lowers pCaMKII) rather than from a direct interaction between Hrs and CaMKII. The authors note that total CaMKII is elevated and propose impaired proteasomal degradation, but never measure CaMKII ubiquitination, proteasome activity, or half-life. The 'control' claim outruns the evidence. HARD (abstract): 'ESCRT-0 regulates AMPA receptor currents and Ca2+- dependent signaling' — 'regulates' is causal and has not been isolated from the confound that reduced Hrs reduces network activity, which then changes everything downstream. SOFT: change to 'ESCRT-0 depletion alters AMPA receptor kinetics and is associated with changes in Ca2+-dependent signaling.'
- LOAD-BEARING CLAIM 3: 'Hrs mediates activity-dependent synaptic trafficking of GluA1' (Fig. 3M–N, surface biotinylation). The claim is that Hrs depletion does not alter basal surface GluA1 but reduces surface GluA1 after bicuculline treatment. The interaction term (Cre × Bic) is tested via separate t-tests (Cre+Bic vs YFP+Bic, p<0.01), but the four-group comparison is never analyzed as a 2×2 ANOVA with an interaction test. Without that, the authors cannot distinguish a genuine interaction (Hrs selectively impairs activity-driven surface insertion) from a ceiling effect in the control group or a floor effect in the Cre group. The figure suggests the Cre+Bic group mean is approximately half that of YFP+Bic, but the YFP+Bic group also shows high variability (CV ~0.6). Report the interaction test. HARD (as currently analyzed): a claim of differential activity dependence requires the interaction term to be significant, not a pair of marginal t-tests.
- SWEEP 1 — Multiple testing in phosphoproteomics (Fig. 4). The discovery of 16 'differentially abundant' phosphopeptides at p<0.05 from 924 candidates is exactly the null-expected rate (~46 by chance). No multiple-testing correction is reported. Without FDR control (e.g., Benjamini-Hochberg) or replication in an independent cohort, these 16 candidates cannot be treated as findings; the GO/KEGG enrichment on them is built on noise. The authors should either apply FDR correction and note which, if any, survive, or explicitly present these as nominally-significant candidates requiring validation.
- SWEEP 2 — Causal language in Hrs-overexpression experiments (Fig. 7). 'Neuronal Hrs overexpression enhances PKC activity and phosphorylated GluA1-S831' — the data show that PKC substrate phosphorylation is increased, but PKC activity was not measured directly (e.g., by in vitro kinase assay). PKC substrate antibody staining is a proxy, and it is sensitive to phosphatase activity, substrate availability, and cross-reactivity. The claim should be softened to 'is associated with increased phosphorylation of PKC substrates and GluA1-S831'.
- SWEEP 3 — The 'first evidence' (abstract, page 23) that Hrs 'functions bidirectionally as a responder and modulator of synaptic activity' — the bidirectional regulation is demonstrated for Hrs protein levels (Fig. 2), but the 'modulator' direction (Hrs → activity) rests on the electrophysiology data which, as argued above, does not isolate Hrs's own role from general endosomal dysfunction. The claim couples two phenomena that have not been shown to be causally linked in both directions within a single experiment.
- SWEEP 4 — Phosphoproteomics sample description. The text says PSM fractions were prepared from cortex (methods) but Figure 4 legend says 'cortex and hippocampus.' Clarify which tissue was used. If both, were they pooled per animal or processed separately?
- SWEEP 5 — The Hrs knockdown efficiency in organotypic slices is not quantified biochemically. The authors show reduced immunofluorescence intensity in primary hippocampal neurons (Fig. S3), but the slice recordings rely on shRNA-AAV in organotypic slices from a different species (mouse). The extent of knockdown in the recorded cells is not demonstrated. Report the Hrs reduction level in the slice system (e.g., by co-immunostaining post-recording or by parallel western blot from identically treated slices).

## Questions
- In Figures 3B–F, how were 'fast' vs 'slow' AMPAR EPSCs classified in terms of quantitative criteria (e.g., decay time threshold, number of peaks, or a clustering algorithm)? The text gives qualitative definitions; a precise threshold would let the reader assess whether the distribution difference is robust to small changes in the cutoff.
- How many cells/animals contributed to the electrophysiology data in Fig. 3? The figure legend says 41 and 46 neurons, but is not clear if those come from the same or different numbers of slices and animals per condition, nor whether any animal-level clustering was assessed.
- The phosphoproteomics (Fig. 4) identifies 16 differentially abundant phosphopeptides from 924 total. With ~1.7% significant at p<0.05, this is exactly what one would expect from multiple testing under the global null. Was any multiple-testing correction applied? If not, how do the authors rule out that these 16 are false positives?
- In the Hrs-overexpression experiment (Fig. 7), which brain regions were sampled beyond cortex? Were any behavioral or electrophysiological correlates assessed, or is the conclusion limited to the biochemical changes shown?
- Regarding the claim that Hrs 'senses and is modulated by' neuronal activity: the bi-directional regulation in Fig. 2A–B shows Hrs protein changes after 72 h of drug treatment. Is there evidence that these are transcriptional, post-transcriptional, or related to protein turnover? Does 24 h of treatment produce a similar effect, or is this only seen after prolonged manipulation?
