# Rigor & Overclaiming Reviewer

## Summary
The paper's central claims — that Hrs is bidirectionally regulated by activity, that Hrs depletion alters AMPAR currents, and that Hrs levels modulate Ca2+-dependent signaling — are each supported by at least one direct manipulation, but several load-bearing claims are worded more strongly than the evidence licenses, and the causal chain from Hrs to AMPAR function is inferred rather than isolated. The work is sound and useful but needs requalification of specific claims and a few clarifying analyses before the headline statements match the evidence.

## Strengths
- The activity-dependence experiment (Bic up, TTX down) is a clean bidirectional manipulation with a matched control, directly supporting the claim that Hrs levels track neuronal activity.
- The electrophysiology uses a direct shRNA manipulation with scrambled control and reports both the AMPA/NMDA ratio and kinetic parameters, giving the AMPAR-function claim an experimental basis.
- The authors hedge appropriately in several places ('suggestive of', 'is consistent with'), and the phosphoproteomics is presented as a discovery screen with p<0.05 thresholds stated.

## Weaknesses
- LOAD-BEARING 1 — 'Hrs levels impact AMPAR kinetics in CA1 pyramidal neurons' (title/abstract claim). The evidence is the shRNA knockdown recording showing a lower AMPA/NMDA ratio and faster rise time. The alternative explanation the design does not exclude: Hrs depletion could alter NMDAR abundance or function rather than AMPARs, since the ratio uses NMDAR current at 100 ms as the denominator and the authors themselves report no change in NMDAR subunit levels only in the cortex lysate (Fig S7), not at the synapse. A reduced ratio is equally produced by increased NMDAR current or by reduced AMPAR current; the paper's own data (no change in GluN2B in lysate) does not settle the synaptic question. What would distinguish: report the absolute AMPAR and NMDAR current amplitudes separately (not just the ratio), and quantify surface GluA1 in the same slice preparation rather than only in dissociated cortical culture. As worded, 'Hrs levels impact AMPAR kinetics' overstates what the ratio and rise-time data establish.
- LOAD-BEARING 2 — 'Hrs depletion leads to a specific reduction of CaMKII autophosphorylation, suggestive of reduced neuronal activity' (Results). The evidence is the western blot showing reduced pCaMKII-T286/T287 with increased total CaMKII in cortical lysate. The alternative: the increase in total CaMKII could be a compensatory or degradation-blockade effect (the authors themselves suggest proteasomal clearance of CaMKII is impaired), in which case the reduced pCaMKII/total ratio reflects altered turnover rather than reduced neuronal activity. The claim that this is 'suggestive of reduced neuronal activity' is a SOFT over-hedge in the right direction, but the sentence 'loss of Hrs leads to a specific reduction of CaMKII autophosphorylation' is causal language for a correlational lysate measurement — the design (whole-cortex lysate, no activity manipulation) cannot establish that Hrs depletion causes reduced CaMKII activity. Requalify to 'Hrs-depleted cortex shows reduced pCaMKII relative to total CaMKII'.
- LOAD-BEARING 3 — 'Hrs acts as a sensor at the post-synapse to fine-tune neuronal activity' (abstract). The evidence is the bidirectional activity regulation plus the electrophysiology. The gap: the activity-dependence experiment shows Hrs levels track activity, but nothing in the paper shows that Hrs levels in turn modulate neuronal activity — the electrophysiology is on Hrs-depleted neurons at rest, not on neurons whose Hrs was raised or lowered by activity. The 'sensor...fine-tune' framing implies a closed loop that the experiments do not close. SOFT: requalify to 'Hrs levels are bidirectionally regulated by activity and Hrs depletion alters AMPAR-mediated currents'.
- Sweep — the 'dramatic' increase in CaMKII (Results: 'increased by 56%') is described with an importance-laden adjective for a modest effect size; report the effect in interpretable terms.
- Sweep — the claim that Hrs overexpression 'enhances PKC activity' (Results) rests on a 36% increase in phosphorylated PKC substrates plus an 11% decrease in PKCα; the substrate-phosphorylation measure is a proxy for activity and the direction of the PKCα change is not obviously consistent with 'enhanced activity' — the causal chain from Hrs overexpression to PKC activity is inferred, not tested.
- Sweep — the 'approximately 40% reduction in surface GluA1' after Bic in Hrs-ablated neurons is reported with a large SEM (50 ± 7% vs 90 ± 24%) and the basal condition shows no difference; the activity-dependent effect is the load-bearing comparison and its variance is high — report the per-replicate values and the n.
- Sweep — the phosphoproteomics and total-proteomics screens are presented with p<0.05 unadjusted thresholds and no multiple-testing correction; the 16 differentially abundant phosphopeptides out of 924 are likely to include false positives, and the GO/KEGG enrichment is built on that uncorrected set — flag this as a multiple-comparisons concern for the data-analysis reviewer.
- Sweep — the 'slow AMPAR EPSC' classification (more than 10% of sweeps with multiple peaks) is a post-hoc grouping that drives the headline percentage difference; the authors should report the distribution of decay times across all neurons rather than the dichotomized fast/slow split, since the dichotomy may be where the effect lives.

## Questions
- Can you report the absolute AMPAR and NMDAR current amplitudes separately, not just the ratio, to show the reduction is in the AMPAR component?
- In the surface biotinylation experiment, what are the per-replicate values and n for the Bic-treated Hrs-ablated group, given the large SEM?
- For the phosphoproteomics, what is the false-discovery rate at the 16 differentially abundant phosphopeptides after multiple-testing correction?
- Is the reduced pCaMKII/total ratio in Hrs-depleted cortex accompanied by any measure of neuronal activity (e.g., immediate-early gene expression) that would support the 'reduced neuronal activity' interpretation?
