# Novelty & Contribution Reviewer

## Summary
The central novelty claims — Hrs at the postsynaptic density, bidirectional activity-dependent regulation of Hrs levels, and Hrs-dependent modulation of AMPAR currents and CaMKII signaling — survive a related-work and preprint search; I found no published or preprint work reporting the same core results. The contribution is real and builds coherently on the authors' own prior work, but the delta over that prior work and over the presynaptic Hrs literature is stated more thinly than it could be, and the "first evidence" framing slightly outruns the modest effect sizes reported. This is a genuine advance in a non-disease context, positioned at roughly the right altitude, needing sharper differentiation rather than any correction of fact.

## Strengths
- The manuscript explicitly builds on the authors' 2023 prion-disease finding (Lawrence et al., J Neurosci) and extends it to the non-disease state, which is the right way to frame a follow-up.
- The bidirectional activity-dependence result (Bic up, TTX down) is a clean, testable claim that I could not preempt in the literature.
- The electrophysiological AMPAR-kinetics data and the phosphoproteomic profiling give the contribution independent, converging lines of evidence rather than a single assay.

## Weaknesses
- Load-bearing: differentiation from the authors' own 2023 paper. The manuscript's most important novelty boundary is internal, not external: Lawrence et al. (2023) already reported that neuronal Hgs deletion accelerates GluA1 hyperphosphorylation (S831/S845) and ubiquitinated-protein accumulation at synapses in prion disease. The present paper's claim that Hrs regulates AMPAR currents and CaMKII signaling in the healthy state is a genuine delta, but the manuscript never states it as such — it does not say what the 2023 paper established and what this paper adds beyond "in the absence of disease." Because the 2023 paper is the nearest neighbor and is the authors' own, the differentiation obligation is explicit and unmet. The one sentence that would settle it: a statement of the specific non-disease findings (PSD localization, activity-dependent Hrs levels, AMPAR kinetics, CaMKII changes) that were not and could not have been reported in the 2023 disease study.
- Load-bearing: "first evidence" claim vs. presynaptic Hrs activity-dependence. The manuscript claims "the first evidence that Hrs, and therefore ESCRT-0, functions bidirectionally as a responder and modulator of synaptic activity." Prior work (Sheehan et al. 2016; Birdsall et al. 2022) already established activity-dependent Hrs behavior at the presynapse — activity-dependent redistribution of Hrs in axons and its role in synaptic vesicle protein degradation. The present claim is distinct (Hrs levels regulated by activity; postsynaptic function), but the manuscript does not acknowledge this nearest neighbor, so a reader cannot judge whether "first" refers to the postsynaptic/levels claim or to Hrs-activity coupling generally. The one sentence that would settle it: an explicit statement that prior activity-dependence was presynaptic and redistribution-based, whereas this work shows postsynaptic, level-based regulation.
- Sweep: The "Hrs localizes to the post-synapse" claim rests partly on PSD2 enrichment of only 0.33× S1 (vs. synaptophysin 0.008×), which is a modest signal for a load-bearing localization claim — a rigor question, but one that bears on how strongly the novelty can be stated.
- Sweep: The 12–17% reductions in gephyrin/PSD-95 and the 60% pCaMKII reduction are presented as evidence of an "LTD-like" state, but the manuscript never reconciles the small scaffold changes with the large kinase change — the significance framing outruns the effect sizes.
- Sweep: The Hrs-overexpression arm (AAV-Hrs-HA) is a strong contribution, but the manuscript does not state whether the 2.7-fold Hrs increase is within a physiologically plausible range or supraphysiological, which affects how the pGluA1-S831/PKC result should be read.
- Sweep: The phosphoproteomic analysis is presented as supporting "glutamatergic synaptic transmission" but the KEGG hits are dominated by GABAergic synapse and tight-junction terms; the manuscript should say which specific phosphopeptides support the glutamatergic claim rather than the pathway-level enrichment.
- Sweep: The claim that Hrs depletion "reduces surface GluA1" under Bic but not basal conditions is a nice specificity result, but the manuscript does not connect it to the electrophysiological finding that Hrs KD reduces AMPA/NMDA ratio — the two results are left as separate observations.
- Sweep: The authors' own 2023 paper is cited, but the presynaptic Hrs activity-dependence work (Sheehan 2016, Birdsall 2022) is cited only in passing and never engaged as the nearest prior claim on activity-dependent Hrs function.

## Questions
- What specifically does this manuscript establish that the authors' 2023 prion-disease study could not, stated as a list of non-disease findings?
- Is the "first evidence" claim intended to cover postsynaptic/level-based Hrs-activity coupling specifically, and can the manuscript state the presynaptic redistribution work as the prior art it distinguishes from?
- Can the authors state whether the 2.7-fold Hrs overexpression is within or beyond the physiological range, and does the pGluA1-S831 effect persist at a lower, more physiological overexpression level?
