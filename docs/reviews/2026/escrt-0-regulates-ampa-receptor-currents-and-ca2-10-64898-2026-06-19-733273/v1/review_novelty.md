# Novelty & Contribution Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript provides new evidence that Hrs/ESCRT-0 localizes to the post-synapse, is bidirectionally regulated by neuronal activity, and modulates AMPAR function and Ca²⁺-dependent signaling. These are genuine extensions of prior work, but the claim of "first evidence" for bidirectional responder/modulator status is too broad, and the overlap with the authors' own Lawrence et al. (2023) paper — which already linked Hrs to AMPAR derangement — needs sharper demarcation. The core contributions are sound and incrementally useful; the framing needs revision.

## Strengths
- Multi-level characterization (biochemical fractionation, confocal/STED microscopy, electrophysiology, phosphoproteomics) that converges on a consistent picture of Hrs at the post-synapse.
- The bidirectional activity-dependent regulation of Hrs protein levels — up with bicuculline, down with TTX — uses complementary pharmacological manipulations and is cleanly shown.
- Both loss-of-function (conditional KO, shRNA) and gain-of-function (AAV-mediated overexpression) approaches are deployed, strengthening causal inference.

## Weaknesses
- [Load-bearing, HARD] The "first evidence" claim outstrips the prior literature. Birdsall et al. (2022, Life Science Alliance) already demonstrated that Hrs undergoes activity-dependent redistribution at pre-synaptic terminals and that its axonal transport increases with elevated firing — establishing Hrs as an activity responder. The authors cite this work but do not state what their study adds beyond it. The genuinely new elements appear to be (i) that the regulation is bidirectional (both up and down, not just up), (ii) that it occurs at the post-synapse as well as the pre-synapse, and (iii) that total protein levels change, not just redistribution. These are real additions, but the "first" claim as written could mislead a reader. The delta must be stated explicitly, and the claim should be narrowed to what is actually new.
- [Load-bearing, SOFT] The core concept linking Hrs to AMPA receptors is not new. The authors' own Lawrence et al. (2023, Journal of Neuroscience) — titled "Diminished Neuronal ESCRT-0 Function Exacerbates AMPA Receptor Derangement and Accelerates Prion-Induced Neurodegeneration" — already established that Hrs depletion accelerates AMPAR hyperphosphorylation and synaptic pathology. The present study extends this to healthy, uninfected neurons and adds electrophysiological characterization, which is valuable. However, the manuscript should transparently acknowledge that the connection between Hrs and AMPARs is not novel per se; the novelty lies in the specific mechanisms and the non-disease context. As written, the introduction and discussion do not draw this distinction.
- [Sweep, SOFT] The activity-dependent regulation (Figure 2) was measured from whole cortical lysates, not from biochemically isolated post-synaptic fractions. Without a post-synaptic enrichment, the observed changes could reflect pre-synaptic Hrs pools (consistent with Birdsall et al.) or soma/dendritic pools, rather than specifically post-synaptic Hrs. This leaves the claim about "post-synaptic" activity regulation partially unsupported.
- [Sweep, SOFT] The phosphoproteomics dataset (Figure 4) identifies 16 differentially abundant phosphopeptides from ~924, but none of the hits beyond CaMKII are validated by orthogonal methods. As a discovery pipeline this is acceptable, but the paper's pathway-level claims about glutamatergic and GABAergic synapse involvement would be strengthened by at least one targeted validation (e.g., a phospho-western for gephyrin S264/S268 or Cntnap1).
- [Sweep, SOFT] The manuscript does not determine whether the altered AMPAR kinetics (Figures 3B–L) arise from changes in AMPAR ubiquitination and degradation, versus indirect effects via altered scaffolding, TARP composition, or CaMKII signaling. Given that Hrs's canonical function is ubiquitin-dependent sorting, this attribution is plausible but unproven.
- [Sweep, SOFT] The nearest-neighbor analysis from the STED data (Figure 1M and S2) would be more informative if it distinguished between synapses with and without a detectable Hrs cluster. As presented, the analysis averages across all synapses, which conflates different Hrs occupancy states.

## Questions
- Can you report the phosphoproteomics fold-changes with FDR-corrected p-values (e.g., Benjamini-Hochberg) alongside the nominal p-values currently shown? With 924 peptides tested, some of the 16 hits may be expected by chance.
