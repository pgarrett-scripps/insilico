# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The citation record is, on the whole, well-constructed: the foundational ESCRT-0 literature (Bache 2003, Ren & Hurley 2010, Hirano 2006), the AMPAR-ubiquitination line (Schwarz 2010, Lin 2011, Scudder 2014, Rodrigues 2016), and the activity-dependent Hrs work (Birdsall 2022, Sheehan 2016) are all present and appropriately attributed. My searches surfaced no directly competing prior work on Hrs at the post-synapse, so the novelty claim does not appear to rest on a suppressed citation. The revision is needed because three specific quantitative/empirical attributions could not be verified from the sources' metadata, and a foundational Hrs reference is absent. No confirmed HARD misattribution or fabricated reference was found.

## Strengths
- The AMPAR-ubiquitination-to-lysosome pathway is traced to its primary sources (Schwarz 2010, Lin 2011, Scudder 2014, Rodrigues 2016) rather than to reviews.
- Activity-dependent Hrs behavior is anchored to the directly relevant prior work (Birdsall 2022, Sheehan 2016), and the manuscript's own activity-regulation finding is positioned against it fairly.
- Self-citation (Lawrence 2023) is proportionate and directly load-bearing for the prion-disease framing.

## Weaknesses
- The "more than twice the affinity for ubiquitin as compared to STAM1" claim (Mayers et al., 2011) is a specific quantitative attribution I could not verify. Mayers 2011 ("ESCRT-0 assembles as a heterotetrameric complex on membranes and binds multiple ubiquitinylated cargoes simultaneously") addresses multivalent cargo binding, but the abstract/metadata do not establish the stated 2× affinity comparison against STAM1. This value underpins the premise that Hrs is the dominant ubiquitin gatekeeper of ESCRT-0 — the paper's central mechanistic framing. If the comparison comes from a different source, the citation is misattributed; if it is the authors' own measurement, it needs a methods citation. Please confirm the source of this specific value.
- The attribution "Hgs mRNA is highly expressed in brain regions crucial for learning and memory, including the hippocampus, cerebral cortex, and hypothalamus (Tamai et al., 2008)" could not be verified. Tamai 2008 is a pathology paper on Hrs loss causing ubiquitinated-protein accumulation and neurodegeneration; whether it reports regional Hgs mRNA expression is not established by its metadata. This sentence frames the relevance of Hrs to learning/memory circuits, so its source matters. Please confirm Tamai 2008 reports this expression pattern, or re-cite.
- The teetering-mouse claim — "neuromuscular weakness, synaptic dysfunction, and disrupted neurotransmitter release at the neuromuscular junction (Watson et al., 2015)" — is only partially verifiable. Watson 2015 (verified) reports motor and sensory deficits from HGS mutation, but the specific NMJ neurotransmitter-release finding is not confirmed by the abstract/metadata. This claim supports the "essential role for ESCRT-0 in synaptic processes" argument; please confirm the NMJ result is in Watson 2015 or cite the primary source that reports it.
- SOFT — the original Hrs identification (Komada & Kitamura, 1995, "Growth factor-induced tyrosine phosphorylation of Hrs…") is not cited in a paper centered on Hrs; consider adding it as the foundational reference.
- SOFT — the reference list as typed carries no DOIs and is garbled by conversion; authors should confirm every entry resolves, since I could not fully audit hygiene from the parsed list.
- No directly competing prior work on ESCRT-0 at the PSD surfaced in my searches, so the post-synaptic-localization novelty claim does not appear to omit a must-cite — though I could not exhaustively confirm absence of prior PSD-localization reports.
- The citation record is current (2000–2025) and not anchored to stale work; currency is not an issue.
