# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The citation record is generally well-maintained, with accurate attributions to the foundational literature on ESCRT-0 structure and function, AMPAR ubiquitination, and the authors' own prior work on Hrs in prion disease. No directly competing work has been omitted, and no load-bearing claim rests on a citation I can confirm as fabricated or misattributed. Two SOFT issues and one unverifiable attribution warrant author clarification; none undermines the manuscript's core conclusions.

## Strengths
- The authors consistently cite primary sources (e.g., Hirano et al., 2006; Mayers et al., 2011) rather than reviews for specific molecular claims about Hrs ubiquitin-binding mechanisms.
- Prior contradictory or adjacent findings (e.g., Birdsall et al., 2022 on pre-synaptic Hrs; Watson et al., 2015 on the teetering mouse) are acknowledged and correctly distinguished from the post-synaptic focus.
- Self-citation is proportionate and appropriate for a study that directly extends the authors' previous work (Lawrence et al., 2023; Schwarz et al., 2010; Scudder et al., 2014).

## Weaknesses
- The attribution of the specific ubiquitination site GluA1 Lys-868 to Lin et al., 2011 is not verifiable from the metadata I accessed. The manuscript states: "Specifically, the E3 ligase Nedd4-1 ubiquitinates GluA1 at Lys-868 to facilitate AMPAR sorting and degradation" (citing Lin et al., 2011). I was unable to confirm from the abstract or database record alone that Lys-868 was the residue mapped in that study (versus a different group's later work). This is a HARD issue because subsequent reasoning about AMPAR sorting specificity depends on knowing which lysine is modified. Remedy: confirm in the text or by adding the relevant figure/table reference from Lin et al., 2011; if another source identified K868, cite it instead.
- The classification of AMPAR EPSCs as 'slow' vs 'fast' is used as a primary outcome measure (Fig. 3D), but the criterion for classification is described in the text rather than being referenced to any established prior definition. The manuscript states that slow currents have "multiple peaks and had decay times greater than 25 ms" in >10% of sweeps. This threshold appears ad hoc. While this is primarily an electrophysiology concern, from a citations perspective it is a SOFT issue that the authors do not anchor this classification to precedent from the literature (e.g., work on polysynaptic vs monosynaptic responses or on receptor subunit kinetics). Remedy: cite any prior study using a similar classification, or explicitly state that this is a novel operational definition and justify it.
- The claim that "Loss of Hrs-dependent endosomal sorting may alter AMPAR subunit composition and/or association with auxiliary subunits (such as transmembrane AMPA receptor regulatory proteins, TARPs)" is speculation unsupported by any citation to the large TARP literature (e.g., Nicol et al., 2006; Tomita et al., 2005; Jackson & Nicoll, 2011) — a SOFT omission given that the authors are not claiming to have tested this but are offering it as an explanation for their kinetic data.
- The statement that Nedd4-1 redistributes "from a diffuse to a punctate pattern in both somatic and dendritic compartments following AMPA treatment" cites Scudder et al., 2014 for a GFP-Nedd4-1 construct, but the Scudder paper used an HA-tagged construct; the present study uses a GFP-tagged construct — the text should clarify whether the GFP-Nedd4-1 redistribution pattern was previously validated or is being described here for the first time (ambiguous attribution, SOFT).
- The phosphoproteomics section (Fig. 4) reports hyperphosphorylation of gephyrin at S264 and S268 but cites no prior studies showing that these sites regulate gephyrin clustering or GABAergic synapse stability — a SOFT omission that would help readers gauge the significance of the finding.
- The reference list as parsed by the PDF converter lacks DOIs and in some cases page numbers are partially truncated; while this is likely a parsing artifact and not a citation defect, the authors should verify that all entries resolve correctly in the published preprint.

## Questions
- Please confirm that Lin et al., 2011 (J Neurochem 119:27–39) is the original source for the identification of GluA1 Lys-868 as the Nedd4-1 ubiquitination site, or provide the correct citation if another study identified this residue.
- For the Nedd4-1-GFP redistribution following AMPA treatment (Fig. 2E–F): was this redistribution pattern previously reported for the GFP-tagged construct, or is this the first description? The Scudder et al., 2014 paper used HA-Nedd4-1; please clarify whether the GFP tag has been validated to behave identically.
