# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The citation practice is sound: the major claims are attributed to appropriate primary sources, the reference list corresponds to real published work I could verify through the metadata available, and the coverage of foundational AMPAR-trafficking, ubiquitination, and ESCRT biology literature is thorough. I find no HARD citation defects (fabricated references, false attributions, or silently ignored contradicting prior work). Two SOFT issues are noted below. The manuscript's central novelty — that ESCRT-0/Hrs localises to the PSD, is bidirectionally regulated by activity, and modulates AMPAR function and CaMKII signalling — does not obviously overlap with any un-cited directly competing work.

## Weaknesses
- Self-citation proportion — Several citations (Schwarz et al., 2010; Scudder et al., 2014; Goo et al., 2015; Rodrigues et al., 2016; Lawrence et al., 2023; Djakovic et al., 2009, 2012; Keil et al., 2010) originate from the Patrick lab. This is proportionate given that the Patrick lab has been a primary contributor to the ubiquitin–AMPAR trafficking literature, but the authors should confirm that no external group's parallel finding on the same point has been overlooked.
- Citation for the UPS/ESCRT distinction in the introduction — The sentence "ESCRT machinery targets mono- or short-chain ubiquitinated transmembrane proteins for lysosomal degradation" cites Jarome & Helmstetter (2013) and Soykan et al. (2021). Jarome & Helmstetter focuses on the proteasome and LTM, not on ESCRT specifically; Soykan et al. (2021) is the correct reference for the ESCRT part. The Jarome citation is a tangential fit. Consider replacing it with a dedicated ESCRT review or dropping it.
- DOI / resolver information — The reference list is in standard journal format without DOIs. This is acceptable for a preprint review venue, but the authors should ensure that all entries are resolvable through PubMed or their journal's archive. One reference — Quasney & Griffith (2025), "Degradation of CaMKII is stimulated by its active conformation" (JBC 301) — appears very recent (2025); its volume number may update before publication. Please verify the final citation data.
