# Reproducibility Reviewer

## Summary
The experimental procedures are mostly described in recoverable detail — centrifugation speeds, buffer compositions, inclusion criteria, and analysis software versions are given — and the mass spectrometry data carries a ProteomeExchange accession. However, the manuscript has multiple HARD reproducibility gaps: the custom R analysis pipeline is not deposited as code, the non-proteomics data (western blots, electrophysiology, imaging) has no availability statement at all, the STED segmentation recipes are not shared, and the surface biotinylation protocol names two different reagents. These are fixable, but as written an independent group could not rerun the work end-to-end.

## Weaknesses
- The STED overlap statistics (Figure 1I–O) are not reproducible without the trained segmentation recipes. The 89–91% overlap between Hrs and Bassoon/PSD-95, and all cluster volume/density and distance measurements, rest on Aivia Pixel Classifiers trained on "a representative image" and applied by batch. The trained classifiers, the segmentation parameters, and the raw STED images are not deposited, and no numeric threshold is given. An independent group cannot reproduce the overlap statistics without re-deriving a subjective segmentation step. What would settle it: deposit the trained classifiers and exact segmentation parameters, or provide raw STED images with a stated thresholding procedure. I could not verify the STED images themselves, only the text describing them.
- The surface biotinylation protocol (Figure 3M–N) names two different reagents. The opening sentence specifies "Sulfo-NHS-SS-Biotin (thermos #21335)" — a cleavable disulfide reagent — but the procedure states "Neurons were incubated with NHS-LC-Biotin (1 mg/mL in PBS++)", a non-cleavable reagent. The elution step (boiling in 3× SDS sample buffer) matches LC-biotin, not SS-biotin, which requires reductive cleavage. An independent group cannot know which reagent was used or how the surface fraction was actually eluted, and this directly affects the surface GluA1 quantification that supports the activity-dependent trafficking claim.
- The custom R analysis for the phosphoproteomics (Figure 4) is not deposited as code. The pipeline — normalization of phosphopeptide intensities to unmodified protein intensities, Welch's t-tests, GO/KEGG enrichment via clusterProfiler, heatmap and volcano generation — is described in prose with package versions, but there is no repository, commit, archived DOI, or seed statement. The prose is detailed enough that re-implementation is plausible, but the mandate is explicit that custom analysis code must be at a resolvable, versioned location; "described in prose" is not availability. This is a HARD flag for the load-bearing Figure 4.
- No data availability statement exists for the western blots (Figures 5–7), electrophysiology traces (Figure 3), or imaging data (Figures 1–2); these are load-bearing and are neither deposited nor offered as supplementary — HARD.
- The hSyn1-Cre strain is cited as "The Jackson Laboratory" with no stock number, and the Hrs shRNA and scrambled control sequences are not given (VectorBuilder, no target sequence) — reagent traceability, methods_completeness's lane.
- STED software versions (Huygens, Aivia) are not stated — SOFT.
- The "fast/slow" AMPAR classification criteria are specified and therefore reproducible, but whether the classification reflects biology rather than recording artifacts is data_analysis's call.
- The phosphoproteomics significance threshold (unadjusted p < 0.05 across 924 phosphopeptides) yields an expected false-positive count (~46) exceeding the 16 reported — statistical validity, data_analysis's lane.
- No random seed or seed-averaging statement for the R analysis — SOFT.
