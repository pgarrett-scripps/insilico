# Journal Recommendations

## As-is (current quality)
- **bioRxiv (Neuroscience)**
  - Fit: The paper is a preprint-ready empirical neuroscience study on ESCRT-0/Hrs regulation of AMPAR currents and Ca2+-dependent signaling; bioRxiv's Neuroscience section is the standard home for such work and matches the topic and methodology directly.
  - Realism: Posting on bioRxiv requires no peer review, so the manuscript can be shared immediately at its current quality; the panel's major-verdict concerns are documented but do not block deposition.
- **In Silico**
  - Fit: The manuscript is an original, empirically checkable research preprint whose claims (activity-dependence, AMPAR kinetics, CaMKII/PSD-95 changes) can be evaluated from the manuscript and deposited materials, which is exactly the scope of this open overlay journal.
  - Realism: Given the panel's major verdict (3.12/5, 3 major + 2 reject), the paper would likely be scored as needing major revision rather than accepted as-is; it is realistic only as a revision-track submission, not a clean acceptance.

## After required revisions
- **Journal of Neuroscience**
  - Fit: The topic — ESCRT-0/Hrs regulation of AMPAR trafficking, AMPA/NMDA ratio, CaMKII signaling and synaptic scaffolds — is squarely within J Neurosci's cellular/molecular and synaptic physiology scope, and the required revisions (raw amplitudes, FDR-corrected phosphoproteomics, acute-knockout controls) are exactly the rigor this venue expects.
  - Realism: After the required revisions resolve the AMPA/NMDA confound, the developmental-knockout caveat, and the phosphoproteomics FDR issue, this becomes a competitive submission; without those fixes the odds are low.
- **eLife**
  - Fit: eLife publishes mechanistic synaptic-plasticity and membrane-trafficking studies and values the kind of multi-system evidence (electrophysiology, biochemistry, phosphoproteomics, imaging) this paper assembles, provided the causal claims are requalified.
  - Realism: Realistic only after the major revisions are fully addressed; the current major verdict and reproducibility gaps would likely not clear eLife's bar as-is.
- **Molecular and Cellular Neuroscience**
  - Fit: This specialty journal covers ubiquitin-dependent trafficking, endosomal sorting and neuronal membrane-protein turnover, matching the ESCRT-0/AMPAR core of the paper.
  - Realism: A solid fit with moderate acceptance odds after revision; the venue is more tolerant of a hypothesis-generating phosphoproteomics framing than the top-tier journals.

## Alternative outlets
- **Frontiers in Cellular Neuroscience**
  - Fit: A specialty cellular-neuroscience journal that publishes synaptic-trafficking and receptor-regulation studies and is receptive to hypothesis-generating proteomics when framed as such.
  - Realism: A realistic fallback: moderate acceptance odds even with the current major-verdict issues, provided the claims are requalified to match the evidence.
- **PLOS ONE**
  - Fit: PLOS ONE accepts sound empirical work regardless of perceived novelty, which fits a paper whose core activity-dependence finding is solid but whose broader causal claims need requalification.
  - Realism: Reasonable acceptance odds after the required revisions, since PLOS ONE evaluates soundness rather than impact; the reproducibility and reporting gaps would still need to be closed.
- **bioRxiv (Neuroscience)**
  - Fit: If the headline journals prove out of reach, the preprint server remains the durable home for the work and its deposited data (PXD073715, raw traces).
  - Realism: No peer-review barrier; posting is immediate and preserves priority for the novel activity-dependence finding.

## Notes
The manuscript is a wet-lab empirical study, so the 'In Silico' target name is a venue label, not a description of the methods; the paper contains no computational modeling. Fit to In Silico is scope-appropriate but the panel's major verdict means it would realistically enter on a revision track. The strongest after-revision prospects are specialty neuroscience journals rather than generalist venues, given the narrow synaptic-trafficking topic.
