# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript's citation record is generally sound and accurately attributes claims to sources. The key foundational references on proper scoring rules (Gneiting & Raftery [10], Ferro [9]) and fair CRPS variants are present and correctly cited. The AIFS-CRPS baseline [13] is appropriately cited as the primary methodological predecessor. However, there are two notable gaps: (1) the claim about patched energy scores from Pacchiardi et al. [19] performing "best among multivariate scoring rules" is stated but the manuscript does not verify this claim against the actual results in [19], and (2) the related-work section is sparse—it mentions recent global models (FGN, FourCastNet 3, Huracan) but does not systematically position this work against competing approaches to scale-aware training or multivariate scoring in weather forecasting. The reference list itself is clean and resolvable; no fabricated or garbled citations were detected. The work is appropriately positioned as preliminary and incremental, which matches the citation strategy. Currency is reasonable for a 2026 submission, with recent 2024–2026 references to competing methods.

## Strengths
- Core foundational references on proper scoring rules (Gneiting & Raftery [10]) and fair CRPS (Ferro [9], Leutbecher [16]) are correctly cited and accurately attributed.
- The AIFS-CRPS paper [13] is appropriately cited as the direct methodological baseline and predecessor.
- Recent competing work (FGN [1], FourCastNet 3 [4], Huracan [17]) is cited and briefly discussed in the Discussion section, showing awareness of the state of the art.
- The multi-scale loss formulation [14] is cited as the source of the scale-decomposition approach used in experiments.
- References to Pacchiardi et al. [19] for patched energy scores and Scheuerer & Hamill [21] for variogram scores are present and relevant.
- No obvious citation fabrications, garbled references, or unresolvable DOIs detected.

## Weaknesses
- Patched energy score claim not verified: The manuscript states (Section 1, Introduction) that "In weather forecasting experiments of [19], patched energy scores performed best among multivariate scoring rules they considered." This is presented as motivation for the graph-based approach, but the manuscript does not cite specific results or figures from [19] to support this claim. The reader cannot verify whether [19] actually concluded this or whether the claim is accurately characterized.
- Sparse related-work section: The Introduction and Discussion do not systematically review competing approaches to scale-aware training in machine-learned weather forecasting. The manuscript mentions that "different ways have proposed to improve spectral representation" and lists several approaches, but does not critically compare these approaches or explain why the present study's approach is distinct or complementary.
- Missing discussion of strict propriety of graph energy score: The manuscript acknowledges (Section 2, Graph Energy Score) that "graph energy score, however, may fail to strictly proper" and notes that strict propriety may be recovered under additional assumptions, but then cites [19] as justification for combining it with a global anchor without discussing whether the combined score (fGESgraph + 0.1 fES) is strictly proper or citing a source that establishes this.
- No citation for verification of "almost fair CRPS" concept: The manuscript introduces the "almost fair CRPS" (afCRPS) as a convex combination of fair and standard CRPS, citing [13] as the source, but does not verify whether [13] actually introduced this variant or merely used it.
- Limited discussion of why multivariate scores should improve tropical forecasts: The manuscript observes (Section 4.1) that "in tropics, graph energy score experiment appears to perform best, while energy score experiment shows some degradation," but there is no discussion of why multivariate scores might be expected to perform differently in the tropics versus extratropics, or citation of prior work on this regional dependence.

## Questions
- In Section 1, you state that patched energy scores "performed best among multivariate scoring rules" in [19]. Can you cite the specific figure, table, or result from [19] that supports this claim?
- For the combined loss L_graph = fGESgraph + 0.1 fES used in Section 3.1, is the combined score strictly proper? If so, is this established in [19] or elsewhere, or is it an assumption you are making?
- The manuscript notes that graph energy score "may fail to strictly proper" but then uses it in training. What is the practical implication of this for the forecast skill results in Section 4.1? Does the weak global anchor sufficiently recover propriety for your purposes?
- In Section 4.1, you observe regional differences (extratropics vs. tropics) in the sensitivity to scoring rules. Is there prior work on why multivariate spatial scores might be more or less effective in different climate regimes, or is this an open question you are flagging?
- The manuscript mentions that "weighting of different fields, scales, and variables can matter as much as, or more than, the specific mechanism used to inject spatial awareness into loss" (Discussion). Are there prior studies on the sensitivity of machine-learned weather models to these weighting choices, or is this a novel observation from your experiments?
