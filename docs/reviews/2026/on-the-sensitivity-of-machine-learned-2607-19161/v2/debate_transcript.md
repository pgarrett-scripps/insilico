# Debate Transcript

## Advocate — round 1

The manuscript's core contribution is a genuine one: a unified, mathematically precise taxonomy of localized multivariate scoring rules (graph energy, graph variogram, graph edge energy, spectral energy/magnitude scores) implemented consistently in Anemoi and tested under matched architecture and training conditions (Section 3.1). This is not merely incremental restatement—extending patch-based localization (Pacchiardi et al.) to flexible graph neighbourhoods that 'does not rely on fixed rectangular patches' and 'can in principle be applied on irregular grids, sparse spatial meshes, or sparse observation networks' is a real methodological generalization, even if not yet tested on irregular grids. The finding that global energy score 'shows some degradation' while graph energy 'performs somewhat better' in the tropics is a substantive, non-obvious empirical signal consistent with known theory that energy score can be 'relatively insensitive to correlation structure... in high-dimensional settings' [21,19] — this is a genuine strength, not merely fixable presentation.

The reviewers' harshest complaints—missing confidence intervals, no code/seed release, ad hoc weighting sensitivity, vague qualifiers like 'slightly'—are legitimate but classically remediable in revision: they require adding statistics to existing runs, depositing code already built on open-source Anemoi, and running weight-perturbation ablations, not rethinking the experimental design itself. The authors already model good practice by candidly flagging limitations (Section 5: 'greater model capacity and longer training may... reduce differences') rather than concealing them, and the ethics reviewer confirms full compliance with data/licensing norms.

The methodology reviewer's confound concern (asymmetric anchor terms between graph and global energy experiments) is real but affects interpretation of one comparison, not the paper's broader, well-supported finding that scale-awareness—via multi-scale or spectral losses—improves spectral realism relative to single-scale CRPS/energy score, visible consistently across Figures 3–14. This should be accepted with revisions requiring quantification and reproducibility artifacts, not rejected.

**Key points:**
- Unified graph-based localization framework (graph energy, variogram, edge scores) is a genuine methodological extension beyond fixed-patch approaches, motivated by flexibility for irregular grids/sparse networks
- Tropical CRPS degradation for global energy score vs. improvement for graph energy score is a substantive, theoretically-grounded empirical finding, not noise dressed as insight
- Missing statistics, code/seed disclosure, and weighting sensitivity analyses are legitimate but revision-fixable gaps, not flaws in the underlying experimental design
- Authors demonstrate good-faith transparency by explicitly flagging model-capacity and training-duration limitations rather than overclaiming
- The confound in the anchor-term design (Section 3.1) affects one comparison's interpretation but does not undermine the broader, consistent finding across 12 experiments that scale-aware losses improve spectral fidelity

## Skeptic — round 1

The advocate concedes the reviewers' quantification/reproducibility complaints are 'legitimate' but calls them revision-fixable — that's the crux I dispute. Fixing them requires re-running experiments, not adding paperwork. The methodology reviewer identifies a confound: L_graph = fGESgraph + 0.1·fES has an anchor term the global-energy-score experiment lacks, so the tropical result the advocate calls 'substantive' cannot be attributed to graph localization at all — it could simply be the anchor. That's a design flaw, not a missing statistic. Compounding this, every headline claim ('performs somewhat better,' 'some degradation,' 'broadly similar') rests on a single training run per configuration (rigor and data_analysis reviewers both note no seeds, no CIs, no significance tests). You cannot retrofit confidence intervals onto n=1 runs in a revision — that requires new multi-seed experiments, which is a substantive re-run, not an editorial fix. The advocate's own examples of 'genuine findings' (tropical CRPS degradation, spectral improvement) are exactly the claims that lack this support.

I'll concede the taxonomy of graph-localized scores and their clean mathematical exposition is a real, citable contribution — that survives independent of the empirical claims. But the abstract's core assertions ('multivariate scores viable alternative,' 'graph energy score performs somewhat better') are what the paper is being judged on, and those specific claims are unsupported by the evidence as designed, not merely under-reported. That combination — confounded design plus zero replication — is fatal to the paper's stated conclusions as currently written, even if a resubmission with replicated, deconfounded runs could rescue them.

**Key points:**
- Confound (asymmetric anchor term between graph and global energy experiments) undermines attribution of the tropical result, per methodology review — this is a design flaw, not a stats-reporting gap
- Headline claims rest on single training runs per configuration with no seeds/replication; adding CIs in revision is impossible without rerunning experiments, contra advocate's framing as 'fixable'
- The paper's own abstract-level claims (viability of multivariate scores, tropical superiority) are precisely the ones lacking statistical support, not peripheral details
- Concede: the mathematical taxonomy of graph-based localized scores is a genuine, citable contribution independent of the empirical claims
- Fatal as written: confounded design + zero replication means the central empirical conclusions are not currently supportable, though a redesigned resubmission could remedy this
