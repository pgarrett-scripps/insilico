# Clarity & Presentation Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript presents a systematic comparison of different probabilistic scoring rules for training machine-learned weather forecast models, with investigation of scale-aware loss formulations. The core narrative is clear: can multivariate scores match CRPS-based training, and how do different approaches to scale awareness affect forecast field spectra? However, the presentation suffers from several HARD clarity issues that impede full understanding. The abstract is difficult to parse and contains grammatical errors that obscure the main claims. Multiple figures lack clear captions or are referenced ambiguously. Key experimental details are scattered or implicit rather than explicitly stated upfront. The paper would benefit from restructuring the abstract, clarifying figure references, and making the experimental design more transparent before results are presented.

## Strengths
- The paper clearly defines all scoring rules mathematically in Section 2, with explicit formulas for CRPS, energy score, graph energy score, graph variogram score, and graph edge energy score variants.
- Experimental design is reproducible: specific hyperparameters are provided (learning rates, training iterations, rollout schedules, grid resolution O96, ensemble size 8, kernel widths for smoothing operators).
- The two-part structure (forecast skill comparison vs. spectral analysis) is logically organized and the purpose of each experiment set is stated.
- Figure 1 provides a helpful schematic showing how different scores operate on graph neighbourhoods, with clear labeling of source and destination nodes.
- The paper acknowledges limitations explicitly: smaller models, lower resolution, and shortened training schedules compared to operational systems.

## Weaknesses
- The abstract is difficult to parse and contains multiple grammatical errors that obscure the main contribution. Sentences like 'This note summarises preliminary study comparing versions of AIFS-CRPS, global weather forecast model, trained with different univariate and multivariate scoring rules aim to explicitly represent scale awareness' lack clear subject-verb agreement and are ambiguous about what is being compared.
- Figures 3–14 are referenced collectively but their captions are minimal and repetitive ('Accumulated tendency spectra for geopotential at 500 hPa' appears four times with no distinction between panels). It is unclear from the captions alone what the difference is between Figures 3 and 4, or between 5 and 6, or how to interpret the ratio plots versus absolute spectra plots.
- The paper does not explicitly state upfront which experiments use which loss configurations before diving into results. Table 1 and Table 2 appear late; the reader must infer from context which experiment corresponds to which result in Section 4.
- In Section 3.2, the statement 'We used similar approach for spectral losses with per-waveband weighting. These weighting factors ad hoc and chosen only to of right order of magnitude' contains a grammatical error ('ad hoc and chosen only to of') and is vague about what 'right order of magnitude' means quantitatively.
- The claim in Section 4.2 that 'edge-CRPS experiment and spectral magnitude CRPS experiment seem to slightly more successful' uses vague qualifier 'slightly' without quantification. How much is 'slightly'? Readers cannot determine the magnitude of difference from the text alone.
- Figure references are inconsistent: the text refers to 'figure 2' (lowercase) in some places and 'Figure 2' (capitalized) in others, and the narrative flow between results and figures is unclear—e.g., 'see figure 2' appears mid-paragraph without clear indication of what aspect of the figure supports which claim.
- The multi-scale loss formulation (Section 2, equations for L_K-scale) is presented abstractly without concrete specification of how the smoothing operators D_i are implemented in the experiments until Section 3.2, where it states 'four graph-based Gaussian smoothing operators on native O96 grid, with kernel widths of approximately 100, 200, 400, and 800 km.' The word 'approximately' introduces ambiguity about whether these are exact or estimated values.
- The paper does not clearly state whether all 12 experiments in Section 3.2 use the same architecture and training data as Section 3.1, or whether the 'smaller model' and 'shorter training schedule' mentioned imply different hyperparameters. This is critical for interpreting whether differences are due to loss function or model capacity.
- In Section 4.1, the statement 'conclusions also consistent when fair energy score or fair graph energy score used for verification instead of fair CRPS' is vague—it does not specify which conclusions or provide any numbers to support the claim that results are 'consistent.'

## Questions
- Can the authors clarify the abstract? Specifically, what does 'versions of AIFS-CRPS, global weather forecast model' mean—are AIFS-CRPS and the global weather forecast model the same thing, or different? The sentence structure suggests ambiguity.
- Figures 3–14: Can the captions be expanded to distinguish between absolute spectra plots and ratio plots, and to explain what the reader should look for in each? Currently, four figures have nearly identical captions.
- In Section 3.2, what are the exact (not approximate) kernel widths used for the four Gaussian smoothing operators? If they are approximate, what is the tolerance?
- Table 2 lists 12 experiments, but the text in Section 4.2 discusses results from these experiments without always making clear which experiment produced which result. Can the authors add a mapping between experiment names in Table 2 and the results discussed in Section 4.2?
- In Section 4.1, when the authors state that 'conclusions also consistent when fair energy score or fair graph energy score used for verification,' can they provide specific numbers or figures showing this consistency?
- Section 3.2 states the model uses 'embedding dimension of 256 and 12 processor layers' versus the unspecified architecture in Section 3.1. Can the authors clarify the architecture differences between the two experiment sets and confirm whether this affects the comparability of results?
- The paper claims 'scale awareness alone does not guarantee realistic variability at all scales' (Section 4.2), but does not quantify what 'realistic' means. Is this assessed against ERA5 variance, or against some other metric?
- In Section 2, the paper states that graph energy score 'may fail to strictly proper' and proposes combining it with a global anchor. Can the authors clarify whether the combined score (fGESgraph + 0.1 fES) is strictly proper, and if so, under what conditions?
