# Meta-Review

**Draft recommendation:** major

## Synthesis
The panel converges on a consistent picture: the manuscript is an honestly-labelled preliminary study whose mathematical exposition of localized multivariate scoring rules (graph energy, graph variogram, graph edge energy, spectral variants) is clear and citable, but whose empirical claims are under-supported. Ethics (5/5, conf 5) finds no compliance issues beyond a missing COI and data/code availability statement. Literature (4/5) finds the citation record clean and honest, with two specific gaps: the unverified characterization of Pacchiardi et al.'s patched-energy result and no source establishing propriety of the combined fGESgraph + 0.1 fES objective. The remaining five reviewers cluster at 2	he same three defects.

First, quantification. data_analysis, rigor and methodology independently report that every headline claim \u2014 'broadly similar' skill, graph energy 'performs somewhat better' in the tropics, energy score shows 'some degradation', scale-aware losses 'substantially improve' small-scale variability \u2014 rests on visual inspection of Figure 2 and Figures 3\u201314 with no error bars, no effect sizes, no significance tests, and no defined metric for spectral 'improvement'. The title promises a sensitivity analysis; rigor correctly notes that no sensitivity is actually quantified.

Second, design. The methodology reviewer's confound is the sharpest single finding: the graph-energy experiment carries a 0.1 fES anchor that the global-energy experiment does not, so the tropical difference cannot be attributed to graph localization rather than to the anchor. Section 3.2's twelve configurations vary scoring rule, localization, scale decomposition, weighting and spectral representation simultaneously, so the Discussion's claim that weighting matters as much as mechanism is not isolable from the design as run.

Third, reproducibility, the lowest score (2/5, conf 4): no code deposit or Anemoi commit hash, no seeds, no model checkpoints, no preprocessing or split specification, unspecified architecture for the Section 3.1 experiments, and undefined nearest-neighbour metric for the k=16 graph. Clarity adds that Figures 3\u201314 carry near-duplicate captions that make it impossible to tell spectra from ratio plots, which compounds the problem: the figures are the evidence, and they are not readable as presented.

The debate sharpened the split. The advocate is right that the graph-based taxonomy is a real generalization of patch-based localization and that the authors are candid about limitations \u2014 the skeptic conceded this. But the advocate's central move, that the gaps are 'revision-fixable', did not survive: the skeptic's rejoinder that one cannot retrofit confidence intervals onto n=1 training runs, and that an asymmetric anchor term is a design choice rather than a reporting omission, is correct and is backed by the methodology and rigor reports. What the skeptic overreaches on is 'fatal'. The spectral-fidelity finding is supported across twelve configurations and is directionally robust even without formal tests; the taxonomy stands on its own; and the paper explicitly labels itself preliminary, which In Silico's guidelines instruct us not to penalise where claims are scaled to match. The problem is that the abstract's framing \u2014 'viable alternative to CRPS-based training' \u2014 is not scaled to match.

## Decisive Factors
The confidence-weighted average (3.30) and the verdict distribution (5 major) both land on major revision, and I concur rather than escalating to the single reject vote from reproducibility. Reject would be warranted if the central claim did not survive the evidence at all; here a subset does. The spectral-realism result across twelve configurations, and the graph-score formalism itself, survive contact with the criticism. What does not survive is the abstract-level attribution of tropical improvement to graph localization (confounded by the asymmetric anchor) and the framing of multivariate scores as a demonstrated 'viable alternative' from a single O96 run per configuration.\n\nA route forward that would move this to acceptance: (1) rerun the Section 3.1 comparison with symmetric anchor treatment, or state plainly in the abstract and Section 4.1 that the tropical difference is confounded and cannot be attributed to localization; (2) provide multi-seed runs or, failing that, bootstrap CIs over the 84 initialization dates and explicit sample sizes for Figure 2, and replace 'slightly', 'somewhat better' and 'substantially' with numbers; (3) define a scalar metric for spectral fidelity against ERA5 so 'improvement' is measurable rather than visual; (4) deposit code with an Anemoi commit hash, seeds, configs and preferably checkpoints, and add data/code availability and COI statements; (5) rewrite the captions of Figures 3\u201314 so absolute spectra and ratio plots are distinguishable and each experiment is identified; (6) state the Section 3.1 architecture explicitly and confirm comparability with Section 3.2; (7) give a sensitivity check on the ad hoc scale weights and the 0.1 anchor weight, even a coarse \u00b150% perturbation; (8) tighten novelty boundaries by crediting Lang et al. (2025) for the multi-scale formulation and stating explicitly which spectral scores are new. Item (2) and item (4) are the ones that most determine whether a reader can trust this preprint; item (1) is the one that determines whether the abstract is currently accurate.
