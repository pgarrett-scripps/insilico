# Reproducibility Reviewer

## Summary
This manuscript presents a preliminary comparison of different scoring rules for training machine-learned weather forecast models, with experiments on spectral properties of forecast fields. While the research question is sound and the scoring-rule formulations are clearly presented, the work suffers from critical reproducibility gaps that prevent independent replication. The authors provide no code repository, no deposited training data specifications, no random seeds, no exact software versions for key dependencies (PyTorch, Triton, spherical harmonic transform library), and insufficient procedural detail to reconstruct the experimental pipeline. The Anemoi framework is referenced as the implementation vehicle but no commit hash or version is specified. Training configurations are partially described but key details are missing (e.g., exact optimizer state initialization, data preprocessing steps, validation/test split procedures). The results—particularly the spectral analysis in Section 4.2—depend on computational choices (smoothing kernel widths, spectral truncation, waveband grouping) that are described as "ad hoc" and "chosen only to be of right order of magnitude" rather than principled or tuned, yet no ablation or sensitivity analysis justifies these choices. Without access to code, trained model weights, or a reproducible computational environment, an independent group cannot obtain the same outputs from the described inputs. For a venue like In Silico where reviews are public and readers must judge whether to trust a preprint, this level of opacity is disqualifying.

## Strengths
- The mathematical formulation of scoring rules (Section 2) is clear and well-presented, with proper definitions of fair variants and multivariate extensions.
- The research question—whether multivariate or scale-aware scoring rules can match CRPS-based training—is well-motivated and addresses a gap in the literature on machine-learned weather forecasting.
- The paper acknowledges limitations explicitly (Section 5: smaller models, low resolution, shortened training schedule) rather than overstating conclusions.
- The use of accumulated tendency spectra as a diagnostic for physical realism is a principled choice for assessing forecast field quality beyond point-wise metrics.

## Weaknesses
- No code repository is provided or referenced with a commit hash or DOI. The manuscript states 'All scores implemented in Anemoi framework' (Abstract) and later 'We use Anemoi framework (https://github.com/ecmwf/anemoi-core)' (Section 3.1), but provides no version, branch, or commit identifier, and does not state whether custom scoring-rule implementations are deposited anywhere.
- No trained model weights, checkpoints, or intermediate outputs are deposited or promised with an accession number. The results in Section 4 depend entirely on these models, which cannot be inspected or reused without them.
- Random seeds are not specified for any experiment. The manuscript uses AdamW optimizer and neural network training but does not state whether results are averaged over multiple runs, what seeds were used, or whether seed-averaging was performed.
- Software versions are missing for critical dependencies: PyTorch (referenced via torch.compile, Section 3.1), Triton (Section 3.1), the spherical harmonic transform library (Section 3.2: 'spherical harmonic transform with truncation T191'), and the ERA5 data loader. These affect numerical reproducibility.
- Hyperparameter choices in Section 3.2 are explicitly described as ad hoc: 'These weighting factors ad hoc and chosen only to be of right order of magnitude - estimated from data - rather than tuned.' No ablation study, sensitivity analysis, or justification is provided for the scale weights ζ_i, spectral band boundaries (0–10, 11–20, 21–80, 81–120, 121–191), or Gaussian smoothing kernel widths (100, 200, 400, 800 km). Without this, the spectral results (Figures 3–14) cannot be independently validated or understood to be robust.
- Data preprocessing is not fully specified. The manuscript states 'Training uses ERA5 reanalysis data from 1979 to 2020, and inference performed for 2022' (Section 3.1) but does not describe: normalization/standardization procedures, handling of missing values, interpolation to the O96 grid, or train/validation/test split logic. These are load-bearing for reproducibility.
- The encoder–processor–decoder architecture is described as following 'AIFS-CRPS [13]' but Section 3.1 states 'we restrict resolution here to O96 ≈ 1 deg model' without specifying embedding dimension, number of processor layers, or other hyperparameters for the Section 3.1 experiments. Section 3.2 specifies 'embedding dimension of 256 and 12 processor layers' but only for the cheaper configuration, not the main experiments.
- The graph connectivity for graph-based scores is defined as 'k-nearest-neighbour graph with k=16 on O96 reduced Gaussian grid' (Section 3.1) but the method for computing nearest neighbours (Euclidean? geodesic? on the sphere?) and tie-breaking are not specified.
- Spectral analysis methodology (Section 4.2) lacks detail: How are spectra computed from ensemble forecasts (per-member or ensemble-mean)? The text states 'we average spectra over all ensemble members' but does not clarify whether this is done before or after taking the ratio to ERA5. How are the 84 initialization dates (Section 4.2) combined into a single spectrum (mean? median?)? What is the confidence interval or uncertainty estimate?
- Figures 3–14 are difficult to interpret without a legend or caption explaining which line/color corresponds to which experiment. The captions repeat the same text ('Accumulated tendency spectra for [variable] at 500 hPa') without distinguishing between Figures 3 and 4, or between spectra and ratios. This appears to be a formatting error that obscures the results.
- The paper states 'we compute spectra of accumulated forecast tendencies' (Section 4.2) but does not define how accumulated tendencies are computed from multi-step forecasts. Are they computed at each lead time separately and then averaged? Are they computed as the difference between the final forecast and the initial state?
- No statement is made about whether code or data will be deposited upon publication, or under what conditions they will be made available. The acknowledgments mention the Anemoi framework but do not commit to releasing the experimental code or models.
- The training schedule differs between Section 3.1 (150k steps at rollout 1, 30k at rollout 2, 1k per step from 3–12) and Section 3.2 (150k steps one-step, then 1k per step up to 8). It is unclear whether these are the only two configurations tested or whether other schedules were explored and not reported.

## Questions
- What is the exact version (commit hash or release tag) of the Anemoi framework used for these experiments, and where are the custom scoring-rule implementations deposited?
- Are trained model weights, optimizer states, or checkpoints available for download or inspection? If not, will they be deposited upon publication, and under what accession?
- What random seeds were used for model initialization, data shuffling, and any stochastic components of training? Were results averaged over multiple runs, and if so, how many?
- What are the exact versions of PyTorch, Triton, and the spherical harmonic transform library (e.g., SHTns, pyshtools) used? Do results depend on specific hardware (GPU model, CUDA version)?
- How were the ad hoc weighting factors (scale weights ζ_i, spectral band boundaries, Gaussian kernel widths) chosen? Can you provide an ablation study or sensitivity analysis showing how results change if these are varied?
- Can you provide a step-by-step description of data preprocessing: normalization, handling of missing values, interpolation to O96, train/validation/test split, and any other transformations applied to ERA5 data before training?
- For Section 3.1 experiments, what are the embedding dimension and number of processor layers? Are these the same as in Section 3.2 (256 and 12)?
- How are nearest neighbours computed for the k-nearest-neighbour graph (Euclidean distance on the grid, geodesic distance on the sphere, or something else)? How are ties broken?
- In Section 4.2, how are spectra aggregated across the 84 initialization dates? Are they averaged, and if so, is a confidence interval or standard error provided?
- Figures 3–14 appear to have duplicate or unclear captions. Can you clarify which figures show spectra vs. ratios, and provide a legend identifying each experiment?
- Will code and trained models be made available upon publication? If so, under what license and with what accession number or repository URL?
