# Clarity & Presentation Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript is generally well-structured and clearly written for a specialist audience in machine-learned weather forecasting. The mathematical notation is introduced systematically, figures are mostly well-labeled, and the experimental setup is described with sufficient detail. However, there are several soft presentation issues—some redundancy between sections, inconsistent terminology in places, and a few figures that lack clear narrative ordering—that create minor friction. The central contribution (comparing scoring rules and their spectral effects) is stated explicitly, though the framing could be sharper about what constitutes a novel finding versus incremental exploration. No hard clarity failures block understanding, but the presentation would benefit from tightening and consistency improvements.

## Strengths
- Mathematical notation is introduced systematically and consistently applied throughout Section 2; readers can follow the score definitions without ambiguity.
- Experimental setup in Section 3 is sufficiently detailed: architecture, training schedule, hyperparameters (learning rates, rollout lengths, grid resolution), and data sources are all specified, enabling reconstruction of the experiments.
- Figure captions are comprehensive and include axis labels with units; for example, Figures 3–14 clearly indicate the variable, level, and metric (accumulated tendency spectra or ratios).
- The abstract states the central claim explicitly: 'multivariate scores are a viable alternative to CRPS-based training' and 'scale-aware losses improve realism', making the contribution intelligible standalone.
- Section 2 is well-organized: each score type is introduced with motivation, mathematical definition, and a brief statement of its properties (e.g., 'The energy score is strictly proper' vs. 'The graph energy score may fail to be strictly proper').
- The two-part structure (forecast skill comparison, then spectral analysis) is clearly signposted and each part's purpose is stated upfront.

## Weaknesses
- Inconsistent terminology: 'scale-aware' and 'scale-aware loss' are used interchangeably with 'multi-scale loss', but these are not identical concepts (multi-scale is one mechanism for achieving scale-awareness; spectral losses are another). The distinction is blurred in places, e.g., Section 4.2 states 'Making the forecast scores scale-aware substantially improves small-scale variability' without clarifying whether this refers only to multi-scale or to all scale-aware approaches tested.
- Soft redundancy between Sections 2 and 3.2: Table 2 re-describes loss objectives that were already defined in Section 2 (e.g., 'Multi-scale CRPS' is listed without explaining what 'multi-scale' means in this context, forcing readers to backtrack to Section 2). A forward reference in Section 2 would reduce friction.
- Figure ordering and narrative: Figures 3–14 are presented as pairs (e.g., Figures 3 and 4 both show geopotential spectra, Figures 5 and 6 show ratios) without clear explanation of why the same variable is shown twice or what distinguishes the two panels. The caption does not explain the difference between Figures 3 and 4, or between 5 and 6. This creates ambiguity about whether they represent different lead times, different subsets of experiments, or different plotting conventions.
- Vague quantifiers in results: Section 4.1 states 'In the tropics, however, the graph energy score experiment appears to perform best, while the energy score experiment shows some degradation relative to the other two experiments.' The word 'some' is not quantified. Figure 2 shows the differences visually, but the text should state the magnitude (e.g., 'CRPS difference of X%' or 'statistically significant at Y confidence level').
- The term 'accumulated tendency' is used without definition in Section 4.2. While meteorologists may recognize it, the manuscript should state explicitly: 'accumulated tendency = forecast field at lead time t minus initial state' (or reference to ERA5 analyses in the same sentence is insufficient for clarity).
- Soft: Section 5 (Discussion and Conclusion) repeats findings from Section 4 without adding new interpretation. For example, 'scale-aware losses improve spectral fidelity' is stated in Section 4.2 and restated in Section 5 without elaboration on why or implications.
- Soft: The phrase 'ad hoc and were chosen only to be of the right order of magnitude' (Section 3.2, describing weighting factors) is candid but vague. What does 'right order of magnitude' mean quantitatively? Were these weights 0.1, 1, 10? This affects reproducibility.
- Soft: The paper does not explicitly state which experiments are 'cheaper' (Section 3.2) or how much cheaper. Readers cannot assess whether the reduced model capacity and training schedule materially affect the conclusions about spectral behavior.

## Questions
- In Section 4.2, Figures 3 and 4 both show 'Accumulated tendency spectra for geopotential at 500 hPa'—what is the difference between these two figures? Are they different lead times, different subsets of the 12 experiments, or different plotting conventions? The caption should clarify.
- The manuscript states that weighting factors for multi-scale experiments were 'chosen only to be of the right order of magnitude—estimated from the data'. Can you provide the actual numerical values of these weights for each scale and variable? This is essential for reproducibility.
- In Section 4.1, you state the graph energy score 'performs best' in the tropics and the energy score 'shows some degradation'. Can you quantify these differences (e.g., CRPS improvement in %, or statistical significance)?
- Section 3.2 mentions a 'smaller model' and 'shorter training schedule' to reduce computational cost. How do these reductions affect the conclusions about spectral behavior? Would the same ranking of loss objectives hold with the full model and training schedule from Section 3.1?
- The graph energy score is described as potentially not strictly proper (Section 2), yet it is combined with a 'weak global anchor' to recover propriety. How sensitive are the results (Section 4) to the strength of this anchor (currently 0.1 × fES)? Was this weight tuned or chosen ad hoc?
- In Section 2, you define the graph energy score using a k-nearest-neighbour graph with k=16. Was this value chosen based on prior work, tuned on validation data, or fixed a priori? How sensitive are results to this choice?
- The abstract claims multivariate scores are 'a viable alternative to CRPS-based training', but Section 4.1 shows 'broadly similar' skill across all three training objectives. Does 'viable' mean 'equally good', or does it allow for small differences? This framing could be sharpened.
- In Section 4.2, you note that 'differences between scale-aware loss objectives are comparatively small'. Can you quantify 'small' (e.g., as a percentage of variance explained, or spectral slope difference)?
