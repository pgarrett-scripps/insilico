# Journal Recommendations

## As-is (current quality)
- **arXiv (cs.LG / physics.ao-ph)**
  - Fit: The manuscript presents a technical exposition of scoring-rule taxonomy and preliminary empirical comparisons. arXiv is the natural preprint home for machine-learning weather work and imposes no quantification or reproducibility gatekeeping; the authors can deposit immediately and solicit feedback without revising.
  - Realism: Guaranteed if submitted; arXiv is a preprint server with immediate deposition. However, being preprint-only rather than peer-reviewed, it carries no formal vetting. The In Silico major verdict and the quantification/reproducibility gaps mean a reader should expect the empirical claims to need independent verification.

## After required revisions
- **Quarterly Journal of the Royal Meteorological Society (QJRMS)**
  - Fit: QJRMS has published recent work on probabilistic machine-learned forecasting (including Lang et al. 2024, cited here) and values both methodological and empirical contributions. Once the anchor confound is resolved, multi-seed confidence intervals or bootstrap CIs are reported on Figure 2, and spectral fidelity is quantified with a defined scalar metric, the spectral-realism finding becomes publishable as honest preliminary work. The graph-energy taxonomy and Anemoi integration are contributions the community in that journal would cite.
  - Realism: Good prospects after revision if the quantification and reproducibility revisions are completed. The major-revision panel verdict and the clear editorial roadmap suggest acceptance is achievable once the confound is resolved and claims are properly bounded. QJRMS typically accepts well-executed preliminary studies when methods are transparent.
- **Weather and Forecasting (American Meteorological Society)**
  - Fit: A natural outlet for probabilistic forecast methodology. The paper's scope—comparing training objectives for machine-learned ensemble forecasts—fits squarely within the journal's remit. After multi-seed runs or bootstrap CIs replace single-run claims, and code/configs are deposited, this reads as a reproducible preliminary comparative study suitable for a specialist audience.
  - Realism: Moderate-to-good prospects after revision. The acceptance bar is comparable to QJRMS but slightly less demanding of novelty. Emphasis on reproducibility and honest framing of limitations (which the authors have done) typically argues for acceptance in this journal at major-revision stage.
- **Journal of Machine Learning Research (JMLR)**
  - Fit: If the authors reframe the work as a methodological paper on localized and spectral proper-score objectives for generative modelling (rather than primarily a weather-forecasting application), the scoring-rule taxonomy and graph-native generalization of Pacchiardi et al.'s patch-based localization become the lead contribution. JMLR publishes score-based learning methods and would value the clarity of the formalism.
  - Realism: Moderate prospects, but requires explicit reframing away from weather as the primary audience. JMLR reviewers may view the weather-specific scope and small-scale (O96) experiments as limiting impact. Acceptance would depend on positioning the scoring-rule contribution as standing alone, with weather as one application domain, rather than as the main story.

## Alternative outlets
- **ESSOAr (Earth and Space Science Open Archive)**
  - Fit: This community preprint server is standard in geosciences and weather research. Authors can deposit with a DOI and license; it attracts readership in the field without requiring formal peer review. Ideal for making code and data discoverable while work is under revision at a journal or being prepared for resubmission.
  - Realism: Automatic deposition (not reviewed). Appropriate fallback if QJRMS turnaround is long or if authors prefer to circulate a version with full code/data first before pursuing traditional review.
- **NeurIPS/ICML Climate or Generative Modelling Workshop**
  - Fit: Workshops on machine learning for climate and weather, or on generative models and proper scoring, frequently appear at these conferences. The graph-energy and spectral-score contributions fit a methods-focused audience that does not require the same level of empirical validation as a main-conference paper. Ideal for presenting the taxonomy and inviting feedback before full journal submission.
  - Realism: Good prospects for acceptance (lower bar than main conference) and high visibility in the ML-for-science community. However, workshops do not count as peer-reviewed publication and carry shorter deadlines; suitable as a parallel outlet rather than a primary venue.
- **Zenodo (with explicit software/data deposit and documentation)**
  - Fit: If the authors deposit the scoring-rule implementations, training configs, seeds, and hyperparameter specifications as a documented software/data bundle with a permanent DOI, they create a citable, reproducible artifact that addresses the primary critique (reproducibility gap). This is not a journal venue but serves as a fallback proof of reproducibility while journal revision is underway.
  - Realism: Automatic deposition (not reviewed). Not a substitute for peer review but significantly raises the paper's credibility if journal review is delayed or rejected. Many ML-for-science groups cite Zenodo deposits as supplementary evidence of reproducibility.

## Notes
The panel's verdict (3.30/5, 5 major) reflects real substantive gaps: single-run comparisons with no error bars, an asymmetric anchor confound in Section 3.1, and unspecified reproduction parameters. These are not house-style issues; they affect trust in the claims. QJRMS is the natural primary target after revision because (1) it has published related work, (2) the spectral-realism finding is directionally robust across twelve configurations and publishable as preliminary if quantified, and (3) the authors have already mapped most of the required revisions. The alternative outlets serve different purposes: arXiv is immediate visibility; workshops provide peer feedback before formal submission; ESSOAr and Zenodo provide reproducibility assurance and are standard in the geoscience community. Avoid Nature Machine Intelligence or top-tier ML conferences (NeurIPS, ICML main track) even after revision—the scope is too narrow (one weather model, O96 resolution, specific scoring-rule novelty) and the empirical story, while honest, may not move those audiences. The real path forward is quantification + deposit + honest scope-limiting in the abstract ('preliminary single-run comparison at O96', not 'viable alternative to CRPS-based training'), then QJRMS.
