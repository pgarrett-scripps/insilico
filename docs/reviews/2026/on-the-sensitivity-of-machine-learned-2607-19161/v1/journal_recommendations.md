# Journal Recommendations

## As-is (current quality)
- **arXiv (physics.ao-ph or cs.LG)**
  - Fit: This is preliminary but sound work comparing scoring rules for machine-learned weather forecasting. As an arXiv preprint, it reaches the atmospheric science and ML communities immediately and benefits from the authors' candid statement of limitations (single-run experiments, confounded Section 3.2). The methodology audit and required disclosure improvements would be straightforward to incorporate before or after posting.
  - Realism: arXiv posting requires no review; this manuscript is suitable for immediate posting. It does not meet the final form required by peer-reviewed venues until the required revisions are addressed.
- **In Silico (target venue)**
  - Fit: The paper is well-suited to In Silico's charter: original empirical research on machine learning for a physical domain, with public data (ERA5), sound Section 3.1 methodology, honest reporting of confounds and limitations (especially the authors' own concession that weighting and scale-awareness are not separable in Section 3.2), and clear scope for public review. The authors' acknowledgement of single-run design and visual-only spectral comparison aligns with In Silico's criterion that readers can evaluate claims from the manuscript itself.
  - Realism: The editor's verdict is 'minor', not 'reject', and the editor explicitly states that rescoping rather than new experiments is the remedy. At In Silico, the public review process and overlay format tolerate methodological constraints (single-run, no seeds) provided they are plainly stated and do not support claims beyond the evidence. The manuscript's candour on these points is a fit for In Silico's transparency model.

## After required revisions
- **Quarterly Journal of the Royal Meteorological Society**
  - Fit: This is the natural home for methodological work on probabilistic weather forecasting. The Section 3.1 controlled comparison of scoring rules is exactly the kind of work QJRMS publishes. After rescoping Section 3.2 to acknowledge the confound and adding the required numerical spectral-fidelity metric, the spectral analysis becomes a secondary but solid contribution on scale-aware loss design. The graph-based score localizations are a genuine incremental advance for the QJRMS audience.
  - Realism: With the required revisions (rescoped abstract, numerical spectral metrics, explicit seed/run reporting), this paper is at the level QJRMS accepts. Section 3.1 alone would easily pass; Section 3.2 with proper caveating becomes acceptable. The panel's convergence on 'minor' (6 of 8) and the editor's acceptance of the authors' rescoping proposal suggest realistic odds of acceptance at a top-tier meteorological journal after revision.
- **Journal of Advances in Modeling Earth Systems (JAMES)**
  - Fit: JAMES publishes machine-learning advances in Earth system modeling with both methodological and empirical emphasis. This paper's combination of new scoring-rule implementations (graph energy, graph variogram, graph edge scores) and their evaluation on a state-of-art ensemble forecast model (AIFS) fits JAMES's scope. The authors' use of the public Anemoi framework strengthens reproducibility for that audience.
  - Realism: JAMES is slightly broader than QJRMS and welcomes methodological increments in ML for weather and climate. The required revisions strengthen rather than undermine the work's fit: explicit hyperparameters and seeds are expected at JAMES. Realistic odds are good, particularly if the authors emphasize the Section 3.1 controlled comparison and the practical utility of graph-based scores for irregular grids (mentioned but not empirically validated).
- **NeurIPS/ICML Workshop on Machine Learning and the Physical Sciences (or equivalent)**
  - Fit: The paper combines proper scoring rules, graph neural networks, and spectral analysis—topics of genuine interest to ML researchers working on weather and climate. The controlled Section 3.1 comparison and the novel graph-based localizations fit the workshop's remit. The authors' honest discussion of confounds in Section 3.2 is a strength in an exploratory workshop context.
  - Realism: Workshop acceptance is realistic and fast-track. A workshop paper strengthens the authors' position for a subsequent journal submission and allows public discussion of the Section 3.2 confounds without demanding the full ablation campaign a journal might otherwise require.

## Alternative outlets
- **Weather and Forecasting**
  - Fit: A slightly narrower meteorological journal than QJRMS, focused on forecast verification and ensemble methods. The Section 3.1 comparison of ensemble training objectives and the spectral analysis of forecast realism fit this journal's core remit.
  - Realism: Realistic at this narrower specialty journal after revisions. The panel's verdict and the controlled Section 3.1 design should support acceptance, though the Section 3.2 confound may receive closer scrutiny than at a methods-focused venue. Good fallback if QJRMS rejects.
- **Environmental Data Science or Computational Geosciences (open-access, if reviewed)**
  - Fit: These open-access venues accept computational and methodological studies in Earth science with flexible scope. The paper's reproducibility focus (public data, open-source framework) and its incremental but sound contribution fit their model.
  - Realism: Lower selectivity than top-tier journals, but strong fit for open-access, methods-first publishing. Realistic if the paper is rejected at QJRMS or JAMES, though the authors would lose prestige compared to those outlets.
- **arXiv with journal-formatted revision (cs.LG, physics.ao-ph)**
  - Fit: If the manuscript remains unplaced at peer-reviewed venues after revision, or if the authors prioritize rapid dissemination to the ML+climate community, a revised arXiv version with the required disclosure improvements (Anemoi commit hash, full hyperparameters, seeds, spectral metrics) becomes a defensible research artifact. Version 2 with improved Section 4 and revised abstract would be suitable.
  - Realism: Not a journal acceptance, but a stronger preprint that addresses reviewer feedback transparently. Many groups use this route for methods papers in fast-moving domains like ML for weather.

## Notes
The manuscript's core Section 3.1 contribution (fair comparison of CRPS vs. multivariate scores under matched conditions) is journal-ready; the panel verdict 'minor' reflects only the Section 3.2 overclaim (abstract attributes improved spectra to 'scale-awareness' while the design confounds weighting, model size, and scoring rule). The editor's decision letter makes clear that rescoping the abstract to acknowledge this confound—not new experiments—resolves the issue. After addressing the 13 required revisions (mostly disclosure: seeds, hyperparameters, Anemoi commit hash, spectral-fidelity metric, competing interests), the paper fits naturally at specialized weather/climate or ML+science venues. Given the authors' ECMWF affiliation and the Anemoi framework focus, QJRMS or JAMES are the most natural targets; In Silico itself (the stated target) is well-suited to the paper's transparency but may not maximize visibility in the meteorological community.
