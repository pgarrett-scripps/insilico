# Decision Letter

**Decision:** minor

## Summary of Evaluation
Eight referees converged on a positive but conditional assessment (confidence-weighted 4.03/5; 1 accept, 6 minor, 1 major). The panel agrees that the Section 3.1 comparison — almost-fair CRPS vs. fair global energy score vs. fair graph energy score under matched architecture, data and training — is a well-controlled experiment that fairly isolates the scoring rule, and that the graph-based localizations (graph energy, graph variogram, graph edge scores) are a genuine if incremental methodological contribution. Ethics is clean (public ERA5, no human/animal/clinical content). Citation integrity is clean: zero HARD gaps across 24 resolvable references, with seven items merely unverifiable from abstracts.

The one load-bearing critique is that Section 3.2 simultaneously varies scoring rule, model size, training schedule and admittedly ad hoc waveband/scale weights, so the abstract's causal attribution of improved spectral realism to scale-awareness per se outruns the evidence; this is compounded by single-run experiments, no seeds, no error bars over the 84 initialization dates, and purely visual spectral comparison. The authors concede this squarely and propose exactly the remedy the meta-review identified as decisive: rescoping the abstract, title and Discussion so that the claim reads as 'weighting and scale-decomposition jointly, not separably, improve spectral realism in these single-run, reduced-capacity experiments', plus a quantitative spectral-fidelity metric with spread across initialization dates. Because the authors accept the rescoping rather than defending the strong claim, the condition that would have moved this to 'major' does not trigger.

I weighed two author disagreements. The first — that the qualitative pattern across nine independently configured scale-aware losses is itself evidence, even without seed replication — is partially credible: consistency across mechanisms is suggestive, and combined with the conceded rescoping it does not require a new ablation campaign for publication. It does not, however, license unqualified causal language, so the caveat requirement stands. The second — that the irregular-grid applicability of graph scores is already hedged in Section 2 as a structural property ('requires only a neighbourhood graph and associated weights... in principle') — is supported by the quoted text; I accept it and require only that Section 4/the Discussion not restate it as an empirical finding, rather than requiring removal.

The reproducibility review (the lone 3) is largely persuasive and is corroborated by the methods audit's three HARD gaps (no seeds, no train/val split, no version/hardware detail). Every one of these is a disclosure fix, and the authors have conceded all of them with concrete proposed text. That is why the verdict tracks the panel majority rather than the dissent: nothing here requires new computation, and the central Section 3.1 claim is unaffected.

## Required Revisions
1. Rescope the abstract, title and Discussion to match the evidence: state that forecast skill is broadly insensitive to the three scoring rules tested (noting the tropical degradation of the global energy score explicitly in the abstract), and that scale-aware objectives with appropriate scale/variable weighting yield more realistic spectra in these single-run, reduced-capacity experiments — without attributing the improvement to scale-awareness independent of weighting.
2. Add explicit caveat language in Section 4.2 and the Discussion stating that scoring rule, model capacity (256 vs 512 embedding), training schedule and weighting are confounded across the twelve Section 3.2 runs, and that the design cannot separate their contributions.
3. Add a table reporting a scalar spectral-fidelity metric (e.g. integrated log-spectral error relative to ERA5) per experiment and lead time, with the spread across the 84 initialization dates as an uncertainty measure, so Section 4.2 no longer rests on visual inspection alone.
4. Report all numerical hyperparameters currently given only qualitatively: scale weights zeta_i, per-waveband weights, Gaussian smoothing kernel widths (exact, not 'approximately'), the anchor coefficient for every Table 2 row, and the relative weights of all combined loss terms (e.g. multi-scale CRPS + edge CRPS, graph energy + 0.1 fES). An appendix table is acceptable.
5. State the random seed(s) used per experiment and whether any configuration was run more than once; if not, add an explicit statement that run-to-run variance is uncharacterised, with at least a qualitative expectation of its magnitude.
6. Add a code- and data-availability statement giving the exact Anemoi commit hash or release tag, the training configuration files (deposited with a DOI where possible), model checkpoints where storage permits, and confirmation of whether the scoring-rule implementations are merged into the public repository.
7. Specify the exact ERA5 train/validation split (dates or fraction) within 1979-2020 and confirm whether 2022 inference constitutes a fully held-out test set.
8. Name the spherical-harmonic transform library and version used for the T191 transform, and state the PyTorch version and the GPU configuration used for training.
9. Quantify the Section 3.1 result: replace 'broadly similar' and 'somewhat better' with numbers, state n and the independence structure of the verification sample, and report the tropical energy-score degradation numerically.
10. Rewrite the paired figure captions (3/4, 5/6, 7/8, etc.) to state what differs between them, and add a legend or table mapping each panel and line to its Table 2 loss configuration.
11. Add a competing-interests statement noting the authors' ECMWF affiliation and role in developing Anemoi.
12. Add a sentence in the Introduction clarifying the relationship to the companion multi-scale loss paper [14] (which introduces the formalism used here) and what the present study adds; verify the Pacchiardi et al. [19] 'patched energy scores performed best' attribution against a specific section or figure.
13. Regarding the propriety of the combined fGES_graph + 0.1 fES objective: either cite a specific result establishing strict propriety of the combination or state plainly in Section 2 that this is asserted rather than proven here.

## Minor Suggestions
- Retain the Section 2 hedge on graph scores for irregular grids and sparse observation networks — the quoted 'in principle' framing is adequate — but ensure Section 4 and the Discussion do not restate it as a demonstrated result.
- Specify the interpolation method used to regrid fields to 1.5 deg for verification, and the spectral averaging procedure used for the tendency spectra.
- Give the k-NN graph construction details (distance metric, tie-breaking) and any ERA5 preprocessing (regridding, normalisation) even briefly, or cite a resolvable source for them.
- Report an approximate compute budget (GPU hours or wall-clock per run); this helps readers judge the feasibility of the seed-replication study the paper does not attempt.
- Consider adding an environment file or requirements listing to the deposited configuration bundle.
- Confirm the architectural details for the Section 3.1 experiments (processor layer count, embedding dimension, activations) rather than delegating entirely to AIFS-CRPS [13], and note any deviations.
- Confirm the FGN conditional-layer-norm noise-injection description attributed to Alet et al. [1], and the training schedule attributed to AIFS-CRPS [13] (150,000 / 30,000 iterations); the citation audit could not verify either from abstracts alone.
