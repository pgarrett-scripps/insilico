# Decision Letter

**Decision:** major

## Summary of Evaluation
The panel's confidence-weighted score (3.30/5) and verdict distribution (5 of 8 major) both point to major revision, and I concur. The manuscript's formal contribution — a clear, citable taxonomy of localized and spectral multivariate scoring rules (graph energy, graph edge energy, graph variogram, spectral variants) integrated natively into a GNN forecasting pipeline — is a genuine generalization of patch-based localization and is well positioned against the literature. The citation audit found zero hard integrity gaps; the ethics review found no compliance problems beyond missing COI and availability statements. What does not currently survive scrutiny is the empirical layer: every headline comparative claim rests on visual inspection of single training runs with no error bars, effect sizes, seeds or defined metric for spectral 'improvement', and the Section 3.1 tropical result is confounded by a 0.1 fES anchor present in the graph-energy configuration but absent from the global-energy configuration.

I weighed the rebuttal carefully and it materially shaped this decision. The authors concede the anchor confound outright and propose a specific control run plus abstract-level retraction of the attribution; they concede the absence of CIs and commit to multi-seed runs and bootstrap intervals over the 84 initialization dates; they concede the reproducibility deficit and propose a concrete deposit (Anemoi commit hash, configs, seeds, library versions); and they credit Lang et al. (2025) for the multi-scale decomposition. These are the right responses to the three load-bearing critiques and they are the reason I do not follow the single reject vote. I also find the authors' disagreement with the 'fatal' framing credible on one point: the spectral-realism direction is consistent across twelve independently varied configurations, and that directional consistency is evidence even absent formal testing — though it remains weaker evidence than the current wording ('substantially improves') implies. Their defence of the graph-score contribution as structural rather than performance-based is likewise reasonable, provided the manuscript says so explicitly rather than leaving readers to infer superiority. Their defence of the Pacchiardi paraphrase is plausible but should be pinned to a specific table on revision.

The distance to acceptance is real but bounded and the authors have already mapped most of it themselves. Reject would require the central claim to fail entirely; here the formalism and the directional spectral finding survive, while the tropical attribution and the abstract's 'viable alternative to CRPS-based training' framing do not, and both are fixable by rerun or by rewording. Hence: major revision.

## Required Revisions
1. Resolve the anchor confound in Section 3.1. Either (a) rerun the comparison with symmetric anchor treatment (e.g. add an fES + 0.1 fES configuration, or report fGESgraph without the anchor), or (b) if no rerun is feasible, state explicitly in the abstract, Section 3.1 and Section 4.1 that the graph-energy configuration carries a 0.1 fES anchor the global-energy configuration lacks, and that the tropical difference therefore cannot be attributed to graph localization. Option (b) must remove any causal attribution language from the abstract.
2. Quantify the forecast-skill claims. Report multi-seed runs (>=3 seeds) for the three Section 3.1 configurations where compute allows; where it does not, report bootstrap confidence intervals over the 84 initialization dates with explicit sample sizes on all Figure 2 panels. Replace 'broadly similar', 'somewhat better' and 'some degradation' with mean differences and intervals.
3. Define a scalar metric for spectral fidelity against ERA5 (e.g. integrated log-ratio error over specified wavenumber bands) and tabulate it for all twelve configurations, so that 'substantially improves small-scale variability' is a measured quantity rather than a visual impression. Scale the wording in the abstract and Section 4.2 to whatever the metric shows.
4. Add a data and code availability statement (HARD gap, methods audit): deposit the custom scoring-rule implementations and training configs in a public repository, give an Anemoi commit hash or tagged release, and state whether trained checkpoints are available. Add a competing-interests statement.
5. Close the reproducibility HARD gaps identified by the methods audit: (a) state the exact train/validation/test partition of the 1979-2020 ERA5 period and confirm explicitly that 2022 is held out; (b) report random seeds and stochastic-element handling; (c) give PyTorch, Triton and spherical-harmonic-library versions and the ERA5 access/version details; (d) give the numerical values of the ad hoc multi-scale and spectral weighting factors and the exact Gaussian kernel specifications (standard deviations, boundary handling) currently described only as 'approximately 100/200/400/800 km'.
6. Add the promised sensitivity check on hyperparameters: a +/-50% perturbation of the scale/variable weighting factors and of the 0.1 anchor weight for at least one representative multi-scale experiment, reporting the resulting change in the spectral-fidelity metric. Without this the title's promise of a 'sensitivity analysis' is not met.
7. Rewrite the captions of Figures 3-14 so each specifies whether the panel shows absolute spectra or forecast/ERA5 ratios, the lead time(s) shown, and which of the twelve configurations each line corresponds to, with names consistent with Table 2 and a shared legend.
8. State the Section 3.1 architecture explicitly (embedding dimension, processor layers, encoder/decoder connectivity as actually run) and confirm whether it is comparable to the Section 3.2 configuration; state any deviations from Lang et al. (2024) beyond the O96 resolution restriction.
9. Tighten the novelty boundary: add the promised sentence crediting Lang et al. (2025) for the K-scale decomposition at the start of the Multi-Scale Score section and in Section 3.2, and state explicitly which spectral scores are introduced here for the first time. In the graph-score discussion, state plainly that no head-to-head patch-vs-graph comparison was run and that the contribution is structural (grid-agnostic, graph-native) rather than a demonstrated performance gain.
10. Revise the abstract: correct the grammatical errors, clarify the relationship between AIFS-CRPS and the trained variants, and rescale the 'viable alternative to CRPS-based training' framing to what single-run O96 experiments can support (e.g. 'competitive in our preliminary single-run comparison at O96').

## Minor Suggestions
- Add the specific table or section reference in Pacchiardi et al. (2024) supporting the statement that patched energy scores 'performed best among multivariate scoring rules they considered', and make clear the comparison is among multivariate scores rather than against CRPS.
- Note whether any source establishes propriety of the combined fGESgraph + 0.1 fES objective, or state explicitly that propriety of the combination is assumed from the propriety of each term and is not separately proved here.
- State the ensemble size used in Section 3.2 and how ensemble members are initialized (perturbation scheme), which the methods audit flagged as unstated.
- Report hardware type and approximate compute hours per configuration; this is a soft gap but helps readers judge the feasibility of the requested multi-seed runs.
- State the forecast lead times evaluated in the Section 4.1 skill comparison, which are currently specified only for the spectral analysis.
- Name the spherical-harmonic transform library used for the T191 truncation.
