# Methods Completeness & Reagent Traceability Auditor

## Summary
This manuscript presents a computational study comparing machine-learned probabilistic weather forecast models trained with different scoring rules. Computational/ML is the primary category triggered. The work includes two experimental sets: (1) a forecast-skill comparison across three loss objectives, and (2) a spectral-analysis study of twelve loss configurations. Key identifiers are partially present: datasets (ERA5, 2022 inference), model architecture (encoder–processor–decoder, O96 resolution), training hyperparameters (learning rates, iteration counts, AdamW optimizer), and loss functions are documented. However, critical gaps exist: random seeds are not stated, code availability is not explicitly declared (only a GitHub link to Anemoi framework is mentioned without confirmation of reproducibility materials), exact train/val/test splits are not specified, and some implementation details (e.g., exact graph construction parameters, smoothing-kernel specifications) lack precision. The manuscript is presented as preliminary ('preliminary study') and acknowledges limitations (smaller models, low resolution, shortened training). Spectral analysis methodology is sound but some weighting factors are described as 'ad hoc' and 'not tuned,' limiting reproducibility of that component.

## HARD gaps — reproduction blockers
- **[Computational/ML] Dataset version and exact train/val/test split** — Training uses 'ERA5 reanalysis data from 1979 to 2020' and 'inference performed for 2022.' No explicit statement of how the 1979–2020 period is partitioned into training, validation, and test sets. Section 3.2 states 'Spectra computed from 2022 forecasts initialized every four days from 1 January to 1 December at 00 UTC, 84 initialization dates in total' but does not clarify whether 2022 is held-out test data or part of training.
- **[Computational/ML] Random seed(s)** — No random seed is stated anywhere in the manuscript. Section 3.1 describes training configuration in detail (learning rates, iteration counts, optimizer) but does not mention initialization seeds or reproducibility of stochastic elements.
- **[Computational/ML] Library and software versions** — PyTorch is mentioned (torch.compile, AdamW) and Triton kernels are used, but no version numbers are provided. ERA5 data source is cited [11] but no version or access method is specified. Spherical harmonic transform is mentioned but the library (e.g., s2dv, shtns) is not named.
- **[Computational/ML] Exact train/val/test split percentages or dates** — Training data span '1979 to 2020' (42 years) and inference is 'performed for 2022.' No statement of whether a validation set is held out from 1979–2020, or how the split is made. This is critical for reproducibility.
- **[Computational/ML] Reproducibility of weighting factors for multi-scale and spectral losses** — Section 3.2 states: 'For multi-scale experiments, we used different weighting for small scales of geopotential and mean sea-level pressure... These weighting factors ad hoc and chosen only to of right order of magnitude - estimated from data - rather than tuned.' No numerical values for these weights are provided, making exact reproduction impossible.

## Unverifiable (raise as questions)
- **[Computational/ML] Code availability statement** — Manuscript states 'All scores implemented in Anemoi framework' and provides a GitHub link (https://github.com/ecmwf/anemoi-core). However, no explicit statement confirms whether the specific experimental code, trained models, or reproducibility materials are deposited or available. The link points to the framework itself, not necessarily to the authors' experimental code or trained weights.
- **[Computational/ML] Smoothing operators and kernel specifications for multi-scale loss** — Section 3.2 states: 'All multi-scale experiments used four graph-based Gaussian smoothing operators on native O96 grid, with kernel widths of approximately 100, 200, 400, and 800 km.' The word 'approximately' and lack of exact implementation (e.g., standard deviation of Gaussian, boundary handling) makes this unverifiable. Authors acknowledge 'weighting factors ad hoc and chosen only to of right order of magnitude - estimated from data - rather than tuned,' which undermines reproducibility of spectral results.
- **[Computational/ML] Protocol provenance: AIFS-CRPS architecture and training** — Section 3.1 states 'We follow AIFS-CRPS [13] in architecture and general training configuration, but restrict resolution here to O96 ≈ 1 deg model.' Reference [13] is Lang et al. 2024, which is cited as the source. However, the manuscript does not state whether any deviations from [13] exist beyond resolution restriction. The phrase 'follow...but restrict' suggests a modification, but the full protocol in [13] cannot be verified from this manuscript alone.
- **[Computational/ML] Protocol provenance: Multi-scale loss formulation** — Section 2 and 3.2 reference 'multi-scale loss formulation of [14]' and state 'Following [14], we use same idea as scale decomposition for scoring.' Reference [14] is Lang, Leutbecher, and Maciel 2025. The exact implementation and any deviations are not detailed in this manuscript; readers must consult [14].
- **[Computational/ML] Protocol provenance: Spectral transform and analysis** — Section 2 mentions 'spectral transform T' and 'spherical harmonics' but does not cite a specific library or method. The analysis follows standard meteorological practice but is not tied to a specific, citable protocol or software version.

## Documented (for the record)
- **[Computational/ML] Hyperparameters and training procedure** — Section 3.1 specifies: 'training schedule uses 150,000 iterations at rollout 1, 30,000 iterations at rollout 2, and then 1,000 iterations at each rollout step from 3 to 12. For rollout 1 and rollout 2, we use cosine learning-rate schedule with warmup of 1,000 steps followed by decay to zero. learning rate 10^−3 for rollout 1 and 10^−5 for rollout 2. For rollout steps 3 to 12, we use fixed learning rate of 10^−6. In all cases, optimization uses AdamW with weight decay 0.1.' Section 3.2 provides embedding dimension (256), processor layers (12), and similar schedule details.
- **[Computational/ML] Architecture and algorithm specification** — Section 3.1 states: 'AIFS-CRPS has encoder–processor–decoder architecture. In our experiments, encoder maps from data grid to processor grid using graph neural network, with each data-grid node connected to its four nearest processor-grid nodes. decoder maps from processor grid back to data grid, with each data-grid node connected to its eight nearest processor-grid nodes. graph connectivity for graph-based scores defined by k-nearest-neighbour graph with k=16 on O96 reduced Gaussian grid.' Reference [13] is cited for full AIFS-CRPS details.
- **[Computational/ML] Metric definitions and evaluation protocol** — Section 4.1 states: 'For scoring, we interpolate fields to resolution of 1.5 deg and evaluate forecast skill using fair CRPS.' Fair CRPS is defined in Section 2. Spectral analysis uses 'accumulated forecast tendencies' (differences between state at lead time and initial state) and 'spectra computed from 2022 forecasts.' However, exact verification metrics beyond CRPS (e.g., RMSE, anomaly correlation) are not detailed.
- **[Computational/ML] Spectral transform details and truncation** — Section 3.2 states: 'spectral losses used spherical harmonic transform with truncation T191. For weighted spectral experiments, spectral modes grouped according to their total wavenumber ℓ, using bands ℓ = 0–10, 11–20, 21–80, 81–120, and 121–191.' However, the library implementing the transform is not named.
- **[Computational/ML] Data availability statement** — ERA5 reanalysis is a public dataset [11]. However, no explicit statement confirms that the preprocessed training data, validation data, or inference outputs are deposited or available for download. The statement is implicit rather than explicit.
- **[Computational/ML] Ensemble size and initialization** — Section 3.1 states: 'we generate 8-member ensembles to compare forecast scores.' Section 3.2 does not explicitly state ensemble size but context suggests the same. However, how ensemble members are initialized (e.g., from different random perturbations, different model initializations) is not stated.
- **[Computational/ML] Ablation study or sensitivity analysis** — The entire paper is structured as a sensitivity study: Section 3.1 compares three loss objectives (CRPS, global energy score, graph energy score), and Section 3.2 compares twelve loss configurations. This is the core contribution.
- **[Computational/ML] Verification data and evaluation period** — Section 4.2 states: 'Spectra computed from 2022 forecasts initialized every four days from 1 January to 1 December at 00 UTC, 84 initialization dates in total. In addition, we compute spectra for ERA5 analyses as reference.' However, the forecast lead times evaluated are not uniformly stated; Section 3.2 mentions 'up to eight forecast steps, corresponds to 48 hours lead time' but Section 4.1 does not specify lead times for the skill comparison.
