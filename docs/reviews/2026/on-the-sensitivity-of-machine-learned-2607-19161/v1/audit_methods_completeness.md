# Methods Completeness & Reagent Traceability Auditor

## Summary
This is a computational weather forecasting study comparing machine-learned probabilistic models trained with different scoring rules. The manuscript triggers the Computational/ML category. The authors provide dataset sources (ERA5), training configuration details, model architecture, hyperparameters, and loss functions. However, several HARD items are missing or unverifiable: random seeds are not stated (critical for reproducibility of neural network training), code availability is not explicitly declared (only a framework URL is mentioned), and the exact train/val/test split is not specified. Additionally, the Anemoi framework version is not stated. The spectral transform details and some implementation specifics are delegated to cited works without full verification. The paper is presented as preliminary and acknowledges limitations, which is appropriate for the scope.

## HARD gaps — reproduction blockers
- **[Computational/ML/modeling] Dataset(s) with version and exact train/val/test split** — Section 3.1 states 'Training uses ERA5 reanalysis data [11] from 1979 to 2020, and inference is performed for 2022.' The training period is given (1979–2020), but the exact split between training and validation sets is not specified. No validation-set dates or fraction are stated. Inference is on 2022, but whether a held-out test set exists is unclear.
- **[Computational/ML/modeling] Library versions and hardware** — The manuscript mentions 'Anemoi framework (https://github.com/ecmwf/anemoi-core)' but does not specify a version number or release tag. PyTorch is referenced via torch.compile [2] but no PyTorch version is stated. Hardware is mentioned in Acknowledgements: 'Gauss Centre for Supercomputing...JUPITER at Jülich Supercomputing Centre' but no specific GPU/CPU configuration or memory is given.
- **[Computational/ML/modeling] Random seeds (or seed-averaging statement)** — No random seed is stated anywhere in the manuscript. No statement that results are averaged over multiple runs with different seeds is provided. This is critical for reproducibility of neural network training, which is inherently stochastic.

## Unverifiable (raise as questions)
- **[Computational/ML/modeling] Code availability** — The manuscript states 'All scores are implemented in the Anemoi framework' and provides a GitHub URL (https://github.com/ecmwf/anemoi-core), but does not explicitly state whether the specific experimental code, model weights, or training scripts are available. No data-availability or code-availability statement is present. The Anemoi framework is open-source, but whether the exact configurations used here are deposited or reproducible from the public repository is unverifiable from the manuscript alone.
- **[Protocol provenance] Spherical harmonic transform implementation and parameters** — Section 3.2 states 'The spectral losses used a spherical harmonic transform with truncation T191' but does not specify which library or implementation is used, nor does it cite a specific reference for the transform algorithm. This is a load-bearing technical detail for the spectral loss experiments. The manuscript does not delegate this to a resolvable citation.
- **[Protocol provenance] Graph construction (k-nearest-neighbour on O96 reduced Gaussian grid)** — Section 3.1 states 'The graph connectivity for the graph-based scores is defined by a k-nearest-neighbour graph with k=16 on the O96 reduced Gaussian grid.' The O96 grid is a standard meteorological grid, but the exact algorithm for k-NN construction (distance metric, tie-breaking, boundary handling) is not specified and not cited to a resolvable reference.
- **[Protocol provenance] Gaussian smoothing operators for multi-scale decomposition** — Section 3.2 states 'All multi-scale experiments used four graph-based Gaussian smoothing operators on the native O96 grid, with kernel widths of approximately 100, 200, 400, and 800 km.' The word 'approximately' indicates these are not exact; the exact kernel implementation (e.g., standard deviation in grid points, truncation radius) is not specified. The authors cite [14] for the multi-scale loss formulation but do not fully specify the smoothing operator details.
- **[Protocol provenance] Graph energy score definition and propriety claim** — Section 2 defines the graph energy score mathematically and states: 'The energy score is strictly proper. The graph energy score, however, may fail to be strictly proper because different distributions can lead to the same local score even though they differ in their long-range dependence.' The authors cite [19] and claim they 'follow [19]' by combining with a weak global anchor, but the proof or detailed justification that the combined score is strictly proper is not provided and is delegated to [19], which is unverifiable from the manuscript alone.
- **[Protocol provenance] Multi-scale loss formulation (Laplacian pyramid decomposition)** — Section 2 describes the multi-scale loss using ordered smoothing operators D_1, ..., D_{K-1} to partition fields into K residual scale bands. The authors state 'This approach is similar to a Laplacian-pyramid or Laplacian-cascade decomposition [6]' and cite [14] for the scale decomposition for scoring. However, the exact implementation of the smoothing operators (beyond 'spatial kernel smoothers with decreasing width') is not specified. The authors state they follow [14] but do not fully reproduce the method in the manuscript.
- **[Protocol provenance] torch.compile and Triton kernel generation** — Section 3.1 states 'To make score computations efficient, we rely on torch.compile [2] to generate fused Triton kernels [23].' The citation [2] is to PyTorch 2.0 paper. The exact version of torch.compile and Triton used is not specified, and the custom kernel code is not provided or cited.
- **[Protocol provenance] Model architecture details (encoder–processor–decoder)** — Section 3.1 describes the encoder–processor–decoder architecture and graph connectivity but does not specify: the number of processor layers for the main experiments (only given for section 3.2 as 12), the embedding dimension for section 3.1 experiments, activation functions, normalization layers, or other architectural details. The authors state they 'follow AIFS-CRPS [13]' but do not fully reproduce the architecture in the manuscript, making it unverifiable whether deviations exist.
- **[Protocol provenance] Weighting factors for multi-scale and spectral experiments** — Section 3.2 states: 'For multi-scale experiments, we used different weighting for the small scales of geopotential and mean sea-level pressure... These weighting factors are ad hoc and were chosen only to be of the right order of magnitude - estimated from the data - rather than tuned.' The exact numerical values of these weighting factors are not provided. The authors acknowledge this as ad hoc but do not give the actual weights used, making exact reproduction impossible.

## Documented (for the record)
- **[Computational/ML/modeling] Architecture and algorithm specification** — Section 3.1 describes the encoder–processor–decoder architecture: 'The encoder maps from the data grid to the processor grid using a graph neural network, with each data-grid node connected to its four nearest processor-grid nodes. The decoder maps from the processor grid back to the data grid, with each data-grid node connected to its eight nearest processor-grid nodes.' Graph connectivity for graph-based scores uses k-nearest-neighbour with k=16.
- **[Computational/ML/modeling] Hyperparameters** — Section 3.1 specifies: training schedule (150,000 iterations at rollout 1, 30,000 at rollout 2, 1,000 at each rollout 3–12); learning rates (10^−3 for rollout 1, 10^−5 for rollout 2, 10^−6 for rollouts 3–12); cosine schedule with 1,000-step warmup; AdamW optimizer with weight decay 0.1; ensemble size 8 members. Section 3.2 specifies embedding dimension 256, 12 processor layers, and spectral truncation T191.
- **[Computational/ML/modeling] Training procedure (optimizer, schedule, early-stopping)** — Section 3.1: 'optimization uses AdamW with weight decay 0.1' and 'cosine learning-rate schedule with a warmup of 1,000 steps followed by decay to zero.' Section 3.2 uses a two-phase training: one-step forecasting for up to 150,000 steps, then rollout training with 1,000 steps per rollout length up to 8 steps. No explicit early-stopping criterion is mentioned.
- **[Computational/ML/modeling] Metric definitions** — Section 2 defines all scoring rules mathematically (CRPS, fair CRPS, almost fair CRPS, energy score, fair energy score, graph energy score, graph variogram score, graph edge energy score, CRPS edge score, spectral energy score, spectral magnitude CRPS). Section 4.2 specifies 'accumulated forecast tendencies' as differences between state at lead time and initial state, and spectra are computed using spherical harmonic transform with truncation T191.
- **[Computational/ML/modeling] Ablations** — Section 3.2 compares twelve different loss configurations (Table 2), systematically varying single-scale vs. multi-scale, different scoring rules (CRPS, energy score, graph energy, variogram, edge scores), and spectral approaches with and without weighting. This constitutes a comprehensive ablation study of loss design choices.
- **[Protocol provenance] ERA5 reanalysis data access and preprocessing** — Section 3.1 cites 'ERA5 global reanalysis [11]' with full reference (Hersbach et al., 2020, QJRMS). The data source is resolvable. However, no preprocessing steps (regridding, normalization, missing-value handling) are described.
- **[Protocol provenance] Fair CRPS and almost fair CRPS definitions** — Section 2 provides full mathematical definitions of fair CRPS and almost fair CRPS, including the convex-combination parameter α. The authors cite [9, 16] for the fair variant and [13] for the almost fair variant, but the full formulas are self-contained in the manuscript.
- **[Protocol provenance] Energy score and fair energy score** — Section 2 provides complete mathematical definitions of the energy score and fair energy score, including the spatially weighted Euclidean norm. The authors cite [10, 9] but the formulas are fully specified in the manuscript.
- **[Protocol provenance] Variogram score and graph variogram score** — Section 2 provides full mathematical definitions of the graph variogram score, including the variogram exponent p and the fair variant. The authors cite [21] for the variogram score concept but provide self-contained formulas for the graph variant.
- **[Protocol provenance] Graph edge energy score definition** — Section 2 provides complete mathematical definition of the graph edge energy score, including edge-difference vectors and the fair variant applied to these vectors.
- **[Protocol provenance] Spectral energy score and spectral magnitude CRPS** — Section 2 provides mathematical definitions of both spectral energy score (treating complex coefficients as 2-D real vectors) and spectral magnitude CRPS (applying CRPS to modulus of coefficients). The formulas are self-contained.
- **[Protocol provenance] Verification metrics and interpolation** — Section 4.1 states 'For scoring, we interpolate the fields to a resolution of 1.5 deg and evaluate forecast skill using the fair CRPS.' The interpolation target resolution is specified, but the interpolation method is not stated.
- **[Protocol provenance] Spectral analysis of accumulated tendencies** — Section 4.2 specifies: 'Spectra are computed from 2022 forecasts initialized every four days from 1 January to 1 December at 00 UTC, 84 initialization dates in total.' The sampling strategy is clear. Spectral truncation T191 is stated. However, the exact spectral analysis method (e.g., Welch's method, FFT, averaging procedure) is not detailed.
