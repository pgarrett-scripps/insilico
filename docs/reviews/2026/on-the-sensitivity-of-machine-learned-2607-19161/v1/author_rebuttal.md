## Concessions
- **methodology** — The graph energy score experiment includes a weak global energy score anchor (0.1 fES) while the global energy score experiment does not, confounding localization with the anchor term.
  - Proposed change: Add a fourth Section 3.1 experiment using fES + 0.1 fES (i.e. the same anchor weight applied redundantly to the global score) or report fGESgraph alone without anchor, and revise the abstract/Section 4.1 to state explicitly that the tropical difference cannot yet be attributed to graph localization per se until this control is run.
- **data_analysis** — No error bars, confidence intervals, or significance tests support claims like 'broadly similar', 'somewhat better', or 'some degradation' in forecast skill.
  - Proposed change: Rerun the three Section 3.1 configurations with at least 3 random seeds each, report bootstrap confidence intervals over initialization dates for the CRPS panels in Figure 2, and replace qualitative language with quantified differences (mean CRPS difference with CI).
- **reproducibility** — No code repository, commit hash, seeds, software versions, or checkpoint availability statement is given.
  - Proposed change: Add a data/code availability statement with an Anemoi commit hash or tagged release, deposit the custom scoring-rule implementations and training configs in a public repository, and report random seeds and PyTorch/Triton/spherical-harmonic library versions used.
- **clarity** — Figures 3-14 have near-duplicate captions that do not distinguish spectra from ratio plots or identify which experiment corresponds to which line.
  - Proposed change: Rewrite each caption to specify (a) whether the panel shows absolute spectra or forecast/ERA5 ratios, (b) the lead time(s) shown, and (c) add a shared legend identifying all twelve experiments by name consistent with Table 2.
- **clarity** — The abstract contains grammatical errors and ambiguous phrasing about what AIFS-CRPS versus 'the global weather forecast model' refers to.
  - Proposed change: Rewrite the abstract as full grammatical sentences, clarifying that AIFS-CRPS is the baseline global forecast model architecture and that we train variants of it with different loss objectives.
- **novelty** — The multi-scale loss formulation used in Section 3.2 originates from Lang et al. (2025) and is not a novel contribution of this work.
  - Proposed change: Add an explicit sentence at the start of the Multi-Scale Score section and in Section 3.2 crediting Lang et al. (2025) as the source of the K-scale decomposition and stating that our contribution is applying it to new (graph/spectral) scoring rules, not the decomposition itself.
- **ethics** — No competing-interests or data/code availability statement is included.
  - Proposed change: Add a one-line 'Competing interests: none declared' statement and a data/code availability paragraph specifying ERA5 access terms and Anemoi licensing.
- **reproducibility** — Hyperparameter choices (scale weights, waveband boundaries, kernel widths) are described as ad hoc with no sensitivity analysis.
  - Proposed change: Add an appendix ablation perturbing the scale/variable weighting factors by \u00b150% for one representative multi-scale experiment and report the resulting change in spectral ratios, to bound how much conclusions depend on these choices.

## Disagreements
- **skeptic (via methodology/rigor)** — The confound plus zero replication is 'fatal' to all the paper's central conclusions this cycle.
  - Response: We agree the tropical attribution claim is currently unsupported and are conceding that point directly (see concessions), but the broader finding that scale-aware losses (multi-scale and spectral) improve small-scale spectral realism relative to single-scale CRPS/energy score is not a single confounded comparison 	wtwelve independent architectures/configurations spanning graph, edge, variogram, and spectral mechanisms, all pointing the same direction. That directional consistency across independent design choices is itself evidence, even without formal significance testing, and is not undermined by the anchor-term confound, which affects only the Section 3.1 tropical comparison.
  - Manuscript reference: Section 4.2: 'Making forecast scores scale-aware substantially improves small-scale variability and leads to more realistic forecast fields. This true both for multi-scale loss formalism and for spectral scores.'
- **novelty** — The graph energy score lacks novelty beyond substituting k-NN graphs for rectangular patches, and no empirical comparison to patch-based localization is provided.
  - Response: We agree no head-to-head patch-vs-graph experiment exists in this study, but the contribution is not claimed as raw performance superiority over patches; it is the demonstration that a fully graph-native formulation integrates into an end-to-end GNN training pipeline (sharing the same k-NN graph already used for encoder/decoder connectivity) without requiring a regular grid, which is a structural rather than purely empirical advance.
  - Manuscript reference: Section 2, Graph Energy Score: 'advantage of graph-based approach it does not rely on fixed rectangular patches on regular grid... same score can in principle applied on irregular grids, sparse spatial meshes, or sparse observation networks.'
- **literature** — The claim that patched energy scores 'performed best' in Pacchiardi et al. is stated without citing the specific result.
  - Response: This is an accurate paraphrase of their comparative findings among the multivariate scores they tested (energy score, variogram score, and patched variants), not a claim about performance against CRPS or absolute skill; we will add the specific table reference on revision, but the underlying characterization is not incorrect as reviewed.
  - Manuscript reference: Section 1: 'In weather forecasting experiments of [19], patched energy scores performed best among multivariate scoring rules they considered.'

## Load-bearing critiques
- Asymmetric anchor term (0.1 fES) present only in the graph energy score experiment confounds attribution of the tropical forecast-skill difference to graph localization versus the anchor itself, undermining the abstract's headline claim without a controlled rerun
- Headline forecast-skill and spectral-fidelity claims rest on single training runs per configuration with no seeds, replication, or confidence intervals, so 'broadly similar', 'somewhat better', and 'substantially improves' are currently unquantified assertions rather than demonstrated effects
- No code, model weights, commit hash, or seed disclosure is provided, making the reported results currently unverifiable by an independent group
