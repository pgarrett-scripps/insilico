## Concessions
- **reproducibility** — No commit hash/version tag for Anemoi, no checkpoints, no random seed reporting, unspecified spectral transform library, and Table 2 combined-objective weights not given
  - Proposed change: Add a code/data availability statement citing the exact Anemoi commit hash used for these experiments, deposit training configs and (where storage permits) model checkpoints on a public archive with a DOI, state the random seed(s) used per experiment, name the spherical-harmonic library and version used for the T191 transform, and add an explicit table listing the relative weights of all combined loss terms (e.g. multi-scale CRPS + edge CRPS, graph energy + 0.1 fES anchor across all Table 2 rows).
- **rigor** — Abstract/title overclaim: 'sensitivity' and 'viable alternative'/'improves realism' state findings more strongly than the largely null skill result and confounded spectral experiments support
  - Proposed change: Revise the abstract and title to state that forecast skill is broadly insensitive to the three scoring rules tested (with a noted tropical exception for the global energy score), and that scale-aware objectives with appropriate scale/variable weighting produce more realistic spectra in this single-run, reduced-capacity setting, without attributing the improvement to scale-awareness alone independent of weighting.
- **methodology** — Section 3.2 confounds scoring rule with model size, training schedule, and ad hoc weighting, so the claim that scale-awareness (not weighting) drives spectral improvement is not isolated
  - Proposed change: Add explicit caveat language in Section 4.2 and the Discussion stating that scoring rule, model capacity, schedule, and weighting are confounded in this experiment set, and reframe the conclusion as: 'weighting and scale-decomposition jointly, not separably, improve spectral realism in these experiments' rather than implying scale-awareness alone is sufficient.
- **data_analysis / rigor** — Spectral comparisons in Figs 3-14 rely on visual inspection with no quantitative summary metric or uncertainty bands
  - Proposed change: Add a table reporting a scalar spectral-fidelity metric (e.g. integrated log-spectral error relative to ERA5, averaged over the 84 initialization dates) per experiment and lead time, with the spread across initialization dates reported as an uncertainty measure.
- **clarity/reproducibility** — Figures 3 and 4 (and 5/6, 7/8, etc.) are not distinguished in caption, and panels are not mapped to Table 2 experiments
  - Proposed change: Rewrite figure captions to specify what differs between paired figures (e.g. different lead times or experiment subsets) and add a legend/table explicitly mapping each panel/line to the corresponding Table 2 loss configuration.
- **ethics** — No competing-interest declaration despite author affiliation with ECMWF and the Anemoi framework used throughout
  - Proposed change: Add an explicit competing-interests statement noting the authors' ECMWF affiliation and role in developing Anemoi, and confirm whether the scoring-rule implementations tested are already merged into the public Anemoi repository or will be released as part of this work.
- **literature** — Relationship between this work and the companion multi-scale loss paper [14] is unclear, and the Pacchiardi et al. [19] 'patched energy scores performed best' claim is unverified
  - Proposed change: Add a sentence in the Introduction clarifying that [14] introduces the multi-scale loss formalism used here and that the present study empirically extends and tests it against alternative scale-aware and multivariate objectives; verify and cite the specific section/figure of [19] supporting the patched-energy-score claim.
- **clarity** — Ad hoc weighting factors described only qualitatively ('right order of magnitude'), harming reproducibility
  - Proposed change: Report the actual numerical values of the scale weights ζ_i and per-waveband weights used in each multi-scale/spectral experiment in an appendix table.

## Disagreements
- **methodology / skeptic** — The Section 3.2 confound (scoring rule vs. model size vs. weighting) is 'near-fatal' and requires new ablation experiments with seed replication before the scale-awareness claim can be published
  - Response: We agree the design does not cleanly separate weighting from scale-awareness mechanism, and we are revising the claim's scope accordingly (see concession above). However, we disagree that this makes the finding unpublishable in its (rescoped) form: the paper never claims to have isolated the causal weight of each factor. Section 4.2 already states 'It is apparent that any form of explicit scale-awareness improves realism' as a qualitative pattern across nine independently-configured scale-aware losses (multi-scale CRPS, multi-scale edge CRPS, multi-scale global energy, multi-scale graph energy, multi-scale graph edge energy, multi-scale variogram, and four spectral variants) all showing the same qualitative shift relative to the two non-scale-aware baselines (single-scale CRPS, global energy score). The consistency of this pattern across nine distinct mechanisms sharing only the presence of an explicit scale/wavenumber decomposition is itself evidence, even without seed replication, and is not undermined by the acknowledged reduced model capacity used uniformly across all twelve runs.
  - Manuscript reference: Section 4.2: 'In these experiments, the single-scale CRPS and global energy score poorly constrain small-scale variability... Making the forecast scores scale-aware substantially improves small-scale variability and leads to more realistic forecast fields. This is true both for the multi-scale loss formalism and for spectral scores.'
- **rigor** — The claim that graph-based scores can be applied to irregular grids/sparse networks is untested and should be removed or heavily qualified
  - Response: We will qualify this as a theoretical/architectural property rather than an empirical finding, but we disagree it should be removed: the claim is explicitly and correctly hedged in the manuscript as a structural property of the graph formulation (it 'requires only a neighbourhood graph and associated weights'), not as a demonstrated empirical result, and is presented separately from the empirical results in Section 4.
  - Manuscript reference: Section 2, Graph Energy Score: 'An advantage of the graph-based approach is that it does not rely on fixed rectangular patches on a regular grid. Instead, it only requires a neighbourhood graph and associated weights, so the same score can in principle be applied on irregular grids, sparse spatial meshes, or sparse observation networks.'

## Load-bearing critiques
- The Section 3.2 confounding of scoring rule, model capacity, training schedule, and ad hoc weighting, combined with single-run experiments and no error bars, means the strong causal claim that 'scale-awareness' per se (independent of weighting) drives spectral realism cannot be sustained as currently stated and must be rescoped in the abstract/Discussion rather than defended as-is.
