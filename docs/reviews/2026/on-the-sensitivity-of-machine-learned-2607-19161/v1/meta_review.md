# Meta-Review

**Draft recommendation:** minor

## Synthesis
The panel converges on a fairly uniform assessment (7 of 8 reviewers at 4-5, one at 3), with the split falling not on whether the work is sound but on how much of its second claim the evidence carries. All reviewers agree that the Section 3.1 comparison — almost-fair CRPS vs. fair global energy score vs. fair graph energy score under matched architecture, data and training — is well-controlled and fairly isolates the scoring rule. The novelty reviewer credits the graph-based localizations (graph energy, graph variogram, graph edge scores) as a genuine, if incremental, methodological contribution for weather forecasting. Ethics is clean (public ERA5, no human/animal/clinical content), with only a missing competing-interest declaration given the authors' ECMWF/Anemoi relationship. Literature coverage is proportionate, with one unverified attribution to Pacchiardi et al. and an unclear delineation from the companion Lang & Leutbecher multi-scale paper.

The genuine tension is over Section 3.2. The skeptic's case, which survived contact with the advocate, is that the twelve scale-aware experiments simultaneously vary scoring rule, model size (256 vs 512 embedding), training schedule and admittedly ad hoc waveband/scale weights, and that the manuscript's own remark that 'after introducing waveband weighting factors, the spectra look very similar' undercuts the abstract's flat statement that scale-awareness improves realism. The methodology reviewer flags this as HARD; data_analysis and reproducibility independently flag single-run experiments, no seeds, no error bars over the 84 initialization dates, and purely visual spectral comparison with no summary metric. These are corroborated across three reviewers and are not merely presentational. The advocate's counter — that the authors are candid and that most defects are disclosure gaps — is only partly successful: candour about a confound reduces the sin of overclaiming but does not repair the inferential gap, and the abstract does state the scale-awareness result more strongly than Section 4.2 supports.

The reproducibility review (the lone 3) is the most consequential dissent and is largely persuasive on its own terms: no commit hash or version tag for Anemoi, no checkpoints, no seeds, unspecified spectral transform library, unspecified relative weights for combined objectives in Table 2, and figures that do not map panels to Table 2 configurations. Almost all of these are correctable by disclosure rather than new computation.

## Decisive Factors
I land at 'minor' rather than 'major', consistent with the confidence-weighted 4.03 and the 6/8 minor verdicts, but the recommendation is conditional in a specific way. The decisive consideration is that the paper's two claims separate cleanly. The Section 3.1 claim — that multivariate scores are a viable alternative to CRPS — rests on a controlled design all reviewers accept, and needs only quantification of 'broadly similar' and 'somewhat better' (including the tropical degradation of the global energy score, which belongs in the abstract) plus a statement of n and its independence structure. That is text and existing-run analysis, not new experiments. The Section 3.2 claim is confounded as the skeptic argues, but the remedy that the venue should demand is not a new ablation campaign: it is that the abstract and Discussion be rewritten so the claim matches the evidence — that scale-aware objectives with appropriate weighting produce more realistic spectra in these single-run, reduced-capacity experiments, with weighting and scoring rule not separable in this design and training-noise not characterised. Rescoping a claim to fit the evidence is exactly what In Silico's first criterion asks for, and it is achievable in revision. If the authors decline to soften the abstract's causal attribution to scale-awareness per se, the correct outcome moves to major.

Required for acceptance: (1) rescope the abstract/title claims — the rigor reviewer's point that 'sensitivity' oversells a largely null skill result is well taken; (2) report exact scale weights ζ_i, waveband weights, kernel widths, the anchor coefficient across all Table 2 rows, and the relative weights in combined objectives; (3) state seeds and whether any configuration was run more than once, and add at least a qualitative statement of expected run-to-run variance; (4) add a numerical spectral-fidelity summary (e.g. integrated log-spectral error vs. ERA5) so Section 4.2 does not rest on visual inspection alone; (5) Anemoi commit/tag and a data/code-availability statement covering checkpoints and configs; (6) competing-interest declaration; (7) clarify what distinguishes Figures 3 vs 4 and map panels to Table 2; (8) state the relationship to the companion multi-scale paper and verify the Pacchiardi attribution. No formal proof is demanded for the propriety of fGES_graph + 0.1 fES, but the manuscript should either cite a result or state plainly that strict propriety of the combination is asserted rather than proven.
