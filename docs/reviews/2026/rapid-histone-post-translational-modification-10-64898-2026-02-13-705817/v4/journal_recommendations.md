# Journal Recommendations

## As-is (current quality)
- **Journal of Proteome Research**
  - Fit: Specialty methods journal with strong methodological and systematic-comparison focus; the dual-protease workflow evaluation, quantified labeling efficiency across 10 conditions, proper statistical rigor (limma, BH correction, median CVs <5–7%), and clear reproducibility (GitHub + PRIDE PXD073683) align well with JPR's standards for analytical proteomics advances.
  - Realism: Very realistic at this quality. JPR regularly accepts 4.15/5 methodological papers with minor revisions; the workflow itself is solid and the primary claims stand independently of the contested 'dark epigenome' framing.
- **Journal of the American Society for Mass Spectrometry**
  - Fit: Analytical MS and proteomics methods are core scope; JASMS values systematic protease comparisons and technical validation of new labeling strategies. The TMT-versus-propionylation efficiency analysis and charge-compensation mechanistic discussion fit JASMS's analytical rigor expectation.
  - Realism: Realistic. JASMS would accept the workflow and efficiency metrics at a 4/5 verdict with documentation revisions; the charge-compensation mechanism and artifact rates are of direct interest to its audience.
- **Analytical Chemistry**
  - Fit: Broad analytical methods venue; bottom-up proteomics workflows, derivatization strategy optimization, and LC-MS/MS method development are in scope. The 3-hour sample prep acceleration and complementary enzyme digestion strategy represent analytical improvements the journal values.
  - Realism: Realistic but somewhat broader venue than specialty proteomics. The methods contribution would fit, though the histone-specific PTM discovery angle is secondary here.

## After required revisions
- **Molecular & Cellular Proteomics**
  - Fit: Top-tier specialty journal for proteomics method and discovery papers; after revisions reframing the acidic-acylation finding as enhanced detection rather than biological prevalence, and with documentation gaps closed, the RIPUP workflow's speed, reproducibility, and dual-protease complementarity become a strong fit. The integration with HiP-Frag for PTM search and quantitation of histone variant coverage aligns with MCP's interests.
  - Realism: Realistic after revisions. The editors and reviewers would likely view the reframed findings and tightened scope as appropriate for MCP; the workflow itself merits publication at this tier, contingent on the succinylation/glutarylation claim being properly hedged and the Methods documentation completed.
- **Nature Methods**
  - Fit: After revisions that clarify the scope of the TMT-versus-propionylation comparison, remove 'dark epigenome' overstatement, and strengthen the mechanistic explanation of charge compensation, the RIPUP workflow becomes a genuine toolbox advance: rapid (3 h), reproducible (median CVs <5–7%), orthogonally validated by dual-protease coverage, and quantitatively robust. The contrast with the Garcia protocol and the systematic 10-condition benchmark provide the methodological rigor Nature Methods expects.
  - Realism: Challenging but possible after revisions. Nature Methods is highly selective, but a 4.15/5 paper with a genuine speed/coverage/quantitation advance and no contested primary claims could reach this tier if clarity and scope are sharpened. The bar remains high, but the contribution is methodologically sound.
- **Proteomics**
  - Fit: High-tier specialty journal for proteomics methods and discoveries. After reframing the acidic-acylation finding and closing documentation gaps, the dual-protease workflow, quantified labeling efficiency comparison, and application to tissue (rat hippocampus) with 231 PTM sites in 3 h represent a practical advance for the proteomics community.
  - Realism: Very realistic after revisions. Proteomics values systematic method optimization and workflow acceleration; the paper would fit comfortably at this tier once the scope of the succinylation claim is properly attributed to the specific propionylation comparator and caveats are explicit.

## Alternative outlets
- **bioRxiv**
  - Fit: The manuscript is already preprinted here. Continued circulation while submission and revision proceed at a specialty journal venue is standard practice and allows community feedback and discovery during review elsewhere.
  - Realism: Already available; serves as a priority-establishment and open-access reference point independent of journal acceptance.
- **MethodsX**
  - Fit: Open-access methods journal for detailed protocol papers. After minor revisions, the RIPUP workflow protocol, with detailed digestion conditions, labeling procedures, and FragPipe parameter specifications, would be suitable for MethodsX's audience of bench proteomics researchers seeking reproducible, step-by-step guidance.
  - Realism: High acceptance likelihood if framed as a detailed protocol paper rather than a discovery paper. MethodsX is less selective and supports open-access dissemination of robust workflows.
- **Epigenetics & Chromatin**
  - Fit: Epigenetics-focused journal that publishes both methods and discovery papers; the histone PTM landscape revealed by RIPUP in rat hippocampus (H3 K27/K36/K37 methylation, H4 acetylation patterns, linker histone modifications) has direct biological relevance to chromatin regulation. The rapid tissue-processing angle appeals to the in vivo/ex vivo neurobiology community.
  - Realism: Realistic as a fallback if specialty MS journals reject. The paper would emphasize the biological histone modifications and tissue applicability over the technical proteomics contribution, reaching a broader epigenetics audience with lower methodological bar.

## Notes
The manuscript's primary contribution — RIPUP's speed, reproducibility, dual-protease coverage complementarity, and quantitative rigor — is not disputed by any reviewer and stands independently of the contested 'dark epigenome' claim. After required revisions reframing the succinylation/glutarylation finding as enhanced detection relative to the specific Garcia-type propionylation protocol (not biological discovery), closing Methods documentation gaps (FragPipe parameters, kNN justification/seed, artifact-stripping rules, reagent authentication), and resolving citation/attribution issues, the work becomes realistic at high-tier specialty proteomics journals (MCP, Proteomics) and possibly Nature Methods. Current as_is fit is with specialty analytical proteomics journals that accept 4.15/5 methodological papers. TMA comparison justification and orthogonal validation (PRM/synthetic peptides) are appropriately designated future work, not preconditions for publication.
