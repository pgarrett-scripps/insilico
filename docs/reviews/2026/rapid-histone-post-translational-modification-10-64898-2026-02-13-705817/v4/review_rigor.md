# Rigor & Overclaiming Reviewer

## Summary
This manuscript compares alternative proteases and labeling strategies for histone PTM analysis and reports that Arg-C Ultra with TMT labeling detects more PTM sites than conventional trypsin-propionylation, including 58 succinylation and 31 glutarylation sites attributed to charge compensation by TMT's tertiary amine. The core claim — that TMT reveals a 'dark epigenome' of negatively charged acylations — is mechanistically plausible and potentially important, but the evidence has a critical gap. The 58 succinylation sites were not validated against an independent method (e.g., synthetic peptides, orthogonal enrichment, or comparison with published HEK293T succinylomes), and the expanded variable-modification search space in HiP-Frag makes false discovery a concrete concern rather than a hypothetical one. Until such validation is provided, the central novelty claim outruns its evidence. The quantitative experiment with nicotinamide is also weakened by the acknowledged NAM-induced missed-cleavage redistribution, which the authors handle through an unconventional normalization strategy without demonstrating that it recovers true modification-level biology. The paper reports valuable observations — particularly the complementary coverage of Arg-C Ultra and r-Chymotrypsin — but its headline claims require requalification or additional evidence.

## Strengths
- The systematic comparison of 10 protease/derivatization conditions across 40 samples is methodologically thorough and provides a useful resource for the field.
- The discovery that TMT labeling rescues ionization of negatively charged acylations (succinylation, glutarylation) is mechanistically plausible, well-explained, and potentially important for the field if validated.
- The application to frozen-thawed rat hippocampal tissue demonstrates practical utility within a 3-hour workflow, supporting the claim of rapid turnaround.

## Weaknesses
- The central claim — that TMT reveals a 'dark epigenome' of succinylation (58 sites) and glutarylation (31 sites) 'largely undetected by other methods' — is a load-bearing novelty claim that is not yet supported. There are two distinct threats to this claim. First, the authors attribute the enhanced detection to charge compensation by TMT's tertiary amine, but the alternative explanation — that HiP-Frag's unrestrictive search simply assigns these modifications at high false-discovery rates from a very large variable-modification space — is not excluded. The HiP-Frag workflow was designed for discovery and explicitly acknowledges that PTM assignment requires targeted validation; the authors cite this but do not provide it. Second, the claim that these sites are 'largely undetected by propionylation-based methods' is not benchmarked against any published HEK293T succinylome or glutarylome — the authors do not state how many of the 58 sites are novel. Without this, the reader cannot judge whether the finding reflects method bias (which would be interesting) or simply the fact that no one searched for them before (which is a weaker claim). A control experiment using synthetic succinylated peptides or orthogonal enrichment (e.g., anti-succinyl-K antibodies) is the standard for this type of claim. The current evidence establishes correlation between TMT labeling and succinyl detection; it does not establish causation, and even correlation is ambiguous given the search-space confound.
- The quantitative experiment using NAM is presented as evidence that RIPUP 'is particularly robust for detecting differences in multiply modified peptidoforms' (Abstract), but the authors identify a critical problem: NAM induced 'dose-dependent missed cleavage redistribution' in Arg-C Ultra digests, evidenced by 259 peptidoforms detected exclusively in NAM-treated samples. The response is to abandon the standard peptide-family ratio approach and use individual peptidoform intensities with histone-level loading correction. This is a defensible choice, but the authors do not demonstrate that this approach recovers true biological signal rather than cleavage-artifact. The 112 significant peptidoforms (88 increasing with NAM) could reflect increased acetylation, but they could equally reflect a shift in the distribution of peptide lengths or charge states that changes which peptidoforms are selected for fragmentation in DDA — a well-known bias in label-free quantification. The authors acknowledge the missed-cleavage issue but do not provide an independent validation (e.g., Western blot of a key mark like H4K16ac) to confirm that the peptidoform-level changes track with biology rather than with sampling biases. Without this, the quantitative conclusions are suggestive but not robust.
- The abstract claims that RIPUP 'reduces sample preparation to hours while improving PTM coverage and quantitative accuracy.' The improvement in PTM coverage relative to Trypsin+Prop is supported by the peptide counts in Figure 2C (254 vs 179 fully cleaved peptides). However, 'quantitative accuracy' is not demonstrated anywhere in the manuscript. The NAM experiment shows internal consistency (dose-response correlation r=0.803), but that is precision, not accuracy — accuracy requires a known ground truth (e.g., spike-in standards or orthogonal quantitation). The authors show that RIPUP can detect differences; they do not show that those differences are quantitatively accurate. This sentence in the abstract overstates the evidence.
- [Sweep] The 'dark epigenome' language (Introduction, Results, Conclusions) is rhetorically strong for a finding that rests on an unrestrictive search without orthogonal validation. This should be requalified throughout, or the required validation provided.
- [Sweep] The authors state that TMT 'requires only one hour incubation' (Introduction) compared to propionylation's multi-round protocol, but this comparison omits the 15-minute hydroxylamine quench, drying, and resuspension steps required for TMT. The actual time savings are smaller than implied, and this should be stated transparently.
- [Sweep] The claim that 'endogenous propionylation and butyrylation cannot be distinguished from chemical derivatization artifacts in propionylated samples' is presented as an advantage of RIPUP, but the unlabeled Arg-C Ultra data also cannot distinguish endogenous propionylation from background chemical noise without a control (e.g., no-enzyme blank or synthetic peptide). This limitation should be acknowledged.
- [Sweep] The manuscript reports that 58 succinylation sites were identified with TMT but only 1 with propionylation. Yet the authors note that HDACs, not sirtuins, may be the major desuccinylases (citing Li et al. 2023), and the NAM experiment detected only 1 succinylation site. This internal inconsistency — TMT dramatically boosts succinyl detection in one experiment, but a NAM experiment designed to increase succinylation (via sirtuin inhibition) detects none — weakens the mechanistic narrative that TMT is selectively rescuing succinyl ionization. The authors should address this discrepancy explicitly.

## Questions
- Can the authors provide evidence — ideally through a controlled mixing experiment with synthetic succinylated peptides — that the 'dark epigenome' sites are endogenous rather than arising from TMT reactivity during labeling, and that the 58/31 site counts are not inflated by false discovery? The variable-modification search space is large and the MS2 evidence is fragment-based; a targeted validation of the highest-confidence sites would help readers judge the error rate.
- The NAM experiment produced 112 significant peptidoforms, yet the analysis used a per-peptidoform intensity normalization rather than the peptide-family ratio approach standard in the field. Why was the standard approach unsuitable, and how do the authors confirm that the observed changes reflect true modification-level biology rather than redistribution across missed-cleavage states induced by NAM? The authors state that NAM caused 'dose-dependent missed cleavage redistribution' — this seems to be the central quantitative challenge that the manuscript does not fully resolve.
- The authors find 58 succinylation and 31 glutarylation sites in HEK293T cells. How many of these sites have been previously reported in any cell type? This is needed to judge whether the claim that these are 'largely undetected by other methods' (Abstract) is a statement about method bias or simply about the breadth of the search space.
- Figure 4A reports propionylation efficiency of 29–61% by site count and 33–71% by intensity. The text acknowledges that the ammonium-containing buffers in the Garcia protocol reduce efficiency, but the paper consistently frames 'Trypsin + Prop' as the benchmark. If propionylation is performed under optimized conditions (non-ammonium buffers, as in Meert et al. 2015/2016), does the TMT advantage persist, or is it specific to the comparison chosen?
- The claim that TMT 'requires only one hour incubation' (Introduction) for labeling is contrasted with propionylation's multi-round protocol. But TMT labeling also requires quenching (15 min), drying, and resuspension steps. What is the actual hands-on time difference between the two workflows, not just incubation time?
