# Rigor & Overclaiming Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript presents a useful methodological comparison of alternative proteases and labeling strategies for histone PTM analysis, and the RIPUP workflow is likely of practical value. However, several central claims outrun the evidence provided. The mechanistic assertion that TMT "rescues ionization" via charge compensation is not directly supported—the data show only that more succinylation/glutarylation sites are detected, not *why*. The abstract overstates the quantitative output of RIPUP by reporting 112 significant peptidoforms without specifying this figure comes from Arg-C Ultra alone. The term "dark epigenome" is hyperbolic for modifications that have been previously reported. These issues are fixable with hedging and clearer reporting, but the claims in their current form would mislead a reader.

## Strengths
- 1. Systematic side-by-side comparison of three proteases under 10 conditions with four replicates each provides a reproducible benchmark for the field.
- 2. Demonstration that Arg-C Ultra with TMT labeling yields higher cleavage efficiency and more fully cleaved peptides than conventional Trypsin+propionylation is clear and well-supported.
- 3. The application to rat hippocampal tissue within a 3-hour workflow shows practical translatability beyond cell lines.

## Weaknesses
- **Load-bearing: The claim that TMT rescues ionization of acidic acylations via charge compensation from its tertiary amine is a mechanistic assertion without direct evidence.**
   The text states: "TMT's tertiary amine provides charge compensation that rescues the ionization of negatively charged acylations revealing 58 succinylation and 31 glutarylation sites." The evidence offered is that more succinylation/glutarylation sites are identified with TMT than with propionylation. This is a correlational observation; it does not distinguish among several plausible explanations: higher labeling efficiency (>98% vs ~68%), different chromatographic retention, reduced chemical side-reactions, or the proposed charge compensation. The authors do not compare MS1 intensities of the same succinylated peptide under TMT versus propionylation, nor do they include a label that lacks a tertiary amine as a control. The phrase "charge compensation that rescues the ionization" asserts a mechanism the data do not isolate. At minimum, the language should be softened to "is associated with improved detection" or "consistent with charge compensation." I recommend adding a direct comparison of ion signals for the same peptide in both labeling conditions.
- **Load-bearing: The abstract's quantitative claim about RIPUP conflates results from Arg-C Ultra alone with the full dual-protease workflow.**
   The abstract states: "In HEK293T cells treated with the pan-sirtuin inhibitor nicotinamide, RIPUP quantified 112 statistically significant peptidoforms (adj p < 0.05), predominantly increasing with NAM dose (88 up, 24 down)." RIPUP is defined as a dual-protease workflow combining Arg-C Ultra and r-Chymotrypsin. However, the 112 figure comes exclusively from the Arg-C Ultra digest; r-Chymotrypsin yielded only 16 significant modified peptidoforms, and many peptidoforms were exclusive to treated groups and therefore untestable. The abstract misleadingly implies the full method achieved 112 significant hits. This should be corrected to "Arg-C Ultra digestion within the RIPUP workflow quantified 112 significant peptidoforms" or the authors should report combined totals if they performed a joint analysis (which they do not).
- **The term "dark epigenome" to describe 58 succinylation and 31 glutarylation sites is hyperbolic and under-acknowledges prior literature.**
   Succinylation and glutarylation have been reported on histones by multiple groups (Zhang et al. *Nat Chem Biol* 2011; Xie et al. *Mol Cell Proteomics* 2012; Weyh et al. *Nat Chem* 2024). The authors' own unlabeled Arg-C Ultra condition also detected succinylation (Figure 5A), showing these marks are not entirely opaque to other methods. The phrase "largely undetected by propionylation-based methods" is only tested against the authors' own propionylation workflow, not systematically benchmarked against all published propionylation-based studies. The claim should be qualified as "under-detected in standard propionylation workflows as implemented here" to avoid overstating novelty.
- **The quantitative analysis of NAM treatment has low power and an imputation strategy that may bias the set of testable peptidoforms.**
   With n=3 per dose and DDA-LFQ (stochastic sampling), kNN imputation was restricted to dose groups where ≥2/3 replicates had values; groups with ≤1 measured value were left missing. This means peptidoforms appearing only at the highest NAM dose (potential true treatment effects) were excluded from testing, because they lacked a comparator. The authors do not discuss how many such peptidoforms were dropped or whether their inclusion could change the conclusions. The reported 112 significant peptidoforms should be interpreted as the lower bound of the response, and this limitation should be stated explicitly.

## Questions
- 1. Can the authors provide a direct comparison of MS1 peak areas for the same succinylated peptide (e.g., H4 K31-succ) under TMT versus propionylation labeling to support the claim of enhanced ionization?
- 2. Of the 58 succinylation sites identified with TMT, how many were also present (even at lower abundance) in the unlabeled Arg-C Ultra data, and what was their rank order of intensity? This would help distinguish between charge compensation and simply more efficient labeling.
- 3. In the NAM experiment, how many peptidoforms were present in only one dose group and therefore excluded from testing, and what modifications did they carry?
