# Methodology Reviewer

## Summary
The RIPUP workflow is a substantial and genuinely useful contribution: the dual-protease orthogonal coverage strategy is sound, and the observation that TMT labeling enriches for acidic acylations is potentially important. However, three load-bearing claims outrun their evidence. The "exceeds Trypsin-based approaches" benchmark is a known-suboptimal version of the established method, run in a buffer the authors themselves flag as reducing labeling efficiency. The charge-compensation mechanism for succinylation/glutarylation enhancement is inferred from identification counts, not demonstrated. And the NAM quantitative analysis treats technical replicates as independent observations while being confounded by the very cleavage redistribution the authors identify. All three are addressable in revision.

## Weaknesses
- HARD — The "exceeds Trypsin-based approaches" claim rests on a strawman comparator. The Trypsin arm uses the Garcia 2007 protocol with propionylation in ammonium bicarbonate, which the authors themselves acknowledge (citing Meert et al.) reduces labeling efficiency due to competing amine reactivity. The field's optimized propionylation workflows (Meert 2016; Searfoss 2023) are not included. The claim in the abstract that Arg-C Ultra + TMT "achieves a detection of total PTM that exceeds Trypsin-based approaches" is therefore not earned against the current best Trypsin workflow — only against the Garcia 2007 protocol as run here. The fix: include an optimized propionylation arm (e.g., in TEAB, or with the Searfoss modifications) as the Trypsin comparator, or restrict the claim to "exceeds the Garcia 2007 protocol as implemented."
- HARD — The charge-compensation mechanism is inferred, not demonstrated. The claim that "TMT's tertiary amine provides charge compensation that rescues the ionization of negatively charged acylations" is stated as established, but the evidence is identification counts. The comparison Arg-C Ultra + TMT vs Arg-C Ultra + Prop does isolate the label, which is good. But identification counts confound ionization efficiency with charge-state-dependent precursor selection (the authors show +3 ions for succinylated TMT peptides in Figure 6, but do not show the same succinylated peptide is poorly ionized or poorly selected without TMT), fragmentation efficiency, and database search scoring (different mass offsets, 229 vs 56 Da). The alternative — that TMT shifts the charge-state distribution into the DDA selection window, or improves fragmentation/scoring — produces the same data. The fix: compare MS1 signal intensity of the identical succinylated peptide with and without TMT, or with a label that is hydrophobic but lacks the tertiary amine.
- HARD — The NAM quantitative analysis treats technical replicates as independent and is confounded by cleavage redistribution. The text states "n = 3 digestion replicates/condition." If these are technical digests of a single treated lysate, the limma empirical Bayes variance estimate reflects technical noise, and the 112 significant peptidoforms (adj p < 0.05) are overconfident. Separately, the authors identify NAM-induced missed-cleavage redistribution as the reason to avoid peptide-family-ratio quantitation — but peptidoform-level quantitation is equally affected: if NAM changes Arg-C cleavage efficiency, peptidoform intensities change from backbone redistribution, not PTM abundance. The 88 "increasing" peptidoforms could partly reflect this redistribution. The fix: state whether the three replicates are independent biological cultures, and show that the significant peptidoform trends survive summing intensities across backbone lengths sharing the same modification state.
- SOFT — The succinylation/glutarylation site counts (58 and 31) sum across both TMT proteases, which have different sequence coverage than the propionylated Trypsin arm. The comparison is not matched for sequence coverage, so the site-count difference partly reflects coverage, not labeling chemistry. Reporting the counts restricted to sequence regions covered by both strategies would isolate the labeling effect.
- SOFT — The missed-cleavage motif analysis (SI Figures S6, S7) reports enrichment of D at P1′ and E at P2 without a stated statistical test. Is the enrichment significant against a background model of the histone sequences digested?
- SOFT — The dose-response concordance (r = 0.803) is a correlation of log2FC at two doses. This is expected to be positive for any monotonic effect and is not a strong validation of quantitative accuracy.

## Questions
- Are the three NAM replicates independent biological cultures (separate dishes treated separately) or technical digests of a single lysate? The text says "digestion replicates," which determines whether the limma p-values are valid.
- For the charge-compensation claim, is there a direct comparison of MS1 signal for the same succinylated peptide with and without TMT, or is the evidence entirely identification counts?
- Was an optimized propionylation protocol (e.g., Searfoss 2023, Meert 2016) considered as the Trypsin comparator, and if so, why was it excluded?
