# Methodology Reviewer

## Summary
This is a genuinely useful, carefully executed workflow comparison with honest reporting of its own comparator's weaknesses — the authors flag the AMBIC-buffer propionylation inefficiency and the Trypsin IHP overestimation themselves. The benchmarking design (10 conditions, n=4, low CVs) is sound as a descriptive survey. However, the two central novel claims — the charge-compensation mechanism for TMT's enhanced acidic-acylation detection, and the quantitative NAM analysis — each rest on a design that does not isolate the variable claimed to be responsible. Both are fixable with targeted control experiments, but as written the mechanistic and quantitative conclusions outrun the evidence.

## Strengths
- Systematic multi-condition comparison with adequate replication (n=4) and consistently low peptide CVs, giving the descriptive benchmarking real weight.
- The authors candidly identify their own comparator's limitations (AMBIC-buffer propionylation, Trypsin K-cleavage overestimation), which strengthens confidence in the parts of the comparison that do hold.
- The dual-protease coverage rationale is well supported by the sequence-coverage data — the complementarity of Arg-C Ultra and r-Chymotrypsin is demonstrated, not just asserted.

## Weaknesses
- HARD — The charge-compensation mechanism is confounded with stepped collision energy. The paper's headline claim is that TMT's tertiary amine rescues ionization of succinylated/glutarylated peptides, revealing a "dark epigenome." But TMT-labeled samples were fragmented with stepped NCE 30/40/50 while every non-TMT condition used fixed 30% (Methods, LC-MS/MS). Stepped NCE is itself known to improve fragmentation across diverse peptide classes — the authors cite Diedrich et al. 2013 for exactly this. The enhanced detection of acidic acylations and the enhanced b-ion series (Figure 3D) could therefore be entirely a collision-energy effect, with the tertiary amine playing no role. The design does not isolate the label from the fragmentation energy. To earn the mechanism: fragment TMT-labeled peptides at fixed 30% NCE and show succinylation/glutarylation detection is still enhanced, or fragment propionylated peptides at stepped NCE and show it does not reproduce the TMT effect. Without one of these, the "dark epigenome" claim stands only as an empirical observation with an unproven explanation.
- HARD — The NAM quantitative analysis is confounded by the cleavage redistribution the authors themselves report. The authors state that NAM induced dose-dependent missed-cleavage redistribution, with 259 peptidoforms detected exclusively in NAM-treated samples. Quantifying individual peptidoform intensities cannot distinguish a genuine increase in a modification from a shift in cleavage efficiency at that site: the same modified residue appearing in a longer, differently-cleaved peptide under NAM would register as an apparent decrease in one peptidoform and an increase in another, with no change in the underlying modification. Abandoning peptide-family ratios relocates this confound rather than removing it. The 112 significant peptidoforms (88 "increasing") need to be shown robust to this — for example, by restricting the analysis to peptidoforms with matched cleavage states across all dose groups, or by demonstrating the significant changes persist when intensities are summed across all backbone variants of the same modification site.
- SOFT — "Exceeds Trypsin-based approaches" is broader than the comparator tested. The Trypsin comparator is the Garcia et al. protocol with AMBIC buffer, which the authors acknowledge has suboptimal propionylation efficiency. A Trypsin protocol with optimized labeling (TEAB buffer, per Meert et al. or Searfoss et al.) might close the gap. The claim should be scoped to "the Garcia et al. protocol as commonly implemented," or the optimized Trypsin comparator should be included.
- The b-ion enhancement claim (Figure 3D) rests on the same stepped-NCE confound as weakness 1, and the spectra cannot be inspected from the text, lowering confidence in the fragmentation-pattern claim.
- The 58 succinylation and 31 glutarylation sites are identified by unrestrictive search without synthetic validation; the "dark epigenome" prevalence claim should be hedged accordingly (the authors do acknowledge this in Limitations).
- Propionylation was performed in AMBIC while TMT labeling in TEAB — buffer is confounded with label type in the labeling-efficiency comparison (Figure 4A), though the authors flag the AMBIC issue.
- Digestion conditions (time, temperature, enzyme:substrate ratio) differ across enzymes; defensible as a protocol comparison, but "Arg-C Ultra is better" should be read as "this protocol configuration," not the enzyme in isolation.
- The IHP comparison in Figure 4B mixes search parameters (R-only for Trypsin vs FYLM for r-Chymotrypsin) — appropriate per enzyme, but the cross-condition percentages are not directly comparable.

## Questions
- Was any TMT-labeled sample run at fixed 30% NCE, or any propionylated sample at stepped NCE, that could be reported to isolate the collision-energy variable?
- For the 112 significant NAM peptidoforms, what fraction are also detected with the same cleavage state in the untreated control, and do the significant changes persist when restricted to peptidoforms present in all three dose groups with identical missed-cleavage counts?
