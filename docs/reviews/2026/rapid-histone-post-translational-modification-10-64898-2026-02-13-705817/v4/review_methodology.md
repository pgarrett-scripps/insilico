# Methodology Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript presents a systematic comparison of proteases and labeling strategies for histone PTM analysis, with a novel dual-protease workflow (RIPUP) and a mechanistic explanation for enhanced detection of acidic acylations with TMT labeling. The experimental design is generally sound, with appropriate comparators, multiple replicates, and orthogonal validation through complementary proteases. However, several design choices limit the strength of causal claims: the comparison to propionylation uses a suboptimal protocol (acknowledged by authors), the succinylation/glutarylation enhancement is attributed to TMT's tertiary amine via a plausible but untested mechanistic model, and the quantitative validation relies on a single perturbation (NAM) in one cell line. The work is methodologically rigorous for a methods paper but would benefit from direct mechanistic validation and broader biological validation of the key findings.

## Strengths
- Systematic comparison across 10 conditions (3 proteases × derivatization/denaturing states) with n=4 replicates per condition in HEK293T, providing robust baseline data for protease performance metrics (peptide counts, CVs, sequence coverage).
- Dual-protease orthogonal strategy is well-motivated: r-Chymotrypsin provides complementary coverage of H2A variants (>95% vs <6% for Arg-C Ultra + Prop) and linker histones, with overlapping coverage enabling built-in validation.
- TMT labeling efficiency is quantified rigorously using two complementary metrics (site-based and intensity-weighted), showing ~92–99% efficiency vs 29–71% for propionylation, with clear operational implications.
- Mechanistic explanation for succinylation/glutarylation enhancement is plausible: TMT's tertiary amine sequesters mobile protons, shifting fragmentation toward b-ions and rescuing ionization of acidic peptides (Figure 3, supported by fragmentation spectra in Figure 6).
- Quantitative experiment (NAM treatment) demonstrates dose-dependent changes in 112 peptidoforms with expected direction (88 up, 24 down) and includes reciprocal PTM crosstalk analysis, showing the workflow can detect biologically meaningful changes.
- Proof-of-concept on rat hippocampal tissue (n=5) identifies 231 unique PTM sites in 3 h, including biologically critical marks (H3 K27/K36/K37 methylation, H4 N-terminal acetylation), validating applicability beyond cell culture.
- Data deposited in ProteomeXchange (PXD073683) with custom R scripts available on GitHub, enabling reproducibility.

## Weaknesses
- The propionylation comparator is acknowledged as suboptimal: 'ammonium-containing buffers in this protocol introduce competing amine reactivity that reduces labeling efficiency relative to optimized approaches' (page 14). This undermines the claim that TMT 'exceeds' propionylation-based approaches—the comparison is to a known-suboptimal variant, not the best achievable propionylation protocol. A fair comparison would either use an optimized propionylation protocol or explicitly frame the finding as 'TMT exceeds the widely-used Garcia et al. protocol' rather than propionylation in general.
- The mechanistic explanation for succinylation/glutarylation enhancement (TMT tertiary amine charge compensation) is plausible but not directly tested. No experiment isolates this mechanism: e.g., no comparison to a TMT variant lacking the tertiary amine, no synthetic succinylated peptide standards fragmented under identical conditions with/without TMT, or no computational modeling of proton affinity. The finding is correlative—TMT correlates with enhanced succinylation detection—but causality is inferred rather than demonstrated.
- Quantitative validation relies on a single perturbation (NAM, a pan-sirtuin inhibitor) in one cell line (HEK293T). While the dose-response is coherent (r=0.803 for Arg-C Ultra), this does not validate the workflow's quantitative accuracy across diverse PTM types or biological contexts. No orthogonal quantitation method (e.g., targeted PRM, synthetic peptide standards, or an independent quantitative experiment) corroborates the peptidoform-level quantification.
- The claim that succinylation and glutarylation have been 'systematically under-detected' (page 23) rests on the observation that TMT detects 58 succinylation and 31 glutarylation sites vs. fewer in propionylated samples. However, without a positive control (e.g., synthetic succinylated histone standards or a biological perturbation known to increase succinylation), it is unclear whether TMT truly detects more sites or whether propionylation simply suppresses ionization of these peptides. The design does not distinguish 'TMT detects real sites that propionylation misses' from 'TMT and propionylation detect the same sites but with different efficiency'.
- Sequence coverage gaps persist despite the dual-protease strategy, particularly in globular domains (acknowledged on page 25). The manuscript does not quantify what fraction of the histone proteome remains unsampled or discuss whether PTMs in these regions might be systematically missed, limiting claims about comprehensiveness.
- The HiP-Frag computational framework (Vai et al. 2025) is applied with 1% FDR at PSM and peptide levels, but the variable modification search space is not fully specified in the main text (relegated to SI Table S1). The authors acknowledge that 'the number of declared variable modifications should be limited to maximize results' (page 25), raising the question of whether the search space was optimized post-hoc to maximize succinylation/glutarylation detections. No pre-registration or stated analysis plan is provided.
- Missed cleavage motif analysis (SI Figures S6–S7) reveals that basic residues and acidic residues near cleavage sites affect Arg-C Ultra and r-Chymotrypsin efficiency, but the impact on PTM quantification is not quantified. If PTMs preferentially occur near missed cleavage sites, this could introduce systematic bias in PTM abundance estimates.
- The NAM quantitative experiment shows that Arg-C Ultra identifies 112 significant peptidoforms but r-Chymotrypsin identifies only 4 modified peptidoforms reaching FDR significance (Figure 7C). This large discrepancy is attributed to 'greater peptidoform diversity from FYLM cleavage specificity' (page 20), but it raises the question of whether r-Chymotrypsin's quantitative performance is actually inferior or whether the analysis strategy (histone-level normalization) is suboptimal for this protease. The design does not clarify whether r-Chymotrypsin should be used for quantification or only for orthogonal coverage.

## Questions
- Can the authors provide a direct mechanistic validation of the TMT tertiary amine charge compensation hypothesis? For example: (1) fragmentation spectra of synthetic succinylated peptides with and without TMT under identical MS conditions, (2) comparison to a TMT variant lacking the tertiary amine, or (3) computational modeling of proton affinity and fragmentation pathways?
- How does the optimized propionylation protocol (e.g., Meert et al. 2015, 2016, or Searfoss et al. 2023) perform relative to TMT under matched conditions? The current comparison uses a known-suboptimal protocol; a fair head-to-head comparison would strengthen the claim that TMT is superior.
- For the succinylation/glutarylation findings, can the authors provide independent validation? For example: (1) targeted PRM quantification of selected succinylated sites, (2) comparison to a biological sample known to have high succinylation (e.g., SIRT5-deficient cells), or (3) synthetic succinylated histone standards to confirm that detected sites are real and not artifacts?
- What is the quantitative accuracy of the peptidoform-level quantification? Can the authors validate using an orthogonal method (e.g., targeted PRM, isotope labeling, or an independent quantitative experiment) to confirm that the NAM dose-response is reproducible?
- The HiP-Frag variable modification search space is large (SI Table S1). Was this search space defined a priori, or was it optimized post-hoc to maximize PTM detections? A pre-registration or stated analysis plan would strengthen confidence in the findings.
- Why does r-Chymotrypsin yield only 4 significant modified peptidoforms in the NAM experiment compared to 112 for Arg-C Ultra? Is this a limitation of the protease, the analysis strategy, or the histone-level normalization approach? Should r-Chymotrypsin be used only for orthogonal coverage and not for quantification?
- How do missed cleavages affect PTM quantification? The motif analysis shows that PTMs and basic residues are enriched near missed cleavage sites. Does this introduce systematic bias in PTM abundance estimates, and how does this bias differ between proteases?
- The rat hippocampal experiment identifies 231 PTM sites but uses only n=5 animals and requires ≥2 replicates per site. How robust are these findings to sample size and detection threshold? A sensitivity analysis would clarify the stability of the PTM landscape.
- Can the authors clarify the scope of the 'dark epigenome' claim? The 58 succinylation and 31 glutarylation sites are detected in HEK293T cells; are these sites consistently detected across other cell types and tissues, or is this a cell-type-specific signature?
