# Reproducibility Reviewer

## Summary
This is a useful, well-motivated methodological paper: the dual-protease RIPUP workflow is a genuine simplification over the propionylation standard, and the TMT finding is interesting if it holds. But the two load-bearing claims — the "dark epigenome" charge-compensation mechanism and the quantitative NAM results — rest on comparisons that do not isolate the proposed mechanism, and on search-configuration and code-versioning details I cannot verify from the text. The fixes are concrete and within reach, so this is a major revision rather than a rejection.

## Weaknesses
- The charge-compensation claim is not isolated from the labeling-efficiency confound. The dark-epigenome result (58 succinylation, 31 glutarylation sites) is presented as evidence that TMT's tertiary amine rescues ionization of acidic acylations. But TMT labeling efficiency is ~99% versus 33–71% for propionylation (Figure 4A). If succinylated peptides are preferentially lost from the propionylated pool because they are incompletely labeled — and thus fail the informative-peptide filter or carry missed cleavages — the same result would appear with no charge-compensation mechanism at all. The manuscript offers no control that distinguishes these: the clean experiment is the same succinylated peptides in TMT-labeled versus unlabeled Arg-C digests, where the N-terminus is charged in both. If unlabeled Arg-C detects succinylation comparably, the tertiary-amine story is weakened. A second confound compounds this: TMT samples were fragmented with stepped NCE 30/40/50 while all others used fixed 30%, which changes ion-series generation and PTM localization independent of chemistry. The one experiment that would settle it: report succinylation/glutarylation site counts and per-peptide scores for identical peptides under TMT-labeled versus unlabeled Arg-C, with identical search space and collision energy.
- Search-space consistency is unverifiable, and it is load-bearing. The dark-epigenome claim depends on succinylation and glutarylation being declared as variable modifications with identical mass offsets in every search condition. The offsets live in SI Table S1, which I cannot inspect. The text says TMT declarations were "consistent with unlabeled samples" but never states that the propionylated searches used the same offsets list. If the offsets differed, the result is a search-configuration artifact, not a biological or chemical discovery. This needs an explicit statement that all conditions shared the identical variable-modification and mass-offset list, with the list reproduced where a reader can see it.
- The canonical RIPUP protocol is not pinned to a single specification. Enzyme-to-substrate ratios vary across experiments: 1:100 (method comparison), 1:50 (NAM quantitation), 1:10 (hippocampus). The headline "3-hour workflow" claim is tied to the 1:10 hippocampus condition, while the quantitative experiment that demonstrates the workflow's utility used 1:50. A replicator cannot tell which is the recommended protocol. The protocol needs one canonical specification — ratios, buffers, volumes, times — in a single place.
