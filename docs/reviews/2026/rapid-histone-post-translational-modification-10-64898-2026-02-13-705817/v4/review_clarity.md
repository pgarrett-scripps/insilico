# Clarity & Presentation Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript is written in a formally correct, detail-dense style and the argument is broadly traceable. However, several HARD clarity defects block a reader from independently verifying quantitative claims or reconstructing what was done. The most consequential is an ambiguity in how the propionylation efficiency denominator is defined, which directly affects the central quantitative comparison in Figure 4A. A smaller set of SOFT issues — orphaned Methods detail, a figure-panel caption mismatch, and one tautological phrasing — create friction. These are all fixable, but the propionylation-efficiency ambiguity is substantive enough to warrant a major revision before the paper can be read with confidence.

## Strengths
- The decision to test 10 digestion/derivatization conditions in parallel (Figure 2) gives the reader enough information to evaluate which workflow fits a given biological question, and the data are presented in a consistent layout that supports cross-condition comparison.
- The performance metrics are defined operationally — missed-cleavage rate, labeling efficiency by both site count and intensity, informative peptide frequency — so a reader could reproduce the triage without judgement calls.

## Weaknesses
- LOAD-BEARING: Figure 4A reports a propionylation efficiency of ~29–61% (by site count) and ~33–71% (by intensity), but the denominator that generates these percentages is not stated. The text says efficiency was calculated as 'labeled sites divided by the sum of labeled and free sites.' That seems to exclude biologically modified sites from the denominator. The problem: 'free sites' means unmodified lysines that were not derivatized, but in the same samples there are also biologically modified lysines (acetylation, methylation, succinylation, etc.) that are not substrates for propionylation because they are already occupied. If those biologically modified sites are excluded from the denominator, the efficiency number describes only the fraction of originally-free amines that got derivatized — which is the correct metric. But a reader cannot confirm this from the text, because the definition of 'free sites' is never given an operational criterion. The same ambiguity applies to the intensity-weighted calculation. Without knowing exactly which sites enter the denominator, two different labs using the same reagent could report very different efficiencies simply by counting differently. The authors should state unambiguously: are biologically modified lysines counted as 'not available for labeling' (excluded from the denominator) or as 'not labeled' (included)? And they should report the raw counts (labeled + free + biologically modified, per condition) alongside the percentages so the reader can see the actual numbers.
- HARD (methods readability): The Methods section describes two separate TMT experiments but does not give a reader the means to distinguish them operationally. One is the TMT-labeling comparison reported in Figures 3–5 (Arg-C Ultra + TMT and r-Chymotrypsin + TMT, with HEK293T histones). The other is implied by the NAM-treatment experiment (Figure 7), where the text says that unlabeled Arg-C Ultra was used. It is never stated explicitly that the NAM experiment used unlabeled digestion — the reader has to infer it from the absence of any TMT or propionylation step in the NAM workflow description. A Methods subsection should begin with a sentence saying 'For the NAM dose-response experiment, histones were digested with Arg-C Ultra and r-Chymotrypsin without chemical derivatization, and quantified by label-free DDA-LFQ.'
- SOFT (Figure panel reference mismatch): Figure 5's caption begins 'Diversity and abundance of histone post-translational modifications detected across enzyme digestion and chemical labeling strategies.' The caption then labels panels (A–D). Panel B is described in text on page 16 as 'TMT-labeled peptides from Arg-C Ultra and r-Chymotrypsin digestion achieved comparable PTM numbers to conventional Trypsin + Prop methods (~120 PTMs) and enhanced identification of specific modification-types, including pronounced enrichment of negatively charged acylation marks such as succinylation and glutarylation (Figure 5B).' But the figure as described in the caption appears to show a stacked bar for all modification types across conditions in panel A and a second stacked bar for TMT vs Trypsin+Prop in panel B. The reader cannot tell whether panel B is a subset of the same data or an independently grouped analysis. The caption should state what is grouped in each panel and on what units the y-axis is plotted (count of sites? count of peptidoforms?).
- HARD (quantitative claim without comparison state): Page 19 states 'RIPUP detected 231 unique PTM sites across both enzymes within 3 h, including all major PTM classes.' The claim that 231 unique PTM sites were detected is a number that needs an enumerator definition: does 'unique PTM site' mean a single residue-position–modification-type pair (e.g., H3K9ac counts once regardless of how many different peptides carry it) or one modification occurrence on one sequence? If the latter, the same PTM site on two different backbone lengths is counted twice. Neither the Methods nor the Results defines the unit, so a reader cannot tell what '231' counts.
- SOFT (orphaned Methods detail): The Methods describe an SDS-PAGE gel step (SI Methods, Histone Extraction) but nowhere in the Results or main-text Methods is it stated what the gel was used for — whether it was a QC check for extraction purity, a quantification step, or a preparative step. The gel images in SI Figure S9 are referenced, but their role in the workflow is not explained in the main text. This is not a serious flaw, but it leaves a reader to guess whether the gel was a quality control or a stage of sample preparation.

## Questions
- Figure 4A bars: are the propionylation efficiency values (by site count) averages of the four digestion replicates? If so, please report the individual replicate values alongside, because variability in a denominator that includes both occupied and free sites can produce the same ratio from very different absolute numbers and the reader cannot tell which scenario holds.
- Figure 5 uses a persistent two-column panel layout (A–D) that the text references as 'Figure 5B', 'Figure 5C', etc. Panel D labels read 'C–D' in the caption's third sentence ('(C–D) Multiply modified peptidoforms...'). Is panel D the right half of the stacked-bar layout or a separate panel not referenced in text? A caption that labels its own subpanels unambiguously is needed.
- Page 17 says 'Arg-C Ultra and Trypsin showed similar cleavage specificity on propionylated histones' — is this a claim about the two enzymes' intrinsic specificities (both cleave C-terminal to Arg only, so they should be identical by design) or about the observed cleavage patterns in this particular preparation? If the latter, the sentence is correct; if the former, it states the obvious about the proteases' design and should be reworded to avoid a tautology.
- The Methods state that '0 mM NAM' is 'fresh complete media' without supplement. Was vehicle (e.g., a small amount of water or DMSO) added to the control to match the volume of NAM stock added to the treated dishes? If not, the control and treated conditions differ in total volume added to the dish, and any downstream effect (even trivial osmolarity or dilution) is not controlled for.
