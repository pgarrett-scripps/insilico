# Clarity & Presentation Reviewer

SCORE: 3
CONFIDENCE: 4

**Overall take.** This is a well-structured methods paper with a clear through-line and a genuinely useful practical contribution — the RIPUP workflow and the TMT charge-compensation finding are both presented in a way a competent reader can follow. The main clarity problems are concentrated in the figures and in a few quantitative statements where the actual numbers are missing or the comparison basis is ambiguous. The writing is dense but mostly unambiguous; the issues are fixable with targeted revisions rather than structural.

**Strengths.** The workflow comparison is presented as a systematic matrix (Figure 1) that makes the experimental design immediately recoverable. The TMT charge-compensation mechanism for acidic acylation detection is explained with a clear chemical rationale (Figure 3) that connects structure to observed behavior. The paper is honest about its limitations, including the single-cell-line scope and the propionylation-efficiency caveat.

**Weaknesses (load-bearing).**

1. **The central quantitative claim about TMT's advantage over propionylation is not supported by a directly comparable number.** The paper states that TMT labeling "yielded ~2.3-fold more fully cleaved peptides with Arg-C Ultra than the established 'Trypsin + Prop' approach (416 vs 179)" — but this compares TMT-labeled Arg-C Ultra against propionylated Trypsin, which differ in *two* variables (protease and label). The claim that TMT itself, rather than the protease, drives the improvement is asserted but never isolated. The reader cannot determine from the text whether the 2.3-fold difference would persist if Arg-C Ultra were propionylated instead of TMT-labeled — the paper reports "Arg-C Ultra + Prop" yields 254 fully cleaved peptides, which is *closer* to the TMT number (416) than to the Trypsin number (179), suggesting the protease is the dominant factor. To settle this, report the same comparison with the protease held constant: TMT-labeled Arg-C Ultra vs. propionylated Arg-C Ultra, with the fold-change and the actual counts side by side.

2. **The succinylation/glutarylation "dark epigenome" claim rests on a comparison that is not shown as a direct head-to-head.** The paper reports 58 succinylation and 31 glutarylation sites detected with TMT, and states these are "largely undetected by propionylation-based methods" — but the reader cannot verify this because the manuscript does not report the corresponding succinylation/glutarylation counts for the propionylated conditions in the same figure or table. Figure 5B shows TMT conditions but the propionylated comparison is only described in text. Without the propionylated-condition counts for the same enzyme and the same search parameters, the claim that TMT specifically rescues these modifications (rather than the search space or the enzyme simply finding more of everything) is not established. Report the succinylation and glutarylation site counts for Arg-C Ultra + Prop and Trypsin + Prop under identical search settings, alongside the TMT numbers.

3. **The quantitative statements in the NAM experiment frequently lack the actual quantity.** For example: "H3 peptidoforms carrying K9ac/K23ac reached significance at both doses across two backbone lengths (adj p < 0.001)" — the reader is told significance but not the magnitude or direction of the change. Similarly, "88 increasing with NAM dose in the expected direction" gives a count but no effect sizes. The claim that RIPUP "quantified 112 statistically significant peptidoforms" is clear, but the *direction and magnitude* of the key sirtuin-target changes (H3K9ac, H4K16ac) are never stated as numbers. Report the log2 fold-changes for the representative sirtuin-target peptidoforms at both doses, with confidence intervals.

**Sweep (minor weaknesses).**

- Figure 5C and 5D: the panel labels are not defined in the caption — "C–D" is referenced but the reader cannot tell from the caption alone what distinguishes the two panels; add one sentence defining each.
- The term "IHP" (informative histone peptides) is defined in the text but the abbreviation is used in Figure 4B without the definition being repeated in the caption — a reader scanning the figure cannot decode it.
- The sentence "The TMT tertiary amine sequesters a mobile proton at the N-terminal region of the peptide, shifting fragmentation dynamics toward enhanced b-ion series generation" (Figure 3C) is presented as established fact, but the manuscript provides no direct evidence (e.g., b-ion/y-ion ratio comparison between TMT- and propionyl-labeled versions of the same peptide) — this is a mechanistic claim that outruns the data shown; either soften the claim or add the comparison.
- The phrase "~120 PTMs" appears in the results section without a figure or table reference — the reader cannot locate the source of this number; cite the specific figure panel.
- In the Methods, the TMT labeling section states "peptide:TMT ratio 1:8" but does not specify whether this is mass ratio or molar ratio — a reader cannot reproduce the labeling without this.
- The abstract claims "RIPUP quantified 112 statistically significant peptidoforms" but does not state the direction split (88 up, 24 down) that appears in the main text — the abstract's claim is standalone-intelligible but omits the key quantitative detail; consider adding it.
- The term "dark epigenome" is introduced in the abstract and used once in the results but never defined — a reader encountering it for the first time must infer its meaning from context; add a brief parenthetical definition at first use.

**Questions.**

1. In the NAM experiment, the paper states that "NAM induced dose-dependent missed cleavage redistribution in Arg-C Ultra digests, evidenced by 259 peptidoforms detected exclusively in NAM-treated samples." What is the missed-cleavage distribution (0, 1, 2+) for the 0 mM vs. 10 mM conditions specifically — is the shift toward more missed cleavages or fewer, and does it correlate with specific modification states (e.g., acetylation density) at the cleavage site?

2. For the TMT charge-compensation claim, have the authors compared b-ion vs. y-ion series completeness for the *same* succinylated peptide sequence in TMT-labeled vs. propionylated form? If not, is there a plan to do so, since this would directly test the proposed mechanism?

3. The Methods state that for TMT-labeled samples, "the monoisotopic mass of the intact label (+229.162932 Da) was set as a static modification on peptide N-termini and as a variable modification on K." What was the labeling efficiency for K residues specifically (not just N-termini) — the paper reports ~92% by site count, but does this vary by peptide length or modification state at adjacent residues?
