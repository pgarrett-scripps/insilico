# Clarity & Presentation Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript reports a genuinely useful methodological comparison — Arg-C Ultra and r-Chymotrypsin against the conventional Trypsin+propionylation workflow, with TMT as an alternative label — and the finding that TMT detects acidic acylations (succinylation, glutarylation) that propionylation misses is potentially important. The experimental design is thorough (10 conditions, 4 replicates, explicit labeling-efficiency metrics). However, the paper's central contribution is presented ambiguously: the abstract frames RIPUP (unlabeled dual-protease) as the headline while the strongest discovery claims belong to the TMT-labeled arm, which is not RIPUP. Several quantitative attributions in the abstract do not match the results section. These are fixable but require the authors to state clearly which workflow is the contribution and to reconcile the numbers.

## Strengths
- The mechanistic explanation for TMT's effect on acidic acylation detection (Figure 3, mobile proton model) is clearly articulated and testable.
- The honest disclosure of why Trypsin+TMT was not compared, and the explicit labeling-efficiency metrics (by site vs by intensity), are model examples of methods transparency.
- The IHP (informative histone peptide) definition is operationalized precisely enough to be reproduced.

## Weaknesses
- The central contribution is ambiguous between RIPUP and the TMT workflow (HARD). The abstract opens "we develop RIPUP (Rapid Identification of histone PTMs in Underivatized Peptides), a streamlined multi-protease workflow" and then states "Arg-C Ultra with TMT labeling achieves a detection of total PTM that exceeds Trypsin-based approaches." These are two different workflows, and the name "Underivatized Peptides" is directly contradicted by the TMT arm, which is a derivatization. The conclusions compound this: "TMT-labeled Arg-C Ultra/r-Chymotrypsin is the recommended first-choice workflow" for discovery, while "the dual-protease RIPUP approach" is recommended for coverage. A reader cannot determine from the abstract or intro which workflow is the paper's primary contribution.
- The abstract attributes to RIPUP a number that the results attribute to Arg-C Ultra alone (HARD). Abstract: "RIPUP quantified 112 statistically significant peptidoforms (adj p < 0.05)." Results: "Unlabeled Arg-C Ultra identified 112 statistically significant peptidoform changes." RIPUP is defined as the dual-protease workflow (Arg-C Ultra + r-Chymotrypsin); the r-Chymotrypsin arm found 16 significant peptidoforms. Attributing 112 to "RIPUP" conflates the Arg-C-only result with the combined workflow.
- The urea concentration used per enzyme is not recoverable from the main text (HARD). Figure 1's caption says "denaturing agent (2 or 6 M urea)," but the methods specify only "2 M Urea" and only for the Trypsin group. Which enzyme received 6 M urea is not stated anywhere in the main text; the reader must consult Table S2.
- Two distinct mechanisms are conflated as one (HARD). The text says TMT "rescues the ionization of succinylated peptides" (an ESI effect) "and increasing b-ion series flanking PTM sites" (a fragmentation effect), and the abstract attributes the succinylation/glutarylation discovery to "charge compensation that rescues the ionization." These are different effects operating at different stages of the MS experiment, and the paper does not state which one is claimed to be responsible for the improved detection.
- Figure 2's legend does not define the color/condition mapping; I could not verify from the caption and labels alone that each panel is self-explanatory — please confirm the legend names every condition and symbol.
- "following the recommended guidelines for the HiP-Frag workflow, with some modifications" — the modifications are never specified; a reader cannot tell what was changed.
- "Derivatization," "labeling," and "propionylation" are used interchangeably for the same chemical step (e.g., "propionylation labeling," "TMT-labeling," "chemical derivatization"); pick one term per concept.
- Figure 7's caption says "with RIPUP" but panels A–D are per-enzyme (Arg-C Ultra vs r-Chymotrypsin); the caption should name the enzyme per panel.
- "achieves a detection of total PTM that exceeds Trypsin-based approaches" — "total PTM" is undefined (total PTM sites? total PTM identifications? total modified peptidoforms?), and the phrasing is grammatically awkward.
