# Desk Screen Report

**Manuscript:** Rapid Histone Post-Translational Modification Analysis Using Alternative Proteases and Tandem Mass Tags

**Venue:** In Silico (open overlay journal; reviews preprints and publishes the referee record)

---

## Assessment

**Scope check.** This is an original research manuscript in analytical biochemistry/mass spectrometry-based proteomics. The central claims — that a dual-protease workflow (Arg-C Ultra + r-Chymotrypsin) reduces sample preparation time, that TMT labeling rescues ionization of negatively charged acylations (succinylation, glutarylation), and that the combined approach enables quantitative histone PTM analysis in cell lines and tissue — are all evaluable from the manuscript itself and its deposited materials (raw data deposited to ProteomeXchange, scripts on GitHub). This is squarely within In Silico's scope: methodological work with checkable claims.

**Completeness.** The manuscript is complete and intelligible. It includes methods, results, figures, supplementary materials, data availability, and a limitations section. The experimental design (10 conditions, 40 samples, n = 4 replicates) is described in sufficient detail for a reviewer to evaluate.

**Fundamental flaws.** I do not see a flaw evident on its face that would make review pointless. The claims are substantial but not implausible, and the evidence presented is extensive. Whether the TMT charge-compensation mechanism fully explains the succinylation/glutarylation enhancement, and whether the quantitative analysis (kNN imputation, limma with histone-level normalization) is statistically sound, are exactly the kinds of questions that full review should address — not desk-reject.

**Bar check.** This is not work plainly below the venue's bar. It is a systematic, well-controlled methodological comparison with a clear mechanistic hypothesis, deposited data, and a proof-of-concept application. It will be of interest to researchers in epigenetics and proteomics.

**One minor note (not grounds for rejection):** The manuscript is long and dense, and the figure count is high. This is a formatting/preference issue, not a threshold problem.

---

**DESK DECISION: proceed**

The manuscript should be sent for full review. The claims are checkable, the data are deposited, and the work is within scope. Reviewer attention should focus on the statistical approach to quantification, the strength of the TMT charge-compensation mechanism as an explanation for the observed PTM enrichment, and whether the dual-protease workflow's advantages are demonstrated convincingly relative to the comparator methods.
