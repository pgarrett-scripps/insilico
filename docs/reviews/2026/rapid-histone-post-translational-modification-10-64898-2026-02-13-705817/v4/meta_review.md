# Meta-Review

**Draft recommendation:** minor

## Synthesis
All eight specialists rate the manuscript 4/5 (ethics 5/5), with high confidence, and the verdict distribution is 1 accept / 7 minor. The panel converges on a shared assessment: RIPUP is a rigorous, well-documented methods contribution — 10 conditions across 40 samples, median CVs <5–7%, quantified labeling efficiency by two metrics, proper limma/BH statistics for the NAM dose–response, exemplary compliance (IACUC #09-0006, ARRIVE) and strong data/code deposition (PXD073683, GitHub). Clarity and reproducibility reviewers find the work legible and largely replicable, with gaps (FragPipe parameter file, kNN k=10 justification and seed, artifact-stripping and canonical-mapping rules, hippocampal digestion details) that are documentation-level fixes.

The genuine split is over the 'dark epigenome' claim. Rigor (HARD), methodology, novelty, literature and clarity reviewers independently converge that the 58 succinylation / 31 glutarylation finding conflates enhanced technical detection with biological prevalence, is unvalidated orthogonally, is confined to one cell line, and — most substantively — is benchmarked against a propionylation protocol the authors themselves concede is suboptimal (ammonium-containing buffers, p.14). The skeptic's strongest surviving point is the methodology reviewer's formulation: the design cannot distinguish 'TMT detects sites propionylation misses' from 'both detect the same sites at different efficiency.' That critique survived the advocate's rebuttal intact.

What did not survive is the skeptic's inference that this requires new wet-lab work before acceptance. The advocate correctly notes that the confounded comparison undermines only the *scope* of the claim, not the underlying data: the labeling-efficiency, coverage, CV, speed, dual-protease-complementarity and NAM quantitation results stand on their own and are unaffected. The remedy is to restate the claim as what the experiment actually tested — TMT versus the widely used Garcia-type propionylation protocol under matched conditions — and to explicitly frame the acidic-acylation result as improved detection rather than discovery of hidden biology. That is a scoping and framing correction, and the panel's request for orthogonal PRM/synthetic-peptide validation is legitimately future work for a methods paper, not a precondition.

## Decisive Factors
The recommendation matches the confidence-weighted signal (4.15/5, 7 of 8 at 'minor'). Three factors drive it. First, the primary contribution — a systematic, reproducible, fast dual-protease workflow with documented coverage complementarity and sound quantitation — is not in dispute by any reviewer and is independent of the contested succinylation claim. Second, the one HARD criticism shared across rigor, methodology, novelty and clarity is a scope/attribution problem: the abstract's 'dark epigenome' framing asserts biological prevalence the design cannot support. Because the authors already acknowledge the comparator's limitations in the Discussion, aligning the abstract and Results with that acknowledgement is a rewriting task, not an experimental one. Third, ethics and reproducibility gaps are boilerplate/documentation items. Required at revision: (1) retitle/reframe the acidic-acylation finding as enhanced detection against the specific Garcia-type protocol, explicitly stating the design cannot separate novel-site detection from differential ionization efficiency, and confine prevalence language to HEK293T; (2) state whether the 58/31 sites are previously reported or novel, and report their replicate-level detection and localization confidence; (3) soften causal language on the charge-compensation mechanism to 'consistent with'; (4) add statistical testing or explicit hedging for the missed-cleavage motif enrichment; (5) resolve the Whedon et al. (2024) citation and the Paizs/Steen mobile-proton attribution, and justify excluding TMA given Ryzhaya et al. (2025); (6) supply FragPipe config, kNN rationale/seed/sensitivity, artifact-stripping and canonical-mapping rules, and hippocampal digestion details; (7) add COI, CRediT and AI-use statements; (8) discuss the 2–3-fold higher artifact rate under TMT as a limitation. I diverge from the skeptic's 'major' position because no proposed new experiment is needed to make the retained claims true — only to make a broader claim the authors should relinquish.
