# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The reference list is mostly complete and the attributions are accurate, with no fabricated citations detected. The central novelty claim—that TMT's tertiary amine rescues ionization of acidic acylations through charge compensation—rests on an uncontrolled comparison: labeling efficiency differs substantially between the TMT and propionylation workflows (98% vs 33–71%), leaving the mechanism ambiguous. Duplicate references inflate the count; some prior work on optimized propionylation could be more prominently positioned. These issues are fixable but affect how the contribution is framed.

## Strengths
- The reference list is mostly complete and the attributions are accurate, with no fabricated citations detected.
- Self-citation is proportionate — the Yates group appears on refs 1, 7, 23, 66, which is reasonable given the methodology lineage (shotgun proteomics, stepped HCD). No padding.
- Some prior work on optimized propionylation is already cited (e.g., Searfoss et al. 2023, ref 8).

## Weaknesses
- The claim that TMT's tertiary amine "provides charge compensation that rescues the ionization of negatively charged acylations" is not uniquely supported by the data presented. The evidence is a comparison in which TMT labeling achieves ~98% efficiency (by intensity) while propionylation achieves only 33–71% efficiency under the same hands (Fig. 4A). The alternative explanation—that the *higher overall labeling efficiency* of TMT simply makes more peptides (including low-abundance succinylated ones) detectable, without any specific charge-compensation mechanism—has not been excluded.
- The authors acknowledge the buffer incompatibility of the Garcia et al. protocol in a footnote (lines ~1050–1055), but they never present a condition where propionylation and TMT are matched for labeling efficiency and then compare acidic acylation yields.
- Duplicate references inflate the reference list quietly: refs 1/7 (Sidoli 2016), 30/68 (Maile 2015), and 41/44 (Bao 2019) are each listed twice under different numbers.
- The charge-compensation model would benefit from a supporting citation for the general principle that TMT's tertiary amine improves ionization of acidic peptides—the current citation (Thompson et al. 2003, ref 17) describes the TMT structure but not this property; the figure caption cites a Thermo manual but the text does not provide a verifiable reference for the claim.
- The mobile-proton-model attribution to Steen & Mann 2004 (ref 27) is broad—that review covers peptide sequencing generally; the specific mechanistic framing used here (proton sequestration by tertiary amine shifting b-ion formation) is not established there.
- Reference 36 (Jiang et al. 2026, *Biochem. J.*) is cited for "histone modification cross-talk tools and molecular mechanisms"—this paper is published in 2026; please confirm it appeared online by the submission date (or cite the preprint DOI if that is what was available).
- Reference 16 (Adoni et al. 2026, *J. Proteome Res.*) and reference 42 (Stransky et al. 2026, *Aging Cell*) are also 2026-dated; confirm they were published (or posted as preprints) at the time of submission, and if on a preprint server, disambiguate in the reference entry.
- The term "dark epigenome" is used for succinylation/glutarylation that propionylation missed, but similar phrasing has been used for other under-detected PTM classes (e.g., oncohistones, ref 3; formylation, ref 61). No citation problem, but consider a brief disclaimer to avoid appearing to coin it de novo.

## Questions
- In the TMT-vs-propionylation comparison for succinylation/glutarylation detection, what are the numbers if propionylation is performed in TEAB (pH 8–8.5) rather than in AMBIC, matching the buffer used for TMT? This one experiment would distinguish the labeling-efficiency confound from the charge-compensation hypothesis.
