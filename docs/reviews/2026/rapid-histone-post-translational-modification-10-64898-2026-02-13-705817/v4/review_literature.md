# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The related‑work and citation record is broadly sound but has two load‑bearing weaknesses that affect how the contribution can be evaluated. The comparison of TMT vs. propionylation for detecting acidic acylations confounds label chemistry with protease type and digestion workflow, weakening the central mechanistic claim that TMT's tertiary amine provides charge compensation. A directly relevant alternative‑digestion paper (Restellini et al., 2019) is missing from the reference list, and one cited reference (Adoni et al., 2026, ref 16) could not be confirmed as a published or preprint record. These are resolvable, and the manuscript would be stronger for addressing them.

## Weaknesses
- The "charge compensation" claim for TMT is not isolated from other confounds. The manuscript states that TMT's tertiary amine "rescues ionization of negatively charged acylations" and supports this with the observation that TMT‑labeled Arg‑C Ultra digests detected 58 succinylation and 31 glutarylation sites whereas propionylated Trypsin digests detected few. But TMT and propionylation here are confounded with protease (Arg‑C Ultra vs. Trypsin), digestion conditions (2 h vs. 6 h), search parameters (cleavage‑specificity for R‑only vs. KR), and labeling workflow (one post‑digestion round vs. two pre‑ plus two post‑digestion rounds). Any of these could cause the differential. The data needed to isolate the label effect are either absent or not presented as a side‑by‑side: a comparison of TMT‑labeled Arg‑C Ultra vs. propionylated Arg‑C Ultra (same protease, same digestion time, same missed‑cleavage profile) is the only design that would pin the observation on the label. As it stands, the manuscript presents a reasonable hypothesis but not a demonstrated mechanism.
- A directly relevant prior study on alternative protease digestion for clinical histone samples is not cited. Restellini et al. (2019, Proteomics Clin. Appl., PMID: 30471193) evaluated "Alternative digestion approaches improve histone modification mapping by mass spectrometry in clinical samples" — a title that directly overlaps the manuscript's central thesis. The abstract and metadata indicate this work explored multiple proteases for histone PTM analysis in clinical material. Whether it is strictly competing or complementary is not the point; a reader evaluating the novelty of RIPUP needs to know it exists, and the manuscript's introduction does not mention it. This is a soft omission that the authors should remedy by citing the work and stating how RIPUP differs from or extends it.
