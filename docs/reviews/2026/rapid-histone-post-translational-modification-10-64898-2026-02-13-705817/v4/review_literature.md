CONFIDENCE: 3

## Load-Bearing Claims

**Claim 1: TMT labeling provides a mechanistic advantage over propionylation for detecting negatively charged acylations (succinylation, glutarylation) via charge compensation from the tertiary amine.** The manuscript attributes this to the TMT reporter's tertiary amine sequestering a mobile proton, enhancing b-ion series and rescuing ionization of acidic peptides. The evidence is the observation that 58 succinylation and 31 glutarylation sites were detected in TMT-labeled samples but largely absent in propionylated ones. The alternative explanation is that TMT's larger mass and different hydrophobicity profile simply shift which peptides are selected for fragmentation in DDA, or that the stepped NCE (30/40/50) used for TMT-labeled peptides versus fixed 30% for unlabeled peptides produces different fragmentation outcomes. The manuscript does not isolate the charge-compensation mechanism from these confounds — a control comparing TMT-labeled peptides fragmented at fixed versus stepped NCE, or comparing TMT to an isobaric label lacking the tertiary amine, would distinguish the proposed mechanism from a fragmentation-energy artifact. The claim that this reveals a "dark epigenome" is therefore plausible but mechanistically under-supported as presented.

**Claim 2: Arg-C Ultra outperforms the conventional Trypsin + propionylation workflow for histone PTM analysis.** The evidence is higher peptide counts, lower missed-cleavage rates, and comparable PTM diversity. However, the comparison is confounded by the manuscript's own admission that propionylation efficiency in the "Trypsin + Prop" arm was incomplete — the authors searched Trypsin data with both R-only and KR cleavage specificity and found that ~58% of peptides were lost to cleavage at unlabeled K residues. This means the "Trypsin + Prop" comparator was operating at suboptimal labeling efficiency, and the authors acknowledge this in the Discussion (citing Meert et al. 2015, 2016). The claim that Arg-C Ultra "outperforms" Trypsin is therefore partly an artifact of the comparator's known inefficiency rather than a clean demonstration of Arg-C Ultra's superiority. The authors do acknowledge this, which is fair, but the framing in the abstract and results ("exceeds Trypsin-based approaches") overstates what the comparison can support.

**Claim 3: The dual-protease RIPUP workflow provides complementary sequence coverage enabling detection of PTMs in H2A variants, linker histones, and regions poorly represented by arginine-specific cleavage.** The evidence is the sequence coverage plots showing r-Chymotrypsin uniquely covering H2A.Z, H1 variants, and aromatic-rich regions. This claim is well-supported by the data presented and is the most defensible of the three load-bearing claims. The complementary coverage is clearly demonstrated in Figure 2D and SI Figure S1. The claim is appropriately scoped and does not overreach.

## Sweep

- **HARD — Directly competing work on Arg-C Ultra for histone analysis is cited (Ryzhaya et al. 2025, ref 10), and I verified this reference exists (PMID 40505065), but the manuscript's claim that Ryzhaya et al. used "trimethylacetic anhydride (TMA)" should be confirmed against the actual paper — the abstract I retrieved does not specify the derivatization agent, and the authors should verify this attribution is accurate rather than inferred.**

- **HARD — The manuscript cites Meert et al. 2015 and 2016 (refs 9, 12) for the claim that propionylation efficiency is variable and problematic, and acknowledges this in the Discussion ("the ammonium-containing buffers in this protocol introduce competing amine reactivity that reduces labeling efficiency relative to optimized approaches"). This is an honest and accurate acknowledgment, but the manuscript's own "Trypsin + Prop" comparator uses the same suboptimal buffer system, meaning the head-to-head comparison in Figure 2 is not against the best-available propionylation protocol. The authors should state this limitation more prominently in the Results, not just the Discussion.**

- **SOFT — The manuscript cites Vai et al. 2025 (ref 20) for the HiP-Frag workflow but does not cite the companion paper by Vai et al. 2024 (ref 24) on propionylation detection until later in the reference list. The 2024 paper is directly relevant to the manuscript's claim that endogenous propionylation is a real biological modification that propionylation-based workflows cannot distinguish from derivatization artifacts — this should be cited in the Introduction where the limitation is first raised, not only in the Discussion.**

- **SOFT — The claim that "TMT's tertiary amine provides charge compensation that rescues the ionization of negatively charged acylations" is presented as a mechanistic insight, but the manuscript does not cite prior work on TMT's effect on peptide ionization or fragmentation of acidic peptides. A search for prior literature on this specific mechanism returned no directly relevant results, suggesting this may be a novel observation — but the authors should state explicitly whether they are aware of any prior reports of TMT improving detection of acidic modifications, or whether this is a new finding.**

- **HARD — The reference list contains duplicate entries: refs 1 and 7 are the same Sidoli et al. 2016 paper (Journal of Visualized Experiments, "Complete Workflow for Analysis of Histone Post-Translational Modifications Using Bottom-up Mass Spectrometry"), and refs 30 and 68 are the same Maile et al. 2015 paper (Molecular & Cellular Proteomics, "Mass Spectrometric Quantification of Histone Post-Translational Modifications by a Hybrid Chemical Labeling Method"). These duplicates should be consolidated.**

- **SOFT — The manuscript cites ref 19 (Weyh et al. 2024, Nat. Chem.) for the claim that succinylation and glutarylation "destabilize nucleosome structure and promote chromatin accessibility" and that dysregulation is "implicated in cancer, protein-protein and DNA-protein interactions, and defective DNA repair." The Weyh paper is about genetic code expansion for studying succinylation/glutarylation function, which is relevant but may not be the primary source for the nucleosome destabilization claim — the manuscript also cites refs 39-41 (Zorro Shahidian et al., Jing et al., Bao et al.) which are more directly on point. The authors should verify that ref 19 actually supports the specific claim about nucleosome destabilization or move that citation to the more specific refs.**

- **SOFT — The manuscript's claim that "formylation of K, S, T, and Y residues was a prominent feature" and that "others have shown it to be an abundant and biologically important histone PTM on K" cites refs 61-63 (Wiśniewski et al. 2008, Jiang et al. 2007, Wiśniewski et al. 2007). These are appropriate primary sources. However, the manuscript also notes formylation "can arise as a sample preparation artifact" (ref 60, Zheng & Doucette 2016) — the authors should clarify whether their workflow uses formic acid at any step that could introduce this artifact, since the manuscript states "this was not a feature of our workflow" without specifying what steps were taken to exclude it.**

- **SOFT — The manuscript cites ref 16 (Adoni et al. 2026, J. Proteome Res.) for the recombinant chymotrypsin enzyme characterization. This is a very recent citation (2026) and I could not verify its existence through my searches. The authors should confirm this reference is published and correctly cited, as it is a load-bearing citation for the r-Chymotrypsin enzyme's properties.**

## Questions

1. Can the authors confirm that the Ryzhaya et al. (ref 10) paper specifically used trimethylacetic anhydride (TMA) as the derivatization agent, and not another reagent?

2. Can the authors confirm that the Adoni et al. (ref 16) paper on recombinant chymotrypsin is published and correctly cited, given its very recent date?

3. Can the authors clarify what specific steps in their workflow exclude formic acid or other sources of formylation artifacts, given that formylation was detected as a prominent feature?

4. Can the authors state whether the charge-compensation mechanism for TMT's effect on acidic acylation detection is a novel observation or whether prior literature exists that they are aware of?
