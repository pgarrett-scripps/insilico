# Novelty & Contribution Reviewer

## Summary
The paper's two central novelty claims—(i) that TMT's tertiary amine provides charge compensation enabling detection of negatively charged acylations (succinylation, glutarylation) largely missed by propionylation workflows, and (ii) that the dual-protease RIPUP combination (Arg-C Ultra + r-Chymotrypsin) yields complementary histone sequence coverage within a 3-hour preparation window—are both real and not preempted by published or preprint work I could find. The TMT charge-compensation mechanism in particular is, to my knowledge, a genuinely new mechanistic insight that goes beyond simply noting that TMT labels something differently. The paper's strength is the systematic head-to-head comparison (10 conditions, 40 samples), which will be useful as a community benchmark. The main novelty weakness is insufficiently sharp differentiation from the very recent Ryzhaya et al. (2025, *Anal. Chem.*) paper, which achieves a similar time saving with Arg-C Ultra + TMA derivatization and is cited but not directly benchmarked against. The "dark epigenome" framing overstates the finding relative to prior literature (Zhang et al. 2011, Xie et al. 2012, Tan et al. 2014).

## Weaknesses
- The RIPUP workflow's novelty over Ryzhaya et al. (2025) is asserted but not demonstrated by direct comparison. Ryzhaya et al. (2025, *Anal. Chem.* 97(24):12486–12492) already showed that Arg-C Ultra with a single post-digestion derivatization (trimethylacetic anhydride, TMA) reduces histone sample preparation to ~3–4 hours. RIPUP (Arg-C Ultra ± r-Chymotrypsin, optionally TMT-labeled) is clearly a sibling approach rather than a parent. The manuscript acknowledges Ryzhaya et al. in a citation but provides no head-to-head comparison of RIPUP versus the TMA workflow on the same cell line, same instrument, same day. Without that, a referee cannot assess whether RIPUP's use of TMT over TMA or the addition of r-Chymotrypsin yields a quantitatively meaningful improvement in PTM coverage, or whether the differences are small and sample-dependent. This is a HARD issue for the novelty claim because the delta over the nearest neighbor is the entire remaining novelty of the workflow. The authors should either (a) provide a direct side-by-side experiment (RIPUP vs . Ryzhaya protocol on the same sample )or (b) sharply delimit what RIPUP adds that Ryzhaya's protocol lacks , and concede that the core rapid—Arg-C idea is not new .
- The "dark epigenome" claim for succinylation and glutarylation is overstated. Histone lysine succinylation was identified in 2011 (Zhang et al. 2011, *Nat. Chem. Biol.* 7:58–63) and histone glutarylation in 2014 (Tan et al. 2014, *Cell Metab.* 19:605–617); both have been repeatedly detected by MS in multiple cell types since then . The manuscript's contribution is detecting *more* sites (58 succinylation, 31 glutarylation) and offering a mechanistic explanation for why propionylation misses them — which is genuinely valuable . But "dark epigenome" implies these marks were invisible , whereas they were merely underrepresented in propionylation —based workflows . This is a SOFT framing issue : the authors should replace "dark epigenome" with a quantitative claim (e.g ., "58 succinylation sites not detected by propionylation in parallel analysis ") that does not erase the existing literature.
- The r-Chymotrypsin results are presented as complementary coverage, but no metric quantifies how many PTM sites are exclusive to r-Chymotrypsin versus accessible to Arg-C Ultra at a longer digest or with different missed-cleavage settings.
- The NAM quantitative experiment uses an unconventional normalization approach (histone-level scaling) rather than the widely used peptide-family-ratio method; the choice is defended, but not benchmarked against the standard approach on the same data, so the novelty of the quantitation pipeline is hard to assess.
- No comparison with the hybrid chemical labeling (PIC-TMT) of Maille et al. (2015, *Mol. Cell. Proteomics* 14:1148–1158) is offered, even though that earlier work also used TMT on histones and could have detected similar acylation enhancements without noting the mechanism.
- The claim that formylation is "not a feature of our workflow" (Discussion) is unsupported—no control experiment (e.g., labeling with isotopically labeled formaldehyde) is presented to exclude it as an artifact.
- The missed-cleavage motif analysis (SI Figures S6–S7) is interesting but the sample size is too small to draw generalizable conclusions about Arg-C Ultra / r-Chymotrypsin specificity rules.
- The per-sample reagent-cost table (SI Table S5) is useful but does not include the cost of the TMT-zero reagent needed for the non-multiplexed labeling the paper advocates, leaving the cost comparison incomplete.
