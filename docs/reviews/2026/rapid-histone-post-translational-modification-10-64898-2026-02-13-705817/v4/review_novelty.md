# Novelty & Contribution Reviewer

## Summary
The central empirical observation — that TMT labeling detects substantially more succinylation and glutarylation sites than propionylation — is new and potentially important, but its explanation as a specific "charge compensation" mechanism is over-interpreted from the data presented. The dual-protease RIPUP workflow is a useful incremental advance over the closely related Arg-C Ultra + TMA protocol reported by Ryzhaya et al. (Anal. Chem. 2025), but the manuscript does not adequately differentiate itself from that work. The systematic comparison across 10 conditions is well-executed but largely confirms what Ryzhaya already reported for Arg-C Ultra. The contribution is real, modestly incremental, and would benefit from sharper positioning and a more cautious mechanistic claim.

## Weaknesses
- The "charge compensation" mechanism for enhanced succinylation/glutarylation detection is asserted, not demonstrated. The evidence offered (TMT detects 58 succinylation and 31 glutarylation sites vs very few for propionylation) does not isolate charge compensation from other consequences of differential labeling efficiency. No charge-state comparisons or comparison against a high-efficiency label without a tertiary amine are provided.
- The delta over the closest prior work (Ryzhaya et al. 2025) is stated but its magnitude is overstated. The two genuinely new elements (r-Chymotrypsin as a second protease, TMT as a labeling agent) are not compared head-to-head against Ryzhaya's TMA-based approach, so the reader cannot assess whether RIPUP is an improvement or merely a variation.
- The "first systematic evaluation" framing is partially preempted by Ryzhaya et al.'s own systematic comparison; the contribution should be positioned as an extension rather than as the first such evaluation.
- The TMT-derived succinylation and glutarylation sites (58 and 31) are reported as absolute counts without false-discovery estimation specific to these modification classes; a class-level FDR or manual validation of a representative subset is needed.
- The manuscript lacks a direct performance comparison (PTM counts, labeling efficiency, workflow time) between TMT and the TMA-based approach of Ryzhaya — the most directly relevant benchmark.
- The rat hippocampal experiment is presented as 'proof-of-concept' but it is unclear from the text whether this preparation was unlabeled or TMT-labeled; the abstract mentions TMT in connection with HEK293T but the hippocampal workflow description says 'unlabeled DDA-LFQ' (Methods).
- The terms 'dark epigenome' and 'largely undetected by propionylation-based methods' are rhetorically strong for a comparison against a protocol whose own authors acknowledge suboptimal labeling efficiency.

## Questions
- Can the authors provide direct evidence (e.g., charge-state distribution comparison or comparison against a high-efficiency label lacking a tertiary amine) that distinguishes the proposed charge-compensation mechanism from a general efficiency-driven improvement?
