# Novelty & Contribution Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript presents RIPUP, a multi-protease workflow combining Arg-C Ultra and r-Chymotrypsin with TMT labeling for rapid histone PTM analysis. The core novelty claims are: (1) systematic comparison of alternative proteases vs. Trypsin; (2) TMT as a superior alternative to propionylation for histone PTM analysis; (3) discovery of a "dark epigenome" of succinylation and glutarylation sites via TMT's charge compensation mechanism. Claims (1) and (3) are genuine and well-supported; claim (2) is partially preempted by concurrent work (Ryzhaya et al. 2025 on Arg-C Ultra; Vai et al. 2025 on HiP-Frag). The manuscript appropriately acknowledges these neighbors but does not clearly articulate its delta over them. The succinylation/glutarylation finding is the strongest novelty claim and is mechanistically well-explained, though validation scope is limited to one cell line.

## Strengths
- Systematic, large-scale comparison: The evaluation across 10 distinct conditions (3 proteases × propionylation/urea/TMT variants) on 40 samples is comprehensive and provides genuine empirical data on protease performance in histone PTM analysis—a gap in the literature.
- Mechanistic insight into TMT advantage: The explanation of how TMT's tertiary amine sequesters mobile protons and enhances b-ion formation (Figure 3, mobile proton model) is novel and well-articulated, providing a chemical rationale for why TMT rescues ionization of negatively charged acylations.
- "Dark epigenome" discovery is substantial: Detection of 58 succinylation and 31 glutarylation sites in HEK293T cells, with the claim that these have been "systematically under-detected" by propionylation workflows, is a significant finding with a mechanistic explanation for why prior methods missed these modifications.
- Dual-protease complementarity is well-demonstrated: Figure 2D and SI Figure S1 clearly show that r-Chymotrypsin provides superior coverage of H2A variants (>95%) and linker histones (H1), while Arg-C Ultra dominates N-terminal tails—this orthogonal coverage is genuinely useful.
- Rapid turnaround validated in tissue: The proof-of-concept on rat hippocampal sections (3-hour workflow, 231 unique PTM sites) demonstrates practical applicability beyond cell culture.
- Quantitative rigor: The NAM dose-response experiment (Figure 7) with proper statistical testing (limma, Benjamini-Hochberg correction) and dose-response concordance (r = 0.803 for Arg-C Ultra) shows the workflow is quantitatively sound.

## Weaknesses
- Arg-C Ultra comparison is not fully novel: Ryzhaya et al. (2025, ref. 10) already demonstrated that Arg-C Ultra with post-digestion derivatization (TMA) reduces histone sample prep to 3–4 hours. The manuscript's delta is the addition of TMT labeling and r-Chymotrypsin, not Arg-C Ultra per se.
- TMT for histone PTM analysis is not entirely novel: The manuscript acknowledges that TMT application to histone PTM analysis has been suggested and demonstrated elsewhere (refs. 30–32), with the contribution being the first direct comparison to propionylation and mechanistic explanation, not the first use.
- HiP-Frag computational framework is not the authors' contribution: Vai et al. (2025, ref. 20) developed HiP-Frag for unrestricted PTM identification. The manuscript uses HiP-Frag but does not advance the computational method itself, meaning the succinylation/glutarylation discovery is enabled by HiP-Frag's open search rather than RIPUP's sample prep alone.
- Succinylation/glutarylation validation limited to one cell line: The manuscript acknowledges systematic comparisons were performed in a single cell line (HEK293T), and while broader applicability is demonstrated through hippocampal tissue analysis, the "dark epigenome" claim would be stronger with validation across multiple cell types.
- No synthetic validation of novel succinylation/glutarylation sites: The 58 succinylation and 31 glutarylation sites remain unvalidated by orthogonal methods (e.g., synthetic peptides, antibodies, or targeted PRM), which is a notable gap for a "dark epigenome" claim.
- Quantitative advantage of TMT over propionylation not directly demonstrated: Figure 4A shows TMT labeling efficiency exceeds propionylation, but the manuscript does not show head-to-head quantitative comparison of the same peptidoforms labeled with TMT vs. propionylation in the same samples. The NAM experiment uses unlabeled Arg-C Ultra, not TMT-labeled.
- Missed cleavage analysis incomplete: The manuscript reports motif analysis for missed R cleavage but does not systematically compare missed cleavage rates between Arg-C Ultra and r-Chymotrypsin under the same conditions. Figure 2C shows Arg-C Ultra has ~84% peptides with 0 missed cleavages vs. ~20–25% for r-Chymotrypsin, but the reasons for this large difference are not mechanistically explored.

## Questions
- On Ryzhaya et al. (2025) differentiation: The manuscript cites Ryzhaya et al. as demonstrating Arg-C Ultra + TMA achieves 3–4 hour prep time, while RIPUP achieves 3 hours with Arg-C Ultra + TMT. Is the speed improvement (1 hour) the primary delta, or is it the mechanistic insight into TMT's charge compensation? Please clarify the specific advance over Ryzhaya et al. in the main text.
- On succinylation/glutarylation prevalence: How many of the 58 succinylation and 31 glutarylation sites are on the same peptides as other modifications (i.e., multiply modified peptidoforms)? Are these sites enriched at specific histone regions (e.g., N-terminal tails vs. globular domains), or distributed broadly?
- On charge compensation mechanism: Did the authors observe the predicted mobile proton sequestration in the MS/MS spectra? A quantitative comparison of b-ion vs. y-ion abundance in TMT-labeled vs. propionylated peptides would directly support the mechanism.
- On quantitative comparison: Why not compare quantitative accuracy (e.g., fold-change precision, CV) between TMT-labeled and propionylated samples in the same NAM dose-response experiment? This would directly test the claim that TMT offers superior quantitative accuracy.
- On hippocampal validation: In Figure 8, are succinylation and glutarylation sites detected in all 5 animals, or only a subset? The manuscript states "peptidoforms retained if detected in ≥2 replicates," but does not report the frequency of these acidic acylations across animals.
- On r-Chymotrypsin specificity: Is the stated specificity of r-Chymotrypsin for F, Y, and L (with no cleavage after W) a known property of recombinant Chymotrypsin, or a novel finding? If known, cite the source; if novel, provide evidence.
- On FDR and variable modification space: How many variable modifications were declared in the HiP-Frag searches? Is there a risk that the "dark epigenome" is an artifact of expanded search space rather than a genuine biological phenomenon?
