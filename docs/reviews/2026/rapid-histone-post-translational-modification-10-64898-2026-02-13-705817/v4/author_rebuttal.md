## Concessions
- **rigor** — 'Dark epigenome' framing conflates enhanced technical detection with proof of increased biological prevalence of succinylation/glutarylation
  - Proposed change: Revise the Abstract and Results ('Enhanced identification of histone succinylation and glutarylation with TMT') to replace 'dark epigenome' with 'enhanced detection of acidic acylations' and add an explicit statement that the design shows improved detection relative to the widely-used Garcia-type propionylation protocol, not proof that these sites were biologically unknown or systematically underestimated across cell types; confine prevalence language explicitly to HEK293T cells.
- **methodology** — The design cannot distinguish 'TMT detects real sites propionylation misses' from 'both detect the same sites at different ionization efficiency'
  - Proposed change: Add a sentence to the Results/Discussion explicitly stating this limitation, and reframe the succinylation/glutarylation finding as a differential-ionization-efficiency effect consistent with the charge-compensation mechanism, rather than as discovery of previously unknown sites.
- **literature** — Reference 31 (Whedon et al. 2024) is cited for TMT-based histone H3 middle-down analysis but its title suggests a sortase-based method, indicating possible misattribution
  - Proposed change: Verify the Whedon et al. 2024 citation against its actual content and either replace it with the correct reference for TMT-based histone middle-down proteomics or remove the citation if no such comparison exists in that paper.
- **literature** — Ambiguous dual-citation of Paizs & Suhai (2005) and Steen & Mann (2004) for the mobile proton model underlying the TMT mechanism
  - Proposed change: Restructure the citation in the 'Chemical rationale for TMT labeling' section to attribute the mobile proton model primarily to Paizs & Suhai (2005) and cite Steen & Mann (2004) separately for general fragment-ion nomenclature/behavior.
- **literature** — No explicit justification for excluding TMA (Ryzhaya et al. 2025) from the systematic comparison despite its similar 3-4h timeline
  - Proposed change: Add a sentence in the Introduction or Discussion explicitly justifying the exclusion of TMA from the main comparison (e.g., reagent availability, scope of study focused on TMT vs propionylation) and note that a future head-to-head with TMA would be valuable.
- **rigor** — Causal language ('is explained by') for the TMT charge-compensation mechanism overstates inference from fragmentation pattern correlation
  - Proposed change: Change 'is explained by' to 'is consistent with' throughout the succinylation/glutarylation mechanism discussion (Results and Discussion sections).
- **rigor** — Missed cleavage motif enrichment (SI Figures S6-S7) is presented without statistical testing
  - Proposed change: Add a Fisher's exact test or permutation test with multiple-testing correction for the motif enrichment analysis, or explicitly hedge the language to 'consistent with enrichment of D/E near missed cleavage sites' if statistical testing is not performed.
- **rigor** — 2-3 fold higher artifact rates (Met oxidation, dehydration) with TMT labeling are not discussed as a limitation
  - Proposed change: Add a paragraph to the Limitations section discussing the increased artifact rate under TMT labeling and its potential impact on PTM localization confidence and quantification.
- **ethics** — No competing interests, authorship contribution, or AI-tool-use statement provided
  - Proposed change: Add explicit Competing Interests ('none declared'), CRediT author contribution statement, and AI-tool-use disclosure sections to the manuscript.
- **reproducibility** — FragPipe search configuration, kNN imputation rationale/seed, artifact-stripping algorithm, canonical-mapping rules, and hippocampal digestion conditions (histone input mass, propionylation status) are underspecified
  - Proposed change: Deposit the full FragPipe params.txt/workflow file alongside the raw data on PRIDE, add a paragraph to SI Methods specifying k=10 rationale and any sensitivity analysis performed, document the exact artifact-stripping and canonical-mapping decision rules (with an example table), and add histone input mass and propionylation status for the hippocampal RIPUP digestions to the Methods.
- **clarity** — IHP definition, peptidoform terminology, and normalization method are introduced without adequate main-text explanation
  - Proposed change: Add a one-sentence definition of 'informative histone peptides (IHP)' and 'peptidoform' at first use in the main text (not only in figure legends/Methods), and add 2-3 sentences in the Results summarizing the histone-level normalization logic before Figure 7.

## Disagreements
- **skeptic/methodology** — The confounded comparison to a suboptimal propionylation protocol requires new wet-lab experiments (head-to-head against optimized propionylation, orthogonal PRM/synthetic-peptide validation) before the paper's conclusions can be accepted
  - Response: We agree the scope of the succinylation/glutarylation claim must be narrowed (see concessions), but the request for new experiments as a precondition for acceptance goes beyond what is needed to support the claims we actually make. Our core, load-bearing contributions—digestion efficiency, sequence coverage complementarity, labeling efficiency, reproducibility (CVs), and NAM dose-response quantitation—do not depend on the propionylation comparator being 'optimal'; they are self-contained comparisons across our own conditions. The succinylation/glutarylation finding, reframed as differential detection under matched HiP-Frag search conditions, remains a valid and useful methodological observation even without antibody/PRM validation, which we explicitly flag as future work.
  - Manuscript reference: Discussion, 'Practical Considerations'/labeling-efficiency section: 'While the Garcia et al. propionylation protocol was selected as the comparator given its dominant adoption in the field (291 citations), we note that the ammonium-containing buffers in this protocol introduce competing amine reactivity that reduces labeling efficiency relative to optimized approaches... The TMT advantage apparent in Figure 4A should therefore be interpreted as reflecting performance relative to the widely used protocol rather than against the best achievable propionylation efficiency.'
- **rigor** — Rat hippocampus proof-of-concept lacks biological replication because n=5 animals sampled the same coronal region are 'technical' not independent replicates
  - Response: The n=5 animals are independent biological replicates (separate rats, separate hippocampal dissections), not technical replicates of a single tissue sample; each animal contributes one biological hippocampal extraction subjected to one RIPUP digestion. The consistency requirement (peptidoforms retained in ≥2/5 replicates) was applied precisely to guard against idiosyncratic single-animal detections. We agree the study is intended as a proof-of-concept for workflow speed/coverage rather than a biological discovery study, and we do not claim functional validation of individual PTM sites in tissue.
  - Manuscript reference: Methods, 'Adult male Sprague-Dawley rats used in this study... (n = 5, 446 ± 17.8 g)... hippocampi were isolated and collected in 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tubes' and Data processing: 'For histones extracted from frozen-thawed rat hippocampal sections processed using the RIPUP protocol, peptidoforms were retained if detected in ≥2 replicates.'
- **novelty** — The manuscript's delta over Ryzhaya et al. (2025) and Vai et al. (2025) is unclear, since Arg-C Ultra speed gains and the HiP-Frag search framework are not the authors' own contributions
  - Response: We do not claim novelty for Arg-C Ultra digestion kinetics or the HiP-Frag search algorithm themselves; our stated contribution is the systematic, quantitative comparison of labeling chemistries (propionylation vs. TMT) combined with a dual-protease strategy, and the mechanistic explanation (charge compensation via TMT's tertiary amine) for why labeling chemistry biases PTM class detection—an axis neither Ryzhaya et al. nor Vai et al. addressed. This is stated explicitly in our framing of the research gap.
  - Manuscript reference: Background: 'the key question of whether different sample preparation and chemical labeling strategies preferentially detect certain PTM classes remain unaddressed and has implications for potentially biasing our view of the epigenetic landscape... our analysis revealed that TMT labeling provides unique advantages for detecting negatively charged acylations through charge compensation at the peptide N-terminus.'
