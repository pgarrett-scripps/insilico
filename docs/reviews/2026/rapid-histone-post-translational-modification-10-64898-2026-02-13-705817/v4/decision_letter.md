# Decision Letter

**Decision:** major

## Summary of Evaluation
The panel's numerical signal (3.25/5, 6 major + 2 minor) and the editorial debate converge on major revision. The empirical findings — the systematic 10-condition benchmark, the TMT enhancement of acidic-acylation detection, and the dual-protease complementarity — are genuinely useful and the manuscript is a contender for In Silico. However, the two load-bearing claims outrun their evidence. The charge-compensation mechanism is confounded with stepped vs fixed NCE and with the large labeling-efficiency gap between TMT and propionylation; the design does not isolate the label from fragmentation energy or labeling extent. The NAM quantitative analysis has an acknowledged cleavage-redistribution confound and its 112-significant-peptidoforms headline conflates Arg-C Ultra alone with the full RIPUP workflow. The 'Arg-C outperforms Trypsin' comparison is ambiguous about which search (R-only vs KR) anchors Figure 2C. The methods audits identified three HARD completeness gaps (cell-line authentication, random-seed declaration, animal-study randomization/blinding) and citation ambiguities (TMT property claims attributed to succinylation papers; '291 citations' figure; DOI formatting artifacts). None of these problems requires new wet-lab experiments — the mechanism test could be done by re-analyzing existing data with fixed-NCE or matched-labeling comparisons; the NAM robustness check is a re-analysis; the remaining issues are disclosure, hedging, and housekeeping. That is the definition of major revision. The advocate's claim that 'every criticism is addressable in revision' is correct for a major revision standard, but the skeptic correctly notes that as written the claims are not yet earned. I adopt the majority verdict.
