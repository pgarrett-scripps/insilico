# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
This is a genuinely useful methodological contribution: the dual-protease RIPUP workflow demonstrably shortens preparation time and broadens sequence coverage, and the TMT charge-compensation finding, if it survives normalization, is a real advance for studying acidic acylations. The authors also do something rare and admirable: they identify the missed-cleavage redistribution problem created by NAM treatment and refuse to paper over it with the standard peptide-family ratio. But the two headline quantitative results — the 112 significant NAM peptidoforms and the succinylation/glutarylation enhancement — both rest on analyses with a replicate-unit ambiguity and, respectively, a potential pseudoreplication problem and a normalized-site-count gap that the manuscript never closes. Both are fixable, but the numbers as stated outrun the evidence as presented.

## Weaknesses
- The TMT "ionization rescue" claim is supported only by raw site counts, not by a tested rate.
- The "112 statistically significant peptidoforms" may rest on pseudoreplication.
- The tested set is a non-random subset, and the largest treatment effect is excluded by it.
- Per-histone total normalization imposes a constant sum; "24 down" may be partly an artefact of "88 up."
