# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

SCORE: 3
CONFIDENCE: 4

## Summary

This is a method-development paper with a strong quantitative core: it compares 10 digestion/labeling conditions for histone PTM analysis, reports a novel TMT advantage for acidic acylations, and presents a quantitative NAM dose-response experiment. The statistical handling is uneven — some parts are exemplary (the kNN imputation restriction, the explicit statement of the missed-cleavage redistribution problem), but several headline claims lack the stated test, n, or definition of variability that would make them checkable. The central quantitative claims — the TMT succinylation/glutarylation enrichment, the 112 significant peptidoforms, and the digestion-efficiency comparisons — are each missing at least one element that a referee needs to verify them. The paper is publishable in principle, but the numbers as presented do not yet support the strength of the claims made.

## Strengths

1. The authors explicitly identify and work around the missed-cleavage redistribution confound in the NAM experiment, choosing a peptidoform-level analysis over a peptide-family approach that would have been artifactually biased — this is exactly the kind of design thinking that should be standard.
2. The labeling-efficiency analysis distinguishes site-based from intensity-based metrics and reports both, which is more transparent than the field typically provides.
3. The TMT charge-compensation hypothesis for acidic acylation detection is mechanistically plausible and is supported by the observation that the effect concentrates in exactly the peptides where a tertiary amine would rescue ionization.

## Weaknesses (load-bearing)

**1. The 112 significant peptidoforms claim is not traceable to a test.** The text reports "112 statistically significant peptidoforms (adj p < 0.05)" from the limma analysis, but the Methods describe a cell-means model with contrasts for 3 mM vs 0 and 10 mM vs 0. It is unclear whether the 112 is the union of both contrasts, the intersection, or the count at one dose. The n is stated (3 digestion replicates per dose), but the independent unit of replication is ambiguous: are these three independent cell-culture dishes, or three aliquots from one dish? If the latter, the effective n is 1 and the entire inference collapses. The manuscript says cells were "divided into 3 x 5 mL aliquots" and plated in "3 x 5 mL aliquots" — this reads as technical replicates from a single culture, which would make the limma analysis pseudo-replication. This needs an explicit statement of whether the three replicates are independent biological replicates. The one thing that would settle it: state the culture and treatment design explicitly — were the three NAM-treated dishes derived from three independent cultures or one split culture?

**2. The TMT succinylation/glutarylation enrichment claim lacks a denominator.** The paper reports "58 succinylation and 31 glutarylation sites" detected with TMT labeling, and claims this exceeds what propionylation-based methods detect. But the comparison is not quantified: how many succinylation sites were detected in the propionylated conditions? The paper shows a bar chart (Figure 5B) but does not report the raw counts for the comparator conditions. Without the denominator — total succinylation sites detected across all conditions — the claim that TMT "reveals" these sites cannot be distinguished from the possibility that the search parameters or the HiP-Frag workflow itself are responsible. The strongest form of this concern: the authors attribute the effect to TMT's tertiary amine, but they did not test the alternative that the effect is driven by the stepped NCE (30/40/50) used for TMT samples versus the fixed 30% for unlabeled samples. The fragmentation energy difference alone could explain enhanced b-ion coverage and thus improved site localization. The one thing that would settle it: run the same TMT-labeled samples with fixed 30% NCE and report whether succinylation identifications persist.

**3. The digestion-efficiency comparisons (Figure 2C) are presented without error bars or statistical tests.** The claim that "Arg-C Ultra yielded substantially higher peptide identifications than the conventional 'Trypsin + Prop' approach" rests on counts of 217 vs 179 peptides, but there is no variance estimate across the n=4 replicates, no test, and no statement of whether the difference is stable. Given that peptide identification counts in DDA are notoriously stochastic, a difference of 38 peptides could easily be within run-to-run noise. The one thing that would settle it: report the per-replicate peptide counts with the median and IQR, and state whether the difference survives a paired test across the four replicates.

## Sweep

- The CV analysis (Figure 2A) reports median CVs < 5% but does not state the distribution or the number of peptides contributing to each median; a median over 50 peptides is not comparable to a median over 500.
- The kNN imputation is described as restricted to groups with ≥2 of 3 measured values, which is good, but the manuscript does not state how many peptidoforms were excluded for having <2 values in any dose group — this is a missing denominator for the 112 claim.
- The "informative histone peptides" definition (≤1 missed cleavage, ≥3 of 4 replicates, no artifacts) is reasonable, but the proportion metric in Figure 4B is computed against a denominator that includes peptides that would never be informative (e.g., those with 2+ missed cleavages), which inflates the apparent advantage of conditions with fewer total peptides.
- The dose-response correlation (r = 0.803 for Arg-C Ultra) is reported without a confidence interval or a test against a null of zero; with 342 peptidoforms, the correlation is trivially significant, and the more informative statistic — the proportion of peptidoforms that change in the expected direction at both doses — is not reported.
- The claim that "TMT-labeling efficiency by intensity was ~98%" is computed from peptides that were identified and passed filtering; this is a conditional efficiency that does not account for peptides lost entirely due to failed labeling, which would bias the estimate upward.
- The missed-cleavage motif analysis (SI Figures S6–S7) reports enrichment of D at P1′ and E at P2 but does not state the background model or the test used to call enrichment; without a stated null, these are descriptive observations.
- The rat hippocampal experiment reports 231 unique PTM sites from n=5 animals but does not state whether the five animals are independent biological replicates or sections from the same animal — the Methods say "n = 5, 446 ± 17.8 g" which implies five animals, but the CV analysis pools across them without stating whether the CV is within-animal or between-animal.

## Questions

1. Are the three NAM-treated replicates independent biological replicates (separate cultures) or technical replicates from a single split culture? This determines whether the effective n for the limma analysis is 3 or 1.
2. What were the succinylation and glutarylation site counts in the propionylated conditions (Arg-C Ultra + Prop, Trypsin + Prop) that the TMT comparison is made against?
3. Was the stepped NCE (30/40/50) used for TMT samples the only fragmentation difference, or were any unlabeled samples also run with stepped NCE as a control?
4. For the 112 significant peptidoforms, how many were significant at 3 mM only, 10 mM only, and both? And how many peptidoforms were excluded from testing entirely due to missing data in any dose group?
