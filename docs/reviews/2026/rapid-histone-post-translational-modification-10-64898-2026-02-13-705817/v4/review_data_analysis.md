# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
The paper's three load-bearing quantitative claims are: (1) TMT labeling reveals a "dark epigenome" of succinylation/glutarylation via charge compensation; (2) RIPUP quantifies 112 significant peptidoform changes in NAM-treated cells; (3) TMT labeling is more efficient than propionylation. Claim 1 is confounded with enzyme choice in the presented comparison; claim 2 is vulnerable to cleavage-redistribution artifacts the authors themselves document; claim 3 is directionally robust but rests on biased estimators. The systematic 10-condition comparison with stated n and CVs is a solid foundation, and the authors pre-empt several obvious objections honestly. Major revision.

## Strengths
- The systematic comparison across 10 conditions with n = 4 replicates per condition and reported CVs is a sound basis for the workflow claims.
- The authors disclose rather than hide the main confounds in their own data — the one-round vs two-round propionylation issue, the Trypsin R-only vs KR search restriction, and the 259 NAM-exclusive peptidoforms that forced rejection of family-ratio quantitation.
- The decision to report per-peptidoform quantitation with explicit disclosure of the cleavage-redistribution problem is honest, even if the alternative approach does not fully solve it.

## Weaknesses
- 1. (HARD) The "dark epigenome" claim confounds enzyme with label. The headline comparison (Figure 5B) contrasts TMT-labeled peptides from Arg-C Ultra + r-Chymotrypsin pooled against propionylated tryptic peptides. Nine of the reported succinylation sites are in H1.4, a region only r-Chymotrypsin covers — Trypsin + Prop cannot detect them regardless of label. The clean comparison, Arg-C Ultra + TMT vs Arg-C Ultra + Prop (same enzyme, different label), is not shown, so the reader cannot tell whether the succinylation enrichment is a TMT effect or a coverage effect. The mechanism claim (TMT's tertiary amine rescues ionization) is also not distinguished from the alternative that TMT's enhanced b-ion series improves PTM localization scores, letting the same succinylated peptides pass the 1% FDR threshold. Both mechanisms produce the same observed result. Report succinylation/glutarylation site counts for Arg-C + TMT vs Arg-C + Prop, and separate ionization effects (MS1 intensity of succinylated peptides) from identification effects (localization scores).
- 2. (HARD) The NAM quantitative claims do not separate net PTM change from cleavage redistribution. The authors document that NAM induces missed-cleavage redistribution (259 peptidoforms exclusive to NAM-treated samples) and reject family-ratio quantitation for this reason. But their per-peptidoform approach has the same problem in reverse: a peptidoform's intensity can rise because a PTM blocks cleavage at a neighboring site, shifting signal from a shorter to a longer peptide with no net change in PTM occupancy. The 88 "up" peptidoforms could partly reflect this. For each significant "up" peptidoform, sum the intensities of all peptidoforms (across cleavage states) covering the same modification site and test whether the summed signal increases; this distinguishes net increase from redistribution.
- 3. (SOFT) The labeling-efficiency comparison (TMT ~99% vs propionylation ~X%) uses an MS2 reporter-ion readout for TMT but an MS1-intensity readout for propionylation — the two metrics are not directly comparable.
