# Ethics & Compliance Reviewer

## Summary
This manuscript describes a methodological study on histone post-translational modification (PTM) analysis using mass spectrometry. From an ethics and compliance perspective, the work is well-documented. Animal research involving rat hippocampal tissue is properly approved (IACUC #09-0006 named with protocol number), humane endpoints and welfare considerations are clearly stated, and the study adheres to ARRIVE guidelines. Human cell culture (HEK293T) requires no special ethics approval. Funding sources are disclosed (NIH grants listed in Acknowledgements). No competing interests statement is explicitly provided, though none are apparent from the text. Data and code availability are appropriately addressed with ProteomeXchange deposition and GitHub repository links. No human subjects, clinical trials, dual-use biosafety concerns, indigenous samples, or secondary biobank data are involved. The manuscript is methodologically focused and does not raise cross-cutting compliance red flags.

## Strengths
- Animal research approval is explicitly named with protocol number (IACUC #09-0006) and institution (Scripps Research Institute), meeting HARD requirements.
- Humane endpoints and welfare considerations are clearly documented: anesthesia (isoflurane 3%), rapid decapitation, and appropriate housing conditions (temperature-, humidity-controlled, 12 h reverse light cycle, ad libitum food/water) are stated.
- ARRIVE guidelines compliance is explicitly acknowledged in the Methods section.
- Funding sources are fully disclosed in the Acknowledgements section (multiple NIH grants: T32 AA007456, AA013498, P60 AA006420, AA017447, AA029841, AA021491, and Schimmel Family Chair).
- Data availability is comprehensive: MS raw files, annotations, SDRF-Proteomics metadata, and FragPipe search results deposited to ProteomeXchange (PXD073683); custom R scripts available on GitHub.
- No human subjects, identifiable data, clinical trials, or dual-use biosafety concerns are present, eliminating multiple conditional ethics triggers.

## Weaknesses
- No explicit competing interests or conflicts of interest statement is provided. While none appear evident, best practice in peer-reviewed publication requires an explicit declaration (even if 'none declared').
- Authorship contributions are not explicitly stated. While the author list and affiliations are clear, a formal statement of who performed which roles (conceptualization, methodology, data analysis, writing, etc.) would strengthen transparency.
- AI-tool usage is not acknowledged. The manuscript does not disclose whether any AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Copilot) were used in writing, analysis, or figure generation, which is increasingly expected.

## Questions
- Please provide an explicit competing interests or conflicts of interest statement, even if the declaration is 'none declared.'
- Would the authors consider adding a brief authorship contributions statement (e.g., following CRediT taxonomy) to clarify roles?
- Were any artificial intelligence tools or large language models used in manuscript preparation, data analysis, or figure generation? If so, please disclose their use and role.
- The IACUC protocol number is provided, but can the authors confirm that all animal procedures were conducted in accordance with the National Institutes of Health Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals, as stated? (This appears to be the case, but explicit confirmation would be helpful.)
