## Advocate — round 1

I'm ready to make the case for acceptance. Let me structure the argument around the manuscript's genuine strengths while addressing the most serious criticisms raised by the panel.

**Genuine strengths**

1. **The TMT charge-compensation finding is a real empirical discovery, even if the mechanism needs tighter phrasing.** The skeptic is right that the design doesn't isolate charge compensation from stepped NCE or labeling efficiency. But the empirical observation itself—58 succinylation and 31 glutarylation sites detected with TMT that are missed by propionylation—is novel and important. The mechanism can be tested, but the finding itself stands. The authors should soften the mechanism language to "consistent with charge compensation" and add the control experiment (TMT at fixed NCE, or propionylation at stepped NCE) that the methodology reviewer requests.

2. **The systematic 10-condition benchmark (n=4, low CVs) is a community resource.** The skeptic concedes this is "genuinely useful" and "sound as a descriptive survey." This alone justifies publication—the field needs these comparisons.

3. **The authors flag their own comparator's weaknesses** (AMBIC buffer propionylation, Figure 4A discrepancy). This honesty should be rewarded, not penalized.

4. **The dual-protease coverage complementarity is demonstrated, not just asserted**—that's rare in methods papers.

**Addressing specific criticism**

On the NAM pseudoreplication concern: the skeptic identifies a real confound (cleavage redistribution), but the authors already acknowledge it and made a defensible methodological choice. The request to show robustness to cleavage-state restriction is fair and achievable in revision.

On the 112 significant peptidoforms conflating Arg-C Ultra alone with the full RIPUP workflow: this is a fair point. The abstract should be corrected to specify "Arg-C Ultra digestion within RIPUP." But this is a presentation fix, not a scientific flaw.

On the "dark epigenome" framing: the skeptic's point about prior literature is correct. The authors should qualify the claim—but the detection of more sites than any previous single study with a mechanistic explanation is still a contribution worth publishing.

On the missing competing interests statement: this is a trivial fix. The ethics reviewer is right to flag it but wrong to imply it's a HARD scientific gap.

**Bottom line:** Every criticism in the panel is addressable in revision. No criticism argues the core findings are wrong or unreproducible in principle. The systematic comparison is useful, the TMT finding is novel (even if the mechanism needs testing), and the dual-protease approach is practically valuable. Accept pending revision.

**Key points:**
- The 10-condition, 40-sample benchmark is a genuine community resource that the skepticism concedes is 'sound' and 'genuinely useful.'
- The TMT succinylation/glutarylation finding is an empirical discovery; the mechanism can be refined in revision without invalidating the observation.
- Every criticism in the panel is addressable in revision—no finding is argued to be fundamentally wrong or unreproducible.
- The authors' candor about their own comparator's weaknesses strengthens, not weakens, the paper's credibility.
- The dual-protease coverage complementarity is demonstrated with data, making it a practical contribution the field can immediately use.

## Skeptic — round 1

The advocate's case rests on a false premise: that the panel's criticisms are 'all addressable in revision.' Several are not, because the experimental design as executed cannot distinguish between competing explanations. The charge-compensation claim requires an unambiguous mechanism test, yet the authors did not fragment TMT-labeled peptides at fixed 30% NCE or propionylated peptides at stepped NCE. The advocate says 'the mechanism can be tested' — yes, but that means it has NOT been tested in this manuscript. Publishing a mechanistic claim without the control that separates the label from the collision energy is not a presentation fix; it is a design gap that a revision cannot retrofit if the raw data do not exist.

Second, the advocate treats the 112 significant peptidoforms as a settled count, but the statistics reviewer correctly identifies the pseudoreplication problem: NAM shifts cleavage efficiency, so a change in a peptidoform's abundance could reflect a change in where the protease cuts, not a change in the underlying modification. The authors abandoned peptide-family ratios precisely because of this confound, then reintroduced it at the peptidoform level. The advocate says this is 'defensible' — but it is not defended with the robustness check the methodology reviewer requests (restricting to matched cleavage states). That check is not in the manuscript and would require reanalysis of existing data, so it is fixable in revision. But the advocate should concede this is a present gap, not a resolved one.

Third, the advocate asserts the systematic benchmark justifies publication alone. Yet the benchmark's headline comparison is ambiguous: 'Arg-C Ultra outperforms Trypsin' rests on an R-only Trypsin search that the authors themselves acknowledge overestimates labeling efficiency. The reproducibility reviewer flags this explicitly. Until the manuscript states which search’s numbers anchor Figure 2C, the comparison is uninterpretable.

The missing competing-interests statement is indeed trivial. But the advocate's framing — 'no criticism argues the core findings are wrong' — conflates 'not proven wrong' with 'proven right.' A manuscript whose load-bearing claims rest on confounded comparisons and ambiguous search parameters has not yet earned publication.

**Key points:**
- The charge-compensation mechanism cannot be separated from the stepped-NCE confound without the control data that does not exist; this is a design gap, not a revision touch-up.
- The 112-significant-peptidoforms count is not robust to the cleavage-redistribution confound the authors themselves identified; the requested robustness check is missing.
- The 'Arg-C outperforms Trypsin' comparison rests on an ambiguous search-parameter choice (R-only vs KR) that is not disclosed for Figure 2C.
- Conflating 'not proven wrong' with 'proven right' is an advocate's fallacy; the panel identified specific confounds that have not been resolved.
