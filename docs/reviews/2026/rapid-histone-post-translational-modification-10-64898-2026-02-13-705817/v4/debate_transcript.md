Softening 'dark epigenome' to 'enhanced detection of acidic acylations' preserves 100% of the empirical contribution while resolving the semantic overreach. These are precisely the kind of surface-level revisions journals routinely request without rejecting the underlying science.

## Skeptic — round 1

The advocate frames every objection as a wording tweak, but the load-bearing issue is a confounded experimental design, not semantics. The paper's headline claims — 'Arg-C Ultra with TMT... exceeds Trypsin-based approaches' and the 'dark epigenome' of 58 succinylation/31 glutarylation sites — rest entirely on comparison to a propionylation protocol the authors themselves admit is suboptimal ('ammonium-containing buffers... introduce competing amine reactivity that reduces labeling efficiency relative to optimized approaches,' p.14). The methodology reviewer states plainly this design 'does not distinguish TMT detects real sites that propionylation misses from TMT and propionylation detect the same sites but with different efficiency.' That's not a hedging problem fixable by swapping 'dark epigenome' for softer language — it's a missing head-to-head against an optimized propionylation protocol (e.g., Meert et al., Searfoss et al.), which the manuscript itself cites as available but never runs. Fixing this requires new wet-lab experiments, not copyediting.

Second, the abstract's flagship discovery (58+31 sites) has zero orthogonal validation — no PRM, no synthetic peptides, no antibody confirmation — despite being the paper's most novelty-bearing claim (novelty reviewer, rigor reviewer, methodology reviewer all flag this independently). I concede the advocate is right that clarity, reproducibility, and ethics issues are genuinely minor and fixable. But the core comparative claim underpinning the title and abstract is confounded by protocol choice and unvalidated by any independent method — that combination is FIXABLE only through substantial additional experiments, not the revision-light path the advocate proposes.

**Key points:**
- Headline TMT-vs-propionylation superiority claim is confounded: comparator protocol is admitted-suboptimal (p.14), not the best achievable propionylation efficiency
- Methodology reviewer: design cannot distinguish 'TMT finds real sites propionylation misses' from 'both find same sites, different efficiency' — undermines the causal claim, not just its wording
- The 58 succinylation/31 glutarylation 'dark epigenome' claim, the paper's central novelty, has no orthogonal validation (PRM, synthetic peptides, antibodies) per rigor/methodology/novelty reviewers
- These are FIXABLE but require new experiments (optimized propionylation head-to-head, targeted validation), not the copyediting-level revision the advocate proposes
- Concede: clarity, reproducibility, and ethics concerns are genuinely minor and fixable as advocate describes
