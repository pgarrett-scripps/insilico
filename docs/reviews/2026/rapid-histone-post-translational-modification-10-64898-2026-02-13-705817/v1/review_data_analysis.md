# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
This manuscript presents a comprehensive systematic evaluation of protease and labeling strategies for histone PTM analysis, with rigorous experimental design and generally sound statistical practice. The quantitative analyses are well-executed: reproducibility is demonstrated via CVs, multiple comparisons are controlled via Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction, and the NAM dose-response experiment uses appropriate linear models with proper imputation and normalization. However, several statistical details lack transparency: the kNN imputation strategy is not fully justified, the definition of 'informative histone peptides' introduces post-hoc filtering that could bias results, and some effect sizes are not reported alongside p-values. The hippocampal proof-of-concept uses a small sample (n=5 animals) with presence/absence reporting rather than quantitative analysis, limiting inference. Overall, the work is methodologically sound for a methods paper, but statistical reporting could be more explicit in places.

## Strengths
- Reproducibility metrics (median CVs <5% across conditions, Figure 2A) are clearly defined and reported across all major conditions, demonstrating robust protease activity and pipeline consistency.
- Multiple-comparison correction is properly applied: Benjamini–Hochberg FDR adjustment is named and used consistently for the NAM quantitative experiment (adj p < 0.05 threshold stated throughout Figure 7 and text).
- The NAM dose-response quantitative analysis uses appropriate statistical methods: limma with empirical Bayes moderation, cell-means model with pairwise contrasts, and log2 transformation of intensity data; dose-response concordance is quantified with correlation coefficients (r = 0.803 for Arg-C Ultra, r = 0.791 for r-Chymotrypsin, Figure 7B–D).
- Sample sizes and replication are clearly stated: n = 4 technical replicates per condition for HEK293T, n = 5 biological replicates for rat hippocampi, and independent unit of replication is explicit (digestion replicates or animals, not pseudo-replicates).
- Data handling and exclusions are pre-specified: 'informative histone peptides' criteria (≤1 missed cleavage, detected in ≥3 replicates, no artifacts) are defined before analysis; kNN imputation is restricted to dose groups with ≥2 measured replicates, minimizing bias from missing-at-random assumptions.
- Labeling efficiency is quantified using two complementary metrics (by site count and by intensity-weighted), providing transparency on how the derivatization strategy affects downstream analysis (Figure 4A).
- Raw data are deposited in ProteomeXchange (PXD073683) and custom R scripts are available on GitHub, enabling reproducibility and verification of statistical claims.

## Weaknesses
- The kNN imputation strategy (k=10, restricted to dose groups with ≥2 of 3 replicates) is applied without justification of the k value or sensitivity analysis; no comparison to alternative imputation methods (e.g., mean imputation, multiple imputation) is provided, and the impact on Type I error or bias is not discussed.
- The definition of 'informative histone peptides' (IHP) as fully labeled, ≤1 missed cleavage, detected in ≥3 replicates, and lacking artifacts (Figure 4B–C) is applied post-hoc to filter the dataset; while the criteria are reasonable, this filtering could introduce selection bias if artifact rates or detection patterns differ systematically between conditions. No sensitivity analysis (e.g., varying the replication threshold) is shown.
- Effect sizes are not consistently reported alongside p-values in the main text; while fold-changes are shown in Figure 7A–C, confidence intervals around these estimates are absent, limiting interpretation of practical significance.
- The hippocampal proof-of-concept (n=5 animals) reports peptidoforms detected in ≥2 replicates as 'qualitative presence/absence' (Methods, Data processing) rather than quantitative abundance; no statistical test is applied to these observations, and the small sample size limits generalizability. The claim that '231 unique PTM sites' were detected is descriptive, not inferential.
- Missed cleavage motif analysis (Figures S6–S7, SI text) identifies enriched residues (e.g., D at P1′ for Arg-C Ultra, E at P2 for r-Chymotrypsin) but does not report statistical significance, confidence intervals, or correction for multiple testing across the motif positions examined; the biological relevance of these associations is stated but not quantified.
- Propionylation efficiency is calculated as site-level and intensity-weighted percentages (Figure 4A), but no confidence intervals or variability estimates (e.g., SD across replicates) are provided; the statement that 'propionylation efficiency calculated by site count (29–61%) was lower overall compared to estimation by intensity (33–71%)' lacks error bars or statistical comparison.
- The comparison of TMT-labeled vs. propionylated peptides (Figure 4C, SI Figure S2C) reports fold-differences in IHP counts (e.g., '~2.3-fold more fully cleaved peptides with Arg-C Ultra + TMT than Trypsin + Prop') without statistical testing or confidence intervals; these are descriptive ratios, not tested differences.
- Artifact rates (methionine oxidation, dehydration) are reported as percentages (e.g., '2% and 4.5% for Trypsin + Prop', '9.4% and 4.3% for Arg-C Ultra + TMT', Figure S1E) without confidence intervals or statistical comparison between conditions; no test of whether differences are significant is provided.
- The succinylation/glutarylation enhancement with TMT (58 succinylation and 31 glutarylation sites) is presented as a discovery but not validated quantitatively; the authors note that 'future studies could prioritize synthetic validation' but do not report how many of these sites are detected in multiple replicates or with high confidence scores, limiting the strength of the claim.

## Questions
- For the kNN imputation (k=10) applied to the NAM quantitative analysis: (1) How was k=10 chosen? (2) Was a sensitivity analysis performed varying k? (3) How many peptidoforms required imputation, and in how many dose groups? (4) Did imputation substantially alter the results (e.g., by comparing imputed vs. complete-case analysis)?
- The 'informative histone peptides' (IHP) filter requires detection in ≥3 of 4 replicates (HEK293T) or ≥2 of 5 (rat hippocampi); was this threshold chosen a priori or post-hoc? If post-hoc, does varying the threshold (e.g., ≥2 of 4) materially change the conclusions?
- For the missed cleavage motif analysis (SI Figures S6–S7): Were the enriched residues (D at P1′, E at P2) tested for statistical significance using, e.g., a hypergeometric test or permutation test? What is the multiple-testing correction across all motif positions examined?
- In Figure 4A, propionylation efficiency ranges from 29–71% depending on condition and metric; are these differences statistically significant? What is the SD or 95% CI around each estimate?
- The succinylation and glutarylation sites (58 and 31, respectively) detected with TMT: How many of these are detected in ≥2 replicates? How many have high-confidence localization (e.g., Mascot Ions Score >20, or equivalent)? Are these sites validated by any orthogonal method?
- For the NAM dose-response experiment, the text states '112 statistically significant peptidoforms (adj p < 0.05)' were identified; how many total peptidoforms were tested (i.e., what is the family size for the FDR correction)? Is this the number of peptidoforms with complete data across all dose groups?
- The hippocampal analysis reports '231 unique PTM sites' detected in ≥2 animals; is this a simple count, or were any statistical tests applied to assess whether these sites are significantly enriched or depleted relative to a null model? What is the power to detect a PTM site given n=5 animals?
- In Figure 7B and 7D, dose-response concordance is assessed via Pearson correlation (r = 0.803 and r = 0.791); were these correlations tested for significance, and what are the 95% CIs? Are there any outlier peptidoforms driving the correlation?
- The statement that TMT labeling 'rescues ionization of acidic acylations' (Abstract, Results) is supported mechanistically (Figure 3) but not quantitatively; can the authors report the fold-change in MS1 signal intensity or fragmentation efficiency for succinylated peptides with TMT vs. propionylation?
- For the Arg-C Ultra and r-Chymotrypsin digestion efficiency comparison (Figure 2C): Were the differences in peptide counts tested for statistical significance (e.g., paired t-test or Wilcoxon test across replicates), or are these descriptive counts only?
