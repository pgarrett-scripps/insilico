# Clarity & Presentation Reviewer

## Summary
This manuscript is generally well-written and clearly structured, with strong visual presentation and logical flow. The central contribution—RIPUP, a rapid dual-protease workflow for histone PTM analysis—is stated explicitly and the motivation is well-framed. Methods are detailed and reproducible. However, there are several SOFT clarity issues around terminology consistency, some vague quantifiers, and occasional redundancy that create minor friction. One HARD issue exists: the abstract claims TMT reveals "58 succinylation and 31 glutarylation sites" as a "dark epigenome," but the manuscript does not clearly state whether these are novel discoveries or merely better-detected versions of known modifications—a distinction critical to the novelty claim.

## Strengths
- The central contribution (RIPUP workflow) is stated explicitly in the abstract and early in the main text, making the paper's purpose immediately clear.
- Methods section is detailed and reproducible, with clear step-by-step protocols, enzyme-to-substrate ratios, incubation times, and buffer conditions specified throughout.
- Figures are well-designed with clear legends, axis labels with units, and informative captions. Figure 2 effectively compares multiple conditions across proteases; Figure 3 provides excellent mechanistic explanation of TMT vs. propionylation chemistry.
- The narrative flows logically: motivation → systematic comparison → key findings (TMT advantage for acidic acylations) → quantitative validation → tissue proof-of-concept.
- Quantitative claims are specific: '58 succinylation and 31 glutarylation sites,' '112 statistically significant peptidoforms (adj p < 0.05),' '231 unique PTM sites,' '3-hour workflow'—not vague directions.
- Abbreviations and non-standard terms are generally defined at first use (e.g., 'RIPUP (Rapid Identification of histone PTMs in Underivatized Peptides)', 'IHP (informative histone peptides)', 'HiP-Frag').
- The discussion clearly positions findings relative to recent work (Vai et al. 2025, Ryzhaya et al. 2025) and explains mechanistic rationale for TMT's superiority (tertiary amine charge compensation).
- Limitations are acknowledged transparently: single cell line for main comparisons, sequence coverage gaps in globular domains, constraints of variable modification search space.

## Weaknesses
- {HARD} The abstract and main text claim TMT reveals '58 succinylation and 31 glutarylation sites—a "dark epigenome" largely undetected by propionylation-based methods' (Abstract, Results section). However, the manuscript does not explicitly state whether these sites are (a) previously unknown modifications, (b) known modifications that were simply under-detected by prior methods, or (c) a mixture. The term 'dark epigenome' implies novel discovery, but the evidence presented shows only improved detection of modifications that may already exist in the literature. This distinction is critical to the novelty claim and should be clarified.
- {SOFT} Terminology inconsistency: 'derivatization' and 'labeling' are used interchangeably (e.g., 'chemical derivatization' vs. 'TMT labeling' vs. 'propionylation derivatization'), which creates minor friction. The manuscript would benefit from consistent terminology: e.g., reserve 'derivatization' for chemical modification and 'labeling' for mass-tag addition, or define the distinction once.
- {SOFT} The phrase 'informative histone peptides (IHP)' is defined operationally (Figure 4B: 'fully labeled, ≤1 missed cleavage, detected in ≥3 replicates') but this definition appears only in the figure legend and Methods, not in the main text where IHP is first used. Readers must hunt for the definition.
- {SOFT} Vague quantifier: 'Endogenous propionylation and butyrylation were identified in unpropionylated Arg-C Ultra and r-Chymotrypsin digested peptides' (Results, Histone PTM identification section). How many sites? How abundant? The manuscript later states these were detected in rat hippocampi but does not quantify their prevalence in HEK293T samples.
- {SOFT} Figure 5 is dense and difficult to parse. Panel (A) shows 'total unique PTM sites' but the stacked bar format makes it hard to compare individual modification types across conditions. A grouped or faceted layout might improve clarity. The figure legend does not explain what 'Prop + Urea' abbreviation means on first appearance (though it is defined in Methods).
- {SOFT} The quantitative experiment (NAM treatment) uses 'histone-level normalization' (Methods: 'per-sample total intensities were scaled to the grand mean across all samples within each histone'). This is mentioned briefly but not explained in the main Results section. Readers unfamiliar with this normalization approach may not understand how it preserves treatment-induced changes while correcting for loading.
- {SOFT} Redundancy: The motivation for TMT labeling is explained three times—once in the introduction ('TMT is a well-established amine-reactive label...'), once in the 'Chemical rationale for TMT labeling' section (Figure 3 and surrounding text), and again in the Discussion ('TMT's tertiary amine provides charge compensation...'). The core mechanistic insight could be consolidated earlier.
- {SOFT} The phrase 'multiply modified peptidoforms' is used frequently but never formally defined. The manuscript assumes readers understand that a 'peptidoform' is a peptide with a specific modification state, but this should be stated explicitly on first use.

## Questions
- {HARD} Are the 58 succinylation and 31 glutarylation sites detected with TMT novel modifications (previously unreported in the literature), or are they known modifications that were simply under-detected by propionylation-based workflows? The abstract's claim of a 'dark epigenome' hinges on this distinction. Please clarify whether you are claiming discovery of new PTM sites or improved detection of existing ones.
- In Figure 4A, the labeling efficiency for propionylation varies widely across conditions (29–71% by site count). What accounts for this variation? Is it enzyme-dependent, substrate-dependent, or condition-dependent? A brief explanation in the main text would aid interpretation.
- Figure 5B shows that 'propionylation enabled identification of glyceroyl sites...minimally detected in TMT-labeled or unlabeled samples.' Does this mean TMT is inferior for glycerylation detection, or is glycerylation simply rare in your samples? Should users choose propionylation if glycerylation is of interest?
- The manuscript states that 'NAM induced dose-dependent missed cleavage redistribution in Arg-C Ultra digests' (Quantitative analysis section), evidenced by '259 peptidoforms detected exclusively in NAM-treated samples.' Is this a confounding factor for quantification, or is the histone-level normalization sufficient to account for it? Please clarify the impact on quantitative accuracy.
- In the rat hippocampal proof-of-concept (Figure 8), you identify '231 unique PTM sites' across both enzymes in n=5 animals. How many of these sites are detected in all 5 animals vs. only 1–2 animals? This would help readers assess the robustness of the findings in tissue.
- The manuscript mentions that 'formylation of K, S, T, and Y residues was a prominent feature' in both HEK293T and rat hippocampal histones. You note that formylation 'can arise as a sample preparation artifact' but argue it is biologically important. What evidence supports biological relevance in your samples? Are there controls to rule out artifact?
- Methods state that 'propionyl and TMT masses were stripped from labeled peptidoforms during data processing to enable direct qualitative comparison.' Can you clarify what 'stripped' means here? Are the modifications removed from the mass calculation, or are they simply not counted in the comparison? This affects interpretation of the PTM landscape.
