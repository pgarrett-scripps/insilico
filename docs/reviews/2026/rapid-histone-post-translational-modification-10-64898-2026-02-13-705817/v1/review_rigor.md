# Rigor & Overclaiming Reviewer

## Summary
This manuscript presents RIPUP, a multi-protease workflow for rapid histone PTM analysis, and systematically compares alternative proteases and labeling strategies to the established propionylation-based method. The work is methodologically rigorous and the experimental design is comprehensive (40 samples, 10 conditions). However, several load-bearing claims in the abstract and main text are either over-stated relative to the evidence provided or lack adequate hedging. The most significant issue is the claim that TMT labeling reveals a 'dark epigenome' of succinylation and glutarylation—while the enhanced detection is real and mechanistically explained, the biological prevalence claim (that these modifications have been 'systematically underestimated') is not directly tested and relies on inference from a single cell line. Additionally, the quantitative validation is limited to an in vitro NAM treatment model, and the proof-of-concept tissue application (rat hippocampus) lacks biological replication or validation. The manuscript would benefit from more precise language around the scope of findings and clearer distinction between technical capability and biological prevalence.

## Strengths
- Comprehensive systematic comparison across 10 experimental conditions with robust technical replication (n=4 HEK293T, n=5 rat hippocampi), yielding median peptide CVs <5–7%, demonstrating reproducibility.
- Clear mechanistic explanation for TMT's enhanced detection of acidic acylations: the tertiary amine sequesters mobile protons, shifting fragmentation toward b-ions and rescuing ionization of succinylated/glutarylated peptides (Figure 3, supported by fragmentation theory and empirical spectra in Figure 6).
- Orthogonal protease strategy is well-justified: r-Chymotrypsin provides complementary coverage of H2A variants (>95% vs <6% for Arg-C Ultra) and linker histones (H1 variants), with overlapping validation built in.
- Rigorous data processing: 1% FDR at PSM and peptide levels, minimum detection thresholds (≥3 replicates HEK293T, ≥2 rat), CV filtering, and transparent reporting of missed cleavage motifs and artifact rates (2–12.5%).
- Practical workflow advantage clearly demonstrated: 3-hour total preparation time vs >6 hours for conventional Trypsin+propionylation, with cost analysis provided (SI Table S5).
- Quantitative analysis of NAM-treated cells shows dose-dependent changes in expected direction (88 up, 24 down peptidoforms; r=0.803 dose-response concordance), with reciprocal PTM crosstalk detected (e.g., K9me2/K23ac vs K9ac/K14ac).

## Weaknesses
- 
**HARD: Abstract claim of 'dark epigenome' overstates biological significance.** The abstract states: 'revealing 58 succinylation and 31 glutarylation sites – a 
-  dark epigenome
-  largely undetected by propionylation-based methods.' This conflates two distinct claims: (1) TMT detects more succinylation/glutarylation than propionylation in this experiment (true), and (2) these modifications have been 'largely undetected' and represent a hidden biological landscape (unsupported). The evidence shows enhanced *technical detection* in HEK293T cells only; no evidence that succinylation/glutarylation prevalence was previously underestimated across tissues or cell types. The term 'dark epigenome' is borrowed from genomics jargon and implies biological discovery rather than methodological improvement. Recommend: replace with 'enhanced detection of acidic acylations' or hedge with 'suggests that succinylation and glutarylation may be more abundant than previously detected by propionylation-based workflows.'
- 
**HARD: Generalization of succinylation/glutarylation findings beyond HEK293T is unsupported.** The main text states (Results, 'Enhanced identification...'): 'This improvement in identification of succinylation (58 sites) and glutarylation (31 sites) with TMT-derivatization is attributable to improved ionization/fragmentation efficiency and PTM-site scoring, suggesting that succinylation of histone K residues may be more abundant than previously thought.' The evidence is from HEK293T cells only. The rat hippocampus proof-of-concept detected glutarylation at H2A K119 but no systematic comparison of succinylation/glutarylation between TMT and propionylation in tissue. The claim 'may be more abundant than previously thought' is speculative and should be qualified as 'in HEK293T cells' or supported by additional cell types/tissues.
- 
**HARD: Quantitative validation scope is narrow and not generalizable.** The quantitative experiment uses only NAM (pan-sirtuin inhibitor) in HEK293T cells. The authors state (Results, 'Quantitative analysis...'): 'RIPUP quantified 112 statistically significant peptidoforms (adj p < 0.05), predominantly increasing with NAM dose (88 up, 24 down).' This is a single perturbation in a single cell line. No validation in other cell types, tissues, or with orthogonal methods (e.g., Western blot, immunofluorescence). The claim that RIPUP is 'suitable and sensitive for detecting changes in peptidoform abundance' (Discussion) is based on one model system and should be explicitly limited to 'in this in vitro NAM treatment model' or expanded with additional validation.
- 
**HARD: Rat hippocampus proof-of-concept lacks biological replication and validation.** The manuscript states (Results, 'RIPUP of hippocampal sections'): 'As a proof-of-concept experiment, we extracted histones from rat hippocampal sections and performed RIPUP using separate Arg-C Ultra and r-Chymotrypsin digestions. Total sample preparation time was ~3 h and we identified 212 and 189 peptides with ≤1 missed cleavage.' The n=5 animals are technical replicates of the same tissue region (AP: -2.00 to -3.25 from bregma), not biological replicates across different animals or regions. No validation that the detected PTMs are biologically meaningful or reproducible in independent cohorts. The claim that RIPUP 'enables timely discovery of epigenetic mechanisms' (Abstract) is not supported by functional validation or independent replication.
- 
**SOFT: Causal language used for charge compensation mechanism without direct mechanistic proof.** The manuscript states (Results, 'Enhanced identification...'): 'The enhanced detection of succinyl-K with TMT is explained by the charge compensation provided by the tertiary amine within the TMT reporter region.' While the fragmentation model (Figure 3C) is plausible and supported by b-ion enrichment in spectra, this is an inference from observed fragmentation patterns, not a direct test of the mechanism (e.g., no isotope-labeling experiment, no comparison of charge states, no synthetic peptide validation). The mechanism is consistent with the data but not definitively proven. Recommend: 'is consistent with' or 'likely explained by' rather than 'is explained by.'
- 
**SOFT: Comparison to propionylation is not entirely fair.** The manuscript acknowledges (Discussion, 'Labeling efficiency...'): 'the ammonium-containing buffers in this protocol introduce competing amine reactivity that reduces labeling efficiency relative to optimized approaches.' However, the main comparison uses the Garcia et al. (2007) protocol with ammonium bicarbonate, not optimized propionylation. TMT's advantage over propionylation is therefore partly a comparison to a suboptimal protocol. The authors note this but do not test TMT against optimized propionylation (e.g., with TEAB buffer throughout), which would be a fairer comparison. This limits the generalizability of the TMT advantage claim.
- 
**SOFT: 'First to' claim for TMT in histone PTM analysis is not clearly established.** The manuscript states (Background): 'TMT is a well-established amine-reactive label that has not been systematically evaluated for histone PTM analysis.' The authors then cite prior work (refs 30–32) showing TMT use in histone analysis. The claim of being 'first to systematically evaluate' is defensible but the prior work is not clearly distinguished from this study. The novelty is the systematic comparison to propionylation and the mechanistic insight into charge compensation, not the first use of TMT on histones. Recommend: clarify that prior work used TMT but did not compare it directly to propionylation or explain the charge compensation mechanism.
- 
**SOFT: Missed cleavage motif analysis is presented without statistical testing.** The manuscript states (Results, 'Quantitative analysis...'): 'Modifiable sites (K/R/STY) and basic residues (K/R/H) were enriched within 3 residues of missed R. Aspartic acid (D) was enriched at P1′ (Arg-C Ultra) and glutamic acid (E) was enriched at P2 (r-Chymotrypsin), suggesting that salt bridges may also contribute to cleavage site blocking.' The enrichment is shown in SI Figures S6–S7 but no statistical test (e.g., Fisher's exact test, chi-square) is reported. The claim of enrichment is qualitative and the salt bridge hypothesis is speculative. Recommend: add statistical tests or hedge as 'consistent with' rather than 'suggesting.'
- 
**SOFT: Artifact rates increase substantially with TMT labeling but are not discussed as a limitation.** Figure S2E shows artifact rates (methionine oxidation, dehydration) increase from 2–4.5% (Trypsin+Prop) to 9.4–12.5% (TMT-labeled Arg-C Ultra and r-Chymotrypsin). This is a 2–3-fold increase. The manuscript does not discuss whether these artifacts affect PTM localization confidence or quantification. The 'Limitations' section does not mention this trade-off. Recommend: discuss whether artifact rates impact data quality and whether filtering strategies are needed.

## Questions
- In the rat hippocampus experiment, were the n=5 animals independent biological replicates or technical replicates of the same tissue region? If the latter, how does this affect the claim that RIPUP 'enables timely discovery of epigenetic mechanisms' in tissue samples?
- The succinylation and glutarylation sites detected with TMT in HEK293T cells—have any of these been validated by orthogonal methods (e.g., Western blot with succinyl-K antibodies, immunofluorescence, or synthetic peptide standards)?
- For the charge compensation mechanism (Figure 3C), was this inferred from fragmentation patterns alone, or were direct measurements of charge state or proton affinity performed (e.g., ion mobility, hydrogen-deuterium exchange)?
- The quantitative NAM experiment shows 112 significant peptidoforms in HEK293T cells. Have these findings been replicated in other cell types or tissues to support the claim that RIPUP is 'suitable and sensitive' for quantification?
- Why were the artifact rates (methionine oxidation, dehydration) 2–3-fold higher in TMT-labeled samples (9.4–12.5%) compared to propionylated samples (2–4.5%)? Does this affect PTM localization confidence or quantification accuracy?
- The manuscript states that TMT labeling detects 58 succinylation and 31 glutarylation sites in HEK293T cells. How many of these sites are detected in the rat hippocampus tissue, and do they overlap? This would strengthen the claim that these modifications are biologically prevalent.
- For the comparison to propionylation, was TMT tested against optimized propionylation protocols (e.g., with TEAB buffer throughout, as used for TMT), or only against the Garcia et al. (2007) protocol with ammonium bicarbonate? A fair comparison would use the same buffer conditions.
