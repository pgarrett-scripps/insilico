# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript's citations are largely accurate and appropriately attributed, with correct foundational attribution to Garcia et al. (2007) and proper integration of recent work (Vai et al. 2025, Ryzhaya et al. 2025). However, there is one HARD issue regarding citation chain clarity for the mobile proton model—the dual citation of Paizs & Suhai (2005) and Steen & Mann (2004) creates ambiguity about which source established the mechanistic explanation central to the TMT advantage narrative. Several SOFT issues exist: incomplete justification for excluding TMA from the main comparison despite citing Ryzhaya et al. (2025), potential overstatement of succinylation novelty without quantitative prior benchmarks, a possible misattribution of Whedon et al. (2024) regarding TMT for histone PTM analysis, and underrepresentation of alternative derivatization methods in the related-work discussion. These issues are fixable with minor revisions and do not compromise the scientific contribution.

## Strengths
- Accurate foundational attribution: Garcia et al. (2007) is correctly cited as establishing the propionylation-based histone PTM workflow and appropriately characterized as highly influential with 291 citations noted.
- Recent and relevant citations: The manuscript cites Vai et al. (2025) on HiP-Frag and Ryzhaya et al. (2025) on Arg-C Ultra with TMA, both directly relevant and properly integrated into the narrative.
- Verified key references: Paizs & Suhai (2005) on fragmentation pathways and the mobile proton model is a real, peer-reviewed source confirmed via PubMed PMID:15389847.
- Appropriate scope of related work: The introduction covers histone PTM biology, epigenetic regulation, and disease contexts (cancer, neurobiology, addiction, microbiome) with reasonable citations.
- Data availability and reproducibility: The manuscript deposits raw data, code, and detailed methods to ProteomeXchange/PRIDE, supporting verifiability claims.
- No fabricated or garbled citations: All spot-checked citations are real and resolvable via PubMed or DOI with appropriate preprint vs. published distinction.

## Weaknesses
- Citation chain integrity for mobile proton model (HARD): The manuscript cites Paizs & Suhai (2005) and Steen & Mann (2004) for the mobile proton model and proton migration mechanism, but the specific mechanistic claim about proton migration should be traced to Paizs & Suhai (2005) as the primary source. The current dual-citation structure creates ambiguity about which source established the model, which is problematic because the mechanistic explanation for TMT's advantage (Figure 3C) hinges on this model.
- Incomplete literature on TMT for histone PTM analysis (HARD): The manuscript states there has been 'no direct comparison of TMT to propionylation' but does not clearly distinguish whether this means no prior published comparison or no prior comparison in the histone PTM literature specifically. Additionally, Reference 31 (Whedon et al. 2024) cited for 'histone H3 middle-down proteomics analysis with TMT' has a title ('Circular Engineered Sortase for Interrogating Histone H3 in Chromatin') suggesting a different method (sortase, not TMT), indicating a potential misattribution.
- Underrepresentation of alternative derivatization methods: The manuscript mentions TMA, PIC, and d6-acetic anhydride + PIC only in passing without substantive comparison in the introduction. The decision to exclude TMA from the main comparison despite citing Ryzhaya et al. (2025), which uses TMA and achieves similar 3–4 hour preparation timelines, deserves explicit justification.
- Succinylation prevalence claim may overstate prior findings: The claim that detection of '58 succinylation and 31 glutarylation sites' reveals findings 'above what has been reported using MS-based methods' cites Zhang et al. (2011) which describes the discovery of succinylation as a PTM, not quantification of its prevalence. The statement should cite specific prior quantitative studies of histone succinylation to avoid overstating novelty.
- Missing recent histone PTM reviews: The manuscript does not cite Noberini et al. (2022) on 'Mass spectrometry-based characterization of histones in clinical samples,' which would provide useful context for the clinical/translational framing of RIPUP.
- Incomplete citation of histone variant coverage: The claim that r-Chymotrypsin provides 'exceptionally high proteotypic coverage for H2A.Z (72%)' is not contextualized against prior work on H2A.Z PTM detection if such work exists.

## Questions
- Can you clarify whether the mechanistic explanation in Figure 3C (proton sequestration by TMT's tertiary amine) is derived from Paizs & Suhai (2005) or Steen & Mann (2004)? If primarily from Paizs & Suhai, consider restructuring citations to make this clear.
- You state 'there has been no direct comparison of TMT to propionylation' (p. 12). Do you mean no prior published comparison, or no prior comparison in the histone PTM literature specifically? Clarifying this distinction would strengthen the novelty claim.
- Can you confirm that Whedon et al. (2024) demonstrates TMT labeling for histone PTM analysis? The title 'Circular Engineered Sortase for Interrogating Histone H3 in Chromatin' suggests a different method (sortase, not TMT). If this is a different application, the citation may be misattributed.
- Ryzhaya et al. (2025) demonstrated that Arg-C Ultra + TMA reduces sample preparation to 3–4 hours, very similar to RIPUP's timeline. Why was TMA not included in the systematic comparison (Figure 1, Table S1) alongside propionylation and TMT?
- Can you cite specific prior MS-based quantitative studies of histone succinylation in mammalian cells to support the claim that your 58 succinylation sites are 'above what has been reported'?
- You note (p. 21) that formylation 'can arise as a sample preparation artifact' but cite it as biologically important. Can you clarify whether the formylation detected in your samples is likely biological or artifact-derived, and how you distinguished the two?
