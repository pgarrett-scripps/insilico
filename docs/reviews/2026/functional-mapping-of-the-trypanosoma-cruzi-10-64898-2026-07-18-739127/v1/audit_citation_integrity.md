# Citation Integrity Auditor

## Summary
The manuscript 'Functional Mapping of the Trypanosoma cruzi Serinome by Fluorophosphonate Activity-Based Protein Profiling' reports an activity-based chemoproteomic study identifying 37 enriched serine hydrolase-like proteins in T. cruzi epimastigotes. The audit examined 69 references for resolvability, claim support, number fidelity, self-citation patterns, and retracted/predatory sources. Key findings: (1) Several epidemiological claims (6-8 million infected, 75 million at risk, 10,000 annual deaths) cite WHO/PAHO fact-sheet URLs that lack DOIs/PMIDs and could not be verified in PubMed; (2) Multiple self-citations (refs 17, 29, 67, 69) are germane to the methods but constitute a notable fraction of the bibliography; (3) Specific quantitative claims (e.g., PPI enrichment p-value < 1×10⁻¹⁶, copy numbers of gene families) are attributed to database analyses (STRING, genome assemblies) that are not independently verifiable from the manuscript alone; (4) No retracted or predatory sources were identified among the cited journal articles. Several claim–citation links remain unverifiable because the cited sources (WHO fact sheets, database queries, supplementary figures) are not accessible via standard biomedical literature tools.

## Unverifiable (raise as questions)
- **[reference_resolvability] WHO/PAHO fact-sheet citations (refs 1, 2, 4, 7) for epidemiological statistics** — References 1, 2, 4, and 7 are URLs to PAHO/WHO web pages (fact sheets, news items, roadmap PDF). They lack DOIs/PMIDs. The specific claims—'6–8 million people currently infected', 'more than 75 million at risk across 21 endemic countries', '~10,000 deaths each year', and 'WHO 2021–2030 NTD roadmap'—could not be confirmed via PubMed search. While such fact sheets are legitimate sources, their content cannot be inspected through the available tools, making the citation–claim link unverifiable.
- **[claim_citation_support] Epidemiological numbers attributed to WHO/PAHO fact sheets (refs 1, 2, 4)** — The manuscript states: 'between 6 and 8 million people are currently infected, with more than 75 million people considered at risk of infection across 21 endemic countries' (refs 1, 2) and 'approximately 10,000 deaths attributed to the disease each year' (refs 1, 4). PubMed searches for these exact figures in WHO publications returned no results. The claims may be accurate per the cited fact sheets, but the tools cannot confirm the numbers appear in those specific URLs. This is a central background claim for the disease burden rationale.
- **[claim_citation_support] Benznidazole/nifurtimox developed 'over 50 years ago' and efficacy/side-effect claims (refs 5, 6)** — Refs 5 and 6 are cited for: (a) drugs developed >50 years ago, (b) primarily effective in acute phase, limited efficacy in chronic infections, (c) substantial side effects and treatment discontinuation. Ref 5 is a 2023 Ann Intern Med article (ITC17-ITC32) and ref 6 is a 2022 MMWR report on nifurtimox release via CDC IND program. The tools returned these PMIDs but could not retrieve full text to verify the specific historical and efficacy claims. The claims are plausible but unverified against the cited sources.
- **[claim_citation_support] PPI network enrichment p-value < 1×10⁻¹⁶ attributed to STRING analysis (ref 47)** — The manuscript reports 'The overall network exhibited highly significant enrichment (PPI enrichment p-value < 1 × 10-16)' citing STRING database v11.5 (ref 47). This p-value comes from the authors' own STRING query on their protein list; it is not a literature value from ref 47. Ref 47 is the STRING database methods paper. The claim is about the authors' analysis output, not a statement in ref 47. The citation is misleading—it should reference the authors' analysis, not the database paper.
- **[claim_citation_support] Lysophospholipase homologs in T. brucei and L. mexicana (ref 59)** — The text states: 'This enzyme has homologs identified in T. brucei and L. mexicana' citing ref 59. Ref 59 is 'Phospholipase A1: A novel virulence factor in Trypanosoma cruzi' (Mol Biochem Parasitol 2013). That paper concerns T. cruzi PLA1, not lysophospholipase homologs in other species. The claim appears to be misattributed; the correct source for homolog identification is not provided.
- **[quotation_number_fidelity] Specific numerical claims from WHO/PAHO sources (6–8 million, 75 million, 21 countries, 10,000 deaths)** — As noted above, these exact numbers could not be located in PubMed-indexed WHO publications. The fidelity of the quoted statistics to the cited URLs cannot be confirmed with the available tools.
- **[quotation_number_fidelity] Copy-number claims for gene families (e.g., lipase C4B63_104g97 17 copies, CPB1 12 copies)** — The manuscript reports specific copy numbers from genome assemblies (Dm28c 2018, 2014, 2017). These derive from the authors' bioinformatics pipeline on the cited genome assemblies (refs 26, 38). The numbers are not directly checkable from the manuscript or the referenced genome papers without re-running the analysis. The claim is plausible but unverifiable from the provided citations alone.

## Documented (for the record)
- **[reference_resolvability] Database URLs (refs 27, 28) for TDR Targets and TriTrypDB** — References 27 and 28 are cited as 'Accessed June 16, 2025' URLs for TDR Targets and TriTrypDB. These are live, resolvable web resources. No DOI/PMID expected for database portals. The citation is adequate for a web-accessible database.
- **[self_citation_inflation] Multiple self-citations by the author group (refs 17, 29, 67, 69)** — Four references are from the same research group (Isern, Porta, Steel, et al.): ref 17 (ChemBioChem 2025), ref 29 (Front Pharmacol 2022), ref 67 (Methods Mol Biol 2025), ref 69 (Nat Commun 2026). These are highly relevant to the methods (FP-ABPP probes, Leishmania serinome profiling, microplate assay protocol, inositol pyrophosphate interactions) and are not excessive or non-germane. However, they represent ~6% of the bibliography and are all from the same PI group (Steel/Labadie/Cricco). This is noted for transparency but does not appear to be citation padding.
- **[retracted_predatory_sources] Check for retracted or predatory journal citations** — All journal articles cited (JAMA, Ann Intern Med, MMWR, ACS Infect Dis, Biochim Biophys Acta, J Immunol, Biol Chem, Curr Med Chem, FEMS Microbiol Lett, PLoS Negl Trop Dis, Exp Parasitol, Parasit Vectors, Biochemistry, Adv Pharm Bull, Curr Res Pharmacol Drug Discov, J Biol Chem, Nat Methods, Microb Genom, Rev Soc Bras Med Trop, Lancet Infect Dis, Biochem Pharmacol, Enferm Infecc Microbiol Clin, Biochem Pharmacol, Front Microbiol, Biochimie, Parasitology, PLoS One, J Cell Biol, Med Microbiol Immunol, Mol Biochem Parasitol, Microbiol Mol Biol Rev, Biochim Biophys Acta, iScience, Parasitology, Enzyme Res, Biochem J, Trends Biochem Sci, Cell Chem Biol, PNAS, mBio, Methods Mol Biol, Nucleic Acids Res, Nat Commun) are from reputable, indexed journals. No retractions or predatory venues were detected via the available tools.
