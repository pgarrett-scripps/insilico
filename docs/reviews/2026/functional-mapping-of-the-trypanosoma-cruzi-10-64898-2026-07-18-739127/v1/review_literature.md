# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript accurately positions its work as the first direct activity-based profiling of the T. cruzi serinome, a claim supported by the absence of prior T. cruzi FP-ABPP studies in the literature. Citations to the authors' own Leishmania FP-ABPP work (refs 17, 29) and general ABPP methodology (refs 21-25) are appropriate and accurate. One citation (ref 69) appears to have a future publication date (2026), which needs correction. No directly competing work is missing; the related-work coverage is thorough for this niche.

## Strengths
- The introduction clearly distinguishes between abundance-based omics and activity-based profiling, justifying the ABPP approach with appropriate citations.
- The discussion integrates the current dataset with prior functional characterizations of specific T. cruzi serine hydrolases (OPB, CPB1, Tc80, PLA1), citing primary literature for each.
- The probe panel and workflow are explicitly linked to the authors' prior Leishmania work (refs 17, 29), providing a coherent methodological lineage.

## Weaknesses
- Reference 69 (Richter et al., Nat Commun 2026) carries a future publication year; this must be corrected to the actual year or replaced with a preprint DOI to maintain citation hygiene.
- The claim 'To our knowledge, the active serine hydrolases of T. cruzi have not previously been profiled directly' is not explicitly verified with a negative literature search statement; adding a brief note (e.g., 'no prior FP-ABPP studies in T. cruzi were found in PubMed as of [date]') would strengthen the novelty assertion.
- Citation 17 is used both for probe development and for the statement 'applying the probes to profile the active serinome of T. cruzi' — the latter describes the current work, not ref 17; the sentence structure should be clarified to avoid ambiguity.

## Questions
- Please confirm the correct publication year for reference 69 (Richter et al.) and provide a DOI if it is a preprint.
- Can the authors add a brief statement in the introduction confirming the literature search date/terms used to support the 'first direct profiling' claim?
