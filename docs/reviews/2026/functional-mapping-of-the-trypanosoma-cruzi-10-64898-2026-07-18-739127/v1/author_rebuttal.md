## Disagreements
- **methodology** — No competition ABPP performed; probe specificity 10-43% undermines central claim of activity-based profiling
  - Response: We acknowledge that competitive ABPP is the gold standard for confirming active-site engagement. However, three orthogonal lines of evidence support on-target labelling in this study: (1) 35/37 enriched proteins possess conserved or partially conserved catalytic triads/dyads validated by AlphaFold structural geometry (Ser Oγ-His Nε2 and His-Asp/Glu distances < 5 Å, pLDDT ≥ 80) as described in Results ('35 with conserved or partially conserved catalytic triad/dyad features') and Methods ('catalytic geometry was scored as conserved when the Ser Oγ-His Nε2 and His–Asp/Glu interatomic distances were within 5 Å in the AlphaFold model and the participating residues had an average pLDDT ≥ 80'); (2) fluorophosphonate probes are a chemically validated, mechanism-based chemotype for serine hydrolases with decades of precedent (Simon & Cravatt, 2010, JBC; Barglow & Cravatt, 2007, Nat Methods); (3) the identical probe panel was successfully applied to Leishmania spp. with subsequent functional validation of enriched targets (Isern et al., 2025, ChemBioChem). While competitive ABPP would strengthen the claim, its absence does not invalidate the resource; we have modulated the title and conclusions from 'activity-based chemoproteomic map' to 'chemoproteomic survey' and added a explicit limitation statement (see concessions).
  - Manuscript reference: Results: '35 with conserved or partially conserved catalytic triad/dyad features'; Methods: 'catalytic geometry was scored as conserved when the Ser Oγ-His Nε2 and His–Asp/Glu interatomic distances were within 5 Å in the AlphaFold model and the participating residues had an average pLDDT ≥ 80'; Introduction: 'fluorophosphonate (FP) probes providing a robust platform to interrogate catalytically active members of this enzyme family in complex proteomes' (refs 24, 25).
- **data_analysis** — Uncontrolled family-wise error rate across 7 probes and ~56 candidate SHs; 37-protein list presented as 'prioritized set' without FDR estimate
  - Response: We agree that the uncorrected threshold inflates false discovery risk. The decision to forgo BH correction was explicitly stated as a sensitivity-oriented choice for this discovery workflow ('Benjamini–Hochberg-adjusted values were computed but not used for thresholding, to avoid discarding genuine low-abundance hydrolases in this discovery-oriented workflow, and are reported per protein in Supplementary Data 2-3'). We will add the requested sensitivity analysis (BH-adjusted p-values across all tests, number of proteins surviving FDR < 0.05) using the deposited R scripts, and report the estimated false discovery proportion in the revised Results. This contextualizes the list reliability without requiring new experiments.
  - Manuscript reference: Figure 2 legend: 'Benjamini–Hochberg-adjusted values were computed but not used for thresholding, to avoid discarding genuine low-abundance hydrolases in this discovery-oriented workflow, and are reported per protein in Supplementary Data 2-3.'

## Load-bearing critiques
- The absence of competitive ABPP means the central claim of an 'activity-based chemoproteomic map' cannot be fully substantiated at the level of individual protein validation. This is a fundamental limitation of the current dataset. As conceded, we will modulate the title, abstract, and conclusions to describe a 'chemoproteomic survey of probe-enriched serine hydrolase candidates with in silico catalytic validation' rather than an 'activity-based map', and add an explicit limitation statement. This claim modulation, combined with the FDR sensitivity analysis, addresses the load-bearing concern without requiring new wet-lab experiments in this revision cycle.
- The uncontrolled FDR across 7 probes is a statistical load-bearing issue. The sensitivity analysis (BH correction on existing data) will quantify the false discovery proportion and allow readers to gauge list reliability. If the FDR is unacceptably high (>20%), the 'prioritized set' framing would need further tempering. We commit to reporting this analysis transparently.
