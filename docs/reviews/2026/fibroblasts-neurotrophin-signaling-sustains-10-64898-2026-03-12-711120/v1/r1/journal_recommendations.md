# Venue Recommendations

## as_is
*Empty — the editor's major verdict and the panel's 3.25/5 confidence-weighted score indicate the paper is not ready for acceptance at its current target or comparable venues.*

## after_revision

**Nature Immunology** or **Nature Cell Biology**
- *Fit:* The mechanistic core (NOTCH3 → NGF/NGFR → TRK axis driving fibroblast-to-mural differentiation) is solid and orthogonally validated. The spatial transcriptomics cohort (22 paired pre/post-treatment RA patients, ~2M cells) is substantial. The FDA-approved TRK inhibitor lead is clinically actionable. After resolving the Fig. 1 normalisation, toxicity controls, and fibroblast-specificity claims, this becomes a genuinely novel stromal-vascular crosstalk story with therapeutic implications.
- *Caveat:* Acceptance odds depend critically on whether absolute mural cell density (not fraction) actually increases post-treatment. If it does not, the title and framing collapse, and the paper drops to a narrower specialty venue (see alternative). The toxicity control is also load-bearing: if TRK inhibitor effects are not separable from tissue damage, the therapeutic claim weakens substantially.
- *Notes:* These venues value mechanistic depth, large-scale spatial data, and translational potential. The panel's feedback on mechanism (NGFR-dependent TRKA sensitisation, receptor-specific mural cell fate) is positive; the revision must nail the empirical claims.

**Science Translational Medicine**
- *Fit:* Explicitly values stromal biology, vascular remodelling, and therapeutic repurposing. The explant pharmacology with FDA-approved drugs is a natural fit. The 6-month paired-biopsy design and spatial transcriptomics are rigorous for a translational claim.
- *Caveat:* Requires the same resolution of Fig. 1 normalisation and explant toxicity. Also expects a clearer path toward clinical translation (e.g., pharmacokinetic comparison, discussion of synovial penetrance for larotrectinib/entrectinib). The paper currently offers the lead but not the development roadmap.

**Arthritis & Rheumatism** (or **Annals of the Rheumatic Diseases**)
- *Fit:* These are the natural homes for RA mechanistic work with therapeutic implications. The panel's feedback on the NOTCH3–neurotrophin axis is positive, and the spatial transcriptomics of treatment-resistant stromal remodelling is a direct contribution to the field's understanding of why immunosuppression fails in some patients.
- *Caveat:* After revision, this paper is very strong for a specialty journal. Acceptance odds are higher here than at Nature-level venues because the bar for mechanistic completeness is lower, but the same empirical fixes (Fig. 1 normalisation, toxicity control, fibroblast specificity) are required.
- *Notes:* These journals are more tolerant of ex vivo explant pharmacology as a therapeutic lead and less likely to demand in vivo validation before publication. The 6-month limitation is acknowledged in the text and is acceptable for a stromal-biology paper.

## alternative

**bioRxiv** (preprint server)
- *Fit:* If the major revisions cannot be completed (e.g., if the absolute density reanalysis shows no post-treatment increase, or if toxicity controls reveal off-target effects), deposit as a preprint with a transparent revision note. The mechanistic core is still publishable and will be read by the stromal-biology community. This preserves priority and allows iterative refinement.
- *Notes:* The panel's feedback is detailed and constructive; a revised preprint with point-by-point responses to the decision letter will be credible and useful to the field even if it does not reach a top-tier journal.

**Vascular Biology** (or **Arteriosclerosis, Thrombosis, and Vascular Biology**)
- *Fit:* If the RA-specific framing is narrowed and the paper is repositioned as a general mechanism of fibroblast-to-mural-cell differentiation driven by NOTCH3 and neurotrophins, this becomes a solid vascular-biology contribution. The spatial transcriptomics and in vitro mechanistic work are strong enough to stand on their own without the treatment-resistance claim.
- *Caveat:* This is a fallback that requires reframing away from the RA therapeutic angle. It is a good home for the mechanism but loses the clinical urgency and novelty that makes the paper interesting to rheumatologists.

**Fibrogenesis & Tissue Repair** (or **Matrix Biology**)
- *Fit:* The fibroblast differentiation story and stromal remodelling are on-brand. The NOTCH3 axis and neurotrophin signaling in fibroblast fate are novel contributions to stromal biology. This is a realistic venue if the paper is reframed as a stromal-remodelling mechanism rather than a treatment-resistance story.
- *Caveat:* Narrower audience and lower impact than specialty rheumatology journals, but higher acceptance odds if the major revisions are completed.

## Summary

**Most likely path:** After addressing the three load-bearing revisions (Fig. 1 normalisation, explant toxicity control, fibroblast-specificity claim), submit to **Arthritis & Rheumatism** or **Annals of the Rheumatic Diseases**. These are the right venues for the work as currently framed, and the panel's feedback is constructive rather than dismissive. Acceptance odds are good if the empirical claims hold up.

**If the absolute density does not increase post-treatment:** Reframe as a stromal-vascular crosstalk mechanism and submit to **Vascular Biology** or **Fibrogenesis & Tissue Repair**. The mechanism is still novel and publishable, but the treatment-resistance narrative collapses.

**If toxicity controls reveal off-target effects:** Deposit as a preprint with transparent revision notes. The mechanistic core (NOTCH3 → NGF/NGFR → TRK) is still solid and will be cited; the therapeutic claim can be refined or removed without losing the contribution.
