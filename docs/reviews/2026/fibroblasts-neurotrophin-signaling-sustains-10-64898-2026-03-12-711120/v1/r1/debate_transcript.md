# Debate Transcript

## Advocate — round 1

I'll argue for acceptance of this manuscript as a preprint whose referee record documents both strong findings and identifiable open questions — which is precisely what In Silico exists to surface.

**The core empirical finding is solid and clears the bar on its own.** All four substantive reviewers agree the paired pre/post-treatment spatial transcriptomic dataset (n=22 patients, 368,217 vascular cells, Fig. 1) robustly shows persistent capillary/arteriolar expansion and mural cell coverage after 6 months of standard therapy, "independent of clinical remission status." The data_analysis reviewer raises a real methodological question about the density denominator, but this is a **specified, answerable concern** — the authors already report the finding two ways (density per area, Fig. 1G, and proportion of total cells, Fig. 1H), and both show the same direction with independent post-treatment increases relative to healthy controls, which is hard to explain by immune depletion alone since Fig. 1H's proportions would also be sensitive to that artifact in the opposite direction for other lineages. This is a revision-scale clarification, not a refutation.

**The mechanistic chain is unusually well-triangulated for a first report.** The contribution_context reviewer's own strengths list credits "systematic" pathway integration across spatial data, RNAscope, knockdown, pharmacologic inhibition, and overexpression — a multi-modal convergence rarely seen in a single paper. Every reviewer converges on the same three "unresolved mechanism" points (direct vs. indirect NOTCH3→NGFR transcription; co-receptor vs. independent TRKA activation; primary vs. contributing pathway) — but note these are the *same* three points restated by each specialist model, not four independent confirmations of four independent flaws. Each is answerable with named, feasible follow-up experiments (co-IP, ChIP, dose-response) that the authors can add in revision without redoing the study's foundation.

**Translational payoff is real and stated modestly.** The explant reversal by two already-approved drugs (larotrectinib, entrectinib) is exactly the kind of repurposing signal this journal should flag publicly for readers to weigh themselves.

## Skeptic — round 1

The load-bearing objection: the paper's central quantitative claim — that vascular maturation *persists* despite treatment (Fig. 1G-H) — is measured as a proportion of total synovial cellularity. The data_analysis reviewer identifies that immunosuppression is expected to deplete immune infiltrates, which would mechanically inflate the vascular cell *proportion* even if absolute mural cell density were unchanged. The manuscript never reports mural cell counts normalized to tissue area independent of total cellularity. This is not a stylistic quibble — it is the single number the entire discussion, title claim ("sustains pathological vascular maturation"), and therapeutic rationale (Fig. 6, Discussion's "treatment-resistant disease compartment") rest on. If the apparent persistence is a denominator artifact of immune-cell depletion, the whole premise motivating the neurotrophin/TRK-inhibitor work collapses. **FIXABLE** — but only with a reanalysis reporting absolute density (cells/mm² tissue), which the authors have not done and which no specialist confirms was checked against raw counts.

Second, both scientific_validity and contribution_context independently flag that fibroblast-origin of the pathogenic neurotrophins is asserted, not demonstrated. The abstract and title claim "fibroblast neurotrophin signaling," yet the explant and spatial evidence only shows co-localization and knockdown effects in isolated fibroblast-endothelial co-cultures — not that fibroblasts are the operative source in intact tissue versus endothelial or immune cells. This is a scope-of-claim problem: the title's specificity ("Fibroblasts neurotrophin signaling") outruns what the explant TRK-inhibitor experiments (which perturb the whole tissue, not fibroblasts selectively) can support. **FIXABLE** by narrowing the claim, but as written it is overstated.

Third, a genuine panel blind spot: no reviewer checked whether the reported statistics in Fig. 1G survive multiple-comparison correction across the ~24 group×celltype comparisons; p-values like 0.029 are repeatedly treated as significant findings underpinning the "persistence" narrative. Combined with the denominator issue, this weakens confidence in the paper's foundational claim considerably more than a "3" might suggest.

## Advocate — round 2

Let me address the skeptic's strongest point directly, because it deserves that.

**On the denominator artifact:** this is a legitimate methodological question, but the skeptic overstates how much it threatens the paper. Fig. 1G's density is cells-per-area, not cells-per-total-cellularity — the manuscript states microvascular density is "calculated by enumerating proportion of vascular cells as a function of total surface area of each synovial biopsy," not as a function of total cell count. That is a tissue-area denominator, which does not mechanically inflate with immune depletion the way a total-cellularity denominator would. Fig. 1H is the complementary cell-proportion metric, and both show the same direction. The data_analysis reviewer's concern is real as a request for *additional* clarity — absolute counts alongside both existing metrics — but it is not evidence the reported area-normalized density is contaminated; it's a request the authors can satisfy by explicitly stating what "surface area" excludes/includes. That's a methods-clarification revision, not a collapse of the premise.

**On fibroblast-specificity:** conceded, partially. The title's specificity does outrun the explant data, which perturbs whole tissue. But this is squarely a scope-of-claim fix — soften "fibroblast neurotrophin signaling" to "stromal/fibroblast-associated" pending cell-selective perturbation, exactly as contribution_context's own proposed fix (fibroblast-specific knockout or depletion/add-back) implies. That the fix is well-specified and consistent across reviewers is itself evidence this is a revision item, not a fatal flaw — the mechanistic backbone (NOTCH3→NGF→NGFR/TRK→mural differentiation) is independently demonstrated in isolated co-culture with proper knockdown/rescue logic, which is real evidence of mechanism regardless of which cell supplies ligand in vivo.

**On multiple comparisons:** worth flagging in the published record, but note the key comparisons (capillary/pericyte/VSMC post-treatment vs. pre- and vs. healthy) are directionally consistent across two independent metrics and multiple cell types — that pattern is not what you'd expect from cherry-picked marginal p-values.

None of this is a paper whose central claim doesn't survive its evidence — it's a paper with a well-triangulated mechanism and named, tractable revision items.

## Skeptic — round 2

The advocate's rebuttal on the denominator issue doesn't actually resolve it — it restates the manuscript's own framing. Fig. 1H is described by the authors themselves as "absolute cell proportion changes" (their words, in the figure legend), which is still a proportion of total cells, not an absolute density independent of total cellularity. The advocate claims this cross-validates Fig. 1G because "other lineages" would move oppositely, but no reviewer — and no manuscript text — actually reports those other-lineage denominators changing in the direction needed to rule out the depletion artifact. That's an inference the advocate is supplying, not evidence the panel found in the paper. I'll concede the data_analysis reviewer's point stands undiminished: this is the single number underwriting the title's "sustains," the Discussion's "treatment-resistant disease compartment," and the entire rationale for testing TRK inhibitors on already-mature vasculature. **FIXABLE**, but only by the authors reporting mural-cell counts per mm² tissue area — which is not in the manuscript as submitted, and the advocate has not shown otherwise.

I'll credit the advocate's point that the three mechanistic gaps (direct vs. indirect NGFR transcription, co-receptor vs. ligand-independent TRKA activation, primary vs. contributing pathway) are answerable follow-ups rather than fatal design flaws — that's fair, and I said so in round 1. But the advocate has not engaged at all with the title/scope mismatch: "Fibroblasts neurotrophin signaling sustains pathological vascular maturation" is a causal, cell-type-specific claim that the whole-tissue explant perturbations (DAPT, TRK inhibitors, Fig. 6) cannot isolate to fibroblasts versus endothelial or immune sources — a point scientific_validity raised and the advocate simply didn't answer. **FIXABLE** by retitling/rescoping, not by new data.

Net: nothing here is fatal to the biology, but two claims central to the paper's framing (persistence-despite-treatment as measured, and fibroblast-specific causation as titled) currently outrun the evidence shown, and the advocate's rebuttal doesn't close either gap.
