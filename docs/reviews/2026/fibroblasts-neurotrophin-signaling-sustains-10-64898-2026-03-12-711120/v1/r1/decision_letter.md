# Decision Letter

VERDICT: major

## Summary of Evaluation

This manuscript combines a large spatial-transcriptomic survey of paired pre-/post-treatment RA synovial biopsies (22 patients, 2 healthy donors, ~2M cells) with in vitro co-culture, siRNA/CRISPR perturbation, overexpression, and human synovial explant pharmacology to propose a NOTCH3 → NGF/NGFR → TRK axis that drives fibroblast-to-mural-cell differentiation and sustains pathological vascular maturation in RA. The therapeutic corollary — that larotrectinib and entrectinib reduce mural cell markers and vascular density in human explants — is a genuinely attractive, clinically actionable lead.

The panel was consistent in judging the mechanistic backbone (endothelial NOTCH ligand → fibroblast NGF induction, blocked by DAPT and NOTCH3 loss; receptor-specific knockdown/rescue of pericyte vs VSMC markers; NGFR-dependent sensitisation to low-dose NGF) to be internally coherent and supported by orthogonal assays. That part of the paper is a real contribution and I would recommend it to colleagues working on stromal-vascular crosstalk.

Three problems, however, prevent acceptance as written, and two of them require analysis or data that could change a stated conclusion.

**First, the normalisation underlying the paper's headline claim is not established.** The claim that vascular maturation *persists and increases* despite immunosuppression is the load-bearing empirical result — it justifies the title's "sustains," the framing of the vasculature as a treatment-resistant compartment, and the entire rationale for the TRK-inhibitor experiments. Figure 1G is described in Methods as vascular cells "as a function of total surface area," while Figure 1H is explicitly a proportion of total cells. Because immunosuppression is expected to deplete immune infiltrate, any metric with total cellularity in the denominator can rise without any change in absolute mural cell number. The advocate's defence — that Fig. 1G is area-normalised and therefore immune-independent, and that the two panels cross-validate — could not be confirmed from the text as written, and the debate left the point unresolved. Nowhere does the manuscript report absolute mural or endothelial cell counts per unit tissue area alongside a stated denominator. This is a reanalysis of data you already hold, but its outcome could change the conclusion, which is why this is a major rather than minor decision. The absence of any disclosed family-wise error control across the ~24 comparisons in Fig. 1G compounds the concern for the marginal p-values (0.029, 0.0042).

**Second, the "reversal" of vascular maturation by TRK inhibitors is not distinguished from tissue toxicity.** Two reviewers independently noted that reduced aSMA *and* reduced PECAM1⁺ structures in 3-day explants are equally consistent with loss of viability. The cytotoxicity control (Fig. S9B) is in monolayer fibroblasts, and the concentrations actually used in Fig. 6 are not stated anywhere in the manuscript. A viability/apoptosis readout in the explant system at the exact concentrations used is a modest but genuinely new experiment, and it is required before "reverses pathological vascular maturation" can stand.

**Third, the causal attribution to fibroblasts specifically is broader than the perturbation data.** The advocate conceded this in debate. Figure 6 perturbs whole tissue and cannot exclude endothelial or myeloid neurotrophin sources; the fibroblast-specific evidence comes from isolated co-culture. This is resolvable by narrowing the claim in the title, abstract and discussion — no new experiment needed — but it must be done.

Beyond these, the compliance audits identify an unusually large set of reporting gaps. Most are text-and-file fixes and I have listed the load-bearing ones below; I have deliberately *not* required cell-line STR authentication or mycoplasma certification for primary patient-derived synovial fibroblasts, where standard practice differs, but the HUVEC product identity and the missing ELISA kit, antibody and imaging parameters do need supplying. Two things in this set are more than housekeeping: the bulk RNA-seq that generates the 461-gene NGF/NGFR signature is entirely unspecified (platform, replicates, DE test, threshold) and neither the data nor the gene list is available, so the UCell result in Fig. 5K–M cannot be checked at all; and there is a direct internal contradiction between the Methods ("bulk RNA-seq for the organoid with drug treatment and without drug treatment") and Fig. 5J / the main text (NGFR-overexpressing vs GFP fibroblasts ± NGF). One of these descriptions is wrong, and which one it is determines whether the signature is a fibroblast-intrinsic NGFR programme or an organoid drug-response programme.

Finally, the citation audit plus my own check of the reference list turned up several misattributions in load-bearing positions, notably ref. 33 (Orange et al., NEJM 2020, on PRIME cells) cited for NGF–TRKA binding potentiation by NGFR, and ref. 16 (Domenga et al. 2004) cited as "our previous study." These are text fixes but they matter, because ref. 33 is the sole support offered for the co-receptor mechanism.

Several reviewer requests I judge to be extensions rather than requirements for this paper: ChIP/reporter proof of direct NGFR transactivation, co-immunoprecipitation and binding kinetics, parallel PDGF/Ang1-Tie2/TGFβ inhibition, in vivo TRK inhibition in an arthritis model, and single-cell profiling of converted fibroblasts. They are the right next steps, but the paper does not need them provided the claims are scaled accordingly. Where the current text overreaches — "primary pathway," "critical pathway in sustaining," "transcriptional induction of NGFR" — the wording must be brought into line with what the assays show.

## Required Revisions

1. **Resolve the denominator of the persistence claim.** For every vascular subtype in Fig. 1, report absolute cell counts per unit tissue area (cells/mm² of segmented tissue), with the denominator stated explicitly in both the Methods and the figure legend, and separately report the same quantities as fractions of total cells. Show the corresponding change in non-vascular lineage density so readers can see whether the post-treatment increase in vascular fraction is accompanied by, or explained by, immune depletion. If the area-normalised absolute density does not increase post-treatment, the conclusion and the title must be revised accordingly.

2. **Apply and disclose multiple-comparison control in Fig. 1G–H.** State the number of tests in each family, the correction used, and the corrected significance threshold; report which comparisons survive. Marginal results (p ≈ 0.029) should be described as such.

3. **Distinguish reversal from cytotoxicity in the explant TRK-inhibitor experiments.** State the exact concentrations of larotrectinib, entrectinib, GNF5837, GW441756, ANA-12 and DAPT used in Figs. 6 and S9. Add a viability/apoptosis readout (e.g. TUNEL or cleaved-caspase-3, or a metabolic viability assay on matched explants) at those concentrations in the explant system itself, and report an independent endothelial marker (e.g. VE-cadherin or ERG) alongside PECAM1. If the reduction in PECAM1⁺ structures cannot be separated from tissue-level toxicity, the "reduces pathological vascularization" claim must be withdrawn or restricted to mural marker loss.

4. **Narrow the fibroblast-specific causal claim to match the perturbation data.** Revise the title, one-sentence summary, abstract and discussion so that the whole-tissue explant results are not presented as evidence for a fibroblast-specific source of neurotrophins. Either soften to stromal/fibroblast-associated signalling, or add fibroblast-selective evidence (e.g. NGF neutralisation with concurrent measurement of NGF in fibroblast vs endothelial vs myeloid compartments in the spatial data).

5. **Reconcile and fully specify the bulk RNA-seq underlying the NGF/NGFR signature.** State unambiguously what was sequenced (NGFR-OE vs GFP fibroblasts ± NGF, or drug-treated organoids — the Methods and Fig. 5J currently disagree), the number of biological replicates, library prep, platform, alignment tool and reference build, normalisation, DE test and FDR threshold. Provide the 461-gene list as a supplementary table. Also address the circularity concern explicitly: state in the text that the signature is derived from an NGFR-overexpression system and therefore reports concordance rather than independent validation of endogenous pathway activity.

6. **Deposit the data and provide a code availability statement.** Deposit the Xenium spatial data (all 46 samples, plus the custom-panel dataset) and the bulk RNA-seq in a public repository with accession numbers, and make the analysis scripts available (cell-typing pipeline, QC thresholds, UCell scoring, RNAscope quantification). State QC thresholds (transcripts and features per cell), the batch variable used for Harmony, and random seeds. If patient-level spatial data must be access-controlled, say so and give the controlled-access route.

7. **Report replication structure and n for every panel.** For all in vitro, organoid and explant figures, state the number of independent tissue donors, the number of explants or wells per donor, and how these were treated statistically. Where multiple explants from one donor were used, confirm they were not treated as independent replicates, or reanalyse with donor as the unit of replication.

8. **Validate the NOTCH3 knockout and provide gRNA sequences.** Show knockout at the genotype or protein level (sequencing, or NOTCH3 immunoblot) for the CRISPR line used in Fig. 5E, and list the guide sequence(s).

9. **Temper the NGFR–TRKA mechanism and add the obvious control.** Quantify total TRKA protein in NGFR-overexpressing vs GFP fibroblasts from the existing blots and report it, so readers can judge whether increased pY-TRKA reflects potentiated signalling or increased receptor abundance. Explicitly acknowledge in the text that ligand-independent transactivation is not excluded by the present data, and replace "transcriptional induction of NGFR" in the abstract with wording matching the knockdown-level evidence actually shown.

10. **Correct the misattributed citations.** In particular: replace ref. 33 (Orange et al., NEJM) with the correct source for NGFR-dependent potentiation of NGF–TRKA binding; correct ref. 16, which is cited as "our previous study" but is Domenga et al. 2004; check the refs cited as "(2, 13)" for endothelial control of synovial fibroblast differentiation; and confirm that refs. 36–38 explicitly report pericyte migration defects in TRKB-null and vascular abnormalities in NT3-null animals, or rephrase to what those papers actually show.

11. **Provide cohort description and support the remission-independence statement.** Give patient demographics (age, sex, disease duration, baseline DAS28-ESR, seropositivity), inclusion/exclusion criteria, and the number of patients in each treatment arm. The statement that increased microvascular density occurred "regardless of whether or not patients reached criteria for clinical remission" is currently asserted without data; show the stratified comparison (remitters vs non-remitters, and csDMARD vs TNFi) or remove the claim.

12. **Complete reagent and imaging reporting.** Add: host species, clonality, dilution and RRID (where available) for each antibody, with the target-to-catalogue mapping made explicit (CST #4638 is currently cited for four different receptors); ELISA kit vendor, catalogue number and standard-curve details; HUVEC full product identifier; exposure/gain and illumination settings for the fluorescence and RNAscope imaging, or a statement that acquisition settings were held constant across compared conditions; the nuclear-expansion radius and gating thresholds used in the Cellpose/scikit-image quantification; and software versions for Cellpose, scikit-image, ImageJ, R and Python.

13. **Add a competing interests declaration** (including any relationship to developers of TRK inhibitors), and an explicit statement that informed consent was obtained.

14. **Address the NGF–contractility dissociation in the text.** NGF is presented as the NOTCH3-induced ligand yet has no effect in the collagen contraction assay, while BDNF and NT3 do. Discuss what this implies for the proposed sequence (NOTCH3 → NGF as initiator) rather than leaving it unremarked.

## Minor Suggestions

- Fix internal figure cross-references: the text cites "Fig. S1D, Fig. S1," "Fig. 4S A-B," and directs MYH11/RGS5 knockdown panels to Fig. S6 in one place and Fig. S4 in another; Fig. 6E is cited for aSMA intensity, PECAM1 quantification and vessel counts, where the legend assigns E, F and G separately.
- Gene symbols appear garbled in several places (POOXL, CO34, KOR, POGFRB, MYOCO); correct to PODXL, CD34, KDR, PDGFRB, MYOCD.
- The co-culture ratio is given as 1:3 in the Results and 1:1/1:5/1:10 in the Methods; reconcile.
- Quantify NTRK1 expression against NTRK2/NTRK3 in pericytes and VSMCs (transcripts per cell or normalised counts) rather than describing it as "very low," since the NGF–TRKA arm depends on it being functionally relevant.
- Consider adding a dose–response for the two FDA-approved inhibitors and a note on how the concentrations used compare with clinically achievable plasma/synovial exposures; as written, the repurposing argument invites a pharmacokinetic objection you could pre-empt in one sentence.
- Consider reporting NGF, BDNF and NT3 protein or transcript levels in the healthy/pre-/post-treatment biopsies. This would materially strengthen the in vivo relevance of the axis and is likely achievable from material you already have.
- Direct comparison of the magnitude of aSMA reduction with DAPT versus TRK inhibition in the same explant cohort would help readers judge where in the pathway intervention is most effective.
- The speculation linking fibroblast neurotrophins to sensory nerve coupling is clearly flagged as untested and reads appropriately; keep it labelled as a hypothesis.
- Longer treatment intervals, fibroblast-specific genetic tools, and in vivo TRK inhibition in an arthritis model are the natural next steps; the current limitation paragraph on the 6-month window is candid and welcome, and could be extended to cover the ex vivo-to-in vivo gap.

We would be glad to consider a revised version. The mechanistic core of this work is solid, and if the Fig. 1 normalisation holds up under absolute-density reanalysis and the explant toxicity control comes back clean, this becomes a paper I would expect to be widely read.
