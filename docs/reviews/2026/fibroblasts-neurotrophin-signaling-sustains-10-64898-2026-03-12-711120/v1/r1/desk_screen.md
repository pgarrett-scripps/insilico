# Desk Screen Report

## Summary

This is a spatial transcriptomics study of rheumatoid arthritis (RA) synovial tissue investigating the role of neurotrophin signaling in pathological vascular maturation. The authors use high-dimensional spatial transcriptomics, cell culture, tissue explants, and pharmacological interventions to propose that fibroblast-derived neurotrophins drive abnormal vascular maturation through a NOTCH3-dependent mechanism, and that FDA-approved TRK inhibitors can reverse this process.

## Scope Assessment

**In scope for In Silico:** Yes. This is original research with empirical and mechanistic claims that can be evaluated from the manuscript and its methods. The work combines spatial transcriptomics, molecular biology, and tissue culture—all inspectable approaches. The claims are research-focused, not clinical guidance.

**Not a clinical safety concern:** While the work mentions FDA-approved drugs, it does not propose dosing, clinical trial data, or treatment recommendations. It is mechanistic research suggesting a potential therapeutic avenue, which is appropriate for a research venue.

## Threshold Issues

**Completeness:** The manuscript is complete. Methods are detailed (though dense), figures are provided, and supplementary figures are referenced. Data availability is not explicitly stated, which is a limitation but not grounds for desk rejection.

**Intelligibility:** The manuscript is readable despite PDF conversion artifacts (run-together words, spacing issues noted in the preamble). The scientific narrative is clear: NOTCH3 → NGF induction → neurotrophin receptor signaling → mural cell differentiation → vascular maturation.

**Fundamental flaws:** None evident on first reading. The experimental logic is sound: spatial transcriptomics identifies a phenomenon (persistent vascular maturation post-treatment), mechanistic studies in culture and co-culture test the pathway, tissue explants validate it in human tissue, and pharmacological tools test reversibility.

## Substantive Concerns (Not Desk-Rejection Level)

These are appropriate for full review, not grounds to reject now:

1. **Treatment duration limitation:** The authors acknowledge that 6-month post-treatment biopsies may be too early to see normalization of vasculature. This is a real constraint but does not invalidate the finding that vascular maturation persists at this timepoint.

2. **Mechanistic completeness:** The NOTCH3 → NGF → NGFR/TRK pathway is demonstrated, but some steps rely on pharmacological tools (e.g., DAPT for NOTCH inhibition) rather than genetic validation in all contexts. This is typical and reviewable, not disqualifying.

3. **Explant culture duration:** Synovial explants are cultured for only 3 days. Whether this is sufficient to model in vivo vascular remodeling is a fair question for reviewers, though the authors do show preservation of vascular markers.

4. **TRK inhibitor specificity:** The paper tests two FDA-approved TRK inhibitors (larotrectinib, entrectinib) but does not deeply characterize off-target effects or whether the vascular effects are truly TRK-specific vs. broader kinase inhibition. This is a limitation worth noting but not a fatal flaw.

5. **Data availability:** The manuscript does not state whether spatial transcriptomics data, RNA-seq data, or code will be deposited. This should be clarified but is not grounds for desk rejection if the authors commit to it.

## Strengths Supporting Acceptance

- **Novel finding:** Neurotrophin signaling in RA vascular maturation appears to be a new observation, not previously well-characterized in this context.
- **Multi-modal validation:** Spatial transcriptomics, RNAscope, immunohistochemistry, cell culture, co-culture, tissue explants, and pharmacological tools all converge on the same pathway.
- **Translational relevance:** Testing FDA-approved drugs (larotrectinib, entrectinib) is pragmatic and potentially impactful.
- **Honest reporting:** The authors acknowledge limitations (6-month timepoint, explant culture duration) and do not overstate claims.

## Minor Issues

- PDF conversion has left some formatting artifacts (acknowledged in preamble; not the authors' fault).
- Some figures are dense and could benefit from clearer legends, but this is a presentation issue, not a scientific one.
- The paper is long but not excessively so for the scope of work presented.

---

## DESK DECISION: proceed

This manuscript presents sound, multi-modal evidence for a novel pathway in RA vascular pathology and tests it with translational tools. The claims are appropriately scaled to the evidence, limitations are acknowledged, and the work is complete enough for peer review. Send to full review.
