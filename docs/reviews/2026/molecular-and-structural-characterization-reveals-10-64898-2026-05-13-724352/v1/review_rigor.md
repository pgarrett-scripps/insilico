# Rigor & Overclaiming Reviewer

## Summary
The paper's central claims — that AD organoid EVs differ from WT in size distribution, multilamellar morphology, and that co-culture transfers endosomal material and increases WT amyloid — are asserted in the abstract and title at a strength the presented evidence does not license. The size-distribution claim is contradicted by the authors' own text ('trended towards a wider, though not significantly different, size distribution'), and the co-culture claim conflates co-purifying Aco-650 dye with transferred endosomes and a correlational A-beta increase with transfer. The careful hedging in the body is largely abandoned in the abstract and title, which is where overclaiming concentrates.

## Strengths
- The body text is substantially more careful than the abstract, hedging the size difference as a non-significant trend and the co-culture result as 'suggest' — this candour is worth preserving.
- The proteomic analysis is presented with explicit thresholds (FC ≥ 2, adj p ≤ 0.05) and the autophagy-machinery interpretation is flagged as a hypothesis.
- The study uses an isogenic WT/PSEN1 pair, which isolates the mutation's effect from genetic background.

## Weaknesses
- HARD — Title/abstract: 'AD organoid-derived EVs have a wider size distribution' is contradicted by the authors' own text: 'they trended towards a wider, though not significantly different, size distribution' (Fig 4H). The abstract states as fact what the Results report as a non-significant trend.
- HARD — Abstract: 'After a 1-week co-culture of AD organoids with WT organoids, there is evidence of endosomal membrane transfer between the isogenic cultures with an increase in amyloid-β peptides in the WT organoids.' The Aco-650 signal in WT organoids is not established as transferred endosomes: the dye is a small fluorophore that can be released from the stained AD organoid, diffuse through shared media, and be taken up by the WT organoid independently of any vesicle. The authors' own design (shared well, full media changes) cannot exclude this. The A-beta increase is correlational and not tied to the dye transfer.
- HARD — Abstract: 'These observations support the notion that non-cell-autonomous spread of amyloid-containing EVs in human AD brains can be modeled in a cerebral organoid system.' The co-culture experiment never demonstrates that A-beta moved inside EVs; it shows a dye signal and an A-beta increase in the same well. The claim that this models EV-mediated spread is not supported by the evidence presented.
- SOFT — 'EVs purified from AD organoid conditioned media have a wider size distribution' (abstract) also conflicts with the vFC data (Fig 3), which reports similar size ranges for WT and AD (~85–270 nm, median 124 nm for both). The wider-distribution claim rests on the cryo-TEM subset (24 vs 65 EVs), a small and possibly biased sample, and is not corroborated by the flow cytometry.
- SOFT — The claim that AD EVs 'show differential expression of tetraspanins CD63, CD9, and CD81' (abstract) is not supported by the vFC data, which report TS expression at or below the limit of detection for both genotypes; the differential expression rests on the proteomics (CD9 up in AD), not on the flow cytometry, and the abstract does not distinguish these.
- SOFT — The multilamellar-vesicle claim ('AD organoid-derived EVs can have single, double, and even triple membranes') is based on a small cryo-TEM subset (63 AD micrographs positive for vesicles) without quantification of the frequency of multilamellar forms in each genotype; 'can have' is defensible but the implied divergence is not quantified.
- SOFT — The fibrillar material in AD EVs is described as 'amyloid-like' and 'resembling PHF-type tau' in the body, but the abstract's 'luminal fibrillar material' is presented without the caveat that its identity is unconfirmed; the authors themselves note immunogold and cryo-ET are needed.
- SOFT — The claim that 'EVs can transfer from AD to WT organoids' (section heading) overstates the evidence, which shows dye co-localization and an A-beta increase, not demonstrated vesicle transfer.

## Questions
- Can you report the Aco-650 signal in WT organoids from a control where the dye is added to media without an AD organoid, and from a transwell/separate-well design that prevents direct contact while sharing conditioned media — to distinguish vesicle-mediated transfer from free-dye diffusion?
- Can you quantify the frequency of multilamellar vesicles and fibril-containing vesicles per genotype from the cryo-TEM data, rather than presenting representative micrographs?
- Can you reconcile the abstract's 'wider size distribution' claim with the vFC data showing similar size ranges for WT and AD, and with the non-significant cryo-TEM trend?
