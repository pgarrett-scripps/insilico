# Rigor & Overclaiming Reviewer

## Summary
This is a well-executed descriptive characterization of EVs from isogenic WT and PSEN1-M146V cerebrocortical organoids, with genuinely useful descriptive findings (cryo-TEM morphology, proteomics, dye binding). Its central mechanistic claim — that the co-culture experiment demonstrates EV-mediated, non-cell-autonomous spread of amyloid — is not supported by the design, which cannot isolate EVs as the vector. The abstract also states as established fact several results the body reports as non-significant trends or below-detection signals. The descriptive core is sound and worth publishing, but the headline claims need requalification and the co-culture needs controls before the mechanistic interpretation can stand.

## Strengths
- The isogenic single-mutation design cleanly isolates the PSEN1-M146V effect from genetic background, a genuine strength for an EV study.
- The multi-modal characterization (vFC, cryo-TEM, proteomics, dye binding) is thorough and the authors are generally candid about limitations (e.g., ThT/RNA, non-significant size trend).
- Proteomics data and code are deposited, supporting reproducibility.

## Weaknesses
- HARD — the co-culture experiment does not support EV-mediated transfer. The abstract's closing claim ('non-cell-autonomous spread of amyloid-containing EVs in human AD brains can be modeled') and the section heading 'EVs can transfer from AD to WT organoids' rest on two observations: Aco-650 fluorescence appearing in WT organoids, and increased Aβ XP puncta in WT+AD co-cultures. Aco-650 is a small-molecule fluorogenic dye, not an EV marker; free dye released into the shared medium (or from dying cells) would be taken up by WT cells and produce the identical result. Likewise, the increased WT Aβ signal could be endogenous upregulation driven by any secreted factor or co-culture stress in the shared medium, not transferred Aβ. Nothing in the design distinguishes EV-mediated transfer from soluble-factor or free-dye transfer. A transwell that permits soluble factors but blocks vesicle contact, or an EV-depleted medium control, would isolate the vector; a species- or label-specific Aβ tracer would distinguish transferred from induced material. As worded, this is the paper's central claim and it is not established.
- HARD — the abstract states as fact what the Results report as non-significant or below-detection. 'EVs purified from AD organoid conditioned media have a wider size distribution' contradicts Results 3.3 ('trended towards a wider, though not significantly different, size distribution'; n=24 vs 65) and vFC, which found similar sizes. 'Differential expression of tetraspanins CD63, CD9, and CD81' contradicts the vFC finding that WT and AD EVs were 'phenotypically similar, exhibiting very low levels of tetraspanin expression (>20 ABC per EV)' at or below the limit of detection; proteomics shows CD9 up but not CD63/CD81. Both abstract sentences should be requalified to match the body.
- SOFT — the abstract converts immunostaining into peptide quantification: 'an increase in amyloid-β peptides in the WT organoids' is actually an increase in Aβ XP immunoreactive puncta ('cumulative Aβ XP segment volume per cell'), which is not a peptide measurement and could reflect aggregate or cross-reactive signal.
- SOFT — the Discussion's secretory-autophagy mechanism ('the defect lies upstream of amphisome formation, most likely at the level of autophagosome biogenesis via ATG5/ATG7'; 'aberrantly packaged into conventional CD9-enriched exosomes') is a causal narrative inferred from co-occurring protein abundances in a correlational proteomics comparison, with no functional test. The abstract's 'evidence for modifications' is appropriately hedged; the Discussion is not.
- SOFT — the abstract's generalization from a single isogenic donor line (one male donor, one mutation) to 'human AD brains' is unhedged; the single genetic background is acknowledged in Methods but not near the claim.
- SOFT — the luminal fibrils are described as 'resembling PHF-type tau,' but the authors themselves note 2D class averaging yielded no identifiable features; the amyloid identity of the fibrils is not established, and the ThT signal (which binds RNA, as acknowledged) does not independently confirm it.
- Pseudoreplication: the co-culture Aβ quantification uses images (n=27 WT+AD, n=49 WT+WT) as the unit of analysis rather than independent organoids; multiple images per organoid inflate n. Attribute to the data-analysis reviewer.

## Questions
- What control distinguishes EV-mediated transfer of Aco-650 from free-dye or soluble-factor transfer — e.g., a transwell insert, or co-culture with EV-depleted conditioned medium?
- Can you show the increased WT Aβ signal is transferred material rather than induced endogenous production, e.g., with a labeled Aβ tracer or species-specific detection?
- Please report the size-distribution comparison with its p-value and the TS ABC values for both genotypes, since the abstract presents these as established differences the body reports as non-significant or below LOD.
