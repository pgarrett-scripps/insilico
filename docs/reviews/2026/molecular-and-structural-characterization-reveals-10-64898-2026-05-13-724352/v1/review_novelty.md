# Novelty & Contribution Reviewer

## Summary
The core contribution — a multi-modal characterization of EVs from PSEN1-M146V vs. isogenic-WT cerebro organisational organoids, including cryo-EM evidence of multilamellar vesicles and luminal fibrillar material — survives my related-work and preprint searches and appears genuinely new. The manuscript cites the closest prior work (Fowler et al. 2025 on tau filaments in brain EVs; Liu et al. 2025a/b, Silver et al. 2025, Forero et al. 2024 on organoid EVs), and I found no preempting preprint. The contribution is real but its significance is framed at a higher altitude than the evidence supports, chiefly the 'non-in-autonomous spread of amyloid' claim, which outruns what the co-culture experiment demonstrates.

## Strengths
- The isogenic PSEN1-M146V vs. WT design cleanly isolates the effect of a single mutation on EV phenotype, which the prior organoid-EV papers (which are not AD-mutation-focused) do not.
- The cryo-EM observation of multilamellar vesicles and fibrillar luminal material in AD organoid EVs is a concrete, novel structural finding with a clear delta over Fowler et al. (brain-derived EVs) — organoid-derived EVs as a tractable, longitudinal source.
- The manuscript is honest about what it did not find (no tau spread in co-culture), which strengthens the credibility of the claims it does make.

## Weaknesses
- The 'non-cell-autonomous spread of amyloid' claim outruns the evidence: the co-culture experiment shows (a) the transfer of Aco-650 endosomal label from AD to WT organ acts and (b) increased amyloid signal in co-cultured WT organ acts, but neither establishes spread of pathology — the Aco-650 transfer shows membrane/endosome transfer, not that the transferred material is amyloid-bearing, and the increased signal in WT could reflect uptake of amyloid monomer/oligomer rather than templated aggregation. The claim of 'modeling non-cell-autonomous spread of amyloid-containing EVs' should be scaled to 'transfer of endosomal material and amyloid-associated signal,' with the templating interpretation explicitly flagged as unproven.
- The 'divergent EV profiles' claim rests partly on a comparison that may not isolate genotype: quantification of EV count and size distribution is not normalized to cell number or organoid mass, so the 2x difference may reflect more cells or more secretion rather than the mutation itself.
- The fibrillar material in AD EVs is not molecularly identified — no immunogold, cryo-ET, or proteomic confirmation identifies it as tau, amyloid, or anything else; molecular identification would strengthen the novel claim.

## Questions
- Were EV counts and concentrations normalized to organoid number, mass, or cell count, and if not, can the 2x EV concentration difference (3.1 vs 1.6 e9/mL) be attributed to the mutation rather than culture size?
- Can the luminal fibrils in AD EVs be identified (immunogold or targeted proteomics), and do they co-localize with the transferred Aco-650 signal in co-cultured WT organoids?
- What is the biological negative control for the ThT/AmyTracker signal — do EVs from a non-AD or non-neuronal source show comparable binding?
