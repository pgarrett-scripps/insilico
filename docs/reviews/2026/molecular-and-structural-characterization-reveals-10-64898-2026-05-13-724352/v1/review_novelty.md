# Novelty & Contribution Reviewer

## Summary
The core contributions — isogenic PSEN1 AD vs WT cerebrocortical organoid EV characterization, cryo-TEM multilamellar/fibrillar structures, and the organoid-to-organoid transfer experiment — appear genuinely new; my related-work and preprint searches (queries: "extracellular vesicles cerebral organoids Alzheimer's," "tau filaments extracellular vesicles Alzheimer's cryo-EM," "brain organoid extracellular vesicle proteomics," "amyloid beta exosome transfer neuron-to-neuron organoid," plus the manuscript's own cited neighbors) found no preempting work. The delta over the closest prior work (Forero et al. 2024 on developmental EV trafficking; Liu et al. 2025a/b and Silver et al. 2025 on organoid EVs; Fowler et al. 2025 on tau filaments in AD brain EVs) is real and stated. However, the central contribution is framed at an altitude the evidence does not support: the co-culture experiment is presented as modeling EV-mediated spread of amyloid, yet nothing in it isolates EVs as the transfer vector.

## Strengths
- The isogenic PSEN1M146V/WT design cleanly isolates the effect of a single mutation on EV phenotype, a genuine advance over prior organoid-EV work that compared developmental stages or regional identities rather than disease genotype.
- The manuscript names its nearest neighbors (Forero, Liu, Silver, Fowler, Sardar Sinha) and its delta over them is concrete rather than cosmetic.
- The cryo-TEM finding of multilamellar vesicles and luminal fibrils in AD organoid EVs, absent in WT, is a specific, checkable observation that extends Fowler et al.'s brain-tissue result into a live organoid system.

## Weaknesses
- The contribution as stated — "non-cell-autonomous spread of amyloid-containing EVs" modeled in organoids — is not established by the evidence offered. The co-culture experiment (Fig. 7) shows Aco-650 endosomal-membrane transfer and increased intra-tissue Aβ puncta in WT organoids, but nothing distinguishes EV-mediated transfer from free Aβ, soluble oligomers, or other non-vesicular particles in the shared medium. The Aco-650 dye labels endosomes broadly, not EVs specifically, and the Aβ increase in WT organoids could equally arise from uptake of free Aβ42 secreted by the AD organoid (which the authors themselves show is abundant in the media). As a novelty claim, "EV-mediated spread" is the load-bearing part, and the experiment does not isolate it. What would settle it: an EV-depleted co-culture control (e.g., size-exclusion or ultracentrifugation of the AD-conditioned medium before co-culture), or a labeled-EV-only (not whole-organoid) transfer experiment. Without this, the claim should be demoted to "membrane and Aβ transfer between co-cultured organoids," which is itself novel and sufficient.
- The "novel extracellular biomarker signature of failed AEV biogenesis" (RAB11/RAB8/STX3 without LC3 cargo) is a strong interpretive claim built on a single biological replicate. The proteomics is n=1 organoid pair with 3 technical replicates (Fig. 5, PCA in Supp. Fig. 4), and the signature rests on a handful of proteins, several below the stated FC≥2 threshold (RAB11B at 1.86-fold, adj. p=0.056; ATG5/ATG7 at ~1.1–1.3-fold). As a contribution, the signature is new, but its significance is overstated relative to the evidence base; it should be framed as hypothesis-generating pending a second biological replicate, or the claim scaled down.
- The "first" framing is implicit rather than explicit, which weakens the priority claim's auditability. The abstract claims "a thorough characterization" and "divergent EV profiles" without stating what is claimed as novel relative to Forero/Liu/Silver. Given that EV characterization of organoids is an active area (multiple 2024–2025 papers), the authors should state explicitly what is new (the isogenic AD comparison, the cryo-TEM morphology, the transfer experiment) so the delta is testable.
- The MLV/fibril observation is presented without acknowledging that MLVs were already reported in PD patient CSF EVs (Emelyanov et al. 2020) and tau filaments in AD brain EVs (Fowler et al. 2025), so the "divergent morphology" claim should position itself as an extension into organoids rather than a first observation of the phenomenon.
- The claim that AD EVs show "low TS expression" and thus are not predominantly MVB-derived is presented as a phenotype, but the vFC assay's TS detection limit (~38 ABC) means "low" may be an assay-floor artifact rather than a biological difference; this is partly methodology's call but it undercuts the biogenesis interpretation the authors draw.
- The tau-in-flow-through vs Aβ-in-eluate split (Fig. 2C/D) is used to infer distinct EV subpopulations, but the authors do not test whether tau is simply weakly bound to TS+ EVs and stripped during the IP wash; a wash-fraction analysis would separate these accounts.
- The ThT signal in both WT and AD EV isolates (Fig. 6A) is presented as amyloid evidence, but ThT also binds RNA and other polymers (Sugimoto et al. 2015, cited by the authors); the AmyTracker STED data partially addresses this, but the bulk ThT claim should be qualified.
- The co-culture Aβ quantification (Fig. 7D) is reported as a per-cell segment volume with n=27 vs n=49 images, but the number of independent organoids and biological replicates is not stated, which limits how much the transfer claim can be generalized.

## Questions
- In the co-culture experiment, was the AD-conditioned medium ever depleted of EVs before transfer, or was any non-vesicular Aβ control run, to test whether the WT Aβ increase requires intact EVs?
- How many independent organoid differentiations (biological replicates) underlie the proteomics and the co-culture quantification, respectively?
- For the Aco-650 transfer, is the dye confirmed to be EV-associated in the medium, or could it transfer as free dye or via non-EV particles?
