# Methodology Reviewer

## Summary
The paper's central claims — that AD organoid EVs are morphologically and molecularly distinct from WT EVs, and that EVs mediate non-cell-autonomous transfer of amyloid pathology — rest on designs that do not yet exclude the most plausible alternative explanations. The co-culture experiment, in particular, does not isolate EVs as the vehicle of transfer, and the cryo-TEM morphology comparison uses unequal sampling that cannot support the claimed AD-specific features. The purification and characterization pipeline is otherwise careful, with appropriate negative controls (TS- beads, permeate, detergent treatment) that many EV papers omit. The proteomics is a strength, but the mechanistic inference about secretory autophagy outruns what EV cargo can establish.

## Strengths
- The EV purification workflow includes appropriate negative controls (EV-depleted permeate, TS- beads, detergent-treated vFC samples) that are frequently absent from EV literature.
- The vFC assay follows MISEV guidance with dilution series and calibration, and the authors honestly report that TS expression is at the detection floor rather than overinterpreting it.
- The isogenic WT/AD organoid pair is a well-designed system for isolating the effect of the PSEN1 mutation.

## Weaknesses
- The co-culture experiment does not establish EV-mediated transfer (HARD). The claim that 'endosomal membrane transfer' and increased Abeta in WT organoids result from EV-mediated spread is not earned by the design. Aco-650 is a lipophilic membrane-intercalating dye; nothing in the design excludes the possibility that dye leaches from the stained AD organoid into the media and is taken up directly by the WT organoid, or that the increased Abeta XP signal reflects the WT organoid upregulating endogenous Abeta production in response to co-culture stress rather than receiving transferred Abeta. The WT+WT co-culture controls for co-culture stress generally, but not for free-dye transfer or for Abeta transfer versus induction. A transwell co-culture (physical separation, shared media) or a conditioned-media control would distinguish EV-mediated transfer from free-dye/diffusible-factor transfer. Additionally, the Abeta quantification ('cumulative Abeta XP segment volume per cell') is not described as blinded; the segmentation threshold and nuclei normalization are subjective steps that should be performed blind to condition.
- The AD-specific EV morphology claim rests on unequal sampling (HARD). The cryo-TEM comparison examined 24 WT EVs versus 65 AD EVs. The claim that multilamellar vesicles and luminal fibrils are AD-specific features is not supported when the WT sample is less than half the size of the AD sample — absence in WT could be sampling. The authors themselves note the size difference 'trended towards a wider, though not significantly different' distribution, which is honest, but the MLV and fibril claims are stated as divergent features without the same hedge. The free-floating fibrils 'co-purified with the AD EVs' could be purification contamination rather than EV cargo, which the authors acknowledge but do not resolve. Reporting the fibril/MLV frequency per vesicle examined, with confidence intervals, would address whether the difference is real.
- The secretory autophagy inference outruns the evidence (HARD). The claim that AD EVs reveal 'failed AEV biogenesis' with 'the defect lies upstream of amphisome formation, most likely at the level of autophagosome biogenesis via ATG5/ATG7' is a strong causal inference about intracellular autophagy status drawn from extracellular vesicle cargo. EV content reflects what is secreted, not necessarily the intracellular state of the secreting cell — reduced LC3 in EVs could reflect reduced secretion of LC3-bearing vesicles, altered packaging, or differential EV subpopulation proportions, none of which establish an intracellular autophagosome biogenesis defect. The authors have organoid lysates available (used for Western blots in Figure 2A); direct measurement of ATG5/ATG7 and LC3 in the lysates would test the intracellular claim. As stated, the design cannot distinguish 'autophagy is impaired' from 'autophagy is normal but its EV packaging is altered.'
- EV counts are not normalized to cell number or viability. The vFC finding that AD media contains ~2x more EVs than WT could reflect more dying cells in AD organoids (the authors themselves interpret reduced L1CAM as 'indicative of early neuronal cell death') rather than a disease-specific EV secretion phenotype. Normalizing EV counts to organoid cell number, protein content, or a viability measure would address this.
- Batch effects are not addressed. The methods state conditioned media was pooled 'from the same differentiation batch' (Figure 1 legend), but it is unclear whether WT and AD organoids were from matched batches. If WT and AD EVs come from different differentiation runs, batch effects could confound every comparative measurement. The authors should state whether WT and AD organoids were differentiated in parallel and whether EV isolations were interleaved.
- The TS expression comparison operates at the measurement floor. The text reports 'very low levels of tetraspanin expression (>20 ABC per EV)' while the LOD is ~38 ABC per vesicle — the measured values are below the detection limit. The claim that AD and WT EVs are 'phenotypically similar' in TS expression is therefore a claim that both are undetectable, not that they are similar. This should be stated as such.
- The ThT assay lacks a protein-matched negative control. The Lipo100 control is a reasonable membrane control, but the ~25x signal could reflect EV protein content (ThT binds RNA and other biological polymers) rather than amyloid specifically. The HMW protein fraction is shown in Figure 6A but the comparison to it is not discussed; if the HMW fraction also shows elevated ThT signal, the amyloid specificity claim weakens.
- The AmyTracker STED FWHM measurement uses one representative punctum per condition. The size estimate from a single punctum does not establish the size distribution of amyloid-positive vesicles.

## Questions
- Were WT and AD organoids differentiated in parallel, and were EV isolations from the two genotypes interleaved or run in separate batches?
- In the co-culture experiment, was the Abeta XP quantification performed blind to condition, and was the segmentation threshold fixed a priori?
- What is the EV count per organoid (or per cell) rather than per mL of concentrated media, and how does viability compare between WT and AD organoids at the time of media collection?
- Does the HMW protein fraction from the SEC column show elevated ThT signal comparable to the EV isolate?
