# Debate Transcript

## Advocate — round 1

The manuscript makes four genuine contributions that outweigh its presentational and methodological shortcomings — each is new, checkable, and advances the field beyond what the closest prior work achieves. (1) The isogenic PSEN1 AD vs WT cerebrocortical organoid EV characterization is, to the reviewers’ own searches, genuinely new. Every prior organoid-EV paper (Forero 2024, Liu 2025a/b, Silver 2025) compared developmental stages or regional identities, not disease genotype. This is the first head-to-head isogenic AD-vs-WT EV comparison in organoids, and that alone earns publication. (2) The cryo-TEM finding of multilamellar vesicles and luminal fibrils in AD organoid EVs (Fig. 4I) extends Fowler et al.’s brain-homogenate result into a live, growing organoid system where the same pathology can be tracked longitudinally. The skeptic is right that the sampling is uneven (24 vs 65 EVs) and that frequency counts with CIs are needed. But the observation is real, specific, and reproducible — it is not overclaimed as a statistical comparison, and the authors flag the non-significant size trend honestly. (3) The proteomics data (PXD076102) is deposited with detailed DIA-NN and MSstats parameters, putting it ahead of most EV proteomics papers in reproducibility. The failed-AEV-biogenesis signature (RAB11/RAB8/STX3 without LC3 cargo) is hypothesis-generating, as the skeptic notes, but it is a testable pattern extracted from rich data and framed with appropriate caveats. (4) The co-culture transfer experiment (Fig. 7) shows, for the first time in any organoid system, that endosomal membrane material from an AD organoid appears inside a WT organoid after shared culture, and that this correlates with increased intra-tissue Aβ immunoreactivity in the WT. The skeptic correctly notes that free dye or soluble Aβ could produce the same result, and I concede this: the mechanistic claim (“EV-mediated spread”) outruns the design. But the phenomenological observation — membrane transfer between organoids co-occurs with an Aβ increase — is itself new and worth reporting as a model system observation, provided the interpretation is requalified to “membrane and Aβ transfer” rather than “EV-mediated spread.”

The clarity and rigor reviewers are right that the abstract overclaims (size difference, TS expression). These are fixable with rewritten abstract and figure citations in revision. The core descriptive work is sound; the overclaiming is in the framing, not the data. Accept with revision requirements to requalify the transfer claim, add biological replication to the proteomics, and correct the citation errors.

**Key points:**
- First isogenic AD-vs-WT cerebrocortical organoid EV characterization — genuinely new contribution.
- Cryo-TEM reveals MLVs and luminal fibrils in AD organoid EVs, extending Fowler et al.’s brain-tissue finding into live organoids.
- Proteomics is deposited (PXD076102) with unusual methodological detail, supporting reproducibility.
- Co-culture transfer experiment is phenomenological, not mechanistic — conceding the EV-mediated spread claim is unsupported, but the membrane-transfer observation itself is novel.
- Overclaiming and presentation errors are fixable in revision; the descriptive core is sound.
