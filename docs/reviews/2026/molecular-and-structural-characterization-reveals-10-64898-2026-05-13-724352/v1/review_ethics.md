# Ethics & Compliance Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript is clean on the two checks that would normally raise alarms — funding is disclosed and the conflict-of-interest statement is unusually thorough. The problem is entirely on the human-material side: the experiments rest on iPSC lines "derived from a male donor" (Section 2.1), yet no ethics approval, no consent statement, and no determination that the work is exempt from human-subjects review appear anywhere in the text. All of this is fixable in a short paragraph, which is why the concern is a revision rather than a rejection — but it must be stated, not assumed.

## Strengths
- Funding is fully and specifically acknowledged with grant numbers.
- The conflict-of-interest statement is detailed and appears to cover all authors with relevant relationships.
- The cell line provenance is at least described (Tessier-Lavigne laboratory, New York Stem Cell Foundation, with a citation to the derivation paper) — a step that many manuscripts of this kind omit.

## Weaknesses
- **Load-bearing claim 1 — human-derived cells used without an ethics/consent statement.** The entire study rests on hiPSC lines "derived from a male donor" (Section 2.1), obtained from the Tessier-Lavigne laboratory and the New York Stem Cell Foundation. No sentence states (a) the original derivation received informed consent / IRB approval of the donor, or (b) the present laboratory use was reviewed. Under the standard that silence is not compliance, the absence of any statement is a hard finding, not a shrug. The plain alternative explanation for the omission — the donors were consented at the NYSCF and the current work falls under standard cell-line reuse — may be true, but the reader cannot verify it, and the manuscript as written is not, on its face, in compliance. The one thing that would settle it: a statement in Methods or Acknowledgements explicitly noting informed consent for donor-sourced lines, naming the derivation body and giving evidence of the receiving site classification of this reuse. It is a one-paragraph amendment but a required one.
- **Load-bearing claim — continuous scope of secondary use.** The organoid EV work goes beyond the original cell-line derivation: the cells are being used in organoid and a related protocol the donor likely never encountered, and the manuscript gives no material-transfer or biobank statement covering the ISCI lines. If the original repository consent is restricted, the research is conducted and the claimed de-identification and evidence disappears. The alternative (the original consent is broad or the reuse is covered by the NYSCF Material Distribution Agreement, which is common) also exists. A line must specify that the requisite scope of reuse is covered; before that, the entire model rests on the provenance of the cells. The change: confirm for the existing repository that the cell lines' use is in agreement with the MTA and donor; the word "male donor" is not anonymity for personal data — and this sentence gives a λεγmen.

## Questions
- What is the explicit sentence at Methods (or its same level) that covers (a) the original donor consent for the hiPSC derivation and (b) that the current reuse/EV study falls under the scope of that consent or an IRB "not human subjects research" determination by the receiving institution?
- Doyou have the protocol or MTA identifier from Tessier-Lavigne/NYSCF that documents the applicable informed-consent limits? (A simple citation to that protocol would resolve both of my stated claims.)
