# Citation Integrity Auditor

## Summary
I audited citation integrity for this manuscript. The reference list contains 69 entries (some merged/truncated in conversion). The manuscript cites several 'as previously described' protocols (organoid differentiation, EV purification, FASP proteomics) that are load-bearing for reproducibility but not fully written out. Several references are cited for specific claims (e.g., Rajendran 2006 for Abeta in EVs, Saman 2012 for tau in EVs, Fowler 2025 for tau filaments in EVs, Paquet 2016 for the isogenic lines, Labra 2026 for organoid phenotypes). I could not verify the contents of most references from the manuscript alone, so I marked them unverifiable rather than asserting error. No retracted/predatory sources were identifiable. No conspicuous self-citation inflation beyond normal. Quotation/number fidelity could not be checked for most sources.

## Unverifiable (raise as questions)
- **[Reference resolvability] Protocol provenance citations resolve to specific published protocols** — Methods cite 'a previously established protocol' (Labra et al. 2026) for organoid differentiation, 'according to the manufacturer's instructions' for TFF/qEV columns, and 'a modified filter aided sample preparation protocol' (Turner et al. 2022; Wisniewski et al. 2009) for proteomics. These are load-bearing for reproducibility but their contents cannot be confirmed from the manuscript alone.
- **[Reference resolvability] Isogenic hiPSC line provenance (Paquet et al. 2016)** — Methods state lines 'were obtained from the Tessier-Lavigne laboratory and New York Stem Cell Foundation (Paquet et al. 2016)'. The reference is cited but its contents (that these specific lines derive from that work) cannot be confirmed from the manuscript alone.
- **[Reference resolvability] Reference list completeness/parse integrity** — The reference list block shows several entries merged or truncated in conversion (e.g., Forero 2024 entry runs into Fowler 2025; Labra 2026 runs into Li 2025; Wang 2017 runs into Welsh 2023). This is a parse artifact, not evidence of missing references, but it means full resolvability of every entry cannot be confirmed from the provided block.
- **[Claim-citation support] Abeta peptides are released in association with exosomes (Rajendran et al. 2006)** — Discussion states 'Abeta is known to be present in EVs (Rajendran et al. 2006)'. The claim is plausible and the reference is a well-known study, but its specific contents cannot be confirmed from the manuscript alone.
- **[Claim-citation support] Tau is secreted in exosomes and phosphorylated in CSF in early AD (Saman et al. 2012)** — Discussion states tau 'was observed by Saman et al. in M1C cells and in the CSF of AD patients (Saman et al. 2012)'. The claim is plausible but not confirmable from the manuscript alone.
- **[Claim-citation support] Tau filaments are tethered within brain EVs in AD (Fowler et al. 2025)** — Discussion states 'Fowler and coworkers were the first to show tau filaments present within EVs purified from AD brain homogenates (Fowler et al. 2025)'. The reference is cited but its contents cannot be confirmed from the manuscript alone.
- **[Claim-citation support] PSEN1 M146V organoids exhibit higher Abeta, p-tau, and hyperexcitability (Labra et al. 2026)** — Introduction and Discussion attribute the organoid phenotypes (higher Abeta peptides, phosphorylated tau, neuronal hyperexcitability) to Labra et al. 2026. This is the companion paper; its contents cannot be confirmed from the manuscript alone.
- **[Quotation/number fidelity] Numerical/statistical values attributed to sources** — No direct quotations or specific statistics from cited sources are reproduced in the text that could be checked against the originals. All quantitative claims are the authors' own measurements. Quotation/number fidelity could not be assessed.

- **[Retracted / predatory sources] Retracted or predatory cited sources** — No cited work was identifiable as retracted or from a known predatory venue. All references appear to be from established journals.
