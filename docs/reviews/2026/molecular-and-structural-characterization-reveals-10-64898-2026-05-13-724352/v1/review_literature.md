# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The citation record is broadly sound and current, with the foundational EV-amyloid literature (Rajendran 2006, Saman 2012, Sardar Sinha 2018) and the directly relevant recent work (Fowler 2025 tau filaments in brain EVs; Halipi 2024 EV inhibition of Aβ aggregation) properly cited. The main citation-level concern is that the paper's central novelty claim — non-cell-autonomous spread of amyloid via EVs between organoids — is asserted without a directly competing prior-work comparison, and the load-bearing interpretation of the co-culture experiment rests on a causal attribution the cited evidence does not establish. I could not verify several attributions through my search tools (which routed to arXiv rather than biomedical databases), so those are flagged for author confirmation rather than asserted as errors.

## Strengths
- The foundational and directly relevant prior work (Rajendran 2006; Saman 2012; Sardar Sinha 2018; Fowler 2025; Halipi 2024) is cited, and the discussion engages it substantively rather than as decoration.
- The reference list is current for a fast-moving area, with 2023–2025 EV-organoid and EV-amyloid work represented (Liu 2025a/b, Silver 2025, Forero 2024, Fowler 2025, Mao 2025).
- Self-citation is proportionate: the organoid protocol (Labra 2026) is the authors' own and is cited as the source of the model, which is appropriate.

## Weaknesses
- Load-bearing. The paper's central claim — that co-culturing a WT organoid with an AD organoid transfers endosomal material and "potentially pathogenic amyloid-β peptides," implying EV-mediated non-cell-autonomous spread — is not established by the evidence cited. The Aco-650 transfer (Fig. 7B) shows membrane transfer but not that it is EV-mediated: the dye could transfer via any secreted vesicle, free dye carryover, or direct cell contact, and the authors state only that Aco-650 "intercalates into endosome membranes," not that it is EV-exclusive. The increased Aβ puncta in co-cultured WT organoids (Fig. 7C–D) is correlative; nothing in the cited data distinguishes EV-mediated Aβ transfer from the WT organoid's own upregulated Aβ production in response to diffusible factors, or from the AD organoid's proximity. The one-week co-culture with full media changes every other day does not exclude soluble Aβ or non-EV particles as the vector. The claim in the abstract that these observations "support the notion that non-cell-autonomous spread of amyloid-containing EVs... can be modeled" outruns the evidence: to support it, the authors would need to show the transferred material is inside EVs (e.g., EV-depleted media control, or fluorescent Aβ tracked within Aco-650+ vesicles) and that the WT Aβ increase is causally linked to that transfer rather than to bystander effects.
- Sweep. The novelty claim of EV-mediated inter-organoid amyloid spread is asserted without a comparison to directly competing prior work on EV-mediated Aβ propagation (e.g., Sardar Sinha 2018, which is cited but not positioned against the organoid result) — a completeness gap I could not fully resolve because my searches returned no directly competing organoid-EV-transfer study.
- The claim that "amyloid fibrils are established to mediate the rupture of membranes" (Jakhria 2014) is cited to a β2-microglobulin study; the attribution is plausible but I could not verify the specific claim from the abstract, so I ask the authors to confirm the source supports the membrane-rupture point.
- The claim that "EVs can enhance the aggregation propensity of amyloidogenic proteins" (Mirdha 2024; Budvytyte & Valincius 2023) is presented as established, yet the directly relevant Halipi 2024 (cited elsewhere) reports EVs slow Aβ aggregation — the contradicting finding is not acknowledged in that sentence, which is a fairness issue.
- The Mao 2025 citation (autophagic EVs distinct from exosomes) is used to support the AEV framework; I could not verify this reference resolves, and it appears in the reference list without a volume/page — I ask the authors to confirm the citation is complete and real.
- The reference list has several entries with garbled or truncated fields from the PDF conversion (e.g., the Forero 2024 entry runs into page numbers, the Fowler 2025 entry lacks volume/pages), which I flag as a hygiene issue rather than evidence the references are fabricated.
- The proteomics section cites the MSstats and DIA-NN pipelines appropriately, but the claim that specific autophagy markers (RAB8A/B, STX3, LC3A/B) constitute "a novel extracellular biomarker signature" is a novelty claim resting on the proteomics interpretation, which the citation record alone cannot support — I attribute this to the novelty reviewer.

## Questions
- Can the authors confirm that the Aco-650 dye is EV-exclusive and does not transfer via free dye or non-EV carriers, and can they report an EV-depleted media control for the co-culture transfer?
- Can the authors confirm that Jakhria 2014 (β2-microglobulin) is the correct source for the claim that amyloid fibrils rupture membranes, and that Mao 2025 is a complete, resolvable citation?
- Is the increased Aβ signal in co-cultured WT organoids attributable to transferred Aβ rather than to the WT organoid's own upregulated production, and what control would distinguish these?
