  - Proposed change: Revise the Abstract, Title, and Results language throughout (e.g., 'we demonstrate' -> 'these preliminary observations suggest') to explicitly state sample sizes (n=3 gigaseal-preserved, n=12 total) and frame all quantitative associations as hypothesis-generating; retitle to indicate proof-of-concept scope (e.g., 'A Framework for Interpreting Patch-SCP in Acute Brain Slices: A Proof-of-Concept in Rat mPFC Pyramidal Neurons').
- **data_analysis** — Figure 3D regression (n=3, R²=0.998) is presented without acknowledgment of instability, confidence intervals, or leave-one-out sensitivity.
  - Proposed change: Add leave-one-out sensitivity analysis and report the range of slope/intercept estimates when each point is removed; explicitly state in the text that with n=3 the fit cannot be generalized and should be treated as a preliminary trend requiring validation with a larger cohort.
- **clarity** — The '25-50% soma loss' estimate is stated without derivation or supporting calculation.

  - Proposed change: Add an explicit paragraph in Results/Discussion acknowledging that soma size and spike integrity are not disentangled in this dataset, and reframe the 'spike integrity predicts synaptic enrichment' claim as a covariate-confounded observation requiring neurons of matched size but differing retrieval quality in future work.
- **clarity** — Figure 5A category breakdown (n per outcome) is not stated explicitly and terminology for retrieval outcomes is inconsistent.

## Disagreements
- **data_analysis** — Figure 5C-D 'no correlation' claims from n=6 are an underpowered null presented as evidence of decoupling.
  - Response: We agree the n=6 test is underpowered to rule out a correlation of moderate effect size, but we do not claim this proves decoupling in a strong statistical sense; the surrounding text explicitly frames this as suggestive ('these results suggest that... mechanical retrieval can introduce sample loss that decouples...'). We will add a sentence noting the limited power of this test, but the qualitative pattern is corroborated by independent evidence: torn neurons (visually confirmed disruption) show the lowest protein yields despite prior stable recordings (Figure 5A-B), which does not depend on the n=6 correlation test.
  - Manuscript reference: Results, 'Retrieval Loss Decouples Proteomic Measurements from Electrophysiology Recordings': 'these results suggest that while recordings performed in situ provide valuable physiological insight, mechanical retrieval can introduce sample loss that decouples proteomic measurements from electrophysiological context, limiting how directly they can be co-interpreted.'
- **methodology** — No positive control (e.g., enzymatic dissociation) establishing theoretical maximum synaptic protein recovery.
  - Response: A gold-standard positive control was outside the scope of this proof-of-concept study, which was explicitly designed to benchmark relative retrieval quality within our patch-SCP workflow rather than establish absolute recovery ceilings. Our core proteome of >1500-1700 proteins per neuron, including known synaptic and channel markers (Figure 3G, Table S1), already demonstrates the assay's sensitivity is sufficient to detect the target protein classes; comparison to an absolute maximum is a separate validation question we flag explicitly as future work.
  - Manuscript reference: Limitations and Future Directions: 'our framework is therefore best suited for benchmarking retrieval integrity and molecular coverage in relatively large neurons, rather than for making definitive claims about subtype-specific extraction efficiency.'
- **novelty** — The 'shotgun' framing is imprecise/metaphorical and the framework is merely incremental organization of known principles.
  - Response: We use 'shotgun' by direct analogy to shotgun proteomics (indiscriminate sampling without pre-selection), which is an established and recognizable convention in the MS field, and we define this usage explicitly at first use. Regarding incrementality, the specific contribution is not the retrieval categories themselves but the demonstration, via real-time gigaseal-preserved recording during retrieval, that a passive electrophysiological readout can be measured concurrently with soma withdrawal and linked to yield -- a capability not previously reported for patch-SCP in acute brain slices, as distinct from Choi et al. (2022) who recorded prior to but not throughout retrieval.
  - Manuscript reference: Introduction: 'we developed a shotgun patch-SCP strategy... neurons were collected for proteomic analysis regardless of whether the high-resistance seal was preserved, lost, or never established' and Results: 'careful relocation of the soma to the slice surface did not disrupt the whole-cell configuration (n=3), as evidenced by stable hyperpolarizing steps.'
