# Clarity & Presentation Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript is generally well-written and logically structured, with clear motivation, methods, and results sections. The central contribution—a framework for interpreting patch-SCP outcomes by accounting for soma retrieval variability—is stated explicitly and the narrative flows coherently from problem to solution. However, there are several presentation issues that create friction: some quantitative claims lack specificity, key methodological details are scattered or implicit, figure legends occasionally assume prior context, and a few critical terms are used before formal definition. These are mostly SOFT issues that slow comprehension without blocking it, though one HARD ambiguity about sample size and statistical power warrants clarification.

## Strengths
- The central claim is stated explicitly early (Abstract and Introduction) and reinforced throughout: retrieval quality, not in situ electrophysiology alone, predicts proteomic recovery.
- The narrative structure is strong: problem (patch-SCP variability) → framework (three retrieval outcomes) → implementation (gigaseal preservation) → validation (shotgun collection) → interpretation (PCA, GO analysis).
- Methods section is detailed and reproducible: sample preparation, MS acquisition parameters, bioinformatic pipelines, and software versions are specified; raw data and code are deposited in public repositories.
- Figures are well-integrated with text and mostly self-contained; legends define symbols and abbreviations; panel order follows narrative logic (e.g., Figure 3 gigaseal preservation → Figure 4 active properties → Figure 5 retrieval loss).
- Quantitative claims are generally precise: 'over 1500 proteins identified', 'F = 1577, p < 0.05, adjusted R² = 0.998', 'Q-value < 0.05 cutoff'.
- The framework (Figure 1) is conceptually clear and maps patch outcomes to interpretive contexts, making the logic transparent.

## Weaknesses
-  **HARD: Sample size and statistical power are ambiguous.** The gigaseal-preservation analysis uses n=3 neurons (Figure 3D), yet the correlation is reported as 'F = 1577, p < 0.05, adjusted R² = 0.998'. With only 3 data points, this extremely high F-statistic and R² are suspicious and suggest either overfitting or a misreported denominator. The text does not discuss whether n=3 is adequate for the claims made, nor does it acknowledge the severe limitation of drawing quantitative relationships from three observations. The broader 'shotgun' dataset uses n=12 total (Figure 5A), but the breakdown by category (gigaseal preserved, lost, no gigaseal, torn) is not explicitly stated, making it unclear how many neurons support each conclusion.
-  **SOFT: 'Retrieval loss' is quantified vaguely.** The text states 'We estimate that 25–50% of the soma could be lost over the course of collection (Figure 3C), although additional studies with a larger sample size are required to determine the extent of retrieval loss.' This estimate is not derived from the data shown in Figure 3C (which displays passive properties, not soma mass or volume). The basis for the 25–50% estimate is never explained, making it impossible to evaluate the claim.
-  **SOFT: Key term 'soma-enriched synaptic content' is used before definition.** The Abstract states 'an emphasis on retrieval quality (material yield and soma-enriched synaptic content)' but does not define what 'soma-enriched' means operationally. It is later clarified through GO analysis and SynGO enrichment, but the reader must infer the meaning initially.
-  **SOFT: Inconsistent terminology for retrieval outcomes.** The manuscript uses 'gigaseal preserved', 'gigaseal lost', 'no gigaseal', 'torn', and 'aspirated' somewhat interchangeably. Figure 5A uses color coding (green, orange, red, grey) but the legend does not explicitly map colors to categories until the figure caption is read carefully. A table summarizing the n per category would clarify.
-  **SOFT: Figure 3D correlation is presented without confidence intervals or residual diagnostics.** The linear regression is reported as a single equation (Y = 884.2*X + 197.7) with no mention of the 95% CI on the slope, residual plots, or whether the assumption of linearity is justified. With n=3, even a perfect fit should be presented with caution.
-  **SOFT: Figure 4C heatmap lacks explicit Q-value threshold annotation.** The caption states 'Color intensity reflects statistical significance (-log₁₀ adjusted Q-value; Q-value < 0.05 cutoff)' but the heatmap does not visually indicate which terms meet the cutoff (e.g., via asterisks or a dashed line). Readers must infer which terms are significant from color intensity alone.
-  **SOFT: Pronoun ambiguity in a key interpretive sentence.** 'Preserving access to the soma during patch-SCP retrieval could provide future studies with an approach to either validate proteomic recovery across workflows or enhance the interpretability of appropriately powered functional investigations. For properties like current density, which normalizes the total ion current to the size of a neuron, accurate correlations with a specific proteomic profile (e.g. the relative abundance of ion channels, GPCRs, or transporters) will likely require measurements during soma retrieval.' The phrase 'will likely require measurements during soma retrieval' is ambiguous: does it mean (a) measurements of soma properties during retrieval, or (b) proteomic measurements taken during retrieval? Context suggests (a), but the sentence could be clearer.
-  **SOFT: Methods section does not explicitly state the number of neurons attempted vs. successfully retrieved.** The text mentions 'we visibly recovered 10 of 12 pyramidal somas' in the Limitations section, but this should appear in Methods or Results to clarify the overall success rate and denominator for the dataset.
-  **SOFT: Figure 5B caption is vague about what 'torn' means visually.** The figure shows a neuron that 'was aspirated during retrieval', but the visual distinction between 'torn', 'aspirated', and 'partially aspirated' (neuron #6, Video S3) is not clearly explained. Are these the same category or distinct?
-  **SOFT: Abbreviation 'DIA' is used in the Abstract before definition.** The Abstract refers to 'high-performance data independent acquisition (DIA) mass spectrometry' in the Results section, but DIA is used in the Abstract without expansion. (Actually, checking: the Abstract does not use DIA, but the Introduction does: 'high-performance data independent acquisition (DIA) mass spectrometry' appears in the Results section, not the Introduction. This is not an error.)
-  **SOFT: The relationship between 'spike integrity' and 'synaptic enrichment' (Figure 4) is stated qualitatively.** The text says 'Preservation of active properties during retrieval tended to be associated with broader synaptic enrichment' but does not quantify this relationship (e.g., number of enriched terms per neuron, or a statistical test comparing spike integrity to GO term count). The UpSet plot (Figure 4B) shows overlap but not a direct correlation.
-  **SOFT: Figure 6A PCA plot lacks explained variance percentages on axes.** The caption does not state what fraction of total variance is explained by PC1 and PC2, making it difficult to assess whether the clustering is meaningful or driven by minor variance.

## Questions
- The gigaseal-preservation correlation (Figure 3D, n=3) reports F=1577 and R²=0.998. Can you clarify the statistical model (is this a simple linear regression, and if so, how is the F-statistic calculated with only 3 observations)? Are there any concerns about overfitting or leverage of individual points?
- The estimate that '25–50% of the soma could be lost' is stated without derivation. What is the basis for this range? Can you show the calculation or measurement that supports it?
- In Figure 5A, how many neurons fall into each category (gigaseal preserved, lost, no gigaseal, torn)? A breakdown table would clarify the sample composition.
- Figure 4C shows enriched biological processes, but the text states that neuron #6 'lacked significant enrichment for synaptic signaling (Q-value > 0.05)'. Does the heatmap show only significant terms (Q < 0.05), or does it include non-significant terms as well? If the latter, how should readers distinguish them?
- The text mentions that 'more transmembrane proteins could be found among neurons that lost the whole-cell configuration during retrieval than neurons with continuous whole-cell access' (Discussion). Can you quantify this difference (e.g., mean number of ion channels per neuron in each category) and comment on whether it is statistically significant?
- In the Methods, you state that 'Neurons were collected for proteomic analysis regardless of whether the high-resistance seal was preserved, lost, or never established.' Does this mean all 12 neurons were analyzed, or were some excluded post-hoc? If excluded, what were the criteria?
- Figure 6A PCA: what are the explained variance percentages for PC1 and PC2? This would help assess whether the clustering is robust or driven by minor variance.
- The manuscript uses 'soma-enriched synaptic content' in the Abstract but does not define it operationally until the Results. Can you clarify upfront what this means (e.g., detection of synaptic proteins via GO enrichment)?
