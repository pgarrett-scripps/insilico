# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
This is a methodological proof-of-concept study combining patch-clamp electrophysiology with single-cell mass spectrometry proteomics. From a statistical perspective, the work is exploratory and appropriately framed as such, with honest acknowledgment of small sample size (n=3 for gigaseal-preserved neurons, n=12 total) and limited generalizability. The primary quantitative claims are modest and largely supported: a correlation between soma capacitance and protein identifications (n=3, R²=0.998), and qualitative patterns in protein recovery across retrieval categories. However, several statistical issues reduce confidence: (1) the correlation analysis relies on only 3 neurons with gigaseal preservation, making the R² value unreliable and the regression equation unstable; (2) multiple comparisons across SynGO enrichment terms lack correction despite dozens of tests; (3) claims about 'no correlation' in Figures 5C–D use small n (n=6) without power analysis or equivalence testing; (4) PCA interpretation is largely visual without statistical support; (5) key descriptive statistics (means, SDs, ranges) are often missing. The work succeeds as a framework-building exercise and honest assessment of technical variability, but the statistical foundation is thin for hypothesis-testing claims.

## Strengths
- The authors appropriately frame this as an exploratory, proof-of-concept study and explicitly acknowledge the small sample size and limited generalizability (e.g., 'our sample size is insufficient to determine whether these differences are technical or biological').
- The primary quantitative finding—correlation between log-transformed capacitance and protein identifications in gigaseal-preserved neurons (Figure 3D: F=1577, p<0.05, R²=0.998, n=3)—is clearly stated with test name, F-statistic, p-value, and n, even though the sample size is very small.
- The authors transparently report negative findings, such as the lack of correlation between membrane resistance and protein identifications (Figure 3E: F=0.748, p>0.05), and do not overinterpret protein counts in isolation.
- Exclusion criteria and sample categorization are stated a priori (gigaseal preserved, lost, never formed, torn), and all patch attempts are analyzed rather than selectively excluding poor outcomes, which reduces post-hoc bias.
- The use of SynGO for gene ontology enrichment is appropriate and the Q-value threshold (Q<0.05) is stated, though multiple-comparison correction is not explicitly named.

## Weaknesses
- The correlation in Figure 3D (n=3, R²=0.998) is based on only three neurons with gigaseal preservation. With n=3, the regression equation (Y=884.2*X+197.7) is highly unstable and prone to overfitting; the near-perfect R² is not credible for prediction or generalization. No confidence interval, bootstrap resampling, or sensitivity analysis is provided. This is a HARD issue: the claim that 'capacitance correlated with protein identifications' is supported, but the magnitude and precision of the relationship are overstated.
- Figure 5C–D report 'no significant correlation' between in situ capacitance/RM and protein identifications (n=6, p>0.05) but provide no power analysis or equivalence test. With n=6, a non-significant result does not support the claim that retrieval loss 'decouples' electrophysiology from proteomics; this is a HARD issue of claiming absence of effect from an underpowered test.
- SynGO enrichment analysis (Figures 4B–C, 6B) reports dozens of biological process and cellular component terms with Q-values but does not state the family size, correction method (Benjamini–Hochberg assumed but not named), or the total number of tests performed. This is a HARD issue: multiple-comparison correction is applied but not transparently reported.
- PCA in Figure 6A is presented with visual clustering and qualitative interpretation ('torn neurons clustered apart', 'neuron #6 grouped more closely') but no statistical test of cluster separation, variance explained by each PC, or permutation test. The claim that 'neuronal structures can be lost or retained in ways that are almost imperceptible' is not quantitatively supported.
- Key descriptive statistics are missing throughout: no means, standard deviations, or ranges for protein identifications, capacitance, or other metrics across neurons. For example, Figure 5A shows protein counts for each neuron but no summary statistics or comparison of distributions across outcome categories.
- The claim that 'we estimate that 25–50% of the soma could be lost over the course of collection' (Results section) is stated without a method, calculation, or supporting figure. This is a HARD issue: an estimate without a derivation is uninterpretable.
- Figure 3C (ladder plots of passive properties) shows changes from in situ to retrieval but no statistical test of whether changes are significant or systematic. Are the changes in VM, C, and RM statistically different from zero or from each other?
- The comparison of ion channel recovery across outcome categories (Figure 7) is presented as a binary heatmap without quantitative analysis. No test of whether gigaseal-preserved neurons recover more ion channels than torn neurons is reported, despite the visual impression.
- Figures 4A and 4C–D compare neurons #4, #6, and #7 on spike integrity and synaptic enrichment, but no statistical test (e.g., correlation, ANOVA) links spike amplitude or firing rate to the number or diversity of enriched GO terms. The claim that 'preservation of active properties during retrieval is associated with recovery of synaptic proteins' (Figure 4 title) is not tested.

## Questions
- Figure 3D: With only n=3 neurons, how stable is the regression equation Y=884.2*X+197.7? Have you performed a sensitivity analysis (e.g., leave-one-out cross-validation) to assess how much the slope and intercept change if one neuron is removed? The R²=0.998 seems implausibly high for biological data; is this a true fit or an artifact of small n?
- Figures 5C–D: You report p>0.05 for the correlation between in situ capacitance/RM and protein identifications (n=6). What is the statistical power of this test to detect a correlation of, say, r=0.5 or r=0.7? Without power analysis, can you claim that the lack of correlation is meaningful rather than a consequence of small n?
- SynGO enrichment (Figures 4B–C, 6B): How many total biological process and cellular component terms were tested across all neurons? What is the family size for multiple-comparison correction? You report Q<0.05 but do not name the correction method (Benjamini–Hochberg assumed?). Can you provide the corrected threshold and the number of terms passing it?
- Figure 6A (PCA): What fraction of total variance is explained by PC1 and PC2? Is the clustering of torn neurons (grey) statistically significant, or could it arise by chance? Have you performed a permutation test or silhouette analysis to quantify cluster separation?
- The estimate that '25–50% of the soma could be lost' (Results): How was this calculated? Is it based on a change in capacitance, visual inspection, or a model? Please provide the method and supporting data.
- Figure 3C: Are the changes in VM, C, and RM from in situ to retrieval statistically significant? Did you perform paired t-tests or Wilcoxon tests to test whether each property changed significantly during retrieval?
- Figure 4A: Can you quantify the relationship between spike amplitude or firing rate during retrieval and the number or diversity of enriched synaptic GO terms (Figure 4C)? Is there a correlation or ANOVA comparison?
- Figure 7: You state that 'neurons with gigaseals generally retained broader sets of ion channel subunits' but provide no statistical test. How many ion channels were detected in each outcome category (mean ± SD)? Is the difference significant?
- Across all analyses, why are individual data points not shown in bar or box plots? For example, Figure 5A shows protein identifications for each neuron, but a box plot with overlaid points would reveal the distribution and any outliers.
- You report that 'torn samples still showed impressive protein recovery' (Results). How many proteins were detected in torn neurons (mean ± SD) compared to other categories? Is the difference statistically significant or practically meaningful?
