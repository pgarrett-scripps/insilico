# Reproducibility Reviewer

## Summary
This manuscript presents a methodological framework for combining patch-clamp electrophysiology with single-cell proteomics (patch-SCP) in acute brain slices. From a reproducibility standpoint, the work demonstrates strong commitment to transparency: raw mass spectrometry data are deposited with accessions (PXD068359, MSV000099156), videos of soma retrieval are publicly available on Zenodo (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.18189812), custom analysis scripts are promised on GitHub, and the bioinformatic pipeline is specified with software versions. However, several procedural gaps and data-availability issues prevent full end-to-end reproducibility. The electrophysiology protocol relies heavily on a prior publication ('as previously described'), critical sample-processing parameters lack detail, and the small sample size (n=12 neurons) limits the statistical power of key claims. The framework is conceptually sound and the proteomics analysis is well-documented, but a replicator would encounter friction in reconstructing the soma-retrieval procedure and validating the electrophysiology-proteomics correlations without author contact.

## Strengths
- Raw mass spectrometry data deposited with ProteomeXchange accessions (PXD068359, MSV000099156) and accessible via MassIVE, enabling reanalysis of peptide identifications and quantification.
- Bioinformatic pipeline fully specified: DIA-NN v1.8.1 (library-free mode, match-between-runs enabled), UniProt Mus musculus reference, FDR thresholds (1% precursor and protein-level), MaxLFQ quantification, and SynGO enrichment parameters (Q < 0.05) all documented.
- Supplementary videos of soma retrieval (Videos S1–S4) publicly available on Zenodo, allowing visual inspection of retrieval quality and mechanical outcomes for three of the key neurons.
- Custom analysis scripts promised at GitHub (https://github.com/LarryThePharmacologist), supporting reproducibility of GO enrichment, PCA, and heatmap generation.
- Mass spectrometry instrument and acquisition parameters clearly specified: Orbitrap Astral, DIA mode with FAIMS Pro (CV = -50), 20 m/z windows, HCD fragmentation (NCE 25), enabling method replication on similar hardware.
- Electrophysiology recording parameters detailed: internal solution composition, holding potential (−70 mV), step protocol (500 ms, 5–10 pA increments), and passive property analysis via NeuroExpress v19.4.09.

## Weaknesses
- Electrophysiology protocol incompletely specified: core procedures described as 'as previously described [10, 26–31]', requiring readers to consult external sources; the soma-retrieval procedure during patch-SCP (light negative pressure, −50 to −140 mmHg, adjustment criteria) is described qualitatively ('adjusted to facilitate soma retrieval') without quantitative thresholds or decision rules, making replication of retrieval mechanics difficult.
- Sample processing lacks critical detail: the 'careful snapping' of the micropipette tip in the well plate (Methods, 'Sample Processing') is not quantified (e.g., force, velocity, number of snaps, visual confirmation of soma release); no positive/negative controls for soma lysis efficiency are reported; the 15 µL DDM solution volume and 2 µL formic acid quench are specified but justification and sensitivity analysis are absent.
- Small sample size (n = 12 neurons total, n = 3 with gigaseal preserved) severely limits statistical power for the key claim that 'capacitance correlates with protein identifications' (Figure 3D: F = 1577, p < 0.05, R² = 0.998, n = 3); this high R² on three points is not robust and cannot be validated without a larger independent cohort; the authors acknowledge this ('additional studies with a larger sample size are required') but present the correlation as a primary finding.
- Reproducibility of electrophysiology-proteomics correlations compromised: the gigaseal-preservation study (n = 3) is presented as proof-of-concept, but the passive membrane properties (capacitance, RM) measured during retrieval are not deposited as supplementary data; a replicator cannot independently verify the correlation or refit the model without raw electrophysiology recordings (e.g., .abf files).
- Gigaseal-lost and no-gigaseal neurons (n = 6) lack electrophysiology data in the main analysis; Figure 5C–D state 'log-transformed capacitance nor RM correlated with protein identifications (p > 0.05; n = 6)' but these in situ measurements are not shown or deposited, preventing independent verification of the null result.
- Inclusion/exclusion criteria for neurons not pre-specified: the manuscript states 'we did not impose selection or exclusion criteria for either soma retrieval or MS analysis' (Results, 'Comprehensive Neuron Retrieval'), but post hoc categorization into gigaseal-preserved, gigaseal-lost, no-gigaseal, and torn groups introduces risk of selective reporting; no pre-registration or analysis plan is cited.
- Proteomics data processing: DIA-NN output filtering at 1% FDR is standard, but the 'match-between-runs' feature is known to introduce batch effects; no sensitivity analysis (e.g., with/without MBR) is provided to assess robustness of protein identifications across the heterogeneous retrieval outcomes.
- SynGO enrichment analysis: the threshold Q < 0.05 is applied, but multiple-testing correction method (Benjamini–Hochberg assumed but not stated) and the number of tests performed are not reported; no supplementary table listing all tested terms and their Q-values is provided, limiting transparency of enrichment results.
- Transmembrane protein detection (Figure 7, S4, S5): the binary heatmaps show presence/absence but not spectral counts, peptide counts, or intensity-based quantification; a replicator cannot assess whether low detection reflects true absence or insufficient sensitivity without raw DIA-NN output (report.pg_matrix.tsv is deposited but not explicitly linked to figures).
- Electrophysiology recordings during retrieval (Figure 3C, 4A) are shown as representative traces but raw data files are not deposited; NeuroExpress analysis parameters (e.g., fitting window, baseline definition) are not specified beyond reference to the software documentation.
- No random seed or seed-averaging statement for any computational step (PCA, clustering, GO enrichment); reproducibility of exact figure layouts (e.g., Figure 6A PCA plot) depends on random initialization unless seeds are fixed.

## Questions
- Can the authors deposit the raw electrophysiology recordings (.abf files or equivalent) for all 12 neurons, including the in situ measurements and retrieval-phase recordings for the three gigaseal-preserved neurons, to enable independent verification of the capacitance–protein correlation and the passive property changes during retrieval?
- The soma-retrieval procedure is described as 'light negative pressure (−50 to −140 mmHg) adjusted to facilitate soma retrieval while preserving the high-resistance seal' — what were the specific pressure values used for each of the 12 neurons, and what visual or electrophysiological criteria determined when pressure was adjusted?
- For the 'careful snapping' of the micropipette tip in the well plate: how many times was the pipette snapped, with what force, and how was soma release into the DDM solution confirmed (e.g., visual inspection, osmotic lysis, or other method)?
- The gigaseal-preservation study (n = 3) yields an R² = 0.998 for the capacitance–protein correlation — can the authors provide the raw data (capacitance values, protein counts) for these three neurons and confirm that this is not an artifact of overfitting or measurement error?
- Figure 5C–D show no correlation between in situ capacitance/RM and protein identifications for gigaseal-lost and no-gigaseal neurons (n = 6, p > 0.05) — can the authors deposit the in situ electrophysiology measurements for these six neurons to allow independent verification?
- The DIA-NN 'match-between-runs' feature is enabled — was a sensitivity analysis performed to assess whether this feature introduces batch effects or spurious protein identifications across the heterogeneous retrieval outcomes?
- For SynGO enrichment analysis, what multiple-testing correction method was used, and can the authors provide a supplementary table listing all tested biological process and cellular component terms with their raw and adjusted Q-values?
- The custom analysis scripts are promised at GitHub — can the authors provide the repository URL and commit hash (or tag) now, or confirm the expected deposition timeline and accession number?
- Can the authors clarify whether the DIA-NN report.pg_matrix.tsv file (which contains protein-level detections) is the same file used to generate Figures 3F, 4B–C, 6A–B, and 7, and if so, provide explicit mapping of data columns to figure panels?
- For the transmembrane protein heatmaps (Figures 7, S4, S5), were these generated from binary presence/absence calls or from quantitative metrics (e.g., spectral counts, peptide counts, or intensity)? If binary, what was the detection threshold (e.g., ≥1 peptide, ≥2 peptides)?
