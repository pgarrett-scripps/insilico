# Decision Letter

**Decision:** major

## Summary of Evaluation
The panel's signal (confidence-weighted 3.30/5; 1 accept, 2 minor, 4 major, 1 reject) converges on a clear diagnosis: this is a genuinely interesting proof-of-concept with a useful conceptual framework and an honest, selection-bias-avoiding shotgun collection strategy, but several load-bearing claims are stated with more causal and generalizing force than the design licenses, and the reproducibility record has hard gaps. I agree with the majority (major revision) rather than the accept or reject minority. The framework in Figure 1 and the indiscriminate-collection design are real contributions that survive regardless of whether the n=3 capacitance correlation is "true," so rejection is too harsh. But I do not accept the advocate's framing that the empirical weaknesses are merely cosmetic: the central "quantitative bridge" claim IS the n=3 correlation (Fig 3D), which is overfit (adjusted R²=0.998 on three points, 1 df) and confounded by retrieval damage (neuron #6 was partially aspirated before its during-retrieval capacitance was measured). The spike-integrity/synaptic-enrichment association rests on one neuron per condition and is partly confounded by construction (both are consequences of the same retrieval damage). The "decoupling" claim is a null result in n=6. These are fixable by requalification plus completion of the reproducibility record (placeholder SI DOI, unversioned code, unspecified SynGO version/FDR method, unnamed statistical tests, missing animal sex/number, missing chemical vendors). The verdict tracks the evidence: the claims must be scaled to the sample before the paper can be accepted, but the core idea and honest reporting justify a revision path rather than rejection.

## Required Revisions
1. Requalify the capacitance–protein-identification correlation (Fig 3D). Report the raw capacitance and protein-ID values for all three gigaseal-preserved neurons, and the in-situ-to-during-retrieval capacitance delta for each (a direct test of whether retrieval damage, not soma size, drives the correlation). Remove the adjusted R²=0.998 framing (a mathematical artefact of fitting a line to three points with 1 df); report a bootstrapped confidence interval or explicitly state the finding as a feasibility demonstration on three observations, not a validated association. Scale every related sentence (abstract, Results, Conclusions) to 'three observations'.
2. Reframe the spike-integrity/synaptic-protein-enrichment association (Fig 4) as a hypothesis-generating observation, not a demonstrated association. It rests on one neuron per condition (#4 stable, #7 reduced amplitude, #6 single spike) and is partly confounded by construction (spike integrity and synaptic protein recovery are both consequences of the same retrieval damage). Either present it as qualitative observation with explicit n=1 caveats or remove the comparative language.
3. Reframe the 'decoupling' claim (Fig 5C–D) as a preliminary null result. With n=6 the power to detect a correlation is minimal; absence of evidence is not evidence of absence, and the pooled heterogeneous outcomes (no gigaseal, gigaseal lost, torn) could dilute a real size effect. State this limitation explicitly, or add an equivalence test if the claim is that the correlation is truly absent.
4. Provide the derivation of the '25–50% of the soma could be lost' estimate from the capacitance changes in Fig 3C, or remove the claim. As written it has no stated basis.
5. Complete the reproducibility record: (a) resolve the placeholder Supporting Information DOI ('https://pubs.acs.org/doi/xxxxxxxxxxx/') and confirm Tables S1–S3 are accessible there; (b) give a versioned code repository (specific repo name, commit hash or tag, or a Zenodo DOI) rather than the bare user URL; (c) specify the SynGO database version/date, the enrichment algorithm, the background distribution, the minimum gene-set size, and the multiple-comparison correction behind the Q-values; (d) specify PCA preprocessing (normalization, imputation, scaling), number of components, and variance explained by PC1/PC2; (e) report exact p-values (e.g., for F=1577 with df=1,1) rather than 'p<0.05'; (f) justify or remove the log transformation of capacitance.
6. Add a statistics subsection and complete the methods reporting: state what error bars/ribbons represent (SD/SEM/CI) wherever they appear; name the statistical test for every comparison; state the sex of the animals, the number of animals used (12 neurons from how many rats?), genotype/background, and housing conditions; add a randomization/blinding statement; state whether the 12 neurons are biological or technical replicates; provide chemical vendors and catalog numbers (or CAS) for the internal solution, aCSF, DDM, and trypsin; state the UniProt release date, LC gradient profile (%B over time) and column temperature, and the FAIMS CV scheme (single vs multiple).
7. Fix the citation record: remove or replace Ref 31 (Guo et al., saxitoxin total synthesis), which is implausibly non-germane to the slice-electrophysiology methods claim it is cited for; complete the incomplete entries for Refs 30, 15, and 24 (volume/pages/DOI); remove the 'For Table of Contents Only' artifact entry (#34); confirm whether Refs 8 and 9 actually impose the 'all-or-nothing' protein-identification thresholds described, and cite the relevant methods sentence if so.
8. Address the competing preprint (Johnson et al. 2026, bioRxiv, doi:10.64898/2026.04.22.720006, 'aspiration patch proteomics') if it was available at the time of preparation, and state explicitly what whole-soma retrieval adds over the cytoplasmic-aspiration approaches of Choi 2022 and Lee 2024 beyond higher protein counts.
9. Benchmark the transmembrane-protein coverage (Fig 7, S4, S5): state the minimal detectable expression threshold and how the 4–27 ion-channel-subunit recovery compares to expected copy numbers of GABRA1, SCN2A, GRM3 etc. in a single mPFC L2/3 pyramidal soma, and report the PSM-level FDR / unique-peptide evidence for low-abundance targets (including SST and VGF, with sequence coverage and PSM counts).

## Minor Suggestions
- Define 'shotgun patch-SCP' at first use in the abstract/introduction rather than leaving the reader to infer it from the Results.
- State the torn-neuron protein counts explicitly so the reader can compare categories quantitatively (the text gives ranges for no-gigaseal retrievals but not for torn neurons).
- For the PCA (Fig 6A), state whether the clustering is robust to normalization/scaling choices and whether it reflects retrieval quality rather than total protein yield driving the first component.
- Report the variance explained by the first two principal components and whether clustering was tested against measured confounds (neuron depth in slice, time after slice preparation, cell size from DIC images).
- State whether the per-neuron SynGO gene lists were normalized for the number of detected proteins, so the reader can assess whether the apparent lack of synaptic enrichment in neuron #6 reflects lower total IDs rather than selective loss.
- Specify R package versions (ComplexHeatmap, ggplot2, UpSetR) and the compute environment, and add a random seed for any stochastic analysis.
- Consider a head-to-head (even qualitative) comparison of the present whole-soma framework against what was already achievable with cytoplasmic aspiration in slices, to sharpen the novelty claim.
- Consider prospectively defining an inclusion criterion for 'high-context retrieval' (e.g., capacitance threshold, spiking amplitude during relocation) to be tested on an independent set of neurons.
