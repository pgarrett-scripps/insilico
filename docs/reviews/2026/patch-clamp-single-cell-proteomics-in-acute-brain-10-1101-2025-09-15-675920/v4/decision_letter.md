# Decision Letter

**Decision:** major

## Summary of Evaluation
The panel is unanimous on the manuscript's strengths: this is a transparent, ethically clean, technically competent proof-of-concept for patch-SCP in acute brain slices, with deposited raw MS data (PXD068359/MSV000099156), Zenodo videos, a specified DIA-NN pipeline, and a principled all-outcomes ('shotgun') inclusion strategy that guards against selection bias. The panel is equally unanimous that the quantitative backbone is thinner than the current framing implies: the Figure 3D capacitance-protein regression (n=3, adjusted R2=0.998), the undocumented '25-50% soma loss' estimate, and the Figure 5C-D absence-of-effect claims are all currently presented with more force than n=3/n=6 can support, and methodology's HARD confound (neuron #4 is simultaneously largest, best-spiking, and most enriched) means soma size and retrieval integrity are not disentangled.

The rebuttal is unusually constructive and materially improves the outlook. The authors concede, with concrete proposed edits, every one of the load-bearing presentation issues: retitling/reframing to proof-of-concept with explicit sample sizes, leave-one-out sensitivity on Figure 3D, deriving or withdrawing the 25-50% estimate, tabulating per-category n, naming the Benjamini-Hochberg correction and family size, and adding the Johnson et al. (2026) citation with explicit differentiation from Choi et al. (2022). Their disagreements are also credible where offered. On Figure 5C-D, the quoted manuscript text ('these results suggest that... mechanical retrieval can introduce sample loss that decouples...') does read as hedged, and the corroborating torn-neuron evidence does not depend on the n=6 test; I therefore do not require removal of the claim, only an explicit statement of limited power. On the positive control, I accept the scope argument, given the existing Limitations text. On 'shotgun' terminology, the analogy to shotgun proteomics is defensible if defined at first use.

I depart from a minor-revision reading (4 of 8 reviewers) because the required work is substantive rather than cosmetic: a title/abstract/Results rewrite that repositions the paper's headline claims, a new derivation or withdrawal of a quantitative estimate, new sensitivity analyses, a new summary table, and a new confound-acknowledgment section all need to be re-examined by the handling editor. Equally, I depart from any rejection reading: no reviewer identified fabrication, ethical failure, or irreparable design flaw, and every skeptical objection was explicitly labelled fixable. The authors' own worry that reviewers might demand a larger independent cohort is unfounded here: I am requiring reframing and honest statistical reporting, not new data collection. The compliance audits add one genuinely blocking item (Reference 31, Guo et al. 2025 on saxitoxin synthesis, is misattributed to the brain-slice methods section) plus completeness gaps on statistical test naming, error-bar definitions, randomization/blinding, and processed-data availability, all straightforwardly addressable.

## Required Revisions
1. Reframe the headline claims. Revise the title, abstract, and Results throughout so that every quantitative association (capacitance-protein correlation, spike-integrity/synaptic-enrichment link, retrieval-loss 'decoupling') is stated as hypothesis-generating rather than demonstrated. State n=3 (gigaseal-preserved) and n=12 (total) explicitly in the abstract. Adopt a proof-of-concept title along the lines proposed in your response, and replace 'we demonstrate'-type verbs with observation language. Provide a marked-up version so the changes can be checked.
2. Resolve the '25-50% soma loss' estimate. Either delete it entirely, or add a Methods subsection deriving it from pre- versus post-retrieval capacitance ratios (Figure 3C), stating the linear capacitance-to-surface-area assumption explicitly and showing the per-neuron calculation for all three neurons.
3. Report Figure 3D honestly. Add leave-one-out sensitivity analysis reporting the slope/intercept range with each point removed, name the statistical test (ordinary least-squares linear regression), report confidence intervals, and add explicit text stating that an n=3 fit cannot be generalized. If the fit does not survive leave-one-out, demote it from a primary finding.
4. Complete statistical reporting. Name every test used; state the SynGO multiple-testing correction method (Benjamini-Hochberg) and the total number of BP/CC terms tested per analysis; report PC variance explained for the PCA; define error bars (SD/SEM/CI) in every figure legend or state explicitly where individual points are shown without error representation; report descriptive statistics (mean +/- SD) per retrieval category.
5. Add the promised per-category summary table (proposed Table S4) listing n for each of the four retrieval outcomes (gigaseal preserved, gigaseal lost, no gigaseal, torn/aspirated) with mean +/- SD protein IDs and ion channel counts, and standardize retrieval-outcome terminology throughout the text, figures, and legends.
6. Address the soma-size confound explicitly. Add a dedicated Results/Discussion paragraph stating that soma size (capacitance) and spike/retrieval integrity are not disentangled in the n=3 dataset (neuron #4 being simultaneously largest, best-spiking, and most enriched), and reframe 'spike integrity predicts synaptic enrichment' as a covariate-confounded observation requiring size-matched neurons in future work.
7. Fix Reference 31. Guo et al. (2025), 'Scalable total synthesis of saxitoxin and related natural products' (Nature 646:351-357), is cited in support of acute brain slice preparation and does not support that claim. Replace it with the correct methodological source or remove it, and re-verify the full citation list [10, 26-31] used for the delegated electrophysiology protocol.
8. Make the electrophysiology protocol self-contained on the points that carry the analysis: state the quantitative criteria used to assign neurons to each retrieval category (seal-resistance threshold, duration of whole-cell access, timing of retrieval), rather than relying solely on the cited references and Videos S1-S3.
9. Add a randomization/blinding statement to Methods covering neuron selection, retrieval, and proteomic analysis (a statement that no blinding was performed, with rationale, is acceptable and should be given if that is the case).
10. Cite and differentiate Johnson et al. (2026, bioRxiv) in both Introduction and Discussion, and add the paragraph articulating what is incremental versus novel relative to Choi et al. (2022) - specifically the concurrent gigaseal-monitored readout during withdrawal - as proposed in your response.
11. Extend data availability: deposit the raw electrophysiology traces underlying the capacitance and spike-integrity measurements, and add processed analysis files (protein-level matrices, PCA coordinates, heatmap source data) to the existing repositories. Provide accession numbers or DOIs for Supporting Information files in the main text.

## Minor Suggestions
- Add the limited-power sentence to the Figure 5C-D discussion as offered in your rebuttal; the surrounding hedged text you quoted is adequate, so no removal of the claim is required.
- Define 'shotgun' at first use by explicit analogy to shotgun proteomics, as you propose; this resolves the terminology objection.
- Add the euthanasia sentence confirming cervical dislocation under deep isoflurane anesthesia per AVMA guidelines, and confirm IACUC protocol currency.
- Justify or at least contextualize the n=12 sample size (feasibility-based rather than power-based justification is acceptable for a proof-of-concept, but should be stated).
- State explicitly whether any technical replicates or QC injections were run, since each neuron is currently a single measurement.
- Upgrade Reference 14 (NeuroExpress) to a formal publication or archived DOI if one exists; otherwise state clearly that it is an unpublished software tool and give a version-pinned source.
- Specify versions for ComplexHeatmap, ggplot2, and UpSetR, and state the SynGO database version/release date.
- Briefly justify searching rat tissue against the UniProt Mus musculus reference proteome, and comment on the likely effect on identification rates.
- Add a random-seed or determinism statement for any stochastic steps in the R and DIA-NN workflows.
