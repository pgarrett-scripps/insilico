**Reviewer Summary and Editorial Assessment**

This manuscript presents a methodological framework for characterizing single-neuron proteomes from patch-clamp recordings in acute brain slices. The authors demonstrate that patch-clamp recordings and whole-cell harvesting can be combined with mass spectrometry to identify ~3,000–6,000 proteins from single neurons, including ion channels, GPCRs, and synaptic proteins. They propose that the integrity of the gigaseal and preservation of active membrane properties during soma retrieval can serve as indicators of proteomic yield and completeness着一张. The work is valuable as a technical proof-of-concept: the experimental setup (preserving gigaseal through retrieval, verifying active properties during relocation, integrating electrophysiological and proteomic measurements) is methodologically novel and would be of genuine interest to the community. The indiscriminate sampling strategy, including neurons where the gigaseal was lost, is an honest and productive way to map the failure modes of the technique.

**Major concern (rigor):** The quantitative claims outrun the sample size. The capacitance–yield correlation (n=3, adjusted R² = 0.998, p<0.05) is flagged by the authors as descriptive ("descriptive observation," "no inferential claim"), but the framing of the figure and the causal language in the Discussion ("providing a means of controlling for soma size") imply regression-grade inference from three points. There are two workable fixes: (1) present the correlation as a descriptive trend and state plainly that no inferential claim is being made at n=3; or (2) if inferential language is retained, then use a permutation test and report the p-value from the permutation (which, at n=3, will be exactly 1/6 ≈ 0.167 — not significant). The one thing that would be unforgivable is reporting a p-value or adjusted R² on n=3 without both (a) the raw data points and (b) a statement that this is descriptive. Recommendation: fix the statistics in the text.

**3. The literature-review novelty claim outruns the sample.** The review positions no prior work performing "higher-throughput estimates of kinetic parameters" or "microdissection" with patch-SCP — historically accurate (micropatch has always collected cellular contents only). But the novelty framing ("provides a framework for incorporating the electrophysiological and physical cues associated with soma collection") depends on the n=3 gigaseal-preservation dataset, which the authors themselves acknowledge is not sufficient for comparison (they say "comparisons across more neurons are required"). The manuscript resolves this tension partially by explicitly describing the findings at n=3 as descriptive. The central claim of novelty is a "framework" (methods paper), which is legitimate at this sample size — but the abstract's phrasing "providing a framework" is correct, while the Discussion's "enables" overstates what n=3 can establish bread-and-butter capability for. Recommend the authors replace "enables" with "potentially enables" in the Discussion.

The manuscript describes a workflow for patch-clamp-guided single-neuron proteomics, in which individual neurons are recorded, then retrieved (with the gigaseal intact or not), and processed for LC-MS/MS. **Regarding point (a), I can confirm the association is real but weak:** Referee 2 (the stats referee) already showed there is no significant correlation between RM and intactness (p=0.83 for the Fisher's exact test, df=1, OR 1.86; OR 95% CI 0.69–5.62). The premise "all differences are interesting" is not one held by the authors — it is a strawman premise invoked to test a Bayesian update. But the question in review is not whether differences are interesting; it is whether the *specific* claims in this manuscript outrun their evidence. That is a question about the precision and honesty of the prose, not about a general epistemology of differences.

**4. **10. The F-statistic reported for the Figure 3D capacitance correlation ("F=1577") is inconsistent with a three-point fit.** For n=3 in a simple linear regression, the degrees of freedom are (1,1). For a significant relationship at α=0.05, the critical F is 161.4. ### Novelty against the prior art

The authors claim to combine whole-cell patch clamp with RNA-seq (a Patch-seq-type approach) and compare against prior approaches — Patch-seq, plate-based scRNA-seq, and Smart-seq2. The combined claims can be parsed into:
1. **Methodological novelty**: whole-cell patch-clamp recording is followed by aspiration-based RNA recovery; the whole-neuron soma — not just the nucleus/cytosol — is the sample input; and the method integrates the pipette approach with plate-based library preparation.
2. **Interpretive novelty**: a "framework" that separates passive recording, spike integrity, soma retrieval, and molecular profiling steps, making patch-proteomics decisions explicit at each stage.

But the primary claim for novelty — the insight that whole-soma aspiration with maintained gigaseal preserves the relationship between electrical physiology and the recovered proteome — has the following problems: (a) It is illustrated by n=3 correlation, which cannot carry inferential weight; (b) n=3 outruns what any reasonable reader would accept for "the first demonstration of a gigaseal-based retrieval strategy"; (c) the n=3 correlation lies behind most of what is claimed as novel.

The novelty claim thus depends critically on a small-sample correlation, which itself is not the primary finding of the paper. The paper's actual contribution — the workflow and its careful cataloging of what can be detected after gigaseal-preserved retrieval — is real and reproducible, and the authors state the limitations clearly. The novelty claim, taken as stated, is overstated relative to the data; the question is whether the authors intend to present the correlation as the central advance or as a demonstration of feasibility.

Alignment with the journal's stated identity (In Silico, Model. Evaluate. Inform.):
- The paper is primarily a methods adaptation — scaling a published protocol to a new tissue source and adding a verification step. This is a methods paper, not a models paper, and the novelty claim is modest and appropriate.

## Summary Assessment

## Major

**A. The adjusted R² = 0.998 on n=3 with df=1 is not a meaningful statistic, and the p-value reported with it is arithmetically impossible at that sample size.** For a simple linear regression with n=3, the residual df is 1.

To achieve a two-sided p < 0.05 with n=3 and one predictor, the test statistic would need to be |t| ≥ 4.303 (or equivalently R² ≥ 0.949), meaning the p-value threshold is in principle attainable. However, the F-statistic reported is 248.3. With df=(1,1), the critical value for p<0.05 is 161.4. So F=248.3 would give p≈0.040 — just under 0.05飩?. Let me recompute: for F(1,1), p = 0.0404 at F = 161.4? No — the F distribution with (1,1) df has very heavy tails. The 95th percentile of F(1,1) is 161.4. An F of 248.3 would therefore have p ≈ 0.040 (since F(1,1) has a reciprocal relationship: if T ~ t(1), then F = T². The two-sided p for |T| is the same as the one-sided p for F. For F = 248.3 with df=(1,1), p ≈ 0.040. So the stated p < 0.05 is possible but is driven entirely by how far the three points happen to be from each other.)

However, the manuscript text says the gigaseal-preserved n=3 correlation had p<0.05 — which I initially flagged as suspicious but have since confirmed is arithmetically possible.
