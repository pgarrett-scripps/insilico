# Debate Transcript

## Advocate — round 1

## Advocate’s Opening Statement

The reviewers have converged on a clear picture: this is a genuinely novel proof-of-concept with a real methodological contribution, whose headline quantitative claims are under-powered. I want to argue that the *framework* — not the specific correlations — is the contribution, and that the reviewers’ own findings support acceptance with revision rather than rejection.

**The core contribution survives scrutiny.** The novelty reviewer confirms the capacitance→yield correlation is “genuinely new, testable” and “survives preemption.” The methodology reviewer calls the indiscriminate-collection strategy “a thoughtful departure from the field’s common all-or-nothing filtering.” The clarity reviewer finds Figure 1 “a genuinely useful conceptual contribution.” These are not fixable-in-revision details; they are the paper’s substance.

**The n=3 criticism is real but misdirected at the claim’s altitude.** The rigor reviewer’s own ledger shows the abstract uses “tended to be associated” — appropriately hedged. The data-analysis reviewer demands the authors “state explicitly that no inferential claim is being made from n=3.” That is a revision, not a rejection. The authors already frame this as “preliminary assessment” in the Results. What is missing is a single sentence in the Figure 3D legend and abstract: “descriptive observation, n=3, no inferential claim intended.” The reproducibility reviewer’s point that p<0.05 is mathematically impossible with n=3 for a perfect fit is the sharpest catch — but it is a *statistical reporting error*, correctable by reporting the raw data points and removing the p-value.

**The confound between soma size and retrieval integrity is acknowledged by the authors themselves.** The methodology reviewer notes the design “does not separate soma size from retrieval quality.” The authors explicitly describe neuron #6 as both the smallest and partially aspirated. The fix — a sentence stating “size and retrieval integrity are confounded in this dataset” — is precisely what the clarity reviewer requests. This is candor, not concealment.

**The missing Johnson et al. preprint is the one HARD differentiation failure.** The novelty reviewer found a directly competing preprint from the Nemes lab arguing the opposite position. This is a genuine gap. But it is a *positioning* gap — the authors’ gigaseal-preservation approach is distinct from aspiration sampling, and the novelty reviewer confirms the delta is real. Naming the neighbor and stating the difference strengthens, not weakens, the contribution.

The ethics reviewer scores 5/5. The framework is sound. The claims can be requalified in revision. This is what revision is for.

## Skeptic — round 1

## Skeptic’s Rebuttal — Round 1

The advocate’s case rests on a single move: relabel the paper’s central quantitative claims as “framework” and “proof-of-concept,” then treat the n=3 problem as a wording fix. That move does not survive contact with the manuscript’s own text.

**The capacitance→yield correlation is not a framing issue; it is the paper’s headline finding.** The abstract states: “Capacitance during gigaseal-preserved retrieval correlated with protein identifications, suggesting that proteome yield could be linked to soma size.” The Results section reports “F = 1577, p < 0.05, adjusted R² = 0.998” — a p-value the reproducibility reviewer correctly notes is mathematically unattainable with n=3 for a regression. This is not a “statistical reporting error” that a sentence can fix; it is a claim that the data cannot support at any confidence level. The advocate says the fix is “removing the p-value.” But the p-value is not decorative — it is the evidence for the correlation. Remove it and the claim becomes “three points trend upward,” which is not what the abstract claims.

**The confound is not acknowledged in the text where it matters.** The advocate says the authors “explicitly describe neuron #6 as both the smallest and partially aspirated.” True — but they do not state that this confound *invalidates the size→yield interpretation*. Instead, the Results conclude: “soma size, which is proportional to capacitance, plays a more direct role in protein recovery than RM.” That is a causal claim drawn from three points where the smallest neuron was also the most damaged. The methodology reviewer’s point stands: the correlation “could equally be driven by retrieval damage rather than by size.” The advocate’s proposed fix — “a sentence stating size and retrieval integrity are confounded” — would contradict the Results as written, not clarify it.

**The “framework” is not separable from the claims it frames.** The advocate argues the framework is the contribution and the correlations are incidental. But Figure 1’s framework is *built on* the claim that gigaseal preservation enables quantitative prediction. The novelty reviewer confirms the capacitance correlation is “the paper’s one genuinely new, testable claim.” You cannot keep the novelty and discard the evidence for it.

**The Johnson et al. omission is a differentiation failure, not a citation gap.** The novelty reviewer found a directly competing preprint arguing the opposite position on the same question. The manuscript cites the same lab’s earlier work but does not engage the active debate. This is fixable — but it is not a minor polish; it changes how the contribution must be positioned.

**Verdict:** The n=3 correlation is FATAL as reported. The spike-integrity claim is FATAL as a finding (n=1 per condition). The framework is worth publishing — but only after the quantitative claims are either re-supported with data or explicitly demoted to hypotheses. That is more than a revision; it is a re-framing of the paper’s central claims.
