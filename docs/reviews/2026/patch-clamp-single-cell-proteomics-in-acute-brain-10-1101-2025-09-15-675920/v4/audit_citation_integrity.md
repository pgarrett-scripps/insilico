# Citation Integrity Auditor

## Summary
This citation-integrity audit of the manuscript (34 numbered references) checked the categories Reference resolvability, Claim–citation support, and Self-citation/citation inflation; Quotation/number fidelity and Retracted/predatory sources had no triggers and were skipped. Overall the reference list is largely resolvable, but several entries have resolvability gaps (missing volume/pages/DOI for Refs 30 and 24; no persistent identifier for Ref 15; a conversion-artifact entry #34), one claim–citation pairing (Ref 31, a saxitoxin total-synthesis paper) is implausibly non-germane to the slice-electrophysiology methods claim it is cited for (HARD), and two interpretive claims (Refs 8 and 9) could not be verified from the manuscript alone. A cluster of 7 references shares authorship with the current manuscript (self-citation), which is acceptable except for the questionable Ref 31.

## Unverifiable (raise as questions)
- **[Claim–citation support] Reference #31 (Guo et al. 2025) germane-ness to slice-electrophysiology methods claim** — Claim: 'Acute brain slices and electrophysiological recordings were performed as previously described [10, 26–31]' (Methods, lines ~25–26). Ref. 31 — Guo, Y., et al., 'Scalable total synthesis of saxitoxin and related natural products.' Nature, 2025, 646(8084): p. 351–357 — reports total chemical synthesis of saxitoxin, a marine neurotoxin, with no apparent connection to slice preparation, patch-clamp electrophysiology, or single-cell proteomics; it is implausible that it supports the stated claim. May be an error in reference numbering or a padded citation.
- **[Claim–citation support] References #8 (Lee 2024) and #9 (Ghatak 2024) support for stated limitations** — Claims: (1) 'patch-SCP in the locus coeruleus of mice revealed sex-specific differences in both the proteomes and intrinsic excitability of noradrenergic neurons, although collection and analysis were limited to the cytoplasm...' [8]; (2) 'a patch-SCP platform applied to Alzheimer's disease hiPSC-derived neurons found an association between protein expression and a hyperexcitable phenotype, yet a lack of compartment-specific synaptic and membrane proteins...' [9]; (3) 'many patch-SCP studies have adopted an 'all-or-nothing' strategy...' [8, 9]. Full text of these papers could not be retrieved to confirm they state or imply these limitations as described; claims are plausible and citations are to real, indexed papers, but verification is not possible from the manuscript alone.

## SOFT gaps — recommended
- **[Reference resolvability] Reference #30 (Anjos-Santos et al. 2025) volume, page range, and DOI** — Ref. 30 — Anjos-Santos, A., et al., 'Noradrenaline Modulates Central Amygdala GABA Transmission and Alcohol Drinking in Female Rats.' Biol Psychiatry, 2025. The entry lacks volume number, page range, and DOI; only journal name and year are provided. It is load-bearing as part of the methods description ('Acute brain slices and electrophysiological recordings were performed as previously described [10, 26–31],' lines ~25–26 of Methods).
- **[Reference resolvability] Reference #15 (Szücs 2022) DOI or persistent identifier** — Ref. 15 — Szücs, A., 'NeuroExpress program for analyzing patch-clamp data.' ResearchGate, 2022. Published on ResearchGate (not a peer-reviewed journal) with no DOI; cited for electrophysiology data analysis (lines ~33–34 of Methods), so it is load-bearing for reproducibility.
- **[Reference resolvability] Reference #24 (Armstrong & Gilly 1992) volume number and DOI** — Ref. 24 — Armstrong, C.M. and W.F. Gilly, 'Access resistance and space clamp problems associated with whole-cell patch clamping,' in Methods in Enzymology, 1992, Academic Press, p. 100–122. No DOI or volume number for the Methods in Enzymology series is provided; the chapter can be located by searching, but the entry lacks the series volume number and DOI.

## Documented (for the record)
- **[Reference resolvability] Reference #34 conversion-artifact entry** — Ref. 34 — 'For Table of Contents Only.' This is a caption artifact from the table-of-contents graphic, not a real reference; it is not cited in the text and does not affect reproducibility. Recommend removing or ignoring this entry.
- **[Self-citation / citation inflation] Self-citation cluster (Refs 10, 26–30, 32) and germane-ness of Ref 31** — Of the 34 references, 7 involve authors of the current manuscript or their immediate research group (Roberto lab, Scripps Research): Ref. 10 (Patel et al.), Ref. 26 (Rodriguez et al. — first author of current manuscript), Ref. 27 (Vlkolinsky et al. — co-author), Ref. 28 (Varodayan et al.), Ref. 29 (Athanason et al.), Ref. 30 (Anjos-Santos et al.), Ref. 32 (Patel et al.). Additionally, Ref. 31 (Guo et al., saxitoxin total synthesis) appears non-germane to the method described; if it is not supporting the claim, its presence inflates the reference count. Self-citation within a research group is common and generally acceptable when genuinely relevant; the possible exception is Ref. 31.
