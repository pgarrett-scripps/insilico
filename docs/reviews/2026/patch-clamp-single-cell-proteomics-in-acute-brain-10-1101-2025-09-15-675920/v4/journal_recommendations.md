# Journal Recommendations

## As-is (current quality)
- **bioRxiv (Neuroscience section)**
  - Fit: This manuscript is already posted as a preprint and is ideally suited to remain there during revision. Preprints explicitly accommodate proof-of-concept methodological work with transparent limitations and do not require the statistical power or claim-framing demanded by peer-reviewed journals.
  - Realism: Preprints are not peer-reviewed for acceptance but for archival; the paper's transparent reporting, open data (PXD068359, videos), and methodological rigor make it a valuable community resource at its current developmental stage.
- **Journal of Neuroscience Methods**
  - Fit: This journal specifically publishes technical and methodological papers in neuroscience, including proof-of-concept frameworks and single-case or small-scale validations. The paper's emphasis on transparency, all-outcomes inclusion strategy, and honest discussion of retrieval variability align well with the journal's values, even at n=3 gigaseal-preserved and n=12 total.
  - Realism: JNM has a more permissive scope for preliminary methods work than broad-audience journals. However, the major-revision verdict suggests editors may still request explicit n-disclosure and reframing of quantitative claims before acceptance, so this is a mid-tier fallback rather than assured.

## After required revisions
- **Nature Methods**
  - Fit: This journal specifically targets innovative methodological frameworks that transparently address technical constraints and provide actionable guidance for the field. After reframing the paper as hypothesis-generating proof-of-concept, explicitly acknowledging the soma-size/spike-integrity confound, deriving or removing the 25–50% loss estimate, and disclosing n=3/n=12 throughout, the paper becomes a strong fit: it offers a practical interpretive framework, a benchmarking dataset with all retrieval outcomes, and comprehensive methods transparency (DIA-NN pipeline, Zenodo videos, deposited raw MS data).
  - Realism: Nature Methods requires high methodological rigor and honest limitation reporting, both of which the authors are committed to in their rebuttal. The framework and dataset are genuine contributions. Realistic acceptance odds post-revision: 40–50%, conditional on successful restatement of quantitative claims and completion of statistical reporting.
- **eLife**
  - Fit: eLife values transparent, rigorous methodological work and explicitly welcomes papers that frame exploratory findings as hypotheses for future study. The patch-SCP framework, all-outcomes shotgun dataset, and the insight that protein-identification counts do not reliably predict biological quality are significant contributions to the field. The paper's ethics clearance (5@5), data reproducibility (4@4), and open data meet eLife's transparency standards.
  - Realism: After reframing to proof-of-concept, adding the soma-size confound paragraph, completing statistical reporting, and citing Johnson et al. (2026) with explicit novelty differentiation, this paper aligns well with eLife's scope. Realistic acceptance odds: 45–55%, given eLife's broad interdisciplinary reach and willingness to publish methods papers with acknowledged limitations.
- **Molecular Systems Biology**
  - Fit: This journal focuses on integrative approaches linking function (electrophysiology) to molecular composition (proteomics). After revision, the paper's framework for interpreting how soma-retrieval context shapes proteome recovery is a natural fit. The benchmark dataset (n=12, multiple retrieval outcomes) and the systematic use of SynGO enrichment for compartment-aware interpretation add value for systems-level neuroscience.
  - Realism: MSB has published similar single-cell integrated-omics methods papers. Post-revision with honest n-disclosure and confound acknowledgment, realistic odds: 35–45%. The paper is methodological rather than discovery-driven, which may lower odds slightly at a systems journal.

## Alternative outlets
- **PLOS Biology**
  - Fit: Open-access, rigorous-but-inclusive standards, and a strong track record of publishing transparent methodological papers (including proofs-of-concept) with comprehensive supplementary data and reproducibility documentation. The patch-SCP framework and benchmarking resource would be valuable to PLOS Biology's broad readership.
  - Realism: PLOS Biology has a slightly lower bar than Nature-family journals but still requires sound methodology and honest claims. Post-revision, acceptance odds: 50–60%. The open-access model also benefits visibility in the single-cell proteomics community.
- **Analytical Chemistry or Journal of the American Chemical Society (JACS)**
  - Fit: The paper's mass spectrometry methodology (DIA, FAIMS, DIA-NN analysis) and sensitivity for detecting hydrophobic transmembrane proteins (ion channels, GPCRs) in a single-cell proteomics context is a significant technical contribution. MS-focused journals value innovations in sample handling and protein detection depth.
  - Realism: Analytical Chemistry publishes single-cell MS methods regularly. However, the paper's primary focus is neurobiology and electrophysiology integration rather than MS innovation per se, so it is a secondary fit. Realistic odds: 30–40% after revision, depending on emphasis in rewrite.
- **Proteomics or Nature Protocols (as methods paper)**
  - Fit: If reframed as a detailed protocol paper or troubleshooting guide (rather than a research findings paper), the patch-SCP workflow, soma-retrieval benchmarking, and troubleshooting strategies (gigaseal preservation, retrieval categories, SynGO filtering) would be suitable for a specialist proteomics journal or a Nature Protocols protocol. This redirects the manuscript from "novel findings" to "best practices for a challenging technique."
  - Realism: This represents a modest repositioning: instead of claiming to demonstrate soma-size/protein-recovery relationships, the paper becomes 'here is how to think about patch-SCP outcomes and quality control.' Proteomics would likely accept post-revision (60% odds); Nature Protocols requires more extensive protocol detail but is viable if the authors expand Methods and create a reproducible step-by-step workflow. This is a pragmatic fallback if higher-tier venues decline.
