- **Fit:** The manuscript is fundamentally a methods-and-framework paper for single-cell proteomics. JPR regularly publishes technical advances in SCP workflows, including patch-clamp-coupled approaches (e.g., the Choi et al. 2022 paper cited as ref [7] appeared in *Analytical Chemistry*, but JPR has a strong SCP methods track). The journal's audience includes both mass spectrometrists and neuroscientists using proteomics.

However, the n=3 core observation will still be scrutinized; the authors must present it as hypothesis-generating and lean on the framework's conceptual value rather than the correlation's strength.
- **Caveat:** The journal may ask for additional validation (e.g., a larger cohort or orthogonal confirmation of the capacitance–yield link). The authors should be prepared for this.

**2. Analytical Chemistry** (ACS)
- **Fit:** This is the natural home for patch-SCP methods. The Choi et al. 2022 paper (ref [7]) appeared here, and the journal has published several single-cell mass spectrometry workflows. The paper's electrophysiology-proteomics integration is a strong fit for the journal's "analytical methods for biological systems" scope.
- **Acceptance odds:** 35–50% after revision. The journal values technical novelty and reproducibility; the current reproducibility gaps (unnamed GitHub, underspecified DIA-NN parameters) would be flagged immediately. The authors must close these before submission.
- **Caveat:** Analytical Chemistry reviewers may push for more quantitative rigor (e.g., label-free quantification validation, carrier-channel comparisons). The authors should be ready to address this.

**3. Journal of Neuroscience Methods** (Elsevier)
- **Fit:** This is a strong fallback within the neuroscience methods space. The paper's core contribution — a framework for interpreting patch-SCP retrieval outcomes — is a methods contribution that neuroscientists using patch-clamp would find useful. The journal is more tolerant of proof-of-concept studies with modest sample sizes than the proteomics journals.
- **Acceptance odds:** 50–60% after revision. The journal's reviewers are typically electrophysiologists who will appreciate the framework's practical value and the honest treatment of retrieval variability. They are less likely to demand the same depth of proteomic validation as an ACS journal.
- **Caveat:** The journal may ask for a more explicit "how-to" framing (e.g., a step-by-step protocol section) to serve its methods-oriented readership.

The journal has historically focused on electrophysiology and imaging methods, so the authors should check recent issues for similar work.
- **Frontiers in Neuroscience:** This is a broad journal with a variable review experience. The acceptance odds are reasonable, but the impact is lower than the ACS venues.
- **Journal of Proteomics:** This is a solid mid-tier venue, but it may be less visible to the neuroscience community. The authors should weigh whether the paper's impact is better served by a neuroscience-focused venue.

**Overall recommendation:** The authors should prioritize the revision, then submit to **Journal of Proteomechanics** or **Analytical Chemistry**, with **Journal of Neuroscience Methods** as a strong backup. The paper's framework is genuinely novel, and the field needs it — but the claims must be recalibrated to match the evidence before any submission.
