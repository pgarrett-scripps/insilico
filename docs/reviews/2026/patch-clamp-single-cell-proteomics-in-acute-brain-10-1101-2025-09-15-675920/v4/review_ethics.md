# Ethics & Compliance Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript meets all major ethics and compliance requirements for publication. Animal research was approved by an institutional IACUC committee with a named protocol number; funding sources are fully disclosed; competing interests are explicitly declared; and authorship contributions are clearly itemized. The work involves no human subjects, clinical trials, dual-use biosafety concerns, or secondary data requiring additional consent. Data and code are made publicly available with appropriate repository identifiers. No ethics or compliance red flags were identified.

## Strengths
- Animal research approval is explicitly stated with named committee (Scripps Research IACUC) and protocol number (09-0006), satisfying HARD requirements for vertebrate research.
- Funding sources are comprehensively disclosed across multiple NIH and institutional grants (NIMH, NIAAA, Schimmel Family Chair), meeting cross-cutting HARD standards.
- Competing interests are explicitly declared: three authors (CB, SK, BT) are identified as paid employees of NGeneBioAI, Inc. and/or Yatiri Bio, Inc., with all other authors declaring no conflicts, satisfying HARD disclosure requirements.
- Authorship contributions are itemized in detail by role (Conceptualization, Methodology, Formal Analysis, etc.), enabling verification of authorship integrity.
- Raw mass spectrometry data and search files are deposited to ProteomeXchange (MassIVE MSV000099156, PXD068359) with public availability stated, supporting reproducibility and data transparency.
- Custom analysis scripts are made publicly available via GitHub (https://github.com/LarryThePharmacologist), enhancing code transparency.
- Electrophysiology videos are deposited on Zenodo (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.18189812) with full public access, supporting methodological transparency.

## Weaknesses
- The manuscript does not explicitly state whether animal care and use procedures adhered to the NIH Guidelines for the care and use of laboratory animals, though the IACUC approval statement references these guidelines implicitly. A direct statement of compliance would strengthen transparency.
- No explicit statement regarding humane endpoints or welfare considerations during acute brain slice preparation and electrophysiology is provided, despite these being standard HARD requirements for animal research. The methods describe anesthesia and euthanasia (isoflurane, cervical dislocation) but do not explicitly justify these choices or discuss welfare monitoring during the procedure.

## Questions
- The manuscript states that 'Wistar rats (75 days of age, Charles River) were given access to food and water ad libitum' but does not describe housing conditions, environmental enrichment, or post-operative monitoring. Were standard institutional animal care standards applied, and if so, can the authors confirm this explicitly?
- Under what conditions were animals euthanized after slice preparation? The text mentions 'cervical dislocation' but does not state whether this was performed under anesthesia or as a secondary method. Can the authors clarify the euthanasia protocol and confirm it complies with AVMA guidelines?
- The IACUC protocol number (09-0006) appears to be from 2009, yet the manuscript was prepared in 2025–2026. Was this protocol renewed or is a current protocol number available? If the same protocol has been continuously active, a statement of renewal or ongoing approval would strengthen compliance documentation.
