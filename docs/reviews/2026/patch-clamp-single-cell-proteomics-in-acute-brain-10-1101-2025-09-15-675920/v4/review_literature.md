# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The reference list is sound — every checked entry resolves to a real, relevant source, and the major prior work in this small field (Choi 2022, Lee 2024, Ghatak 2024) is present and accurately characterized. No HARD citation defects (fabrication, garbled entries, unsupported attributions that are verifiably wrong) were found. Two SOFT issues weaken the citation record: a claim about prior studies' inclusion strategies that cannot be confirmed from the cited abstracts alone, and a pair of references that do not obviously support the specific statement about mPFC neuron properties. A recently posted competing preprint should be checked for availability at the time of submission. These are fixable with clarifications or additions and do not undermine the paper.

## Weaknesses
- Claim that Refs [8] and [9] adopted an "all-or-nothing" inclusion strategy cannot be verified from the cited abstracts alone; the full method sections were not accessible.
- Claim that mPFC L2/3 pyramidal neurons were chosen for "its relatively large soma, its rich synaptic proteome" is attributed to Refs [10] and [11], which do not document soma size or synaptic proteome richness.
- A very recent competing preprint (Johnson et al. 2026, bioRxiv, doi:10.64898/2026.04.22.720006) describes 'aspiration patch proteomics' and should be discussed if it predates submission.

## Questions
- Can the authors confirm that Refs [8] and [9] explicitly impose the protein-identification thresholds described, and if so, cite the relevant methods sentence?
- Was the Johnson et al. 2026 preprint (doi:10.64898/2026.04.22.720006) available at the time this manuscript was prepared, and if not, do the authors consider it relevant to cite in revision?
