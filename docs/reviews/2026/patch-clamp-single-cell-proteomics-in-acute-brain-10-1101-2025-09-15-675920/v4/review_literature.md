# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript demonstrates generally accurate citation of foundational patch-seq and patch-SCP work (Cadwell et al. 2016, Aerts et al. 2014, Choi et al. 2022, Lee et al. 2024), with appropriate use of methodological tools (DIA-NN, SynGO, IUPHAR-DB). However, a significant omission undermines the related-work section: Johnson et al. (2026, bioRxiv) presents a directly competing study on aspiration patch proteomics addressing nearly identical questions about soma retrieval quality and proteome recovery in patch-SCP, yet is not cited. Given that the present manuscript emphasizes retrieval quality and soma-enriched synaptic content as core novelties, this parallel work must be acknowledged and compared to clarify methodological distinctions (aspiration vs. whole-soma) and relative advantages. Additionally, the characterization of Ghatak et al. (2024) requires verification, and the manuscript would benefit from clearer articulation of how its gigaseal-preservation strategy advances beyond Choi et al. (2022). These gaps constitute hard issues affecting novelty positioning and require substantial revision before acceptance.

## Strengths
- Accurate attribution of foundational Patch-seq work (Cadwell et al. 2016) establishing the combination of patch-clamp with transcriptomics, with correctly characterized progression from RT-PCR to RNA-seq.
- Proper citation and accurate summarization of three key prior patch-SCP studies (Aerts et al. 2014, Choi et al. 2022, Lee et al. 2024), including verified claim that Lee et al. found sex-specific differences in noradrenergic neuron proteomes and intrinsic excitability.
- Correct and appropriate use of SynGO database (Koopmans et al. 2019) as a manually curated synaptic GO ontology database.
- Appropriate methodological citations for DIA-NN (Demichev et al. 2020) and MaxLFQ quantification in the analysis pipeline.
- Honest acknowledgment of limitations in prior patch-SCP studies regarding incomplete recovery of membrane proteins and compartment-specific coverage (Lee et al., Ghatak et al.).

## Weaknesses
- Missing directly competing work: Johnson et al. (2026, bioRxiv) on 'Proteome-Driven Phenotyping of Identified Single Neurons in Intact Brain Tissue by Aspiration Patch Proteomics' addresses nearly identical questions about soma retrieval quality, proteome recovery variability, and electrophysiology-proteomics relationships, yet is not cited despite being a contemporaneous preprint that directly overlaps with the manuscript's core novelty claims.
- Unverified characterization of Ghatak et al. (2024): The manuscript's claim that Ghatak et al. found 'an association between protein expression and a hyperexcitable phenotype' combined with 'a lack of compartment-specific synaptic and membrane proteins' could not be confirmed from available abstracts and requires verification against the full paper.
- Limited explicit comparison of the manuscript's 'gigaseal-preserved retrieval' strategy to prior soma-retrieval approaches: While Aerts et al. (2014) and Choi et al. (2022) are cited, the manuscript does not clearly articulate what is methodologically novel versus incremental refinement, particularly given that Choi et al. (2022) also performed patch-clamp recordings during soma collection.
- Incomplete engagement with recent single-cell proteomics benchmarking literature (2024–2025): The manuscript cites Gatto et al. (2023) for SCP guidelines but does not engage with expanding literature on SCP quality metrics, depth benchmarking, compartmental proteomics, or neuronal subtype-specific proteomes that would contextualize why soma-focused sampling is limiting.
- Non-standard citation format for NeuroExpress software (ref. 15, Szücs 2022): Citation relies on a ResearchGate link to unpublished/in-preparation work rather than peer-reviewed publication, representing a citation hygiene issue.
- Incomplete IUPHAR-DB attribution: The manuscript cites IUPHAR-DB through 'Concise Guide to PHARMACOLOGY' editions (2019, 2023) but does not cite the primary IUPHAR-DB resource paper itself, potentially affecting reproducibility regarding the exact version and date of curated ion channel, GPCR, and transporter lists used.

## Questions
- Can you explain why the Johnson et al. (2026) preprint on aspiration patch proteomics was not cited, and how does your whole-soma retrieval approach and framework differ from their aspiration-based method in terms of relative advantages?
- Please confirm that your summary of Ghatak et al. (2024) regarding 'lack of compartment-specific synaptic and membrane proteins' is an accurate and fair representation of their conclusions, and provide the specific passage or figure supporting this claim.
- Prior work by Choi et al. (2022) also performed patch-clamp recordings during soma collection; what is the specific methodological or analytical advance in your 'gigaseal-preserved retrieval' strategy compared to their approach, and is the novelty primarily in the interpretive framework (Figure 1) rather than the technique itself?
- Which specific version or date of IUPHAR-DB was used to generate the ion channel, GPCR, and transporter annotation lists, and can this information be included in the Methods section for reproducibility?
- Have you considered engaging with the 2024–2025 literature on SCP depth, compartmental bias, DIA-MS performance, and neuronal subtype-specific proteomes to strengthen your discussion of why soma-focused sampling is inherently limiting and to contextualize your findings within the broader SCP field?
