# Debate Transcript

## Advocate — round 1

The panel converges on a genuine, well-supported core contribution: intrafilament nucleotide exchange in MreB, demonstrated not by one assay but by convergent orthogonal evidence—ATP* FRAP recovery (Fig. 3F) with a striking null result in E136A (Fig. S7F), homogeneous ATP* incorporation along preformed filaments including the low-affinity D158A mutant (Fig. 3H-I), and in vivo CCCP delocalization asymmetry between WT and E136A (Fig. 4J). The novelty reviewer explicitly calls this 'the first direct demonstration of this phenomenon in any biological polymer.' This is not a single fragile result but a triangulated finding across TIRF, HS-AFM, biochemistry, and genetics—exactly the kind of multi-method convergence that should carry weight in acceptance decisions. The kinetic symmetry finding (Fig. 2F-G) is similarly robust and resolves a long-standing structural question about MreB polarity with direct dynamic evidence, not mere inference.

I concede real, fixable gaps: statistical tests for Fig. 3D/4B-C comparisons are absent, the Monte-Carlo model's k₋,d and k₋,e are partly fit rather than independently measured (rigor, methodology reviewers rightly flag circularity), and the TIRF/AFM methodological parameters (TSOAX settings, FRAP acquisition details) need disclosure for reproducibility. These are revision-level fixes: add p-values, deposit model code, specify acquisition parameters, and soften 'new class' language to scope it explicitly to the tested regime (B. subtilis, SLBs, 20% CL). None of these undermine the central, replicated observation that MreB filaments exchange nucleotide internally—a mechanistically important and clearly novel departure from actin/tubulin dogma that merits publication once appropriately hedged and statistically buttressed.

**Key points:**
- Intrafilament nucleotide exchange is supported by convergent orthogonal evidence (ATP* FRAP, D158A/E136A incorporation assays, in vivo CCCP), not a single fragile assay
- Kinetic symmetry of MreB elongation (Fig. 2F-G) is a rigorous, novel resolution of a long-standing structural/dynamic question
- Statistical omissions (p-values in Fig. 3D, 4B-C) and model circularity (k-,d fitted not measured) are real but are revision-fixable, not fatal to the core claim
- The 'new class of biopolymer' language should be scoped to the tested regime in revision, but this is a framing fix, not a data problem
- Panel reviewers themselves rate novelty and clarity at 4/5, acknowledging the contribution's genuine significance

## Skeptic — round 1

The advocate concedes stats and model-fitting issues as 'revision-fixable' but doesn't engage the sharpest objection: the paper's own title claims *intrafilament* exchange, distinguishing MreB from ParM's already-known end-exchange (ref 29, literature review). Yet the rigor and methodology reviewers both show the data cannot actually distinguish interior exchange from end-only exchange coupled to diffusion/redistribution. FRAP recovery of ATP* (Fig. 3F) is 'consistent with nucleotide exchange anywhere in the filament, at the ends, or via transient dissociation and rebinding'—the rigor reviewer's words. The one piece of evidence that could speak to interior exchange, 'homogeneous' ATP* incorporation along D158A/WT filaments (Fig. 3H), is precisely the panel's weak spot: clarity reviewer notes 'homogeneous' is never defined quantitatively, so we can't tell if this reflects true bulk incorporation or an artifact of end-proximal loading plus imaging resolution. No experiment blocks filament ends while testing interior exchange, as the rigor reviewer explicitly proposes as the needed control. This isn't a caption-wording problem—it's the absence of the one experiment that would validate the paper's namesake claim. Meanwhile the Monte Carlo model, invoked to explain *why* interior exchange should occur, has k₋,d and k₋,e fit to the very disassembly curves it's meant to explain (methodology reviewer: 'circular'), so it cannot independently corroborate the mechanism either. Softening language to 'filament exchange' in revision, as the advocate suggests, would resolve the overclaim but simultaneously erodes the paper's central novelty pitch relative to ParM—meaning the fix and the contribution are in tension. This is fixable only by new end-blocking experiments, not by text changes, making it a serious, not merely cosmetic, gap.

**Key points:**
- The title's specific claim of 'intrafilament' (interior) exchange, as opposed to end-limited exchange already known in ParM, is not actually distinguished by any current experiment
- FRAP and ATP* incorporation data are explicitly acknowledged by rigor/methodology reviewers as consistent with end-only exchange plus dissociation-rebinding, not proof of interior exchange
- The one datum that could support interior exchange (Fig. 3H 'homogeneous' incorporation) lacks a quantitative definition per the clarity reviewer, undermining its evidentiary weight
- The Monte-Carlo model cannot independently rescue this because its key rate constants are fit to the same disassembly data it purports to explain
- This gap requires new experiments (e.g., end-blocking controls), not just textual softening—and softening the claim would blunt the paper's advertised novelty over ParM
