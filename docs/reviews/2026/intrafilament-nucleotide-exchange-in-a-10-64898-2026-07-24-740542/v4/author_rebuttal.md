## Disagreements
- **data_analysis** — The TFLime-FRAP and ATP*-FRAP experiments are conflated as evidence for the same 'monomers do not turnover' claim, overstating what TFLime-FRAP shows.
  - Response: We do not present these as equivalent measurements of the same phenomenon; the text explicitly separates the two interpretations and attributes the lack of TFLime recovery to a labelling artifact (irreversible photochemical trapping) distinct from the nucleotide-exchange result obtained with ATP*, which is the actual evidence cited for exchange.
  - Manuscript reference: 'Although TFLime binding to pFAST is reversible under normal imaging conditions, the lack of FRAP recovery suggests that the high-intensity laser illumination used for photobleaching induces irreversible photochemical trapping of the fluorogen on the tag, preventing exchange with unbleached TFLime molecules. However, ATP*-labelled filaments showed rapid signal recovery, indicating nucleotide exchange between the filaments and the solution (Fig. 3F).'
- **methodology** — The mechanism of ADP-induced depolymerization is not established because the capping experiment only tests whether ATP-bound caps stabilize, not why ADP destabilizes, and could equally be explained by ADP simply failing to stabilize filaments without requiring exchange.
  - Response: The capping experiment was specifically designed to distinguish exchange-mediated destabilization from simple loss of an end-cap: if ADP acted only by removing a stabilizing ATP cap at the ends, an E136A (permanently ATP-bound, non-exchanging) cap should have fully protected the WT core indefinitely. Instead, filaments broke within the WT segment itself even with intact caps, indicating that free ADP is altering the nucleotide state of subunits within the filament interior, not merely competing at the ends, which is the qualitative signature of intrafilament exchange rather than end-limited cap loss.
  - Manuscript reference: 'When the solution was flushed with free ADP... most filaments started breaking in the WT section or near one cap and depolymerized, leaving only the E136A caps (Fig. 4F-H).'
- **rigor, novelty** — HS-AFM elongation rate (n=7) is too small and variable to validate the TIRF plateau as non-artefactual.
  - Response: We do not rely on the n=7 HS-AFM value alone; we explicitly flag it as a first estimate and use it only as a qualitative cross-check that an independent, tag-free, label-free technique at a very different protein concentration falls in the same range as the pFAST-based TIRF plateau, arguing against a labelling-specific artifact. The claim of a genuine plateau itself rests on the TIRF concentration series (n=77-1142 per condition), not on the HS-AFM data.
  - Manuscript reference: 'Because of these tracking difficulties, only a very limited number of filaments could be tracked for a short timeframe and thus the calculated HS-AFM elongation rate (14.4 ± 10.16 nm·s⁻¹, n=7) serves as first estimate of assembly (Fig. 2B-D, Movie 4).'
- **rigor** — The claim that ATP hydrolysis is 'not required for polymerization' is imprecise because ADP-AlF4- (a phosphate-mimicking transition-state analog) supports filaments while ADP alone does not, suggesting the phosphate moiety, not just ATP binding, may be required.
  - Response: We agree phosphate occupancy matters for filament stability, which is precisely why we tested ADP-AlF4- and reported that it supports polymerization similarly to ATP; but this result is orthogonal to the hydrolysis-independence claim, which rests on the two ATPase-deficient point mutants (E136A, D158A) that cannot productively hydrolyze ATP yet still polymerize with kinetics indistinguishable from WT. The heading 'ATP hydrolysis is not required for MreB polymerization' refers specifically to the catalytic turnover step, not to occupancy of the gamma-phosphate-mimicking pocket, and the text already distinguishes these considerations.
  - Manuscript reference: 'In the presence of ADP-AlF4-, MreB formed filaments similar to those observed with ATP, albeit with a slightly slower polymerization rate (Fig. S6A-C), whereas no filaments were observed in the presence of ADP... We found that both mutants display markedly reduced Pi release relative to wild-type (WT) MreB (Fig 3A)... Notably, both mutants assembled into filaments indistinguishable from WT MreB filaments (Fig. 3C and Fig. S7A) with similar kinetics.'
- **clarity, novelty** — In the CCCP in vivo experiment, E136A filament stability is inferred only from the absence of delocalization, not directly confirmed to remain ATP-bound.
  - Response: Direct in vivo nucleotide-state measurement is not feasible with our current tools, but the inference is not solely visual: it is grounded in independently measured biochemical properties of E136A (near-complete loss of ATPase activity and higher ATP affinity than WT, Fig. 3A-B), which predict that E136A subunits, once ATP-loaded, cannot proceed to ADP even as cellular ATP falls, unlike WT. The CCCP experiment is presented as a qualitative in vivo test consistent with this prediction, not as a direct biochemical measurement, and is described accordingly in the text.
  - Manuscript reference: 'Upon CCCP addition, WT MreB filaments became progressively delocalized, while E136A filaments remained stable on the membrane, as predicted (Fig. 4J and Fig. S11).'
