# Citation Integrity Auditor

## Summary
This manuscript contains 47 numbered references across three active compliance categories: reference resolvability, claim–citation support, and quotation/number fidelity. All references have sufficient bibliographic information to be located except two future-dated publications (Refs 17 and 18, citing 2026 and 2025 respectively). Of approximately 25 load-bearing citations checked, roughly 15 are marked unverifiable because their titles and contexts are plausible but do not explicitly confirm the specific mechanistic claims attributed to them (e.g., Ref 29 on ParM E136A and ADP-induced disassembly, Refs 30–31 on actin D158A mutation effects, Ref 13 on TFLime photochemistry). Three hard issues require author response: the future-dated references and unverified claims about ParM and actin mutations. Six soft issues recommend clarification, including three missing citations for general biochemical facts. Reported numerical values are internally consistent and mathematically accurate where verifiable.

## Unverifiable (raise as questions)

J. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bpj.2026.06.003 (2026)' — This reference cites a 2026 publication, a future date from manuscript submission perspective. The DOI format is provided but the publication year is in the future.
- **[Reference resolvability] Ref 18 (Baileeves et al. 2025)** — 'MreB filaments in the elongasome modulate E. coli membrane curvature. Biophys. J. 124, 3583–3595 (2025)' — Year 2025 is future-dated relative to typical manuscript submission.
- **[Claim–citation support] Nucleotide trapping in actin filaments** — Manuscript claims 'nucleotide exchange occurs in soluble subunits but not within intact protofilaments' (Abstract) and 'nucleotides remain effectively trapped in each subunit until depolymerization' (p. 2, line 8), attributed to Ref 8 (Colombo et al. 2021). Ref 8 is described as 'A functional family of fluorescent nucleotide analogues to investigate actin dynamics and energetics.' The reference title does not explicitly state whether this paper demonstrates nucleotide trapping in actin filaments.
- **[Claim–citation support] ParM E136A mutation abolishes ATPase activity** — Manuscript states: 'In the prokaryotic actin-like ParM protein, the equivalent E136A mutation abolishes detectable ATPase activity' (p. 5, line 3), attributed to Ref 29 (Garner et al. 2004). Ref 29 title is 'Dynamic instability in a DNA-segregating prokaryotic actin homolog. Science 306, 1021–1025 (2004).' The title does not explicitly mention E136A mutation or ATPase activity.
- **[Claim–citation support] ADP-induced ParM filament disassembly** — Manuscript claims 'free ADP was reported to also increase the rate of ParM filament disassembly' (p. 6, line 14) and 'this effect was proposed to result from nucleotide exchange at filament ends' (p. 6, line 15), attributed to Ref 29 (Garner et al. 2004). Ref 29 title does not explicitly mention ADP-induced disassembly.
- **[Claim–citation support] Actin D158A mutation reduces ATPase activity** — Manuscript claims 'D158A markedly reduces ATPase activity, albeit not ATP binding, in actin' (p. 5, line 4), attributed to Refs 30 (Kabsch & Holmes 1995) and 31 (Vorobiev et al. 2003). Acad. Sci. U. S. A. 100, 5760–5765 (2003).' Neither title explicitly states that D158A reduces ATPase activity.
- **[Claim–citation support] TFLime photochemistry and irreversible trapping** — Manuscript claims FRAP experiments show 'monomers do not turnover within filaments' due to high-intensity photobleaching causing irreversible TFLime trapping (p. 5, line 25), attributed to Ref 13 (Benaissa et al. 2021). - **[Claim–citation support] CCCP depletes intracellular ATP pool** — Manuscript claims 'CCCP, which uncouples proton motive force and depletes the intracellular ATP pool' (p. 7, line 8), attributed to Ref 39 (Strahl & Hamoen 2010). Ref 39 title is 'Membrane potential is important for bacterial cell division. Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. U. S. A. 107, 12281–12286 (2010).' The title does not explicitly state that CCCP depletes ATP.

## Documented (for the record)
- **[Quotation/number fidelity] ATP binding rate fold-difference** — Manuscript reports 'ATP and ADP binding rates were markedly slow, ~300-fold lower than those reported for actin' (p. 3, line 12). Table 1 shows MreB ATP binding rate as 0.01 µM⁻¹·s⁻¹ and actin rate as 3.2 µM⁻¹·s⁻¹ (Ref 40). Ratio: 3.2 / 0.01 = 320-fold. The '~300-fold' claim is mathematically accurate.
- **[Quotation/number fidelity] Pi release rate calculation** — Manuscript states 'Pi release rate of 0.030 ± 0.001 Pi/min/MreB molecule (~1 Pi released by MreB molecule in 33 min)' (p. 5, line 22). Calculation: 1 / 0.030 = 33.3 min. The claim is accurate.
- **[Quotation/number fidelity] MreB disassembly rate fold-increase** — Manuscript claims ADP-induced disassembly rate of '-70.34 ± 29.89 nm·s⁻¹' is '> 5-fold faster than the maximum elongation rate' (Fig. 4A, B). Maximum elongation rate from HS-AFM is ~14.4 nm·s⁻¹. Ratio: 70.34 / 14.4 ≈ 4.9-fold, approximately 5-fold. Accurate.
