# Clarity & Presentation Reviewer

## Summary
This manuscript is clearly written and well-structured overall, with strong visual presentation and explicit framing of its central claims. The central contribution—that MreB filaments undergo continuous nucleotide exchange within intact polymers, unlike canonical actin—is stated plainly and supported by multiple complementary experiments. The presentation is generally precise about methods and quantitative results. Minor friction points exist around figure legend completeness and a few ambiguous pronoun references, but these do not impede understanding of the core argument or reproducibility.

## Strengths
- The manuscript explicitly states its central claim early (nucleotide cycling within intact filaments defines a new class of biopolymer dynamics) and returns to it throughout, making the through-line clear.
- Quantitative statements are precise: elongation rates, dissociation constants, and disassembly rates are given with units and error ranges (e.g., 'k₊,ₜ ≈ 45 μM·s⁻¹'; '-70.34 ± 29.89 nm·s⁻¹'), enabling comparison and reproducibility.
- Figures are well-captioned with explicit panel labels, color coding is colorblind-safe, and every major result figure is referenced in text with its purpose stated before presentation.

## Weaknesses
- Figure 3H legend states 'homogeneous binding of ATP* was observed along the length of both WT and D158A filaments, but not of E136A filaments' but does not explicitly define what 'homogeneous' means visually or quantitatively—does it mean uniform intensity, or absence of end-preferential accumulation? The distinction matters because the caption claims this shows nucleotide exchange occurs throughout the filament, yet the visual criterion for 'homogeneous' is not specified in the legend, forcing the reader to infer it from the image alone.
- The model section (Fig. 4D and Supplementary 'Modeling MreB dynamics') is referenced as supporting the main claims, but the manuscript text does not explain the model structure, parameter sources, or validation approach clearly enough for a reader to assess whether the model is an independent test of the hypothesis or merely a restatement of the experimental observations in mathematical form. Specifically, it is unclear whether the model's parameters were fit to the data or derived independently, which determines whether the simulations in Fig. 4I constitute genuine prediction or post-hoc fitting.
- The claim that 'nucleotide exchange within filaments tunes their stability on the membrane' (Abstract) is supported by the ADP-flush experiment (Fig. 4A–C) and the capping experiment (Fig. 4G–H), but the causal chain is not fully isolated: the rapid disassembly upon ADP exposure could result from (1) ADP-bound subunits being inherently unstable, (2) ADP competing with ATP at the nucleotide-binding pocket and destabilizing the filament, or (3) the absence of ATP preventing stabilizing nucleotide exchange. The E136A capping experiment argues against mechanism (3) by showing that ATP-capped filaments remain stable even when the WT core is exposed to ADP, but this does not exclude mechanism (1) or (2), nor does it prove that exchange itself (rather than the nucleotide identity) is the tuning mechanism. A direct test—such as blocking exchange while maintaining nucleotide binding—would strengthen this claim.

## Questions
- In Fig. 2E, the plateau in elongation rate at high MreB concentration is attributed to 2D diffusion on the membrane, but the text does not state whether the plateau is significantly different from the linear extrapolation or merely a visual trend; what is the statistical support for a true plateau versus continued linear growth?
- Figure 1G reports Pi release rates of 0.030 ± 0.001 Pi/min/MreB in the presence of excess ATP, but does not state the number of replicates or whether this is from one or two independent experiments; the figure legend says 'Results of two independent experiments' but does not specify n per experiment.
- The FRAP experiment (Fig. 3E) concludes that 'monomers do not turnover within filaments' based on lack of TFLime recovery, but the text then explains this as photochemical trapping of the fluorogen, not absence of exchange; does this interpretation rest on the assumption that TFLime rebinding is the only source of recovery, or could other mechanisms (e.g., lateral diffusion of labeled subunits) contribute?
- In the in vivo experiment (Fig. 4J), CCCP addition depletes ATP and E136A filaments remain stable while WT filaments delocalize; does the text confirm that E136A filaments remain ATP-bound under CCCP treatment, or is stability inferred only from the absence of delocalization?
