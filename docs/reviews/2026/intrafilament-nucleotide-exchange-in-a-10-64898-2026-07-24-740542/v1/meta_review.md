# Meta-Review

**Draft recommendation:** major

## Synthesis
The panel agrees the manuscript reports a substantial, multi-method study of MreB dynamics with a genuinely novel core observation: MreB filaments exchange nucleotide while intact, contrasting with actin's trapped-nucleotide behavior (novelty 4/4, clarity 4/4, data_analysis 4/4, literature 4/4). Ethics and reporting transparency are exemplary (5/5). Convergent evidence — ATP* FRAP recovery, its absence in E136A, D158A incorporation, and in vivo CCCP delocalization asymmetry — supports the headline claim that MreB nucleotide cycling differs categorically from actin. Kinetic symmetry of bidirectional elongation (Fig. 2F-G) is judged a rigorous, independent advance. Where the panel splits is on mechanistic specificity and validation: methodology, reproducibility and rigor reviewers (all 3/5, conf 4) converge on the same structural problem from different angles — the paper's namesake claim of *intrafilament* (interior) exchange, as opposed to end-limited exchange already known in ParM, is not actually isolated by any reported experiment, and the Monte Carlo model that is invoked to explain the mechanism has key rate constants (k₋,d, k₋,e) fitted to the same disassembly/FRAP curves it purports to explain — a circularity flagged independently by rigor and methodology reviewers. Reproducibility further notes that FRAP acquisition parameters, TSOAX tracking settings, model code, and mobile-fraction data are all missing, meaning the disambiguating quantities needed to adjudicate interior-vs-end exchange are not even reported. The debate sharpened rather than dissolved this: the advocate correctly showed that even under the skeptic's end-only-exchange alternative, the actin contrast (zero exchange even at filament ends in intact actin polymers) survives, so the core novelty claim is not eliminated. But the skeptic's rebuttal held that the Monte Carlo model's explicit assumption of interior-independent exchange (Fig. 4D caption) drives several downstream figures (Fig. 4I 'rejuvenation' predictions, the D158A incorporation-rate interpretation, the ADP-flush causal narrative), and this assumption is currently unsupported by any reported analysis or end-blocking control — a gap the advocate ultimately conceded is unresolved in the manuscript as submitted, proposing only a feasible post-hoc reanalysis of existing data as a fix. Statistical reporting gaps (no named tests/p-values for Fig. 3D, 4B-C) and unresolved ambiguity in the 'homogeneous' ATP* incorporation criterion (Fig. 3H, flagged independently by clarity) compound the concern that a load-bearing visual/qualitative claim is doing causal work it has not been shown to support quantitatively.

## Decisive Factors
No reviewer recommended rejection, and the actin-contrast finding at the heart of the paper's novelty is not undermined by the debate — this rules out reject or a harsher-than-panel verdict. However, three high-confidence reviewers (methodology, reproducibility, rigor; all conf 4, same weight as the 4/5 reviewers) independently converge on one specific, structural gap that survived the debate largely intact: the model's mechanistic premise (interior-independent nucleotide exchange) and the paper's central 'new class'/intrafilament-vs-end-exchange distinction from ParM are asserted but not disambiguated by any reported experiment or analysis, and the model used to support downstream stability claims (Fig. 4I) is partly fit to the data it explains. The advocate's best rebuttal — that this can likely be resolved via reanalysis of existing FRAP/dissociation-rate data rather than new wet-lab work — is a plausible and encouraging path, but it is a proposal, not work present in the manuscript, as the skeptic correctly noted in the final exchange. Combined with unreported statistical tests for key figure comparisons and missing reproducibility artifacts (model code, FRAP/TSOAX parameters), this tips the balance from 'minor' to 'major': the fixes required are substantive (quantitative disambiguation of end vs. interior exchange, deposited model code and parameters, named statistical tests, a defined quantitative criterion for 'homogeneous' incorporation, and scoped 'new class' language) rather than cosmetic, even though they do not appear to require abandoning the core, well-triangulated finding that MreB nucleotide is not trapped within intact filaments as it is in actin.
