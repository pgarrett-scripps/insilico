## Disagreements
- **rigor** — The Monte Carlo model reproduces observed filament behaviors without requiring intrafilament nucleotide exchange, implying exchange was invoked post-hoc rather than mechanistically necessary.
  - Response: This mischaracterizes the model. We explicitly ran control simulations with exchange rates set to zero and compared them to simulations with exchange active, and found starkly different outcomes: filaments without exchange retain their initial randomly-assigned nucleotide state and do not respond to the free nucleotide pool, whereas filaments with exchange are destabilized by free ADP and stabilized/'rejuvenated' by free ATP. The exchange term is therefore not decorative; removing it eliminates the model's ability to reproduce the nucleotide-pool-dependent aging and rejuvenation we observe experimentally.
  - Manuscript reference: Fig. 4I legend: 'Green curve simulates the case where intrafilament nucleotide exchange does not occur (k+,n = k-,n = 0); Magenta curve simulates the case where intrafilament nucleotide exchange with free ADP (100 µM) occurs; Blue curve simulates the case where intrafilament nucleotide exchange with free ATP (100 µM) occurs.' and Main Text: 'In the presence of ADP, filaments aged and disassembled rapidly, whereas ATP stabilized them, effectively acting as a filament rejuvenation mechanism (Fig. 4I).'
- **novelty** — FRAP recovery of ATP* (Fig. 3F) could reflect lateral diffusion of labeled subunits or nucleotide rebinding rather than genuine nucleotide exchange, and this is not experimentally excluded.
  - Response: While we agree a direct single-molecule dissociation measurement would further strengthen this claim (see load-bearing critique below), the existing data already argue against pure lateral-diffusion or generic-rebinding artifacts. If recovery reflected only subunit mobility or nonspecific dye rebinding, it should occur independently of catalytic state; instead, recovery is abolished in the ATPase-dead E136A mutant, which is structurally intact and equally labeled, and is markedly faster in the low-affinity D158A mutant when exposed to pure ATP*, tracking with predicted nucleotide affinity/occupancy rather than filament mechanics.
  - Manuscript reference: 'No significant recovery was detected for the ATPase-deficient mutant E136A (Fig. S7F).' and 'homogeneous binding of ATP* was observed along the length of both WT and D158A filaments, but not of E136A filaments (Fig. 3H)... D158A filaments incorporated the nucleotide about twice as fast as WT filaments (Fig. 3I).'
- **methodology** — The capping experiment (Fig. 4G-H) only rules out a simple end-cap-only model; a static ADP-bound-end/nucleotide-free-interior scenario could produce the same fragmentation pattern without invoking true interior exchange.
  - Response: The capping experiment was not our only evidence for interior exchange and should not be read in isolation. Independently, Fig. 3H/I directly show homogeneous, rapid ATP* incorporation along the entire length of preformed WT and D158A filaments (not concentrated at ends) within minutes of exposure to free nucleotide, which is inconsistent with a static, non-exchanging interior. The capping experiment complements this by showing that interior subunits, once loaded with ADP via this demonstrated exchange, become intrinsically unstable and fragment even when protected by stable ATP-locked caps.
  - Manuscript reference: 'When preformed pFAST-MreB filaments labelled with TFCoral were exposed to pure ATP* instead, homogeneous binding of ATP* was observed along the length of both WT and D158A filaments... (Fig. 3H).'
- **literature** — Reference 17 (Adriaans et al., in press) is used as primary evidence for MreB membrane binding and ATP-dependent polymerization rather than the manuscript's own data.
  - Response: Figure 1E-H liposome co-sedimentation, ATPase, and QCM-D data presented in this manuscript are our own primary evidence for CL-dependent membrane binding and ATP-driven adsorption; reference 17 is cited only in two narrow contexts, as a comparative benchmark for nucleotide-binding kinetics of a related MreB ortholog and to note convergent affinity findings, not as a substitute for the data shown here.
  - Manuscript reference: 'Through liposome co-sedimentation assays, we found that doping DOPC with negatively-charged cardiolipin (CL)... promote MreB membrane association in the presence of ATP (Fig. 1E, F).' and 'Adsorption of MreB to a DOPC:CL (80:20) SLB in the presence of ATP or ADP (2 mM) measured by QCM-D (Fig. 1H).'
