# Reproducibility Reviewer

## Summary
This is a technically sophisticated study with strong experimental design and clear biological findings, but reproducibility is compromised by a critical gap: the Monte-Carlo model that supports the central claim about nucleotide exchange dynamics is not deposited or fully specified, making it impossible for readers to verify the simulations or rerun the analysis. Data availability is otherwise reasonable, but the model code is load-bearing and its absence is a hard reproducibility failure.

## Strengths
- The manuscript provides detailed protocols for protein purification, lipid preparation, and microscopy procedures with sufficient specificity that most wet-lab components could be independently reproduced.
- Raw microscopy data (movies) are deposited and figure-generation procedures are traceable through named software (TSOAX, SOAX, ImageJ, FIJI) with version numbers where critical.
- Biochemical parameters (binding kinetics, dissociation constants, elongation rates) are quantified with explicit n values and standard deviations, enabling readers to assess statistical power and reproducibility of individual measurements.

## Weaknesses
- The stochastic Monte-Carlo model (Fig. 4D, Supplementary Information 'Modeling MreB dynamics') is the load-bearing artifact for the central claim that intrafilament nucleotide exchange tunes filament stability. The manuscript states the model 'accurately reproduces MreB polymerization kinetics' and uses it to explain why WT, E136A, and D158A filaments behave differently under nucleotide-free and ADP-containing conditions (Fig. 4C, S10). However, no code, pseudocode, or detailed algorithm is provided in the main text or supplementary materials, and the authors state only that data 'will be deposited on Zenodo after publication'—a conditional promise that does not satisfy reproducibility standards. Without the model specification, a reader cannot verify whether the simulations genuinely constrain the interpretation or whether alternative parameter sets or mechanisms would produce the same outputs. This is a HARD reproducibility failure for a claim that depends entirely on computational validation.
- The kinetic parameters used in the model (Fig. 4D caption: k₊,ₜ = 45 µM⁻¹s⁻¹, k₋,ₜ = 0.135 s⁻¹, k₋,ₐ = 60 s⁻¹, etc.) are stated but their derivation from the experimental data is not fully transparent: it is unclear whether these are fitted values, measured values, or hybrid estimates, and no confidence intervals or sensitivity analysis is provided to show which parameters most strongly constrain the model's predictions.
- The FRAP experiments (Fig. 3E–F) are central to claiming that monomers do not turn over within filaments but nucleotides do. However, the authors acknowledge that TFLime photobleaching may cause 'irreversible photochemical trapping' of the fluorogen, preventing recovery—a caveat that undermines the negative result (no FRAP recovery for TFLime) but is not quantified. The recovery observed for ATP* is interpreted as nucleotide exchange, but the kinetics of ATP* binding and the possibility of non-specific rebinding from solution are not fully disentangled from genuine intrafilament exchange.
- The in vivo experiment (Fig. 4J, CCCP-induced delocalization) is qualitative and relies on visual inspection of TIRF images at discrete timepoints; no quantitative metrics (e.g., fluorescence intensity over time, number of cells scored, statistical test) are provided to support the claim that E136A filaments 'remained stable' while WT filaments 'became progressively delocalized.'

## Questions
- Where is the Monte-Carlo model code deposited, and what is the accession or repository link? If it is truly 'to be deposited after publication,' how can readers verify the simulations now?
- For the kinetic parameters in Fig. 4D, which were measured directly from the experiments (e.g., k₊,ₜ from Fig. 2E) and which were fitted or estimated from other sources? Provide a table mapping each parameter to its source and uncertainty.
- In Fig. 3F (ATP* FRAP recovery), what is the timescale of the recovery, and how does it compare to the timescale of ATP hydrolysis (k_hyd = 1.54 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹)? Is the recovery fast enough to rule out simple rebinding from solution?
- For the CCCP experiment (Fig. 4J), how many cells were imaged, what quantitative metric was used to score delocalization, and was a statistical test applied to compare WT and E136A?
