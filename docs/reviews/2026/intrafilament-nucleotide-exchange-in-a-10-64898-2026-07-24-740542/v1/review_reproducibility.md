# Reproducibility Reviewer

## Summary
This manuscript presents a substantial experimental study of MreB polymerization dynamics with novel findings about intrafilament nucleotide exchange. The core claims are well-supported by multiple complementary techniques (TIRF, HS-AFM, biochemistry), and the data availability statement promises deposition on Zenodo. However, critical load-bearing results lack sufficient procedural detail for independent reproduction, and the Monte-Carlo model—central to interpreting the ADP-induced disassembly mechanism—is incompletely specified. These gaps prevent verification of the quantitative conclusions without author contact.

## Strengths
- Multiple independent imaging modalities (TIRF, HS-AFM, TEM) and biochemical assays converge on the same polymerization and nucleotide-exchange findings, reducing the risk of technique-specific artifact.
- Experimental design isolates nucleotide-exchange effects from end-capping through the E136A-capped filament experiment (Fig. 4G–H), which directly tests the proposed mechanism rather than relying solely on indirect inference.
- Kinetic parameters (k₊,ₜ, k₋,ₜ, Cc) are derived from concentration-dependent elongation rates with explicit fitting procedures and reported with uncertainty, enabling external validation.

## Weaknesses
- **Load-bearing claim: Intrafilament nucleotide exchange tunes filament stability.** The evidence rests on three observations: (1) ATP* FRAP recovery in Fig. 3F, (2) rapid ADP-induced disassembly in Fig. 4A–B, and (3) the Monte-Carlo model predicting nucleotide-state-dependent stability (Fig. 4I). For (1), the FRAP protocol is described only as 'standard ATP*-labelling conditions (ATP:ATP* 17:1)' with no specification of laser power, dwell time, bleach region size, or recovery frame rate—parameters that directly affect the measured exchange rate and could be misinterpreted if the fluorophore is photochemically trapped rather than exchanging. For (2), the disassembly rates are reported (−70.34 ± 29.89 nm·s⁻¹ for WT in ADP) but the quantification method is not detailed: how were filament ends tracked in TIRF? Were both ends measured? How were transient filament breaks distinguished from end depolymerization? For (3), the model is the linchpin for interpreting why D158A disassembles faster than WT in ADP despite lower nucleotide affinity, yet the model specification is incomplete—the paper states 'intrafilament nucleotide exchange dynamics are independent of the nucleotide state' (Fig. 4D caption) but does not specify whether exchange rates differ between ATP-bound and ADP-bound subunits, whether the model includes cooperative effects, or how the 0.014 s⁻¹ exchange rate was fitted to the FRAP data. Without these details, an independent group cannot verify whether the model's predictions arise from the proposed exchange mechanism or from hidden parameter choices.
- **Load-bearing claim: ATP hydrolysis is not required for polymerization.** The evidence is that E136A and D158A mutants polymerize at rates indistinguishable from WT (Fig. 3C–D) despite markedly reduced Pi release (Fig. 3A). However, the elongation-rate quantification in Fig. 3D lacks error bars or statistical comparison (n values are given but no p-values or confidence intervals), making it unclear whether the apparent equivalence is statistically supported or merely visually similar. The claim also depends on the assumption that E136A and D158A mutations truly abolish or severely impair hydrolysis, but the paper does not directly measure ATP hydrolysis rates for these mutants in the polymerization assay—only Pi release in a separate liposome-binding assay. If hydrolysis occurs but Pi release is blocked, the mutants would still be ATP-hydrolysis-competent, and the conclusion would not hold. The paper should report kₕᵧ𝒹 for each mutant under polymerization conditions.
- **Load-bearing claim: Nucleotide exchange within filaments defines a new polymer class.** This claim is supported by the contrast with actin (which shows no FRAP recovery) and by the model simulations. However, the FRAP experiment in Fig. 3F uses a fluorescent ATP analog (ATP*) that is 17-fold diluted relative to unlabeled ATP, and the paper notes that ATP* is 'less efficient for polymerization than ATP' (Fig. S5C, F). If ATP* is incorporated into filaments at a lower rate or with different kinetics than ATP, the measured exchange rate may not reflect the physiological nucleotide-cycling rate. Additionally, the paper does not report the mobile fraction from the FRAP recovery, which is essential for distinguishing between rapid exchange and slow exchange of a subpopulation. Without the mobile fraction and a direct comparison of ATP* vs. ATP exchange kinetics, the claim that nucleotide cycling is a defining feature remains partially supported.
- TIRF image analysis relies on TSOAX software for filament tracking and length measurement, but the paper does not specify the TSOAX parameters (ridge detection threshold, minimum filament length, tracking algorithm settings) needed to reproduce the elongation-rate calculations in Fig. 2E and Fig. 3C–D.
- The Monte-Carlo model code is not deposited or linked; the paper states 'Supplementary Information, Modeling MreB dynamics' but provides only a prose description of reactions and rate constants, not the simulation code or a runnable implementation.
- The data availability statement promises deposition 'after publication' on Zenodo with no accession number or pre-registration; this is a HARD flag for load-bearing quantitative results (elongation rates, disassembly rates, FRAP recovery curves) that cannot be independently verified until publication.
- HS-AFM image processing uses FFT bandpass filtering with specific pixel thresholds (8 and 1 pixel) but does not justify these choices or report sensitivity analysis; the resulting filament-length measurements in Fig. 2B–D depend on these parameters and cannot be reproduced without them.
- The fluorescence anisotropy binding assay (Fig. 1D, Table 1) reports Kd values but does not specify whether the fits were constrained (the methods state rmax and rmin are 'constrained' but do not report the actual values used for each mutant), preventing independent verification of the reported affinities.

## Questions
- What are the exact FRAP acquisition parameters (laser power, pixel dwell time, bleach region radius, frame interval, number of post-bleach frames) for the ATP* exchange experiment in Fig. 3F, and what is the mobile fraction?
- How were filament ends identified and tracked in the TIRF disassembly experiments (Fig. 4A–B), and were both ends of each filament measured or only one?
- Can the authors provide the Monte-Carlo model code (or a detailed pseudocode with all rate equations and parameter values) and specify whether intrafilament exchange rates depend on nucleotide state?
- What are the TSOAX parameters (ridge detection threshold, minimum filament length, tracking settings) used for filament tracking in Figs. 2E and 3C–D?
- For the E136A and D158A mutants, what are the measured ATP hydrolysis rates (kₕᵧ𝒹) under the polymerization conditions (on SLBs with ATP), not just the Pi release rates in the liposome-binding assay?
