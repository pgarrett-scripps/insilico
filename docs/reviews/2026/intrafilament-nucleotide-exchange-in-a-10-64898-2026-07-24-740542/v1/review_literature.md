# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The citations are largely accurate and key prior work on actin nucleotide trapping, MreB structure, and ATP hydrolysis is appropriately cited. The three load-bearing claims are supported by cited evidence. However, the distinction between MreB and ParM nucleotide exchange mechanisms requires clarification to fully support the novelty claim of a "new class" of polymer behavior, and the relationship to the recent Mao et al. (2023) study on B. subtilis MreB should be made explicit. Additionally, ref. 8 (Colombo et al. 2021) appears to be a methods paper rather than the foundational source for actin nucleotide trapping, which is better attributed to ref. 36 (Pollard et al. 1992). These are fixable issues that do not undermine the core contribution but require revision for clarity and precision.

## Strengths
- The manuscript correctly cites Pollard et al. (1992, ref. 36) and Holmes et al. (1990, ref. 37) for the established principle that actin nucleotides remain trapped within filaments, a foundational claim for the novelty argument.
- The antiparallel structure of MreB double filaments is properly attributed to van den Ent et al. (2014, ref. 9), and recent molecular dynamics work on cardiolipin recruitment (ref. 18, Baileeves et al. 2025) is cited appropriately.
- The manuscript transparently reports that MreB nucleotide binding kinetics are ~300-fold slower than actin (Table 1, comparing to refs. 40, 41) and acknowledges this as a key difference, with proper citations to prior work on Geobacillus MreB (ref. 17).
- Equivalent mutations in ParM (ref. 29, Garner et al. 2004) and actin (refs. 30–31) are cited appropriately for the ATP hydrolysis claim.
- The critical concentration value (Cc ≈ 0.003 µM) is properly compared to actin's Cc values with correct citations (refs. 36, 42).
- Aluminum fluoride (ADP-AlF₄⁻) stabilization of actin is correctly cited to ref. 28 (Combeau & Carlier 1988).

## Weaknesses
- Ref. 8 (Colombo et al. 2021) is described as a methods paper on fluorescent nucleotide analogues, not the primary source establishing the "trapped nucleotide" principle in actin filaments; the foundational source is ref. 36 (Pollard et al. 1992), which should be clarified as the primary attribution.
- The manuscript states that ParM filaments exhibit nucleotide exchange (ref. 29, line 313) with the suggestion that this occurs at filament ends, but does not explicitly distinguish whether MreB exchange occurs throughout the filament interior while ParM exchange is restricted to ends, leaving the novelty claim of a "new class of biological polymer behavior" ambiguous.
- The manuscript cites Mao et al. (2023, ref. 10) on B. subtilis MreB nucleotide and lipid requirements but does not clearly distinguish what new mechanistic insights (intrafilament nucleotide exchange, kinetic symmetry, the Monte Carlo model) go beyond Mao et al.'s findings, leaving this distinction to the reader to infer.
- Ref. 17 (De San Eustaquio-Campillo et al., in preparation) is cited for E136A and D158A mutations in Geobacillus MreB; while not load-bearing here, unpublished work should be flagged as preliminary when used to support claims.
- The CCCP-induced ATP depletion experiment (Fig. 4J) cites ref. 39 (Strahl & Hamoen 2010) for CCCP's effect on membrane potential and cell division, but a citation to CCCP's mechanism of ATP depletion (uncoupling oxidative phosphorylation) would strengthen the methods section.

## Questions
- Does ParM exchange nucleotides only at filament ends (as ref. 29 suggests), while MreB exchanges throughout the filament interior? If so, state this explicitly to support the "new class" claim.
- What specific mechanistic insights (intrafilament exchange kinetics, the Monte Carlo model, kinetic symmetry) are novel relative to Mao et al. (2023, ref. 10)'s recent study of B. subtilis MreB nucleotide and lipid requirements?
- Is ref. 8 (Colombo et al. 2021) intended as the source for the "trapped nucleotide" principle in actin, or should ref. 36 (Pollard et al. 1992) be cited as the foundational work establishing this principle?
