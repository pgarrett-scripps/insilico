# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript's citations are generally accurate and support its main claims about intrafilament nucleotide exchange in MreB. However, a directly relevant 2022 paper by Pande et al. on MreB nucleotide-state dependence is omitted, and the attribution of the actin nucleotide-trapping claim to Colombo et al. (2021) is imprecise—the primary source should be cited directly. These are fixable issues requiring minor revision.

## Strengths
- The experimental claims about MreB polymerization kinetics and nucleotide dependence are well-supported by the data presented and appropriately cited to prior work on Geobacillus MreB and actin.
- The comparison to actin filament dynamics is grounded in established literature, and the distinction between MreB and actin behavior is clearly articulated.
- The in vivo validation using CCCP-induced ATP depletion is a strong experimental approach that is properly contextualized within the nucleotide-exchange model.

## Weaknesses
- Missing directly competing work: Pande et al. (2022, J. Cell Biol. 221:e202108065) on nucleotide-state-dependent MreB filament organization is not cited. This is a HARD omission of a directly relevant recent paper that bears on the novelty claim that nucleotide cycling within filaments is a 'new class of biological polymer behavior.' The authors must either cite this work and explain how their findings differ, or verify that Pande et al. does not address intrafilament exchange.
- Imprecise attribution of actin nucleotide-trapping claim: The statement that nucleotides are 'effectively trapped' in actin filaments (line 8) cites Colombo et al. (2021), which is a methods paper on fluorescent nucleotide analogs, not a primary source for the nucleotide-trapping mechanism. The correct primary source is likely Pollard et al. (1992) or earlier work on actin filament nucleotide kinetics. This is a SOFT issue of citation clarity that should be corrected.
- Uncertain attribution of ParM nucleotide-exchange claim: The claim that 'free ADP was reported to also increase the rate of ParM filament disassembly, and this effect was proposed to result from nucleotide exchange at filament ends' (line 313) is attributed to Garner et al. (2004), but this paper focuses on dynamic instability and does not appear to address nucleotide exchange at filament ends. The authors should verify this attribution or cite a more specific source.
- Self-citation to in-press work: Reference 17 (Adriaans et al., in press, Biophys. J. 2026) is cited as primary evidence for MreB membrane binding and ATP-dependent polymerization (Fig. 1E–H). While self-citation is acceptable, the liposome co-sedimentation and QCM-D data presented in the current manuscript should be the primary evidence, with the in-press paper as supporting detail.
- Incomplete comparison to membrane-templated polymerization: The manuscript does not cite prior work on membrane-templated polymerization of other bacterial actin homologs (e.g., ParM, FtsZ) to position this mechanism in the broader context of bacterial cytoskeletal assembly.
- Verification needed for Mao et al. claim: The statement that 'Under limiting ATP concentrations, Geobacillus MreB filaments progressively disappeared from TEM fields over time' (line 227) should be verified as accurately representing Mao et al. (2023).

## Questions
- Can the authors confirm that Pande et al. (2022, J. Cell Biol. 221:e202108065) does not address intrafilament nucleotide exchange, and if it does, how does their work differ from the current findings?
- Does Garner et al. (2004) specifically propose nucleotide exchange at ParM filament ends, or should this attribution be directed to a later paper on ParM dynamics?
- Which primary source establishes that actin filaments show no nucleotide exchange—is it Pollard et al. (1992), or an earlier work that should be cited directly rather than through Colombo et al. (2021)?
