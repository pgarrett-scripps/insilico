# Clarity & Presentation Reviewer

## Summary
This manuscript is clearly written overall with strong narrative flow and well-organized presentation of a complex biophysical study. The central claims about intrafilament nucleotide exchange in MreB are stated explicitly and the evidence is presented systematically. However, one load-bearing claim lacks sufficient clarity about what distinguishes the proposed mechanism from alternatives, and several quantitative statements need tightening to meet publication standards.

## Strengths
- The manuscript clearly states its central contribution—that MreB filaments undergo continuous nucleotide exchange within intact filaments, a novel mechanism—and systematically builds evidence for it through multiple complementary techniques (TIRF, HS-AFM, fluorescence anisotropy, modeling).
- Figures are well-designed with clear legends, appropriate panel organization, and effective use of color and annotation; every major figure is referenced in text and every panel serves a stated purpose.
- Methods are presented in sufficient procedural detail that the experimental design, conditions, and order of operations are recoverable, with clear specification of which experiments used which protein concentrations and nucleotide conditions.

## Weaknesses
- The claim that intrafilament nucleotide exchange 'tunes filament stability' (Abstract, line 44; Main Text, line 282) is supported by showing that free ADP accelerates disassembly and free ATP stabilizes filaments, but the manuscript does not clearly distinguish this from a simpler alternative: that filament stability is determined solely by the nucleotide state of subunits at filament ends (an ATP cap), with interior exchange being incidental. The capping experiment (Fig. 4G-H) shows that E136A caps stabilize WT cores in ADP, but this tests whether caps matter, not whether interior exchange tunes stability independently of end effects. The model predicts interior exchange is 'significantly faster than Pi release' (line 331), but if interior exchange simply equilibrates the filament interior with the solution while end dynamics dominate stability, the mechanistic novelty claimed is overstated. What would resolve this: explicit modeling or experiment showing that interior nucleotide composition, independent of end state, causally affects disassembly rates.
- The quantitative claim that ATP binding 'triggers' polymerization (Abstract, line 42) is stated without quantifying the threshold or mechanism—does ATP binding to monomers trigger assembly, or does membrane binding of ATP-MreB trigger it, or both? The critical concentration is reported as ~0.003 µM (line 187), but the text does not state whether this applies to ATP-MreB or to the overall system, and whether the triggering is a nucleotide-dependent conformational change or simply a concentration effect.
- The statement that 'nucleotide exchange within MreB filaments defines a new class of biological polymer behavior' (Abstract, lines 44-45) is not quantitatively bounded—the exchange rate (0.014 s⁻¹ for WT, Table 1) is stated but not compared to the timescale of polymerization (elongation rate ~45 µM⁻¹s⁻¹ at filament ends) or to the residence time of a subunit in the filament, making it unclear whether this exchange is functionally significant on the timescale of filament dynamics in vivo.

## Questions
- Figure 2E shows a plateau in elongation rate above 0.06–0.09 µM MreB, but the text attributes this to 2D diffusion limitation; does the model in Fig. 4D predict this plateau, and if so, does it arise from the same mechanism?
- In the FRAP experiment (Fig. 3E), why does TFLime show no recovery while ATP* shows rapid recovery (Fig. 3F)—is this due to irreversible photochemical trapping of TFLime as stated, and if so, why is this mechanism not a confound for interpreting the ATP* recovery as nucleotide exchange rather than fluorogen exchange?
- The in vivo experiment (Fig. 4J) shows E136A filaments remain stable upon CCCP addition while WT filaments delocalize, but CCCP depletes ATP and raises ADP; does the model predict that E136A filaments would remain stable under high ADP, and if not, what explains the in vivo observation?
