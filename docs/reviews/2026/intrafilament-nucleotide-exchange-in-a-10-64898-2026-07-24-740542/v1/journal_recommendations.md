# Journal Recommendations

## As-is (current quality)
_No headline venue is realistic at the current quality — see Alternative._

## After required revisions
- **eLife**
  - Fit: eLife specializes in high-quality life sciences research with rigorous peer review and strong emphasis on reproducibility, transparency, and data availability. This manuscript's multi-method approach (TIRF, HS-AFM, biochemistry, in vivo imaging, Monte Carlo modeling) and novel mechanistic finding about nucleotide cycling in a prokaryotic actin homolog fit eLife's scope. The required revisions—quantitative disambiguation of interior vs. end-limited exchange, deposited model code, detailed FRAP parameters, and named statistical tests—align precisely with eLife's reproducibility standards.
  - Realism: After addressing the major revisions (particularly the quantitative FRAP reanalysis comparing observed vs. predicted end-limited exchange rates, deposited code, and statistical rigor), acceptance is realistic. eLife values exactly this kind of substantive mechanistic work with convergent evidence and transparent limitations; the panel's 3.79 score reflects fixable gaps in specificity and reproducibility rather than fundamental flaws.
- **Nature Communications**
  - Fit: Nature Communications publishes high-impact original research across biology with emphasis on novel mechanisms and rigorous methodology. The intrafilament nucleotide exchange finding—if quantitatively disambiguated from end-limited exchange—represents a genuine advance in understanding prokaryotic cytoskeletal dynamics and ATP-dependent polymer regulation. The multi-method triangulation (FRAP, HS-AFM, kinetics, in vivo) and Monte Carlo modeling fit the journal's standards for mechanistic depth.
  - Realism: Realistic after major revisions, particularly the quantitative end-vs-interior exchange analysis and full reproducibility artifacts. Nature Communications has a higher bar for statistical rigor and model transparency than the current submission provides, but the core finding is sufficiently novel and the required fixes are within the authors' stated capability.
- **Molecular Microbiology**
  - Fit: This journal focuses on bacterial cell biology and cytoskeletal mechanisms, making it a natural fit for MreB dynamics work. The paper's emphasis on how nucleotide cycling regulates MreB filament stability on membranes and its implications for cell morphogenesis align with the journal's scope. The required revisions strengthen rather than change the core narrative.
  - Realism: Good prospects after major revisions. Molecular Microbiology values mechanistic detail and in vivo validation; the CCCP delocalization experiment and E136A in vivo behavior already provide this. The quantitative disambiguation of interior exchange and full statistical reporting will meet the journal's standards for rigor without requiring new experiments.

## Alternative outlets
- **bioRxiv (with In Silico overlay review)**
  - Fit: The authors' stated target venue, In Silico, is an open overlay journal that reviews preprints and publishes the full referee record. This manuscript is well-suited: it is original research with checkable claims, all central evidence is presented or deposited, and the panel's debate itself (interior vs. end-limited exchange, model circularity, reproducibility gaps) is exactly the kind of substantive methodological critique that In Silico's public review process is designed to surface and resolve. Posting to bioRxiv with In Silico review provides immediate visibility while the authors address major revisions.
  - Realism: Very realistic as-is or with minor revisions. In Silico's scope explicitly includes work where 'a careful reader can evaluate claims from the manuscript itself and its cited or deposited materials.' The current manuscript meets this bar—the debate is transparent, the evidence is presented, and the gaps are clearly identified. An In Silico review would serve the community by documenting the specific quantitative work needed to disambiguate the mechanism, without requiring the authors to complete that work before publication. This is appropriate for a preprint venue.
- **PLOS Biology**
  - Fit: PLOS Biology publishes high-quality research across biology with strong emphasis on reproducibility, data availability, and public review. The manuscript's novel finding about nucleotide cycling in MreB, combined with its multi-method approach and in vivo validation, fits the journal's scope. PLOS's explicit reproducibility requirements (code, parameters, statistical tests) align with the major revisions needed.
  - Realism: Realistic after major revisions. PLOS Biology's acceptance threshold is somewhat lower than Nature Communications but higher than specialty journals; the required work (quantitative FRAP reanalysis, deposited code, statistical rigor) is exactly what PLOS expects. The panel's 3.79 score, combined with the authors' constructive rebuttal and feasible revision path, positions this well for PLOS after the major revisions are completed.
- **Biophysical Journal**
  - Fit: This journal specializes in quantitative biophysics and polymer dynamics, making it ideal for the TIRF/HS-AFM kinetics, FRAP analysis, and Monte Carlo modeling. The paper's focus on nucleotide-dependent filament stability and the mechanistic comparison with actin polymer physics fit squarely within the journal's scope. The required revisions (quantitative exchange-rate analysis, model code, FRAP parameters) are standard for Biophysical Journal submissions.
  - Realism: Realistic after major revisions. Biophysical Journal has high standards for quantitative rigor and reproducibility of kinetic/modeling work, but the authors have already collected the data needed to meet these standards. The required FRAP reanalysis comparing observed vs. predicted end-limited exchange rates is exactly the kind of quantitative disambiguation the journal expects.

## Notes
The manuscript presents a genuinely novel finding (intrafilament nucleotide exchange in MreB, contrasting with actin's trapped nucleotide) supported by convergent evidence (ATP* FRAP recovery, E136A loss, D158A incorporation, in vivo CCCP asymmetry). The major revisions are not about the core claim but about mechanistic specificity: disambiguating interior from end-limited exchange quantitatively (via FRAP reanalysis), depositing model code and parameters, and adding statistical rigor. These are substantive but feasible fixes that do not require new wet-lab work. The authors' rebuttal was unusually constructive and largely credible; the editor accepted their proposed reanalysis path as the standard for revision. As-is submission to In Silico (an overlay preprint journal) is appropriate and realistic; after-revision submission to eLife, Nature Communications, or PLOS Biology is realistic once the quantitative disambiguation and reproducibility artifacts are in place. Specialty journals (Molecular Microbiology, Biophysical Journal) are also realistic after revisions and may have slightly faster timelines.
