# Novelty & Contribution Reviewer

## Summary
This manuscript reports three load-bearing claims: (1) intrafilament nucleotide exchange occurs within MreB filaments, a phenomenon claimed as unprecedented in biological polymers; (2) ATP hydrolysis is mechanistically uncoupled from and not required for MreB polymerization; and (3) nucleotide cycling within filaments tunes filament stability on membranes. The first claim is genuinely novel and well-supported by FRAP and fluorescent nucleotide exchange experiments. The second claim is substantially supported but requires careful interpretation: the authors show ATPase mutants polymerize, but the mechanism differs from actin in ways that may not generalize the claim as broadly as stated. The third claim is mechanistically plausible but rests on a model whose parameters are partly fitted to the observations it explains, limiting independent validation. The work makes a solid incremental contribution to understanding MreB assembly, but the framing of novelty requires qualification.

## Strengths
- The direct experimental evidence for intrafilament nucleotide exchange via ATP* FRAP recovery (Fig. 3F–I) is clear and appears to be the first direct demonstration of this phenomenon in any biological polymer, distinguishing MreB from canonical actin and tubulin.
- The dual-color TIRF experiments demonstrating kinetically symmetrical elongation at both filament ends (Fig. 2F–G) are well-designed and provide independent confirmation of non-polar growth, a structural prediction now validated dynamically.
- The integration of multiple orthogonal techniques (HS-AFM, TIRF, fluorescence anisotropy, ATPase assays, FRAP, in vivo imaging) with a stochastic Monte-Carlo model creates a coherent mechanistic narrative that is internally consistent and testable.

## Weaknesses
- The claim that ATP hydrolysis is 'not required' for polymerization is supported for initiation (E136A and D158A do polymerize), but the mechanistic distinction between ATP binding and phosphate retention is not fully resolved experimentally; ADP-AlF₄⁻ permits polymerization while ADP alone does not, suggesting the phosphate group may be required, not merely ATP binding.
- The model's predictive power for claim 3 (nucleotide cycling tunes stability) is limited by the fact that several kinetic parameters (especially intrafilament nucleotide exchange rates k₊,ₙ and k₋,ₙ, Table 1) are derived from or fitted to the same experiments the model is used to explain (Fig. 4I, S10), creating a circularity where the model is validated by reproducing the data it was parameterized to fit.
- The in vivo experiment (Fig. 4J) provides independent support for claim 3, but it is qualitative (localization vs. delocalization) rather than quantitative, limiting the strength of the mechanistic claim.
- The critical concentration (Cc ≈ 0.003 µM) is much lower than actin's (0.15 µM), but the authors do not discuss whether this reflects the membrane-templated 2D polymerization mechanism or a fundamentally different nucleotide affinity, affecting generalizability of the model.
- The HS-AFM elongation rate (14.4 ± 10.16 nm·s⁻¹, n=7) is based on only 7 filament segments with high lateral mobility, and the authors acknowledge this is a 'first estimate' (line 177), leaving uncertainty about true elongation kinetics at physiological densities.
- The manuscript does not discuss whether the nucleotide exchange mechanism observed in vitro on supported lipid bilayers (which lack crowded cellular environment, membrane curvature, and associated proteins) reflects the in vivo situation.
- The relationship and complementarity with the concurrent preprint 'A genetic screen unveils the polymerization cycle of the bacterial actin-like MreB' (de San Eustaquio-Campillo et al., 2026) is not discussed, leaving unclear whether they represent independent confirmation or overlapping work.

## Questions
- Were the intrafilament nucleotide exchange rates (k₊,ₙ and k₋,ₙ, Table 1) measured independently of the Monte-Carlo model, or are they fitted parameters derived from matching the model to the FRAP and disassembly data?
- Does ADP-Pi alone (without ATP) support MreB polymerization, and if so, at what rate relative to ATP, to clarify whether the requirement is for ATP binding or for phosphate retention?
- How do the findings in this manuscript relate to the concurrent preprint by de San Eustaquio-Campillo et al. (2026) on the MreB polymerization cycle, and are the two studies independent or complementary?
- The claim that nucleotide binding rates to MreB are '~300-fold lower than those reported for actin' is attributed to MreB being purified in a nucleotide-free state; is this directly tested (e.g., by pre-incubating with nucleotide and measuring rebinding)?
- The FRAP experiment on TFLime-labeled filaments (Fig. 3E) shows no recovery, attributed to photochemical trapping of the fluorogen; is this explanation independently validated?
- Does the in vivo CCCP experiment (Fig. 4J) directly measure filament length or number, or does the interpretation that E136A filaments are 'stable' rely solely on the localization signal as a proxy for filament persistence?
