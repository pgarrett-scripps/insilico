# Decision Letter

**Decision:** major

## Summary of Evaluation
Eight referees judged this a substantial, well-triangulated multi-method study whose core observation — that nucleotide can exchange within intact MreB filaments, unlike actin's trapped nucleotide — is genuinely novel and largely supported by convergent evidence (ATP* FRAP recovery, its loss in E136A, D158A incorporation, and the in vivo CCCP asymmetry). Ethics and transparency were rated exemplary. The panel split (1 accept / 4 minor / 3 major; weighted 3.79) not on whether the headline contrast with actin holds, but on one structural issue that three high-confidence reviewers reached independently: the specifically *intrafilament* (position-independent) character of exchange — the basis for the 'new class of biopolymer behaviour' framing and for the Monte Carlo model's explicit interior-independence assumption — is asserted rather than isolated by any reported experiment or quantitative analysis. Compounding this, several model rate constants (k−,d, k−,e, k+,n, k−,n) are fitted to the same disassembly/FRAP curves the model is used to explain, and downstream claims (Fig. 4I 'rejuvenation', the ADP-flush causal narrative, the D158A incorporation-rate interpretation) inherit that assumption.

I weighed the rebuttal carefully and it is unusually constructive. The authors' disagreements are, in my judgement, largely credible and I am not requiring changes on them: the TFLime-vs-ATP* FRAP distinction is explicitly drawn in the manuscript text as quoted; the HS-AFM n=7 value is already flagged in-text as a first estimate with the plateau claim resting on the large TIRF series; the ADP-AlF4− point is answered by the correct distinction between catalytic turnover and γ-phosphate occupancy; and the CCCP inference is already presented as a qualitative test consistent with independently measured E136A biochemistry rather than a direct nucleotide-state measurement. The capping-experiment defence (breakage within the WT segment despite intact non-exchanging caps) is a genuine argument for interior involvement and should be made more prominent — but it is qualitative, and the load-bearing gap the authors themselves identify remains: interior versus end-limited-plus-diffusion exchange is not disambiguated in the manuscript as submitted.

Crucially, the authors propose a route that does not require new wet-lab work: a quantitative comparison of the observed ATP* recovery rate against the rate predicted for purely end-limited exchange given the measured k−,t and the filament-length distribution, using data already collected. I accept that as the standard for this revision; no reviewer demanded a new end-blocking experiment as a precondition, and I am not imposing one. The decision is major rather than minor because the required work is substantive analysis and new deposited artifacts (reanalysis, sensitivity analysis, deposited model code and imaging parameters, named statistical tests, an operational definition of 'homogeneous') rather than editing — but the core finding is expected to survive, and the authors' conceded change list already maps onto nearly all of it. The compliance audits raise no gap that blocks evaluation of the central claim; the citation misattribution (Ref. 8 for actin nucleotide trapping) is conceded and easily fixed, and the HARD methods items are reagent-traceability and code/parameter reporting rather than unresolvable protocol delegation.

## Required Revisions
1. Disambiguate interior from end-limited exchange quantitatively, as the authors propose: compare the observed ATP* FRAP recovery rate (Fig. 3F) against the rate predicted for purely end-limited exchange given the measured k−,t and the measured filament-length distribution, and report the comparison explicitly with uncertainties. State plainly in the Results and Discussion what this analysis can and cannot exclude. If the analysis cannot exclude an end-limited mechanism, retitle and rescope the 'intrafilament' claim accordingly rather than retaining it as established.
2. For the Monte Carlo model, add a table distinguishing independently measured parameters (each with citation to the source experiment) from parameters fitted to reproduce Fig. S8/S10, and report a sensitivity analysis varying k−,d, k−,e, k+,n and k−,n across their plausible ranges. State explicitly which conclusions (in particular Fig. 4I 'rejuvenation' and the ADP-flush narrative) depend on the interior-independent-exchange assumption, so readers can see where model output is a prediction versus a restatement of fitted data.
3. Deposit runnable model code and analysis artifacts in a public repository (Zenodo/GitHub) with a DOI cited in the manuscript, including: the Monte Carlo implementation with simulation procedure, number of runs, initial conditions and random seed handling; the TSOAX parameter files and MATLAB extraction script; and the raw FRAP curves, filament-tracking outputs and anisotropy fits underlying Figs. 2-4. Replace the 'deposited after publication' statement with a private-for-review link now and a public DOI at acceptance.
4. Add the missing FRAP acquisition methods for both TFLime and ATP* experiments: bleach ROI radius, laser power/percentage, pixel dwell time, frame interval, total acquisition time, and number of frames; and report the mobile fraction and recovery half-time with propagated uncertainty for Fig. 3F, at the level of detail currently given only for the CL-FRAP Soumpasis fit. Without these the reported exchange rate cannot be independently assessed.
5. Add named statistical tests, test statistics and multiplicity-corrected p-values for Fig. 3D (elongation rates across WT/E136A/D158A) and Fig. 4B-C (disassembly rates across conditions), and state the test and correction in the Statistical Analysis section. Also make the error-bar definition (SD, SEM or CI) explicit in every figure legend, which is currently inconsistent.
6. Define 'homogeneous' ATP* incorporation (Fig. 3H) operationally and quantitatively — e.g. line-scan intensity profiles with a coefficient of variation along filament length and an end-proximal versus mid-filament intensity comparison over time — and report the metric in the figure and legend. As written this is a qualitative visual claim doing causal work.
7. Scope the 'new class of biopolymer behaviour' language to the tested regime (B. subtilis MreB on cardiolipin-containing SLBs, 0.05-1.2 uM) and add an explicit contrast with the ParM end-exchange literature stating whether and how MreB's behaviour is distinguishable from an end-limited mechanism. Elevate the E136A-capping result (breakage within the WT segment despite non-exchanging caps) into this discussion, with its qualitative status stated.
8. Correct the citation attribution for the actin trapped-nucleotide principle to refs. 36/37 (Pollard et al. 1992 and companion), retaining ref. 8 only where the fluorescent-nucleotide methodology is discussed. Additionally, verify and correct the load-bearing attributions flagged by the audit — ParM E136A ATPase loss and ADP-induced ParM disassembly (ref. 29), actin D158A ATPase reduction (refs. 30/31), TFLime photochemistry (ref. 13), and CCCP-mediated ATP depletion (ref. 39) — citing primary sources that actually demonstrate each claim.
9. Add a Discussion paragraph delineating what is new relative to Mao et al. (2023) and the concurrent MreB polymerization-cycle preprint, citing the latter as independent complementary work. Clarify the publication status of the future-dated refs. 17 and 18 (2026, 2025), and if either is the authors' own concurrent or in-press work, say so.
10. Provide vendor catalog numbers for the ATTO-488 nucleotide analogs (ATP*, ADP*, AMPPNP*; Jena Bioscience) and for CCCP, DOPC and cardiolipin, since the nucleotide analogs are the critical reagents for the paper's central experiments. Reproduce, or make accessible in the deposited materials, Supplementary Tables S1-S3 (primers, strain genotypes with parental background, plasmid sequence) and the 'Modeling MreB dynamics' supplementary text.

## Minor Suggestions
- Consider adding the concentration-dependent liposome-binding titration for DOPC:CL versus DOPC:PG that you propose. This would strengthen the cardiolipin claim, but I am treating it as optional: if it is not included, rescope the Fig. 1E-F conclusion to state that cardiolipin is a contributing determinant measured at a single MreB concentration rather than the major determinant.
- Add citations for the general statements currently unsupported: cardiolipin's cone-shaped tetra-acylated structure, the intracellular MreB concentration relative to Cc, and the absence of canonical actin-binding-protein homologues in bacteria.
- Report software versions consistently (ImageJ/Fiji, SOAX/TSOAX, MATLAB, PRISM) and note any validation of the custom Omnipose model used for cell-morphology segmentation.
- State briefly how model 'accuracy' in reproducing Fig. S8 kinetics was assessed (goodness-of-fit metric or tolerance), since the current phrasing is qualitative.
- No IACUC statement is needed for B. subtilis, but a one-line biosafety/ethics statement would pre-empt the question for readers outside microbiology.
