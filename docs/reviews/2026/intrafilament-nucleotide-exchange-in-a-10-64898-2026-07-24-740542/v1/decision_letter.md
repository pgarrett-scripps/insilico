# Decision Letter

**Decision:** major

## Summary of Evaluation
The panel is unanimous that this manuscript contains at least one solid, well-supported and citable result: that ATP hydrolysis is mechanistically dispensable for MreB polymerization, established with two independent ATPase-dead mutants (E136A, D158A) plus ADP-AlF4-, and converging across TIRF, HS-AFM, fluorescence anisotropy, QCM-D and in vivo imaging. No reviewer disputes this. Ethics (5/5), clarity (4/5), novelty (4/5) and literature (4/5) are strong, and the confidence-weighted score (3.79/5) sits near the minor/major boundary.

The contested territory is the headline claim: that continuous intrafilament nucleotide exchange 'tunes' filament stability and represents 'a new class of biological polymer behavior.' Five reviewers independently identified the same gap — that FRAP recovery of ATP* (Fig. 3F) versus TFLime non-recovery (Fig. 3E) does not by itself discriminate true interior nucleotide exchange from lateral subunit diffusion, generic dye rebinding, or end-restricted exchange, and that the capping experiment (Fig. 4G-H) is also compatible with an ADP-end/nucleotide-free-interior scenario.

I weighed the rebuttal carefully and it moves the needle on two of these points. First, the charge that the Monte Carlo model encodes exchange circularly is credibly answered: the authors point to Fig. 4I, which explicitly contains the k+,n = k-,n = 0 control, and to the divergent nucleotide-pool-dependent outcomes with and without exchange. The model is therefore not decorative, and I do not treat the circularity objection as load-bearing. Second, the FRAP-artifact objection is partly answered by the catalytic-state dependence of recovery (abolished in E136A, accelerated in D158A) and by the homogeneous, non-end-biased ATP* incorporation along preformed filaments in Fig. 3H/I; these are genuinely dissociative-leaning controls that the reviewers did not fully credit. Third, the citation-integrity concern about Ref. 17 being used as a substitute for the authors' own membrane-binding data is credibly rebutted by pointing to Fig. 1E-H.

What remains, however, is enough to place this above minor revision. (i) There is a HARD reproducibility failure: the Monte Carlo model code, the simulation algorithm, the full equations and the MATLAB analysis scripts are undeposited, with deposition promised only after publication. Under this journal's priority ordering, checkability of a load-bearing computational argument outweighs novelty, and a reader cannot presently evaluate the model on which the central claim partly rests. The authors concede this and propose a fix, but it must be delivered and inspected in revision, not promised. (ii) A HARD citation issue: a quantitative kinetic comparison (~10-20-fold lower than Geobacillus MreB) is supported by an unpublished, future-dated reference that no reader can check. (iii) Substantive analytical gaps the authors themselves concede — unspecified statistical tests and multiple-comparison handling in Fig. 4B, an unquantified CCCP experiment (Fig. 4J), and the absence of any quantitative comparison of k-,n to k_hyd and subunit residence time, which is precisely the arithmetic that would make or break the physiological significance of exchange. (iv) The Pande et al. 2022 omission, which bears directly on the novelty framing.

On the authors' stated load-bearing concern: I am not requiring new single-molecule tracking or stopped-flow off-rate measurements as a precondition for this revision cycle. Those would settle the mechanism definitively, and I encourage them, but the appropriate remedy here is that the mechanistic claim be scaled to the evidence actually presented — the exchange interpretation stated as the authors' preferred interpretation, with the alternatives (lateral diffusion, end-restricted exchange, static ADP-end/nucleotide-free-interior) named in the main text and the evidence bearing against each set out explicitly. Combined with the promised de-escalation of 'tunes' and 'new class of biological polymer behavior', that brings the claim within reach of the data. The volume of concrete, checkable work required — code deposition with sensitivity analysis, new quantification of Fig. 4J, restated statistics, a new timescale comparison, and reframing of the central claim — is a major revision rather than a minor one, but this is a repairable manuscript with a genuine result at its core, not a rejection.

## Required Revisions
1. Deposit, concurrent with the revised submission, the complete Monte Carlo simulation code, the MATLAB data-extraction script, model equations and simulation algorithm (specify the Gillespie variant or equivalent), and a parameter-provenance table marking each rate constant as directly measured or fitted with its uncertainty, in a public repository with a permanent DOI (Zenodo/GitHub). Post-publication deposition is not sufficient. Include the promised sensitivity analysis varying k+,n and k-,n over 5-10x, and state goodness-of-fit for the comparisons in Fig. S8 and S10.
2. Rescale the central mechanistic claim to the evidence presented. In the abstract, title framing and Discussion, (a) replace 'tunes' with the non-agentive formulation the authors propose, (b) drop or explicitly qualify 'a new class of biological polymer behavior', scoping conclusions to B. subtilis MreB and noting that generality across MreB orthologs is untested, and (c) add an explicit paragraph in the Results or Discussion naming the alternative interpretations of the Fig. 3E/F FRAP result — lateral subunit diffusion, nonspecific nucleotide rebinding, and end-restricted exchange — and setting out which specific data (E136A abolition, D158A acceleration, homogeneous ATP* incorporation in Fig. 3H/I) argue against each, and what each still leaves open. State plainly which experiment would be definitive.
3. Resolve the Ref. 17 citation. The quantitative statement that B. subtilis MreB nucleotide-binding kinetics are ~10-20-fold lower than Geobacillus stearothermophilus MreB is currently supported only by an unpublished, future-dated reference. Either replace it with published, resolvable sources, support it with data presented in this manuscript, or remove the numerical comparison and state that the comparison awaits publication of the companion work. Apply the same check to the other 'as previously described' delegations to Adriaans et al. (liposome binding, TEM, QCM-D, ATPase); since full protocols are given in-text, it is sufficient to make clear that the manuscript is self-contained on these points.
4. Add the quantitative timescale comparison the authors propose: a table or paragraph in Results setting k-,n (0.0141 s-1) against k_hyd (1.54x10-3 s-1) and against estimated subunit residence times in the filament, with the assumptions and uncertainties stated. This comparison is load-bearing for the claim that intrafilament exchange is fast enough to be physiologically consequential and is currently absent.
5. Complete the statistical reporting for Fig. 4B: name the test used (Mann-Whitney U as proposed), state the distributional justification, define whether n counts independent filaments or independent experiments and how pooling was handled, and apply and report the Holm-Bonferroni correction for the pairwise genotype comparisons. Extend the same explicit test/n/error-bar annotation to Figs. 2E and 4I, where the error-bar definition is currently unstated.
6. Quantify the CCCP delocalization experiment (Fig. 4J). Report the number of cells and independent experiments for WT and E136A, provide a quantitative measure of fluorescence retention or delocalization over time, and give a statistical comparison between the two strains. As presented, this in vivo support for the model is qualitative only.
7. Cite Pande et al. 2022 (J. Cell Biol.) and add a short Discussion paragraph comparing their findings on nucleotide-state-dependent MreB organization with the present data, stating explicitly what remains new here (exchange within intact filaments) and what does not.
8. Correct the two flagged attributions: replace or supplement Colombo et al. 2021 with primary sources (e.g. Pollard et al. 1992; Holmes et al. 1990) for actin nucleotide trapping in filaments, and verify the Garner et al. 2004 attribution for ParM end-exchange-driven disassembly, correcting or rephrasing to match what that paper actually reports.

## Minor Suggestions
- Add version numbers for ImageJ/Fiji, MATLAB, PRISM and SOAX/TSOAX, and vendor/catalogue details for non-fluorescent ATP/ADP/AMPPNP/AMPPCP, CCCP and phosphatidylglycerol.
- For the FRAP experiments, state laser power, dwell time and the number of independent filaments/replicates per condition alongside the reported bleach radius, half-time and mobile fraction.
- Add the clarifying statement and Table 1 footnote on the critical concentration (Cc ~0.003 uM), distinguishing the thermodynamic Cc from the higher-concentration kinetic plateau, as proposed in the rebuttal.
- For the statement that Mao et al. 2023 previously suggested ATP hydrolysis mediates MreB filament formation at the membrane, add a specific figure or section pointer, since the present work reinterprets that result.
- Consider ensuring Supplementary Tables S1-S3 (primer sequences, strain genotypes, plasmid sequence) are deposited alongside the code, as the reagent audit could not verify them from the main text.
- Brief details on B. subtilis strain construction (Gibson assembly conditions, transformation and selection) would make that section self-contained.
