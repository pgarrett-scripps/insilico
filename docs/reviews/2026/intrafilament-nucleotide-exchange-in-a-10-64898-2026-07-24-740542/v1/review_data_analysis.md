# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
This is a well-executed experimental study with rigorous quantitative analysis of MreB filament dynamics. The core claims are supported by appropriate methods and multiple independent lines of evidence. Statistical reporting is generally transparent, though a few comparisons lack explicit test names and some effect sizes could be more precisely stated. The work merits publication with minor revisions to clarify statistical details.

## Strengths
- Polymerization kinetics are quantified across multiple independent experiments with stated sample sizes (n values), appropriate error reporting (SD), and linear regression to extract rate constants from concentration-dependent data (Fig. 2E, Table 1).

## Weaknesses
- The critical claim that MreB filaments exhibit intrafilament nucleotide exchange rests on FRAP recovery of ATP* (Fig. 3F) versus no recovery of TFLime (Fig. 3E). The authors attribute the difference to photochemical trapping of TFLime but do not directly test this mechanism—they could have used a reversible TFLime-binding condition or compared recovery kinetics quantitatively to distinguish reversible exchange from irreversible trapping. Without this control, the conclusion that nucleotide exchange occurs (rather than that TFLime simply does not exchange) remains an inference rather than a direct observation. The paper would be strengthened by either demonstrating TFLime recovery under conditions where photochemical trapping is prevented, or by quantifying the ATP* recovery rate and showing it matches the predicted nucleotide exchange rate from the Monte Carlo model.
- The disassembly rate comparisons (Fig. 4A–C, quantified in Fig. 4B) report mean ± SD for WT, E136A, and D158A filaments under three conditions (buffer wash, ADP wash) but do not state which statistical test was used to compare rates between genotypes, whether assumptions (normality, equal variance) were checked, or whether multiple comparisons were corrected. The figure legend states 'n = 219, 206, 87, 157, 408 and 60' but does not clarify whether these are independent filaments or replicates, and whether the data are normally distributed (given the wide SDs and apparent non-linearity in Fig. 4C, non-parametric tests may be more appropriate). A student's t-test is mentioned in the Methods (line 845) but not applied to these headline comparisons.
- The Monte Carlo model (Fig. 4D, Supplementary Information) is presented as validation of the nucleotide exchange hypothesis, but the paper does not report goodness-of-fit metrics, confidence intervals on fitted parameters, or sensitivity analysis showing which parameters most strongly drive the predicted behavior. The model uses experimentally determined rate constants but does not quantify uncertainty in those constants or show how parameter uncertainty propagates to the model predictions. Without this, it is unclear whether the model's agreement with experiment (Fig. S8, S10) is tight enough to distinguish the proposed mechanism from alternatives.

## Questions
- Figure 2E shows elongation rate plateaus above 0.06–0.09 µM MreB; the text states this plateau 'is not an artefact of the pFAST tag' by comparing to HS-AFM at 1.2 µM, but HS-AFM measures only 7 filaments with high SD (14.4 ± 10.16 nm·s⁻¹) and the authors acknowledge 'only a very limited number of filaments could be tracked'—is the HS-AFM rate actually consistent with the TIRF plateau within error, or is this a post-hoc comparison without a formal test?
- Table 1 reports ATP and ADP binding rates for MreB as ~300-fold slower than actin and attributes this to conformational rearrangement, but the A260/A280 ratio (0.62) indicates MreB is nucleotide-free at purification; was the nucleotide-free state confirmed for each batch used in kinetic experiments, and could residual nucleotide or aggregation state explain the slow binding kinetics?
- The in vivo CCCP experiment (Fig. 4J) shows E136A remains localized while WT delocalizes, supporting the model, but only qualitative images are shown; were multiple cells quantified, and was the delocalization rate or extent compared statistically between WT and E136A?
- FRAP experiments (Fig. 3E, F) report 'no significant recovery' for TFLime but do not state the threshold for significance, the number of replicates, or the statistical test used to conclude recovery was absent.
