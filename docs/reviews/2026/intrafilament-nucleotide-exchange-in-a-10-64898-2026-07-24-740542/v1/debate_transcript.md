# Debate Transcript

## Advocate — round 1

The panel converges on a genuine, important advance: MreB polymerization is ATP-binding-driven and mechanistically uncoupled from hydrolysis, demonstrated not by inference but by two independent hydrolysis-dead mutants (E136A, D158A) that still polymerize with WT-like kinetics (Fig. 3C-D), plus the ADP-AlF4- stabilization result (Fig. S6). This alone is a solid, citable contribution. Beyond that, the capping experiment (Fig. 4G-H) is a genuine mechanistic test, not just consistency: E136A-ATP*-capped WT cores still fragment internally under ADP flush, actively excluding the simpler 'ATP-cap-only' model that reviewers themselves raise as the alternative—this is data, not modeling. The multi-modal convergence (TIRF, HS-AFM, anisotropy, QCM-D, liposome co-sedimentation, in vivo CCCP) noted by the novelty and methodology reviewers as a strength reduces single-technique-artifact risk substantially. Critically, the authors do not overclaim uniformly: they explicitly flag the HS-AFM n=7 limitation, the weak apparent polarity in Fig. 2G, and attribute it honestly to crowding/stochastic noise rather than hiding it. The reviewers' sharpest objection—that FRAP/exchange rate versus simple end-effects isn't fully disentangled—is a legitimate call for additional controls (e.g., quantifying ATP* recovery kinetics against hydrolysis timescale, depositing the Monte Carlo code now rather than promising Zenodo later, softening 'tunes' to more precise causal language). These are revision-tractable: they require clarifying wording and releasing already-generated model code/parameters, not new experimental paradigms. None of the reviewers dispute the core polymerization-hydrolysis uncoupling or the capping result; they dispute the strength of the exchange-causality language, which can be recalibrated without undermining publication.

**Key points:**
- ATPase-dead mutants (E136A, D158A) polymerizing normally is direct, reviewer-uncontested evidence that hydrolysis is dispensable for assembly
- Fig. 4G-H capping experiment is an actual causal test that rules out simple end-cap-only stabilization, not merely consistent modeling
- Multiple independent techniques (TIRF, HS-AFM, QCM-D, anisotropy, liposome assays, in vivo CCCP) converge on the same conclusions, reducing artifact risk
- Authors transparently flag their own limitations (HS-AFM n=7, weak apparent polarity), indicating good-faith rigor rather than concealment
- Remaining objections (FRAP mechanism ambiguity, model code deposition, 'tunes' language) are revision-fixable clarifications, not fatal flaws in the core findings

## Skeptic — round 1

I concede the hydrolysis-uncoupling result (Fig. 3C-D, S6) stands on solid, reviewer-uncontested ground. But the advocate mischaracterizes the central objection as a wording fix. Five independent reviewers — rigor, novelty, methodology, data_analysis, and reproducibility — converge on the SAME unresolved gap: the FRAP evidence for 'intrafilament nucleotide exchange' (Fig. 3F) cannot be distinguished from lateral subunit diffusion, nucleotide rebinding from solution, or exchange between neighboring subunits without dissociation. This isn't peripheral — it IS the paper's headline claim: 'nucleotide cycling within intact filaments defines a new class of biopolymer dynamics' (Abstract). As the rigor reviewer notes, the model 'predicts the same filament behaviors...without requiring any intrafilament exchange at all — exchange is invoked post-hoc to explain the FRAP data, not independently predicted.' The capping experiment (Fig. 4G-H), which the advocate calls decisive, only rules out simple end-capping-only; the methodology reviewer explicitly states it 'does not prove that interior nucleotide exchange is the mechanism' since 'a nucleotide-free interior with ADP-bound ends could produce the same fragmentation pattern.'　This is FIXABLE only if resolved by genuinely new experiments (single-molecule tracking, stopped-flow off-rate measurement, or a mutation blocking interior exchange) — not by rephrasing 'tunes' as the advocate suggests. Compounding this, the reproducibility reviewer flags that the Monte Carlo model — the only tool currently used to argue exchange matters mechanistically — has no deposited code, calling this a 'HARD reproducibility failure' for a claim that 'depends entirely on computational validation.' Together, these mean the paper's most novel, title-bearing claim is currently unfalsifiable by readers and empirically underdetermined by the data shown.
