Filament-level n will be reported as technical replicates only.

We will explicitly state in the Fig 3F legend that the recovery kinetics are consistent with nucleotide exchange and not end-wise addition because (i) recovery is homogeneous along the filament in the pure-ATP* assay, and (ii) end-wise addition would produce polarized labeling.

## Disagreements
- **methodology** — FRAP negative control (TFLime) compromised by irreversible photochemical trapping, so absent recovery cannot rule out subunit turnover; annealing/fragmentation could produce ATP* FRAP recovery.
  - Response: We acknowledge the TFLime FRAP limitation. However, the pure-ATP* incorporation assay (Fig 3H-I) provides direct evidence for intrafilament nucleotide exchange: homogeneous ATP* binding along the entire length of preformed filaments is incompatible with end-wise addition or annealing (which would label only ends). This assay does not rely on photobleaching and uses a different labeling strategy (ATP* vs TFLime). The convergence of ATP* FRAP recovery (Fig 3F) and homogeneous ATP* incorporation (Fig 3H-I) on WT filaments, plus the absence of ATP* incorporation in hydrolysis-dead E136A (Fig 3H-I), strongly supports intrafilament exchange as the mechanism. We will clarify this logic in the text.
  - Manuscript reference: When preformed pFAST-MreB filaments labelled with TFCoral were exposed to pure ATP* instead, homogeneous binding of ATP* was observed along the length of both WT and D158A filaments, but not of E136A filaments (Fig. 3H). Under these conditions, D158A filaments incorporated the nucleotide about twice as fast as WT filaments (Fig. 3I).
- **methodology** — Capping experiment (Fig 4G-H) does not verify cap completeness/symmetry; incomplete capping could allow ADP access to WT subunits from an uncapped end.
  - Response: We agree that cap completeness was not directly verified in the current data. However, the observation that filaments break specifically in the WT segment or near the cap junction (Fig 4H, blue arrows) — and that E136A caps remain stable while WT segments disassemble — is consistent with the model that ADP accesses WT subunits via intrafilament exchange, not from an uncapped end. If ADP entered only from an uncapped end, we would expect processive depolymerization from that end, not internal breakage. We will add a control experiment in revision: dual-color end-tracking of E136A cap addition to verify symmetric capping, or acknowledge this limitation and present the capping experiment as supportive but not definitive evidence.
  - Manuscript reference: filaments no longer underwent rapid (< 1 min) end-wise depolymerization. Instead, after less than 1 min, most filaments started breaking in the WT section or near one cap and depolymerized, leaving only the E136A caps (Fig. 4F-H).
- **novelty** — Critical concentration exceptionally low (0.003 µM) and not independently validated; plateau attributed to 2D diffusion but alternatives not ruled out.
  - Response: The low Cc is derived from the linear regime of elongation vs. concentration (Fig 2E) using standard polymerization theory (k+/k-). The plateau is consistent with a membrane-diffusion-limited on-rate, as supported by the concentration-independence above 0.06 µM and the agreement with HS-AFM rates at high concentration. We will add a discussion of alternative explanations (e.g., end-capping, cooperativity) and note that the 2D diffusion model is the most parsimonious given the membrane-templated assembly. We will also note that the Cc is an apparent value under these specific lipid conditions and may differ in vivo.
  - Manuscript reference: A linear fit at low concentrations yielded the association rate constant k+,t ≈ 45 µM.s⁻¹, the dissociation rate constant k-,t ≈ 0.135 s⁻¹, and the critical concentration Cc ≈ 0.003 µM (Table 1, see Supporting Data for calculations details).
