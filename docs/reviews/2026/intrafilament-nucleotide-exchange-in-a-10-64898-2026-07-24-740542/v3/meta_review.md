# Meta-Review

**Draft recommendation:** major

## Synthesis
The manuscript reports a striking discovery: intrafilament nucleotide exchange in MreB filaments, a behavior that distinguishes bacterial MreB from eukaryotic actin. The specialist panel converges on the novelty and potential significance of this finding (novelty 4/5, clarity 4/5, rigor 4/5), but three load-bearing gaps survive the advocate/skeptic debate and prevent the central mechanistic claim from being fully supported by the current evidence.

1. **D158A evidence chain broken (fatal, conceded by advocate).** The pure-ATP* incorporation assay (Fig 3H-I) is the strongest direct evidence for intrafilament exchange, showing homogeneous labeling along preformed filament lengths — inconsistent with end-wise addition. However, the text explicitly states D158A filaments "were not visible" under standard ATP* labeling (line 228), and the method for forming/visualizing the preformed D158A filaments used in Fig 3H-I is absent. Without this, the hydrolysis-independent exchange claim rests on WT data alone. The advocate's speculation ("likely polymerized with ATP then washed\)) is not in the manuscript. This gap cannot be closed by text revision alone; it requires either a complete method description (if the experiment was properly controlled) or new data.

2. **Pervasive pseudo-replication invalidates quantitative parameters (fatal, conceded by advocate).** Every kinetic comparison (elongation rates, ATP* incorporation, ADP depolymerization, symmetry ratios) treats filaments (n=10s–100s) as independent replicates despite originating from 1–2 SLB preparations. The data_analysis reviewer (3/5, conf 4) documents this across Figs 2, 3, 4, S8–S10. With n=1–2 true biological replicates, no reanalysis can supply statistical power for the claimed effect sizes (>5-fold ADP acceleration, 2-fold D158A vs WT ATP* incorporation). The Monte Carlo parameters (k_hyd, k_Pir, k_+n, k_-n, k_-e) inherit this uncertainty. This is an experimental design limitation, not an analytical oversight.

3. **Monte Carlo model lacks independent validation (fatal).** The model fits the same disassembly curves it predicts (Fig 4C, S10); Table 1 shows key rates derived from this study's fits or marked "ND." Fig 4I's ATP/ADP switching prediction is a model output, not experimental confirmation. The advocate's claim of "independently measured rates" is not borne out by Table 1.

4. **FRAP negative control compromised (methodology 3/5, conf 4).** TFLime photobleaching may irreversibly trap fluorogen (acknowledged in-text), so absent TFLime recovery (Fig 3E) cannot rule out subunit turnover. Annealing/fragmentation (Fig 1L-P) could produce identical ATP* FRAP recovery. Fig 3H-I supersedes this for WT, but the D158A gap (point 1) leaves the hydrolysis-independence claim dependent on the compromised FRAP.

5. **Capping experiment unverified (methodology 3/5, conf 4).** E136A cap completeness/symmetry was not demonstrated; incomplete capping could allow ADP access to WT subunits from an uncapped end, producing breakage near caps without intrafilament exchange.

The in vitro reconstitution of ATP-driven, hydrolysis-uncoupled polymerization on cardiolipin membranes (Figs 1–3C-D) is solid and novel. The WT intrafilament exchange evidence (Fig 3H-I) is strong. But the full mechanistic model — nucleotide cycling within intact filaments as a stability rheostat, operating identically in hydrolysis-deficient mutants, with quantitatively defined kinetics validated by modeling and in vivo — is not established by the current evidence. Per In Silico's standard: "A modest claim that is fully supported is better work than an important claim that is not." The manuscript makes an important claim whose quantitative and mutant-generalization pillars are unsupported.

## Decisive Factors
1. D158A filament formation method for Fig 3H-I is absent from the manuscript — the evidence chain for hydrolysis-independent exchange is broken. 2. All quantitative comparisons rest on n=1–2 biological replicates (SLB preps); pseudo-replication cannot be fixed by reanalysis. 3. Monte Carlo model parameters are fitted to the same data they predict, with no independent validation. 4. These are not presentation or analysis gaps; they require new experiments (D158A formation verification, additional biological replicates, cap-completeness controls, independent model validation). The confidence-weighted average (3.68) reflects the panel's recognition of the core discovery's value, but the skeptic's fatal critiques survived advocate contact and target the claim's load-bearing evidence, not its presentation. Major revision is warranted because the WT intrafilament exchange finding is genuine and the experimental system is powerful, but the manuscript must scale its claims to the evidence or supply the missing evidence. Reject would discard a real discovery; minor revision would paper over structural evidentiary gaps. Major revision with a requirement for new data on the three fatal points is the only calibration consistent with In Silico's mandate to judge whether conclusions survive the evidence given. The route forward is explicit: (a) provide complete D158A filament formation/visualization protocol for Fig 3H-I or redo with documented controls; (b) repeat key assays with ≥3 independent SLB preparations and report mixed-effects statistics; (c) verify cap completeness in capping experiment (e.g., dual-color end tracking); (d) either validate Monte Carlo predictions against new independent data or present the model as a hypothesis-generating framework with parameters clearly labeled as fitted vs. measured. The in vivo CCCP confound (rigor 4/5) and novelty qualification (literature 4/5) are secondary and addressable in the same revision. Data/code deposition (reproducibility 2/5) is a compliance requirement for publication, not a scientific gap, but must be completed before final acceptance.
