# Journal Recommendations

## As-is (current quality)
- **bioRxiv**
  - Fit: The manuscript reports a genuine discovery (intrafilament nucleotide exchange in MreB) but has structural evidentiary gaps (D158A protocol missing, pseudo-replication, unvalidated model) that require new experiments. As a preprint server, bioRxiv accepts manuscripts at any stage for community feedback without peer-review gatekeeping, making it the only appropriate 'as-is' venue.
  - Realism: Certain acceptance (preprint server, no peer review).
- **Biophysical Reports**
  - Fit: An open-access Biophysical Society journal that publishes shorter reports and preliminary findings in biophysics. Its scope includes cytoskeletal polymers and membrane-protein interactions. The paper's strong biophysical core (HS-AFM, TIRF, QCM-D, modeling) fits well, and the journal may tolerate the current limitations if framed as a preliminary report.
  - Realism: Moderate — the journal is newer and less selective, but the pseudo-replication and missing D158A controls may still trigger major revision requests.

## After required revisions
- **eLife**
  - Fit: eLife publishes mechanistic cell biology and biophysics with emphasis on novel mechanisms. The discovery of intrafilament nucleotide exchange in a prokaryotic actin homolog — distinguishing MreB from eukaryotic actin — aligns perfectly with eLife's scope. The in vitro reconstitution (TIRF, HS-AFM, SLBs), Monte Carlo modeling, and in vivo validation (CCCP depletion) match the journal's preference for multi-modal approaches. After addressing the required revisions (D158A protocol, ≥3 biological replicates with mixed-effects stats, model validation or reframing, cap-completeness controls), the manuscript would be a strong candidate.
  - Realism: Good — eLife has accepted similar MreB/mechanistic cytoskeleton papers (e.g., PMID 33322505, 36574321). The revisions demanded are substantial but achievable; if fully addressed, the paper meets eLife's standard for a 'new mechanism' Resource/Article.
- **Nature Communications**
  - Fit: Broad scope, high visibility, and explicit interest in cytoskeletal dynamics and bacterial cell biology. The paper's combination of high-resolution imaging, quantitative biophysics, and computational modeling fits the journal's interdisciplinary profile. The novelty (first report of nucleotide cycling within a biological polymer) is a strong hook. After major revisions resolving the evidentiary gaps, the manuscript would be competitive.
  - Realism: Moderate-good — Nature Communications receives many submissions; acceptance hinges on the revisions fully closing the three fatal gaps. The in vivo CCCP confound and novelty qualification (ParM/MamK discussion) must also be addressed.
- **Journal of Cell Biology**
  - Fit: JCB has a long-standing focus on cytoskeleton dynamics, polymerization mechanisms, and membrane-cytoskeleton interactions. The paper's detailed polymerization kinetics, symmetry analysis, and nucleotide-state-dependent stability assays are squarely in JCB's wheelhouse. The required revisions (especially biological replication and statistical rigor) align with JCB's high standards for quantitative cell biology.
  - Realism: Moderate — JCB is rigorous on quantification and replication. The pseudo-replication issue is a known pain point; if the authors deliver ≥3 independent SLB preps with mixed-effects models, the paper becomes viable.

## Alternative outlets
- **mBio**
  - Fit: ASM's broad microbiology journal; publishes mechanistic bacterial cell biology. The MreB morphogenesis angle, in vivo CCCP data, and lipid-specific polymerization (cardiolipin) fit mBio's audience. More tolerant of incremental mechanistic depth than top-tier journals, but still requires solid evidence.
  - Realism: Moderate — if revisions partially address gaps (e.g., D158A protocol added, stats improved but n=2 biological replicates), mBio may accept with minor revision.
- **Biophysical Journal**
  - Fit: Core biophysics audience; the HS-AFM, TIRF, QCM-D, fluorescence anisotropy, and Monte Carlo modeling are central to BJ's scope. The journal values quantitative rigor, so the pseudo-replication and model validation gaps must be addressed, but the topic (membrane-coupled filament dynamics) is a perfect fit.
  - Realism: Moderate — BJ demands strong biophysical quantification; the current statistical limitations are a significant hurdle even here.
- **Journal of Bacteriology**
  - Fit: Classic bacterial cell biology venue; the MreB function, lipid interactions, and in vivo morphology/CCCP data align well. Narrower audience than eLife/NatComms but appropriate for the microbiology-focused aspects. May accept with less biophysical depth if the bacterial physiology story is emphasized.
  - Realism: Moderate-good — if the authors scale claims to current evidence (e.g., present model as hypothesis-generating, qualify D158A data), JB is a realistic fallback.

## Notes
The manuscript's core discovery is significant and the experimental system (SLB + TIRF/HS-AFM + modeling) is powerful. However, the editor's 'major' verdict reflects three fatal evidentiary gaps that require new experiments/data, not just text changes. No top-tier venue will accept without these revisions. The 'after_revision' venues assume all required revisions are fully addressed (new D158A data, ≥3 biological replicates with mixed-effects stats, model validation/reframing, cap-completeness controls, citation/methods compliance). If revisions are partial, the 'alternative' venues become more realistic. The 'as_is' bucket is limited to preprint servers and lower-threshold journals because the current manuscript cannot support its quantitative and mutant-generalization claims.
