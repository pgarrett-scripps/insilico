# Decision Letter

**Decision:** major

## Summary of Evaluation
The manuscript reports a striking discovery — intrafilament nucleotide exchange in MreB filaments — that distinguishes bacterial MreB from eukaryotic actin. The specialist panel and this editor agree the core finding (WT intrafilament exchange, Fig 3H–I) is genuine and important. However, three load-bearing gaps identified in the meta-review survive the author rebuttal and prevent the full mechanistic claim from being supported by the current evidence:

1. **D158A evidence chain broken.** The pure-ATP* incorporation assay (Fig 3H–I) is the strongest direct evidence for hydrolysis-independent exchange, but the method for forming and visualizing the preformed D158A filaments used in that assay is absent from the manuscript. The authors concede this gap and propose to supply the complete protocol or redo the experiment with documented controls. This requires new data or a complete method description; it cannot be closed by text revision alone.

2. **Pervasive pseudo-replication.** All quantitative comparisons (elongation rates, ATP* incorporation, ADP depolymerization, symmetry ratios, Monte Carlo parameters) treat filaments as independent replicates despite originating from 1–2 SLB preparations. The authors concede this and will re-analyse with mixed-effects models using SLB preparation as a random effect. However, with n=1–2 true biological replicates, no reanalysis can supply statistical power for the claimed effect sizes (>5-fold ADP acceleration, 2-fold D158A vs WT ATP* incorporation). Additional independent SLB preparations are required for the key quantitative claims.

3. **Monte Carlo model lacks independent validation.** The model fits the same disassembly curves it predicts (Fig 4C, S10); Table 1 shows key rates derived from this study's fits or marked "ND." Fig 4I's ATP/ADP switching prediction is a model output, not experimental confirmation. The authors did not address this in their rebuttal. The model must either be validated against new independent data or presented explicitly as a hypothesis-generating framework with parameters clearly labeled as fitted vs. independently measured.

In addition, the editorial compliance audits reveal 15 HARD gaps (1 citation, 14 methods completeness) that block reproducibility. These include: an unresolved "in preparation" reference (De San Eustaquio-Campillo et al.) used for mutant generality; six future-dated or unverifiable citations (Adriaans 2026, Baileeves 2025) that support load-bearing claims; missing biological/technical replicate distinctions across all figures; absent statistical tests per comparison; missing software versions (ImageJ, MATLAB, PRISM, SOAX/TSOAX); undeployed custom Monte Carlo code and TSOAX scripts; incomplete strain genotypes and sources; missing chemical vendor/catalog numbers; unvalidated mutagenesis; and multiple delegated methods that cite incorrect or implausible references.

The draft recommendation of "major" is correct. The WT intrafilament exchange finding is solid and the experimental system is powerful, but the manuscript must either scale its claims to the evidence currently in hand or supply the missing evidence (new D158A data, additional biological replicates, cap-completeness controls, independent model validation). The compliance gaps are extensive but tractable and must be resolved before publication. A minor revision would paper over structural evidentiary gaps; a reject would discard a real discovery. Major revision with the explicit requirements below is the only calibration consistent with In Silico's mandate to judge whether conclusions survive the evidence given.

## Required Revisions
1. Provide the complete D158A filament formation and visualization protocol for the pure-ATP* incorporation assay (Fig 3H–I), including how preformed D158A filaments were generated, verified as intact, and imaged under ATP* labeling. If the original experiment lacked proper controls, repeat it with documented controls (e.g., polymerization nucleotide, wash steps, verification of filament integrity before ATP* addition). This is required to support the hydrolysis-independent exchange claim.
2. Repeat the key quantitative assays (elongation rates, ATP* incorporation kinetics, ADP depolymerization, symmetry ratios) with ≥3 independent SLB preparations (biological replicates). Re-analyse all multi-genotype and multi-condition comparisons using linear mixed-effects models with SLB preparation as a random effect. Report estimated marginal means with 95% confidence intervals and p-values. Filament-level n must be reported as technical replicates only. This is required for the quantitative claims in Figs 2, 3, 4, S8–S10 and for the Monte Carlo parameters in Table 1.
3. Either (a) validate the Monte Carlo model against new independent experimental data not used for fitting (e.g., the ATP/ADP switching prediction in Fig 4I, or new disassembly kinetics under different nucleotide conditions), or (b) restructure the modeling section to present the Monte Carlo framework as a hypothesis-generating model. In the latter case, Table 1 must clearly label each parameter as "fitted to this study's data," "independently measured in this study," or "taken from literature (with citation)." The current presentation conflates fitted and measured rates.
4. Add a control experiment to verify E136A cap completeness and symmetry in the capping assay (Fig 4G–H). Options: dual-color end-tracking of E136A cap addition, or a direct assay showing no free ends on capped filaments. If this cannot be done, explicitly acknowledge the limitation and present the capping experiment as supportive but not definitive evidence for intrafilament ADP access.
5. Resolve all citation integrity HARD gaps: (i) Replace the "in preparation" reference (De San Eustaquio-Campillo et al.) with a published, resolvable source or move the Geobacillus MreB mutant comparison to Supplementary Discussion with a clear caveat. (ii) For the future-dated references (Adriaans 2026, Baileeves 2025), either provide the final published citations with verified DOIs or replace them with the actual sources of the data/claims they support. (iii) Verify that ref 39 (Strahl & Hamoen 2010) supports the CCCP→MreB delocalization claim; if not, cite the correct source or modify the claim. (iv) Remove the circular self-citation of Adriaans 2026 for data generated in this study (Table 1 affinities).
6. Resolve all methods completeness HARD gaps: (i) Define biological vs. technical replicates for every figure panel; state the number of independent SLB preparations, cell cultures, or animals per condition. (ii) Name the statistical test used for each comparison in every figure legend; define error bars (SD, SEM, 95% CI). (iii) Report versions for all software: ImageJ/FIJI, MATLAB, PRISM, SOAX, TSOAX, and any custom scripts. (iv) Deposit the Monte Carlo simulation code and TSOAX analysis pipelines in a versioned public repository (GitHub + Zenodo DOI) with a documented computational environment (requirements.txt or Dockerfile). (v) Provide full B. subtilis strain genotypes, sources (e.g., BGSC #), and RRIDs; provide E. coli strain catalog number. (vi) Supply vendor/catalog numbers or CAS for all chemicals lacking them (ATP, ADP, AMPPNP, AMPPCP, ATP*, ADP*, AMPPNP*, DOPC, cardiolipin 18:1, phosphatidylglycerol, CCCP, malachite green components, etc.). (vii) Confirm Sanger sequencing validation for E136A and D158A mutagenesis and for all Gibson assemblies. (viii) Correct all delegated-method citations: SOAX/TSOAX must cite the actual software papers (e.g., Soubhaj et al. for SOAX); Omnipose must cite the Omnipose paper; SLB/QCM-D protocols must cite sources that contain the protocols; fluorescence anisotropy protocol must cite a published method; in vivo microscopy must cite the actual setup paper. (ix) Deposit all raw imaging data (TIRF/HS-AFM movies, FRAP stacks, in vivo time-lapses) and processed datasets to Zenodo with persistent DOIs prior to publication.
7. Implement all conceded clarity and reporting improvements from the rebuttal: add mobile fraction and half-time for ATP* FRAP (Fig 3F); restructure Results to present pure-ATP* incorporation before FRAP; define Fig 4F y-axis metric and add breakage vs. end-wise depolymerization quantification; standardize "doublet" vs. "filament" vs. "bundle" terminology; report Fig 2E plateau value with 95% CI from mixed-effects model; revise Abstract to replace "tunes" with explicit ATP-stabilizes/ADP-destabilizes language; qualify novelty statement and add ParM/MamK discussion; correct E136A morphology statement; add CCCP confound acknowledgment; report HS-AFM n and uncertainty; report Cc 95% CI.

## Minor Suggestions
- Consider adding a brief discussion of alternative explanations for the elongation plateau (e.g., end-capping, cooperativity) beyond 2D diffusion limitation, as noted in the rebuttal.
- In the in vivo section, consider adding a control for CCCP specificity (e.g., ATP depletion without membrane potential collapse, or vice versa) if feasible, or explicitly frame the in vivo data as correlative with the in vitro reconstitution establishing causality.
- For the critical concentration, note that the apparent Cc ≈ 0.003 µM is specific to the cardiolipin-containing SLB conditions and may differ in vivo or with other lipid compositions.
- Ensure the data availability statement includes the Zenodo DOIs for raw data and code repositories once deposited.
