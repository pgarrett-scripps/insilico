# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript makes a strong claim about discovering intrafilament nucleotide exchange in MreB as a new class of biopolymer dynamics. The citation record largely supports the mechanistic claims, but two load-bearing citations need verification: (1) the assertion that eukaryotic actin shows 'no evidence of rapid nucleotide exchange with the solution' (citing refs 8, 36) should be checked against more recent single-filament studies that may have detected exchange; (2) the claim that 'to our knowledge, this is the first report of nucleotide cycling within a biological polymer' requires confirmation that no prior work on ParM, MamK, or other bacterial actins has shown similar behavior. The related-work coverage is otherwise thorough for the MreB field.

## Strengths
- The introduction and discussion situate the work clearly against the canonical actin/tubulin paradigm and the existing MreB literature, with appropriate citations to key structural and cellular studies (refs 1-7, 9-12, 14-19).
- The biochemical parameter table (Table 1) provides a valuable side-by-side comparison with actin, citing primary sources for both systems.
- The authors acknowledge prior observations of MreB filament stability in vivo (refs 5, 12) and the proposal that Pi release promotes depolymerization (ref 10), integrating rather than ignoring them.

## Weaknesses
- The claim that 'eukaryotic actin filaments show no evidence of rapid nucleotide exchange with the solution' (main text, citing refs 8 and 36) may be outdated: single-molecule studies (e.g., Jégou et al. 2011, ref 35 in this paper; or more recent work by the Pollard/Blanchoin labs) have measured nucleotide exchange on filament sides under certain conditions. The authors should verify whether their statement holds for the specific conditions they compare against, or qualify it as 'under physiological conditions' or 'detectable by bulk methods.'
- The novelty statement 'To our knowledge, this is the first report of nucleotide cycling within a biological polymer' (main text) is strong and load-bearing. A targeted search for 'nucleotide exchange within filaments' or 'intrafilament nucleotide exchange' in ParM, MamK, Crenactin, or other archaeal/eukaryotic actins is needed to ensure no prior report exists. If such work exists, the claim must be qualified or the prior work cited.
- Reference 17 (Adriaans et al., Biophys. J. 2026, doi:10.1016/j.bpj.2026.06.003) appears to have a future publication date (2026) while the manuscript seems to be from 2024-2025. This should be corrected to the actual publication year or noted as 'in press' with a verifiable DOI.
- The citation of 'De San Eustaquio-Campillo et al., in preparation' for G. stearothermophilus MreB mutant data (main text) supports a load-bearing claim about mutant phenotypes. Unpublished work should not be the sole support for a key comparison; the authors should either cite the published version or move the claim to supplementary discussion with a caveat.

## Questions
- Can the authors confirm that refs 8 and 36 (Colombo et al. 2021 and Pollard et al. 1992) are the most current evidence for 'no rapid nucleotide exchange' in actin filaments, or have subsequent single-filament studies (e.g., using fluorescent nucleotide analogs) detected side-exchange under any conditions?
- Has a literature search for 'intrafilament nucleotide exchange' or 'nucleotide cycling within filaments' in ParM, MamK, Crenactin, or other actin-like proteins been performed? If so, what was found?
- Is reference 17 (Adriaans et al. 2026) correctly dated? The DOI suggests a 2026 publication but the manuscript appears earlier.
- Is the 'De San Eustaquio-Campillo et al., in preparation' reference now published? If so, please provide the citation; if not, the claim about G. stearothermophilus mutants should be qualified.
