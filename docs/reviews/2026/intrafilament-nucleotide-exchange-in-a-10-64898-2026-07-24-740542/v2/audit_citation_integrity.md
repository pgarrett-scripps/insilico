# Citation Integrity Auditor

## Summary
The manuscript cites 47 references across multiple categories including reference resolvability, claim-citation support, and quotation fidelity. Two critical issues were identified: (1) an in-preparation citation by De San Eustaquio-Campillo et al. that cannot serve as evidence in a peer-reviewed manuscript, and (2) a future-dated reference (Adriaans et al., 2026) with an unusual DOI format requiring clarification of publication status. Additional soft-severity issues include multiple future-dated references (Baileeves et al., 2025), several specific claims attributed to cited works that cannot be verified from the manuscript alone, and quantitative values in Table 1 sourced from multiple papers that would benefit from spot-checking. The majority of references are to published, peer-reviewed works with complete citations, and the manuscript does not show widespread citation inflation.

## Unverifiable (raise as questions)
- **[Reference Resolvability] Adriaans et al. (2026) – future date and unusual DOI** — Reference 17, cited throughout (Line 10, Table 1): 'Adriaans, I. E. et al. ATP-driven membrane binding and polymerization of bacterial actin MreB promotes local membrane fluidization. Biophys. J. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bpj.2026.06.003 (2026).' Year is 2026 (future) and DOI format appears non-standard (2026.06.003).
- **[Claim-Citation Support] Mao et al. (2023) – specific observations about Geobacillus MreB behavior** — Line 10: 'Under limiting ATP concentrations, Geobacillus MreB filaments progressively disappeared from TEM fields over time, whereas in the presence of excess ATP, they displayed similar density and lengths for incubation times ranging from minutes to several hours, while continuously releasing Pi at a constant rate.' Attributed to Ref. 10 (Mao et al., 2023), but cannot verify whether Mao et al. explicitly reports these specific observations.
- **[Reference Resolvability] Baileeves et al. (2025) – future date** — Reference 18, Line 18: 'This is consistent with recent molecular dynamics simulations showing that Escherichia coli MreB filaments recruit CL.' Cited as 'Baileeves, B. W. A., Hoang, A. D. Q., Bugg, T. D. H. & Stansfeld, P. J. MreB filaments in the elongasome modulate E. coli membrane curvature. Biophys. J. 124, 3583–3595 (2025).' Year is 2025 (future date).
- **[Claim-Citation Support] Colavin et al. (2014) – conflation of crystal structures and MD simulations** — Line 19: 'Crystal structures of MreB protofilaments in solution and molecular dynamic simulations suggest that polymerization closes the nucleotide-binding pocket of MreB to promote nucleotide hydrolysis, causing subunit flattening, protofilament bending and a twist angle.' Attributed to Ref. 19 (Colavin et al., 2014), which is a computational study, not a crystallography paper. Crystal structures appear to be from a different reference.
- **[Claim-Citation Support] van den Ent et al. (2014) – membrane vs. solution context** — Line 9: 'In contrast to actin, MreB proteins assemble into straight pairs of antiparallel protofilaments on a lipid membrane surface in the presence of ATP.' Attributed to Ref. 9 (van den Ent et al., 2014). The reference studied MreB in solution (crystal structures and cryo-EM), not on lipid membrane surface. Unclear whether authors are extending van den Ent's findings to membrane context.
- **[Quotation/Number Fidelity] Table 1 – quantitative biochemical parameters** — Table 1 compares actin (ACTA2) vs. MreB parameters with specific numerical values cited to multiple references (refs. 8, 25, 35, 36, 40, 41, 42, 43, 44, 45, 46, 47). Example: 'ATP binding rate to monomers: Mg-Actin ACTA2 3.2 µM⁻¹·s⁻¹ [ref. 40].' Cannot verify from manuscript alone whether numerical values accurately match cited sources.
- **[Claim-Citation Support] Garner et al. (2004) – E136A mutation in ParM** — Line 29: 'In the prokaryotic actin-like ParM protein, the equivalent E136A mutation abolishes detectable ATPase activity.' Attributed to Ref. 29 (Garner et al., 2004). Cannot verify whether Garner et al. explicitly reports this specific mutation and its effect, or whether authors are inferring from structural homology.
- **[Claim-Citation Support] Colombo et al. (2021) – nucleotide trapping in actin filaments** — Line 8: 'Within assembled filaments, nucleotides remain effectively trapped in each subunit until depolymerization, preventing any nucleotide exchange.' Attributed to Ref. 8 (Colombo et al., 2021), which is about fluorescent nucleotide analogues. The general principle of nucleotide trapping may be better cited to foundational actin dynamics references.
- **[Claim-Citation Support] Pollard et al. (1992) – no rapid nucleotide exchange in actin filaments** — Line 36, Table 1: 'Eukaryotic actin filaments show no evidence of rapid nucleotide exchange with the solution, consistent with their atomic model, where the helical twist and constrained interdomain motions within the filament irreversibly block the release of the nucleotide.' Attributed to Ref. 36 (Pollard et al., 1992). Title mentions nucleotide exchange, but specific claim cannot be verified from manuscript alone.
