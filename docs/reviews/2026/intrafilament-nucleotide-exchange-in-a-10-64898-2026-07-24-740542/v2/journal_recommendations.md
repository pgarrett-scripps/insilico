# Journal Recommendations

## As-is (current quality)
- **eLife**
  - Fit: eLife publishes high-quality biophysical and cell biology research with rigorous peer review and strong emphasis on reproducibility and transparency. This paper's multi-method approach (HS-AFM, TIRF, biochemistry, modeling) and mechanistic depth on a fundamental cytoskeletal protein align well with eLife's scope. The editor's 'minor' verdict and convergent panel agreement on technical soundness (3.91/5 confidence-weighted, 1 accept + 5 minor) indicate the work is publication-ready with revisions.
  - Realism: Realistic but not assured. eLife's acceptance rate is ~7–10% and the paper's unresolved mechanistic ambiguity (exchange vs. diffusion-rebinding) and prose-only model would likely trigger major revision requests there as well, though the core finding is solid enough to survive.
- **PLOS Biology**
  - Fit: PLOS Biology emphasizes biological significance, methodological rigor, and public accessibility. This work on MreB—a fundamental bacterial morphogenesis protein—with comprehensive biophysical characterization and in vivo validation (CCCP experiments, cell morphology) fits the journal's scope well. The editor's minor verdict and panel consensus on methodology (4/5) and data analysis (4/5) support submission.
  - Realism: Realistic. PLOS Biology has higher acceptance rates (~15–20%) than eLife and explicitly welcomes papers with acknowledged limitations if the core claims are well-supported. The required revisions (model code, data deposition, hedged novelty framing) are standard for this venue.
- **Molecular Microbiology**
  - Fit: This journal specializes in bacterial cell biology and cytoskeletal dynamics, with strong readership in MreB research. The paper's focus on MreB assembly mechanics, nucleotide regulation, and in vivo localization directly matches the journal's scope. The panel's 4/5 marks for methodology and novelty indicate the work is competitive for a specialist audience.
  - Realism: Realistic. Molecular Microbiology typically accepts well-executed mechanistic studies with minor revisions. The unresolved exchange-vs-diffusion question is less of a barrier here than at top-tier venues, provided the authors hedge the novelty claim and deposit code/data as required.

## After required revisions
- **Nature Communications**
  - Fit: Nature Communications publishes high-impact biophysical and cell biology research with emphasis on novel mechanisms and reproducibility. Once the required revisions are complete—particularly depositing the Monte Carlo model code with equations, sensitivity analysis, and goodness-of-fit metrics; clarifying the exchange mechanism with hedged language; and releasing raw data with DOIs—this paper's multi-method characterization of nucleotide-dependent MreB dynamics becomes a strong fit for the journal's scope.
  - Realism: Possible but uncertain. Nature Communications has ~25–30% acceptance rates but is highly competitive. The paper's core finding (hydrolysis-independent polymerization) is solid, but the unresolved mechanistic ambiguity around intrafilament exchange (even after hedging) may still draw major revision or rejection. Success depends on how convincingly the authors frame the evidence and how thoroughly the model disclosure is completed.
- **Biophysical Journal**
  - Fit: Biophysical Journal is the primary venue for quantitative biophysical studies of protein dynamics and assembly. The paper's HS-AFM, TIRF, fluorescence anisotropy, and Monte Carlo modeling are all core to the journal's scope. After addressing the model transparency issues (code, equations, sensitivity analysis, goodness-of-fit) and depositing raw data, the work becomes a natural fit for this audience.
  - Realism: Good. Biophysical Journal has ~40–50% acceptance rates and explicitly values methodological rigor and quantitative detail. The required revisions directly address the journal's standards for computational modeling and data availability. The unresolved exchange-vs-diffusion ambiguity is less of a barrier here if properly hedged, since the journal values mechanistic hypotheses supported by converging evidence even when not fully proven.
- **mBio**
  - Fit: mBio (ASM's open-access journal) publishes microbiology research with emphasis on cellular and molecular mechanisms. The paper's in vivo validation (CCCP delocalization, cell morphology) and bacterial focus make it a good fit after revisions. The journal has strong standards for reproducibility and data sharing, aligning with the required fixes.
  - Realism: Good. mBio has higher acceptance rates (~35–45%) than Nature-family journals and explicitly encourages open data deposition. The required revisions are well-aligned with the journal's expectations. The mechanistic ambiguity is less penalizing here if the authors clearly state the evidence and name the alternative hypothesis.

## Alternative outlets
- **bioRxiv**
  - Fit: bioRxiv is the appropriate preprint server for this work if submission to peer-reviewed journals is delayed or rejected. The paper is complete, data-rich, and makes a clear mechanistic claim suitable for preprint circulation. Posting on bioRxiv with the required supplementary materials (code, data, full methods) would establish priority and allow community feedback before formal submission.
  - Realism: Immediate. bioRxiv accepts all preprints meeting basic scope criteria. This paper would benefit from preprint visibility given the novelty claim and the ongoing debate about the mechanism; posting would also allow the authors to gather feedback on the exchange-vs-diffusion ambiguity before final peer review.
- **Frontiers in Microbiology**
  - Fit: Frontiers in Microbiology is a peer-reviewed open-access journal with lower rejection rates and explicit emphasis on reproducibility and transparency. The paper's scope (bacterial cytoskeleton, MreB dynamics) and methodological rigor fit well. The journal's review model and data-sharing requirements align closely with the required revisions.
  - Realism: Realistic. Frontiers journals have ~50–60% acceptance rates and are designed to accommodate solid, well-documented work that may not meet the bar for top-tier venues. The required revisions (code, data, hedged claims) are standard here. The unresolved mechanistic question would not be a barrier if properly framed as a hypothesis supported by converging evidence.
- **Protein Science**
  - Fit: Protein Science focuses on protein structure, dynamics, and assembly—all central to this work. The HS-AFM, TIRF, and biochemical characterization of MreB polymerization mechanics fit the journal's scope well. The paper's emphasis on nucleotide-dependent conformational changes and filament stability aligns with the journal's audience.
  - Realism: Realistic. Protein Science has moderate acceptance rates (~40–50%) and values quantitative biophysical studies. The required revisions are well-aligned with the journal's standards. The mechanistic ambiguity is less of a barrier if the authors clearly distinguish between the favored interpretation and alternatives.
