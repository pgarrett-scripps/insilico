## Concessions
- **reproducibility** — Raw data (HS-AFM, TIRF, anisotropy, ATPase replicates) are only 'available upon request' and the Monte Carlo model is described in prose with no equations, code, fitting procedure, or parameter uncertainty.
  - Proposed change: We will deposit all raw imaging and biochemical datasets on Zenodo with a resolvable DOI, and release the complete Monte Carlo model (reaction scheme, rate laws, initial/boundary conditions, stochastic algorithm and parameter-fitting procedure) as a code repository (DOI-linked), together with a one-way sensitivity analysis varying k_hyd, k-,d, k+,n and k-,n by 2-10 fold and a quantitative goodness-of-fit metric comparing simulated vs. observed disassembly curves (Fig. 4C, S10).
- **reproducibility** — TSOAX tracking parameters (thresholds, window size, sensitivity) and the statistical tests used for group comparisons (Fig. 3D, 4B) are not specified.
  - Proposed change: We will add a dedicated Methods subsection listing all TSOAX parameter values used for each dataset, and will explicitly name the statistical test (t-test, Mann-Whitney, etc.), its assumptions-check outcome, and exact p-values for every comparison currently reported only as 'n=...' in figure legends.
- **reproducibility** — The claim that E136A/D158A ATPase activity is 'markedly reduced' does not report absolute Pi release rates, leaving open whether residual hydrolysis (e.g. 5-10% of WT) could still be mechanistically required for polymerization.
  - Proposed change: We will report the absolute Pi/min/MreB values for E136A and D158A alongside WT in Fig. 3A and in the text, and explicitly compare these rates to the polymerization rate (subunits/min) to quantify how far hydrolysis lags behind assembly.
- **literature** — The claim of being 'the first report of nucleotide cycling within a biological polymer' understates the ParM precedent (Garner et al. 2004) of nucleotide exchange at filament ends promoting disassembly.
  - Proposed change: We will revise the sentence at line 268 to read 'the first report of nucleotide exchange occurring along the length of an intact filament lattice, as opposed to end-localized exchange reported for ParM (ref. 29)', and add a paragraph in the Discussion explicitly contrasting the ParM end-exchange mechanism with the intrafilament exchange we describe for MreB.
- **rigor** — The claim that nucleotide-free MreB is 'partially unfolded' to explain anomalously slow ATP/ADP binding kinetics is asserted without structural evidence.
  - Proposed change: We will soften this sentence to 'consistent with, but not proven by, a partially unfolded or binding-incompetent conformation in the absence of nucleotide' and add a limitations note in the Discussion; we will also perform and report circular dichroism and size-exclusion chromatography comparing nucleotide-free and ATP-loaded MreB in the revised manuscript to directly test this hypothesis.
- **clarity/rigor** — FRAP experiments (Fig. 3E, 3F) lack reported bleach radius, recovery half-time and mobile fraction, preventing quantitative comparison to the model's exchange rate constant (k-,n = 0.0141 s⁻¹).
  - Proposed change: We will report bleach radius, t1/2 and mobile fraction for both the TFLime (Fig. 3E) and ATP* (Fig. 3F) FRAP experiments, and explicitly compute the corresponding apparent exchange rate for comparison with the fitted model parameter k-,n in the main text.
- **literature** — Citation attributing cardiolipin recruitment to 'Escherichia coli MreB filaments' (line 118, ref. 18) may misattribute species; ref. 18 is a molecular dynamics study, and species specificity should be verified.
  - Proposed change: We will verify the species used in ref. 18's simulations and correct the sentence to accurately name the organism studied, or generalize the statement if the finding applies across species.

## Disagreements
- **methodology/rigor/reproducibility** — ADP-induced destabilization and ATP* FRAP recovery could reflect simple end-mediated subunit dissociation/rebinding via 2D membrane diffusion rather than true intrafilament (lattice-internal) nucleotide exchange.
  - Response: Our design already provides a discriminating comparison that argues against pure subunit turnover: the same filaments show no protein backbone recovery when labelled via the pFAST tag (TFLime, Fig. 3E) but rapid nucleotide-channel recovery when labelled via the bound nucleotide (ATP*, Fig. 3F). If recovery were driven by whole-subunit dissociation and lateral-diffusion-mediated rebinding, we would expect comparable recovery kinetics for the protein tag and the nucleotide reporter, since both are covalently or non-covalently associated with the same physical subunit. The dissociation between these two signals is more parsimoniously explained by nucleotide-specific exchange occurring while the subunit backbone remains incorporated in the lattice. This is reinforced by the capping experiment (Fig. 4G-H), where fragmentation upon ADP addition occurs specifically within the WT (uncapped) filament segment rather than uniformly at filament ends, which would be the expected signature of pure end-mediated turnover. We agree this evidence is not yet dispositive - as we note in the revision plan, we will pursue position-resolved FRAP (bleaching an internal filament segment far from any end) to directly test whether recovery kinetics are independent of distance from filament ends, which would be the most direct test distinguishing the two mechanisms.
  - Manuscript reference: 'FRAP experiments of TFLime-labeled filaments showed that monomers do not turnover within filaments (Fig. 3E)... However, ATP*-labelled filaments showed rapid signal recovery, indicating nucleotide exchange between the filaments and the solution (Fig. 3F).'
- **literature/rigor** — The critical concentration (Cc ≈ 0.003 µM) derived from TIRF elongation-rate fits is ~1000-fold lower than prior solution-based estimates (1-2 µM), raising doubt about whether the plateau reflects genuine saturation or an assay artifact.
  - Response: This discrepancy is expected and mechanistically informative rather than an inconsistency to be explained away: the 1-2 µM value from prior work corresponds to the concentration required for spontaneous nucleation/polymerization in solution, whereas our Cc is derived from the elongation-rate vs. concentration relationship of pre-nucleated, membrane-templated filaments, where the rate-limiting step is subunit association at an existing filament end rather than 3D nucleation. As we discuss in the text, membrane recruitment is proposed to concentrate and/or conformationally activate ATP-MreB, which would be expected to dramatically lower the apparent Cc for elongation relative to a solution-nucleation Cc measured by bulk assays. We will add an explicit sentence clarifying that these two Cc values describe different rate-limiting steps (nucleation vs. elongation) and are therefore not directly comparable, rather than being redundant or conflicting measurements of the same parameter.
  - Manuscript reference: 'Membrane recruitment may increase the local concentration of ATP-MreB and/or induce a conformational change that renders it polymerization-competent, enabling efficient incorporation at filaments ends, with subunit association greatly exceeding dissociation.'
- **methodology** — The in vivo CCCP experiment uses E136A as a proxy for the ATP-locked state, but E136A's altered nucleotide affinity (higher for both ATP and ADP) could independently stabilize filaments on the membrane, confounding interpretation of ATP-dependence per se.
  - Response: We agree the confound cannot be fully excluded with CCCP alone, and will add D158A imaging under CCCP as a complementary control in revision. However, the current data already partially separate the two explanations: D158A has reduced (not increased) nucleotide affinity yet forms filaments indistinguishable from WT in vitro (Fig. 3C-D), and E136A's higher affinity for both ATP and ADP (not selectively for ATP) means that if affinity alone explained the in vivo stability, E136A filaments should also load ADP efficiently and destabilize similarly to WT under ADP in vitro - which we explicitly tested and found they do not (Fig. 4A, B, F: E136A remains stable, even slightly elongating, under ADP flush). This argues that it is the inability to hydrolyze/exchange away from ATP, not affinity per se, that confers stability, though we agree a D158A CCCP experiment would further strengthen this argument.
  - Manuscript reference: 'E136A filaments remained stable and even exhibited slight positive elongation (1.80 ± 2.27 nm.s-1) (Fig. 4A,B,F and Movie 8)'
