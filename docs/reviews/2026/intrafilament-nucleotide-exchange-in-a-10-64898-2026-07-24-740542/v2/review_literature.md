# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript makes three load-bearing claims about MreB filament dynamics: (1) nucleotide exchange occurs within intact MreB filaments, contrasting with actin where nucleotides remain trapped; (2) ATP hydrolysis is not required for polymerization but occurs post-assembly; and (3) intrafilament nucleotide exchange tunes filament stability via the free nucleotide pool. The citation record is substantially accurate on foundational claims, and key prior work is cited. However, one critical attribution requires verification, and a directly relevant recent study may be underweighted in the narrative framing of novelty. The core citations are verifiable and accurate; the load-bearing claims rest on sound experimental evidence, but the novelty scope relative to ParM requires clarification to fully support the "new class" framing.

## Strengths
- The manuscript cites the foundational van den Ent et al. (2014) work establishing MreB's antiparallel structure, and correctly attributes the prior proposal that this implies non-polar growth (ref. 9).
- The claim that nucleotides are "effectively trapped" in actin filaments (line 8, attributed to ref. 8) is properly sourced to Colombo et al. (2021), a primary experimental study on actin nucleotide dynamics.
- The characterization of Geobacillus MreB filament behavior (lines 225–227, ref. 10) is cited to Mao et al. (2023), a recent eLife paper directly addressing MreB nucleotide and lipid dependence, and the authors appropriately note their own findings differ from that prior work.

## Weaknesses
- The manuscript claims nucleotide exchange within filaments is novel to MreB, yet ParM (ref. 29, Garner et al. 2004)—another prokaryotic actin homolog—appears to exhibit nucleotide exchange at filament ends (line 283); the manuscript does not clearly establish whether ParM's end-exchange mechanism is fundamentally different from MreB's intrafilament exchange, or whether the novelty claim should be narrowed to "intrafilament (not just end) exchange."
- The authors argue that ATP hydrolysis is not required for polymerization, supported by E136A and D158A mutants, and cite ref. 29 (Garner et al. 2004) for the equivalent E136A mutation in ParM abolishing ATPase activity; however, the manuscript does not cite or discuss whether Garner et al. tested whether ParM E136A polymerizes, which would establish a precedent for uncoupling hydrolysis from polymerization in prokaryotic actins.
- The manuscript proposes that intrafilament nucleotide exchange defines a new class of self-organizing filament dynamics but does not discuss whether the membrane context is essential to this mechanism or merely facilitates it; it is unclear whether the exchange mechanism would occur in solution-phase MreB filaments or is strictly a membrane-templated phenomenon.
- The claim that MreB nucleotide binding rates are "~300-fold lower than those reported for actin" (line 96) cites Table 1, which compares to actin values from refs. 40–41; these actin values should be verified to be from the same experimental conditions (Mg²⁺ concentration, temperature, pH) to ensure the comparison is fair.
- The manuscript attributes the proposal that "Pi release promotes depolymerization" in Geobacillus MreB to ref. 10 (Mao et al. 2023), but does not cite whether Mao et al. themselves proposed this or were reporting an earlier hypothesis; the attribution chain should be verified as primary.
- Line 268 states "nucleotide exchange within MreB filaments" is "to our knowledge, this is the first report"—the ParM literature (ref. 29) should be explicitly checked for prior reports of any nucleotide exchange in prokaryotic actin filaments to confirm the claim's accuracy.
- The in vivo CCCP experiment (Fig. 4J, lines 338–365) uses E136A as a proxy for ATP-bound filaments, but the manuscript does not discuss whether E136A's altered nucleotide binding affinity (Fig. 3B) might confound the interpretation; the experiment would be stronger if it also tested D158A or a true ATP-locked mutant.
- Reference 17 (Adriaans et al., in press, 2026) is cited for nucleotide binding kinetics of Geobacillus MreB; this is a future publication and should be flagged as "in preparation" or "preprint" to signal its status to readers.
- The manuscript cites Mao et al. (2023, ref. 10) as showing that "Escherichia coli MreB filaments recruit CL" (line 118), but Mao et al. studied Geobacillus MreB, not E. coli MreB; the attribution should be verified or clarified for species.
- The critical concentration estimate (Cc ≈ 0.003 µM, line 179) is ~1000-fold lower than prior estimates of 1–2 µM (refs. 14–17), but the manuscript does not discuss why; a brief explanation of whether this reflects differences in assay conditions (membrane vs. solution) would strengthen the claim.
- The model (Fig. 4D, Supplementary) includes a parameter for intrafilament nucleotide exchange rate (k₊,ₙ = 0.28 µM⁻¹·s⁻¹), but the manuscript does not cite or justify this value; it should state whether it is fitted to the FRAP data or derived from literature.

## Questions
- Does the ParM literature (Garner et al. 2004, ref. 29) report whether ParM E136A polymerizes, and if so, does it establish that ATP hydrolysis is uncoupled from polymerization in prokaryotic actins prior to this work?
- Is the nucleotide exchange mechanism in MreB strictly membrane-dependent, or would it occur in solution-phase filaments if they could form?
- Reference 17 (Adriaans et al. 2026): is this a preprint, in-press, or in-preparation manuscript, and should its status be clarified in the reference list?
