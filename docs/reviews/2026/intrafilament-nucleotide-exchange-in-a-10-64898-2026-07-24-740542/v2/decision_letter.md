# Decision Letter

**Decision:** minor

## Summary of Evaluation
The panel (confidence-weighted 3.91/5; 1 accept, 5 minor, 2 major, 0 reject) converges that this is a technically strong, multi-method study whose load-bearing finding — that MreB polymerization proceeds without ATP hydrolysis, with E136A/D158A assembling like WT — is well supported. The two surviving substantive criticisms are (i) that intrafilament nucleotide exchange is not yet fully discriminated from transient subunit dissociation and lateral (2D membrane) rebinding, and (ii) that the Monte Carlo model is presented in prose only, with ~11 fitted parameters, no equations, code, sensitivity analysis or goodness-of-fit metric.

I weighed the rebuttal carefully. On (i), the authors' response is credible and partially decisive: the dissociation between TFLime (no backbone recovery, Fig. 3E) and ATP* (rapid recovery, Fig. 3F) on the same filaments is a genuinely discriminating design, and the capping result (Fig. 4G–H) showing ADP-induced fragmentation within the uncapped WT segment rather than at ends is hard to reconcile with pure end-mediated turnover. The authors nonetheless concede the evidence is not dispositive and commit to position-resolved FRAP. I therefore do not treat this as a fundamental flaw, but I do require the interpretive claim to be scaled to what is shown and the diffusion-rebinding alternative to be discussed explicitly in the text rather than only in the rebuttal. On the Cc discrepancy, the authors' distinction between nucleation-limited (solution, 1–2 µM) and elongation-limited (membrane-templated) regimes is a reasonable and testable explanation; adding it to the text resolves the point. On the E136A/CCCP confound, the rebuttal's argument from E136A's stability under ADP flush despite increased ADP affinity is logically sound and partly separates affinity from hydrolysis; the promised D158A CCCP control strengthens it further but is not a precondition for publication if the claim is appropriately hedged.

On (ii) and the compliance audits, the authors conceded essentially every disclosure item and proposed specific, checkable fixes (Zenodo deposition with DOI, released model code with equations and fitting procedure, one-way sensitivity analysis, goodness-of-fit metric, TSOAX parameters, named statistical tests and exact p-values, absolute Pi release rates, FRAP t1/2 and mobile fraction, narrowed novelty framing relative to ParM). These are verifiability gaps, not evidence the analysis is wrong, and concessions of this specificity are good evidence the manuscript can be brought to standard in one revision round. Two audit items require attention beyond what the rebuttal covered: the load-bearing in-preparation citation at line 282 (a claim about G. stearothermophilus E136A/D158A that a reader cannot check), and the citation-attribution issues flagged for refs. 8, 9, 18, 19 and 29. Neither undermines the central claim, but the in-preparation citation must be removed or converted into data presented in this manuscript.

I adopt the panel's minor-revision recommendation. No reviewer recommended rejection, the conceded finding stands on the evidence presented, and every remaining item is a specific, bounded fix rather than new conceptual work. In Silico's standard is that candidly admitted, fixable limitations should not be penalised as fresh flaws — but the corresponding claims must be worded to match. Note that I am not requiring the promised new experiments (position-resolved FRAP, CD/SEC, D158A CCCP) as conditions of acceptance; if they are not included, the associated claims must be hedged accordingly.

## Required Revisions
1. Remove or replace the in-preparation citation at line 282 ('We observed similar effects for E136A and D158A of G. stearothermophilus MreB (De San Eustaquio-Campillo et al., in preparation)'). Either present the underlying data in this manuscript (main or supplementary figure with methods and n), or delete the claim. An unpublished, unresolvable source cannot carry a mechanistic claim in a published record.
2. Release the Monte Carlo model in full: deposit code with a resolvable DOI and state in the Methods the reaction scheme, rate laws, initial/boundary conditions, stochastic algorithm and time step, number of simulation runs and whether plotted traces are single runs or averages, random seed(s) or a seed-averaging statement, and the parameter-fitting procedure. Add the promised one-way sensitivity analysis (k_hyd, k-,d, k+,n, k-,n varied 2–10 fold) and a quantitative goodness-of-fit metric for simulated vs. observed disassembly (Fig. 4C, S10). State explicitly how many of the ~11 parameters were fitted versus constrained by independent measurement.
3. Deposit all raw data (HS-AFM, TIRF, anisotropy, ATPase replicates, in vivo imaging) on Zenodo with a resolvable DOI and cite the accession in the Data Availability statement. Replace 'available from the corresponding authors upon request' and 'will be deposited after publication' with a live identifier. Also deposit the custom MATLAB extraction/rate-fitting script.
4. Revise the novelty framing as the authors propose: change the line-268 'first report of nucleotide cycling within a biological polymer' to a claim restricted to exchange along an intact filament lattice, explicitly contrasted with end-localized exchange in ParM (ref. 29), and add the promised Discussion paragraph making that contrast.
5. In the Results and Discussion, state explicitly that transient subunit dissociation with lateral membrane diffusion and rebinding is an alternative account of the ATP* FRAP recovery, and lay out the argument given in the rebuttal (TFLime vs. ATP* recovery dissociation; segment-localized fragmentation in the capping experiment) as evidence favouring intrafilament exchange without claiming it is dispositive. Name position-resolved FRAP as the decisive future test. If position-resolved FRAP is not added, the mechanism must be presented as the favoured interpretation rather than an established result, including in the abstract.
6. Report absolute Pi release rates (Pi/min/MreB) for WT, E136A and D158A in Fig. 3A and the text, replacing the qualitative 'markedly reduced', and compare the residual rate to the measured polymerization rate (subunits/min) so readers can judge whether residual hydrolysis could be mechanistically sufficient.
7. Add complete FRAP quantification for Fig. 3E and 3F: bleach radius, recovery half-time, mobile fraction, and n filaments per condition, plus the apparent exchange rate computed for comparison with the fitted k-,n = 0.0141 s-1.
8. Add a Methods subsection specifying all TSOAX/SOAX parameter values (thresholds, window size, sensitivity, ridge/stretch settings) per dataset, and the criteria used for the 'visual inspection' selection of the linear fitting window for elongation rates.
9. For every group comparison, name the specific test used, report the normality/assumptions check outcome, and give exact p-values — in particular for Fig. 3D and Fig. 4B, where only n is currently given.
10. State sample sizes and number of independent biological replicates for all experiments currently shown as representative only: the CCCP delocalization experiment (Fig. 4J, number of cells and replicates), the FRAP experiments (Fig. 3E, 3F) and the capping experiment (Fig. 4G–H).
11. Correct or clarify the flagged citation attributions: (a) verify the species simulated in ref. 18 and correct the 'Escherichia coli MreB filaments recruit CL' statement at line 118; (b) at line 19, separate the crystallographic source from the MD source rather than attributing both to Colavin et al. 2014; (c) at line 9, clarify whether van den Ent et al. 2014 studied MreB in solution and whether the membrane-surface statement is an extension; (d) at line 29, confirm that Garner et al. 2004 reports the equivalent ParM E136A ATPase phenotype directly, or mark it as inference from structural homology; (e) cite a foundational actin reference for nucleotide trapping at line 8 alongside ref. 8.
12. Clarify the publication status and dates of refs. 17 (Adriaans et al., Biophys. J., dated 2026, DOI 10.1016/j.bpj.2026.06.003) and 18 (Baileeves et al., 2025). If ref. 17 is in press by the same first author, label it as such and flag the self-citation; because the protein purification and ATPase protocols cite it as provenance, confirm in the Methods that the versions given here are self-contained (the audit indicates they largely are).
13. Add the clarification the authors propose regarding the ~1000-fold discrepancy between the elongation-derived Cc (~0.003 uM) and prior solution estimates (1–2 uM), stating explicitly that the two values report different rate-limiting steps (membrane-templated elongation vs. solution nucleation) and are not directly comparable.
14. Soften the 'partially unfolded' interpretation of nucleotide-free MreB to a hypothesis consistent with, but not demonstrated by, the kinetics, and add the corresponding limitation to the Discussion. If CD/SEC data are added, report them with full methods; if not, the hedged wording stands.
15. Hedge the in vivo ATP-locked interpretation of the E136A CCCP experiment to acknowledge that altered nucleotide affinity is not fully excluded as a contributor, incorporating the rebuttal's D158A/ADP-flush argument into the text. If the D158A CCCP control is performed, report it with n and replicates.
16. Ensure all supplementary materials referenced (Supplementary Text on modeling, Figs. S1–S11, Tables S1–S4 including primer and plasmid sequences and lipid compositions, Movies 1–9) are posted with the preprint and reachable, since primer sequences, plasmid sequences and the model description are currently only promised there.

## Minor Suggestions
- Add software version numbers for ImageJ/FIJI, SOAX, TSOAX, MATLAB and PRISM (Omnipose, Igor Pro, Kintek Explorer and SP2000 versions are already given).
- Provide catalogue numbers for the nucleotides and the ATTO-488 nucleotide analogues (Jena Biosciences), and the vendor for phosphatidylglycerol used in Fig. 1E–F.
- State whether E136A, D158A and the pFAST fusion constructs and B. subtilis strains were sequence-verified, and give the parental genetic background of the RCL strains.
- Spot-check the numerical entries in Table 1 against their primary sources (refs. 8, 25, 35, 36, 40–47) and indicate in the legend which values are direct quotations, which are recalculated, and the error convention for entries currently lacking one.
- Standardise error-bar definitions across all figure legends (SD, SEM or CI), including Fig. 4E–F where bars are shown on one side only.
- Specify the CCCP vehicle/solvent and its final concentration, and whether a vehicle-only control was imaged.
- Consider a brief statement of how many independent HS-AFM sessions and cantilevers contributed to the n=7 elongation-rate measurements, since tip geometry is fabricated per-cantilever.
