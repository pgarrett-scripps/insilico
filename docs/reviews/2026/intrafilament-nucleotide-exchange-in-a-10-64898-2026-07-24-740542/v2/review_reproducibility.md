# Reproducibility Reviewer

## Summary
This is a rigorous experimental study with strong microscopy and biochemical evidence for a novel mechanism—intrafilament nucleotide exchange in MreB filaments. The core findings are well-supported by multiple independent techniques (TIRF, HS-AFM, FRAP, fluorescence anisotropy, ATPase assays). However, the reproducibility of the quantitative analysis is compromised by incomplete specification of image-processing parameters, missing raw data, and deferred code/data availability. The Monte-Carlo model is described in prose without sufficient detail to reimplement, and critical procedural decisions (e.g., TSOAX thresholds, photobleaching analysis) lack the precision needed for independent replication.

## Strengths
- Multiple orthogonal experimental approaches (TIRF, HS-AFM, FRAP, fluorescence anisotropy, liposome binding, ATPase assays) converge on the same conclusions, reducing the risk that a single technical artifact drives the claims.
- Dual-color TIRF experiments and photobleaching experiments directly demonstrate symmetric, bidirectional filament growth and nucleotide exchange, isolating these mechanisms from confounds like filament bundling or end-specific effects.
- The authors transparently report limitations (e.g., HS-AFM tracking difficulties, pFAST tag effects, stochastic fluctuations in growth-ratio analysis) and validate key findings across different protein concentrations and labeling strategies.

## Weaknesses
- **Load-bearing claim: Intrafilament nucleotide exchange occurs in MreB filaments.** The evidence rests on FRAP experiments (Fig. 3F, 3H) showing rapid recovery of ATP* signal in preformed filaments, interpreted as nucleotide exchange. However, the manuscript does not exclude the alternative that ATP* fluorescence recovery reflects rebinding of ATP* monomers from solution to filament ends or to transiently dissociated subunits, rather than exchange within the intact filament lattice. The authors state "Although TFLime binding to pFAST is reversible under normal imaging conditions, the lack of FRAP recovery suggests that the high-intensity laser illumination used for photobleaching induces irreversible photochemical trapping" (line 254–256), but this explanation applies only to the TFLime experiment (Fig. 3E), not to ATP* (Fig. 3F). For ATP*, the recovery could arise from 2D diffusion of ATP*-MreB monomers on the membrane surface and rebinding at filament ends, a mechanism the authors acknowledge for polymerization (lines 189–191) but do not rule out for the FRAP recovery. The capping experiment (Fig. 4G–H) shows that ADP-induced depolymerization occurs in the WT section of a filament capped with E136A, consistent with intrafilament nucleotide exchange, but the fragmentation observed could also result from nucleotide-dependent conformational changes that destabilize the filament without requiring nucleotide exchange within the lattice. To distinguish intrafilament exchange from end-mediated rebinding, the authors would need to show that recovery occurs at positions far from filament ends, or that recovery rate is independent of filament length or end density.
- **Load-bearing claim: The Monte-Carlo model accurately reproduces experimental observations and supports the mechanistic interpretation.** The model is described only in prose and supplementary text (referenced as 'Supplementary Information, Modeling MreB dynamics', line 286), with no equations, pseudocode, or parameter-fitting procedure provided in the main text or accessible supplementary materials. The authors state standard values (Fig. 4D) but do not specify: (i) how these values were derived (fitted to which data, using which algorithm, with what objective function?); (ii) sensitivity analysis or confidence intervals for fitted parameters; (iii) the exact sequence of reactions and their rate laws; (iv) initial conditions and boundary conditions for simulations; (v) the number of stochastic replicates and convergence criteria. Without this detail, the model cannot be reimplemented or validated independently. The agreement between simulations (Fig. S10) and experiments (Fig. 4A–C) is qualitative; no quantitative goodness-of-fit metric is reported. The model's predictive power is therefore unverifiable.
- **Load-bearing claim: ATP hydrolysis is not required for MreB polymerization.** The evidence is that E136A and D158A mutants, which have reduced ATPase activity (Fig. 3A), still polymerize with kinetics similar to WT (Fig. 3C–D). However, the manuscript does not report the absolute ATPase activity of these mutants—only that Pi release is 'markedly reduced' (line 232). If the mutants retain even 5–10% of WT ATPase activity, hydrolysis could still be mechanistically required for polymerization but kinetically slow enough that it does not limit the observed elongation rate. The authors should quantify the residual ATPase activity of E136A and D158A in absolute terms (Pi/min/MreB) and show that it is negligible compared to the polymerization rate.
- Image-processing parameters for TSOAX filament tracking (line 808) are not specified: no threshold values, window sizes, or sensitivity settings are given, making the elongation-rate measurements (Fig. 2E) non-reproducible.
- The FRAP analysis (Fig. 3E–F) does not report the bleach radius, recovery half-time, or lateral diffusion coefficient, preventing verification of the claim that 'monomers do not turnover within filaments' (line 254).
- The photobleaching experiment (Fig. 2G) uses a 'growth-ratio definition' (line 221) that is relegated to the figure legend without explicit statement of the formula or how stochastic fluctuations were distinguished from intrinsic polarity.
- Raw data (HS-AFM images, TIRF time-lapses, fluorescence anisotropy traces, ATPase assay replicates) are stated to be 'available from the corresponding authors upon request' (line 883) rather than deposited with a DOI or accession number, violating the In Silico standard for load-bearing empirical data.
- Code for the Monte-Carlo model is not mentioned as available anywhere; the supplementary text reference (line 286) is not a resolvable artifact.

## Questions
- What is the absolute Pi release rate (Pi/min/MreB) for E136A and D158A mutants, and how does it compare to the polymerization rate (subunits/min) to establish whether hydrolysis is truly dispensable?
- For the ATP* FRAP experiment (Fig. 3F), what is the bleach radius, recovery half-time, and fitted lateral diffusion coefficient, and how does the recovery curve distinguish intrafilament exchange from end-mediated rebinding?
- What are the TSOAX parameter values (threshold, window size, sensitivity) used for filament tracking, and are these parameters consistent across all experiments?
- Can the authors provide the complete Monte-Carlo model (reactions, rate laws, initial/boundary conditions, fitting procedure, parameter uncertainties) as a supplementary text or deposited code repository with a DOI?
- Where will raw data (HS-AFM stacks, TIRF movies, fluorescence anisotropy traces, ATPase replicates) be deposited, and what is the accession number or DOI?
