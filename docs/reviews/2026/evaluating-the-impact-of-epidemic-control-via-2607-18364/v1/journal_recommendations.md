# Journal Recommendations

## As-is (current quality)
- **Journal of Mathematical Biology**
  - Fit: Regularly publishes stochastic finite-population epidemic models with Markovian structure, distributional analysis of extinction time, and state-dependent intervention mechanisms. The recursive method for joint transforms and the SIR-with-regime-switching framework align squarely with the journal's core methodological scope.
  - Realism: Realistic with minor revision. The editor's verdict is 'minor' and the paper's central theorems are sound; reproducibility gaps (code availability, numerical validation) are stated as conditions of acceptance but not grounds for escalation to major. JMB typically requires code deposit and validation but accepts conditional, scenario-based empirical analyses when the mathematical framework is rigorous.
- **Mathematical Biosciences**
  - Fit: Established outlet for finite-state continuous-time Markov chain epidemic models, infection-count distributions, and mixed moments linking duration and burden. The paper's methodological positioning (exact transforms via recursion, Laplace-Stieltjes inversion) fits the journal's quantitative epidemiology scope.
  - Realism: Realistic without new science. The paper's technical level and the explicit caveats about conditional model-based (not causal) comparisons are consistent with the journal's standards. Reproducibility requirements are less stringent than top-tier statistical journals, though code deposition would strengthen the case.
- **Applied Mathematical Modelling**
  - Fit: Broad scope for applied stochastic process models and intervention-scenario analysis. The Luxembourg mpox case study, calibration methodology, and the systematic comparison of control regimes (transmission reduction, timing, vaccination) fit the journal's applied-mathematics-meets-public-health profile.
  - Realism: Realistic at current quality. The journal is receptive to conditional scenario analyses and is less stringent on code availability if the mathematical development is transparent. However, adding validation and sensitivity analysis would substantially strengthen the manuscript's standing.

## After required revisions
- **Journal of the Royal Statistical Society Series A**
  - Fit: Matches the paper's shift to joint distributional analysis and causal-inference positioning once framing is corrected. The correlation structure between T and C_I, the sensitivity analysis on epidemic parameters, and the methodological contribution on distributional comparison of interventions all fit the journal's statistical methodology and applications scope.
  - Realism: Realistic after required revisions. Once code is deposited, numerical validation is supplied, and the title/abstract frame the work as conditional model-based comparison rather than historical evaluation, this becomes a natural home for the paper's message about joint distributional analysis and the value of examining multiple outcome dimensions together.
- **PLOS Computational Biology**
  - Fit: Established, high-profile venue for stochastic epidemic models with computational validation. The finite-population CTMC framework, the recursive solver (reducing O(|S_T|^3) to O(P^3N^2 + P^2N^3)), and the comparative scenario analysis meet the journal's standards for methodological rigor and empirical illustration in epidemiology and public health.
  - Realism: Realistic after validation is added. PLOS Computational Biology requires computational reproducibility, independent validation, and open code/data — all now promised by the editor's conditions. The paper's distinguishing contribution (exact distributional quantities versus point estimates) becomes checkable and defensible once validation appendix and Zenodo deposition are in place.
- **Bioinformatics**
  - Fit: If reframed as a computational-methods paper, the level-wise recursion exploiting monotonicity of S(t), the efficient numerical solver for high-dimensional Markov systems, and the software engineering (handling state-dependence in regime transitions) fit the journal's computational focus.
  - Realism: Realistic after revision emphasizes computational contribution. The paper's novelty (state-dependent λ_{p,p'}(s,i) and the resulting block-structure change in the generator) and the O(P^3N^2 + P^2N^3) speedup would be positioned prominently. Bioinformatics is receptive to tools that reduce computational burden in systems biology and epidemiology, provided validation and code availability are met.

## Alternative outlets
- **bioRxiv**
  - Fit: Preprint server for quantitative biology and epidemiology. The paper is already on arXiv (q-bio.PE). Posting to bioRxiv as well or exclusively would reach an audience of epidemiologists and computational biologists. As a preprint, it is immediately disseminatable without waiting for journal acceptance.
  - Realism: Immediate visibility as a preprint. bioRxiv does not peer-review manuscripts the way In Silico does; it is purely a preprint archive. However, combined with an In Silico review, it would provide a public record and citable DOI. This is a good fallback if journal submission encounters delays.
- **Stochastic Processes and their Applications**
  - Fit: Theory-focused journal on continuous-time Markov chains, transforms, and distributional analysis. Theorems 2 and 4, the level-wise recursion, and the proofs are the paper's main contribution. A theory-first journal is more forgiving of conditional empirical illustrations and less demanding of exhaustive numerical validation if the underlying mathematics is rigorous.
  - Realism: Realistic if the paper is reframed as a pure methods paper (theory, proofs, recursion, transforms) with the mpox case study demoted to an illustration. The disadvantage is a narrower epidemiology audience; the advantage is lower reproducibility barriers and explicit recognition of the mathematical innovation.
- **Mathematical Population Biology workshop or special issue**
  - Fit: Venue-specific outlet for stochastic epidemic modeling within mathematical ecology and population dynamics communities. The paper's treatment of finite-population extinction time, final-size distributions, and intervention-dependent regime switching fits naturally within this specialized audience.
  - Realism: Realistic as a workshop paper or contributed chapter if a dedicated special issue or conference stream emerges. This is a lower-tier fallback if manuscript venues are exhausted, but it provides a rapid publication channel and allows the work to be built into a specialized monograph. Not a primary option but a useful holding option.
