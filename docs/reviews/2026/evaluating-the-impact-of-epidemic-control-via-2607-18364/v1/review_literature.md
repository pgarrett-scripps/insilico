# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript's citations are accurate, resolvable, and properly attributed. The related-work section clearly positions the contribution relative to prior work, particularly Almaraz and Gómez-Corral (2018). The main concern is verification of the publication status of several future-dated self-citations (2025, 2026). Self-citation is moderately elevated (~16% of references) but not egregious. The applied epidemiological context is intentionally thin, which is appropriate given the stated scope of conditional model comparisons rather than causal inference. No foundational or directly competing work appears to be omitted.

## Strengths
- All cited references are real, resolvable, and properly formatted with valid journal names, volumes, and page numbers.
- The manuscript clearly differentiates its contribution from prior work, particularly Almaraz and Gómez-Corral (2018), explaining why the new approach differs in interpretation, state-dependence, and computational method.
- Foundational work on stochastic epidemic models (Artalejo, Gómez-Corral, Allen, Britton) is appropriately cited.
- The authors honestly acknowledge limitations (e.g., imperfect/waning immunity not represented) rather than silently omitting them.
- The novelty claims are scaled to match the actual contribution: distributional analysis via recursion, state-dependent switching, and joint extinction-time/infection-burden characterization.

## Weaknesses
- Self-citation is moderately high (~8 self-citations, ~16% of the reference list), including several future-dated works (Papageorgiou 2025, 2026a, 2026b, 2026). The publication status of these should be verified; if they are preprints or in preparation, this violates the guideline against using unpublished work to support load-bearing claims.
- The applied epidemiological context is thin: very few citations to epidemiological studies of the 2022–2023 mpox outbreak or to vaccination/immunity literature. However, the authors explicitly frame the application as 'conditional comparisons' rather than causal inference, which limits the scope of what must be cited.
- The state-dependent regime-switching literature is not comprehensively surveyed. Beyond Artalejo & Gómez-Corral (2010), the manuscript does not cite other work on state-dependent switching in control theory, queueing, or other fields that might provide relevant methodological context.
- The characterization of Almaraz and Gómez-Corral (2018) as using 'Markov-modulated infection and removal processes to accommodate non-exponential waiting-time distributions' (p. 3) cannot be verified from the title alone and should be confirmed.

## Questions
- Can you confirm the publication status of Papageorgiou (2025, 2026a, 2026b, 2026)? Are these accepted/in-press papers, or preprints? If preprints, how do they support the claims in the introduction?
- In your characterization of Almaraz and Gómez-Corral (2018) on page 3, you state they use 'Markov-modulated infection and removal processes to accommodate non-exponential waiting-time distributions.' Can you confirm this is an accurate summary of their work?
- Have you surveyed the broader literature on state-dependent regime switching in control theory, queueing, or other fields? Are there other relevant references beyond Artalejo & Gómez-Corral (2010) that should be cited to contextualize your state-dependent switching mechanism?
