# Novelty & Contribution Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript presents a mathematically rigorous framework for finite-population SIR epidemics with Markovian regime switching, where intervention phases modulate transmission and recovery rates, and phase transitions may depend on the current epidemic state. The core methodological contribution—recursive formulas for the joint Laplace–Stieltjes transform and probability generating function of extinction time and infection burden—is technically sound and appears novel in its specific form. However, the novelty is incremental rather than transformative. The work builds directly on Almaraz & Gómez-Corral (2018), which already introduced Markov-modulated SIR models with finite populations and joint distributional analysis. The key claimed differentiators (state-dependent phase transitions and interpretation of phases as intervention regimes) are meaningful but represent a reinterpretation and extension of existing machinery rather than a fundamentally new mathematical structure. The empirical application is intentionally limited and does not demonstrate new epidemiological insights. The contribution is real and publishable but should be positioned as an incremental methodological advance with a specific application focus, not as a major innovation in stochastic epidemic modeling.

## Strengths
- Rigorous mathematical framework: Theorem 2 and its corollaries provide exact recursive formulas for joint transforms of extinction time and infection burden, with proofs in the appendix. The level-wise recursion exploiting monotonicity of the susceptible population is computationally efficient (O(P³N²) vs. O(|S_T|³) for naive approaches).
- State-dependent phase transitions: The generalization to allow λ_{p,p'}(s,i) to depend on current epidemic state (S,I) is a meaningful extension beyond prior state-independent models. This enables representation of policy escalation that reacts to infectious population, which is epidemiologically relevant.
- Joint distributional analysis: The framework simultaneously yields infection-count distributions, extinction-time transforms, conditional quantities, and mixed moments (Cov(T, N_I)), moving beyond marginal summaries. This is useful for intervention comparison.
- Transparent scenario design: The Luxembourg mpox application clearly separates effects of control intensity, intervention timing, vaccination, and state-dependent escalation through conditional comparisons on fitted baseline parameters. The authors explicitly acknowledge these are model-based comparisons, not causal estimates.
- Clear positioning of limitations: The authors candidly state that switching intensities and intervention effects are specified rather than estimated, and that the empirical study should be interpreted as conditional mechanism comparison, not historical causal evaluation.

## Weaknesses
- Insufficient differentiation from Almaraz & Gómez-Corral (2018): The manuscript cites Almaraz & Gómez-Corral (2018) as introducing Markov-modulated infection and removal processes in finite-population SIR, and states their framework uses modulation mainly to generate correlated event times while the present work uses it to represent random or epidemic-responsive intervention changes. However, the mathematical structure is nearly identical: both use a finite-state phase process J(t) that modulates transition intensities, both derive recursive formulas for joint transforms, and both analyze extinction time and final size. The claimed distinction (phases as intervention regimes vs. temporal structure enrichment) is interpretive rather than structural. The manuscript does not provide a head-to-head comparison showing what mathematical or computational capability is new beyond Almaraz & Gómez-Corral (2018).
- State-dependent transitions not clearly novel: While state-dependent λ_{p,p'}(s,i) is presented as a contribution, the manuscript cites Artalejo & Gómez-Corral (2010) as introducing state-dependent Markov-modulated mechanisms. The present work applies this existing concept to intervention phases. The novelty appears to be the application and interpretation, not the mathematical mechanism itself.
- Limited empirical validation of claimed advantages: The Luxembourg mpox case study does not demonstrate that the framework provides insights unavailable from simpler approaches. The baseline one-phase SIR is fitted deterministically; the switching scenarios are then evaluated conditionally on fitted parameters. No comparison is provided showing that (a) the recursive method is faster/more accurate than direct simulation or (b) the joint distributional analysis reveals intervention differences that marginal analyses would miss. The correlations Corr(T, C_I) reported in Section 3.6 (0.85–0.93) are high across all scenarios, suggesting limited practical differentiation in dependence structure.
- Vague claim about level-wise recursion novelty: The manuscript states the recursion replaces a large global linear system with small phase-level solves. However, this is a standard consequence of exploiting block structure in Markov chains; the block-bidiagonal form of Q_T,T follows directly from the monotonicity of S(t), which is not new. The computational advantage is real but not a novel insight.
- Preprint search found concurrent work on regime-switching epidemics: The search returned A Switching State-Space Transmission Model for Tracking Epidemics and Assessing Interventions (Feng & Wang, 2023, PPR), which addresses regime switching and intervention assessment. While that work appears to use a different state-space framework, the manuscript does not cite or compare against it, raising questions about awareness of concurrent work in this space.
- Empirical application does not test the framework's core claims: The mpox analysis uses only 14 weekly observations and fits a one-phase deterministic SIR. The switching scenarios are then specified (not estimated) and evaluated conditionally. This does not demonstrate that the framework can identify or estimate regime-switching parameters from real data, nor does it show that the joint distributional analysis provides actionable insights beyond what deterministic models or simulation would yield.

## Questions
- Specific mathematical novelty over Almaraz & Gómez-Corral (2018): Can the authors provide a detailed comparison showing which equations, theorems, or computational steps in the present work are genuinely new versus direct applications of Almaraz & Gómez-Corral (2018)? For instance, is Theorem 2 (the recursion for Φ_{s,i,p}) a new derivation or a restatement of their result with state-dependent λ_{p,p'} substituted?
- Computational efficiency claim: The manuscript claims O(P³N²) cost vs. O(|S_T|³) for dense solution of the full system. However, sparse solvers can reduce the latter. What is the actual wall-clock speedup of the level-wise recursion vs. a sparse direct solver on realistic problem sizes (e.g., N=100–1000, P=2–5)? Is this speedup demonstrated empirically?
- Practical utility of joint distribution: In the mpox scenarios (Section 3), the high correlations Corr(T, C_I) ≈ 0.85–0.93 suggest that T and C_I move together. Does the joint analysis reveal intervention trade-offs (e.g., one regime reduces C_I but increases Var(T)) that would not be apparent from marginal comparisons? If not, what is the practical value of computing the joint distribution?
- Why not estimate switching parameters from data?: The authors specify λ_{p,p'} rather than estimate them from the Luxembourg intervention history. Given that the framework is designed to handle state-dependent switching, why not use the mpox data to calibrate a two-phase model (no measures → strict control) and compare its fit to the one-phase baseline? This would test whether the added complexity is justified.
- Relationship to Artalejo & Gómez-Corral (2010): The manuscript cites Artalejo & Gómez-Corral (2010) as introducing state-dependent Markov-modulated mechanisms. How does the present state-dependent λ_{p,p'}(s,i) differ from their framework? Is the novelty only in the application to intervention phases?
- Concurrent work awareness: The preprint search identified A Switching State-Space Transmission Model for Tracking Epidemics and Assessing Interventions (Feng & Wang, 2023). Is the authors' framework aware of and differentiated from this work? If not, a comparison would strengthen the novelty positioning.
