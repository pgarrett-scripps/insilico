# Ethics & Compliance Reviewer

## Summary
This manuscript presents a stochastic epidemic modeling framework with regime-switching intervention mechanisms, applied to mpox data from Luxembourg. From an ethics and compliance perspective, the work is sound: it uses only publicly available, non-identifiable aggregate incidence data; contains no human subjects, clinical trials, animal research, or dual-use biosafety concerns; and makes appropriate disclosures of funding, competing interests, and data availability. The authors are transparent about the limitations of their empirical illustration—explicitly stating that switching mechanisms are specified rather than estimated, and that results are conditional model comparisons rather than causal claims about historical interventions. This candor about the scope and limitations of the inference is commendable and meets the standard for responsible reporting.

## Strengths
- Funding sources are clearly disclosed: 'This work was partially supported by the French National Research Agency (ANR) under grant ANR-21-CE40-0005 (HSMM-INCA).'
- Competing interests are explicitly declared: 'The authors declare that they have no known competing financial interests or personal relationships that could have appeared to influence the work reported in this paper.'
- Data availability is appropriately stated: 'The empirical application uses publicly available mpox incidence data from the Our World in Data mpox repository' with a URL provided, and 'The processed data and computer code used to reproduce the numerical results are available from the authors upon reasonable request.'
- The work uses only aggregate, publicly available incidence data with no identifiable individual-level information, making it exempt from human-subjects oversight.
- The authors are transparent about the conditional nature of their empirical results, explicitly stating: 'the results are conditional model-based comparisons rather than estimates of the historical effects of interventions in Luxembourg' and 'the switching intensities and intervention effects are specified rather than estimated from the intervention history.'
- Authorship is clearly attributed with ORCID identifiers for all three authors, reducing ambiguity about contributions.
- The authors acknowledge limitations and future work directions, including the need to 'calibrate the full regime-switching model to richer real-world epidemic data, together with documented intervention histories.'

## Weaknesses
- The manuscript does not explicitly state that the work is exempt from IRB/ethics-committee review because it uses only aggregate public data with no human subjects, identifiable information, or interventional components. While this exemption is reasonable and the work appears compliant, an explicit statement of exemption would strengthen transparency (SOFT).
- Code availability is stated as 'available from the authors upon reasonable request' rather than deposited in a public repository with a persistent identifier (e.g., GitHub with DOI, Zenodo). For a computational methods paper, immediate public access to code would better support reproducibility (SOFT).
- The manuscript does not acknowledge any use of artificial intelligence, machine learning, or large language models in the writing, analysis, or code generation. If none were used, an explicit statement would be appropriate for current standards (SOFT).

## Questions
- Would the authors be willing to deposit the processed data and code in a public repository (e.g., GitHub, Zenodo, or a discipline-specific archive) with a persistent identifier, to facilitate immediate reproducibility and long-term archival?
- Can the authors confirm that no IRB or ethics-committee review was sought or required because the work uses only publicly available, non-identifiable aggregate data and involves no human subjects, clinical interventions, or identifiable information?
- Were any AI tools, large language models, or automated code-generation systems used in the preparation of this manuscript or its computational implementation? If so, please disclose their role and any limitations they may introduce.
