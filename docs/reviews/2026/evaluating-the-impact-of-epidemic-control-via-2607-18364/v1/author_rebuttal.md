## Concessions
- **reproducibility** — Code and processed data are gated behind 'available upon reasonable request' with no repository, DOI, software versions, or random seed disclosed.
  - Proposed change: Deposit the processed weekly incidence data, the recursion/solver code, and the calibration scripts in a versioned public repository (Zenodo/GitHub with a tagged release and DOI) prior to publication. The revised Data and Code Availability section will list the DOI, software versions (Python/NumPy/SciPy or equivalent), the fixed random seed(s) used for the L-BFGS-B multistart, and the exact partial-week aggregation rule applied to the Poisson likelihood.
- **methodology** — No positive-control validation (e.g., Monte Carlo simulation or matrix exponentiation) of the recursive implementation against an independent benchmark.
  - Proposed change: Add a validation subsection (new Appendix or Section 3.1 addendum) comparing, for the fitted N=63, P=3 Luxembourg configuration and for a small synthetic P=2 example, the recursion-derived PMF of C_I, extinction-time moments, and density (via Abate-Whitt inversion) against (i) direct sparse solution of the global generator system and (ii) Monte Carlo simulation of the CTMC with reported Monte Carlo error bars.
- **rigor** — Abstract and title imply causal/historical evaluation of interventions before the conditional-comparison caveat is reached.
  - Proposed change: Retitle to 'Assessing Conditional Effects of Epidemic Control via State-Dependent Markovian Switching Modeling' (or similar) and reorder the abstract so the sentence stating that switching mechanisms are specified rather than estimated precedes the sentence describing what the results show, making the conditional scope the first framing element rather than a trailing qualifier.

  - Proposed change: Add a supplementary sensitivity table/figure reporting E[C_I], E[T], and Corr(T,C_I) for γ ∈ {1/4, 1/3, 1/2}, for q ∈ {0.02, 0.05, 0.10, 0.35}, for ψ ∈ {0.01, 0.05, 0.10}, and for the Eq. 19 constants perturbed by ±50%, to show whether the qualitative rankings of scenarios are stable.

  - Proposed change: Validate the Abate–Whitt inversion against a one-phase SIR case with a closed-form or independently-simulated extinction-time distribution, report the maximum relative inversion error, and disclose in the caption/text how many grid points were zeroed due to inversion artifacts in each figure.

  - Proposed change: Add 'the mixed moments μ^{(k,r)}_{s,i,p} = E[T^k(N_I)^r | (s,i,p)]' immediately at first use in Section 2.3, rather than only before Theorem 4.

## Disagreements
- **novelty** — The mathematical structure is 'nearly identical' to Almaraz & Gómez-Corral (2018), and the distinction (intervention regimes vs. temporal correlation) is merely interpretive rather than structural.
  - Response: The structural difference is not merely interpretive: in Almaraz & Gómez-Corral (2018) the modulating phase transitions are exogenous and state-independent, so the phase and epidemic processes only interact through the event-rate modulation, not through feedback from (S,I) into the phase generator. Our construction lets λ_{p,p'} depend explicitly on the current epidemic state, which changes the generator's block structure (Q_P(s,i) varies with the level and within-level index) and requires the state-dependent level-wise recursion of Theorem 2, rather than a single fixed phase-generator recursion. This is what permits Scenario 4's escalation mechanism (Eq. 19), which cannot be expressed in a state-independent Markov-modulated framework at all, not merely reinterpreted within it.
  - Manuscript reference: Section 1: 'Phase-transition intensities may depend on the current epidemic state, so that policy escalation can react to the number of infectious individuals... the phaseJ(t) represents the active intervention regime... and may switch at rates that depend explicitly on the current epidemic state(S(t), I(t))'; Section 2.1, definition of λ_{p,p'}(s,i) and Q_P(s,i).
- **rigor** — The abstract's claim that 'the results show how switching mechanisms affect' infection counts and extinction time constitutes an overclaim of causal/historical evaluation.
  - Response: We agree the ordering should be improved (see concession above), but we disagree that this is a hard overclaim as currently written: the very next sentence of the abstract already states the results are conditional and not estimates of historical intervention effects, and Section 3's opening paragraph repeats this before any scenario results are presented. The word 'affect' refers to model-implied distributional differences under specified mechanisms, consistent with the paper's stated scope throughout Sections 3.1-3.6.
  - Manuscript reference: Abstract, final two sentences: 'The results show how switching mechanisms affect both the total number of infected individuals and the extinction time... Since the switching mechanisms are specified rather than estimated from the intervention history, the results are conditional model-based comparisons rather than estimates of the historical effects of interventions in Luxembourg.'
- **methodology** — The correlations Corr(T,C_I) of 0.85–0.93 being similar across all scenarios suggests the joint distributional analysis adds no practical differentiating value over marginal comparisons.
  - Response: The consistently high correlation is itself a substantive finding, not a null result: it demonstrates that under this model class, interventions that alter marginal means of T and C_I differently (e.g., Scenario 4, where mean C_I drops from 33.52 to 28.50 while mean T is essentially unchanged at 14.39 vs 14.44) still preserve strong positive dependence, which is only knowable from the joint machinery. A marginal-only analysis could not establish whether the timing/intensity mechanisms decouple T and C_I; our results show they largely do not, which is itself informative for interpreting mean-based comparisons in Section 3.6.
  - Manuscript reference: Section 3.6, 'Dependence between outbreak duration and size': reported correlations 0.85-0.93 across all four scenario groups, contrasted with Section 3.5's finding that mean C_I changes substantially while mean T does not.

## Load-bearing critiques
- Absence of any independent validation (Monte Carlo or alternative solver) of the recursive numerical implementation underlying every reported PMF, density, and moment in Section 3
- Code and processed data withheld behind 'available upon reasonable request' rather than deposited in a citable public repository, preventing independent verification of the numerical results this cycle
