# Clarity & Presentation Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript is clearly written overall and the mathematical framework is presented with appropriate rigor. The narrative flow from motivation through methods to application is sound, and key terms are defined. However, there are several presentation issues that create friction: the central methodological contribution is somewhat buried, the relationship between the recursive formulas and their practical utility could be clearer earlier, and some quantitative claims in the results lack precision. The figures are well-constructed but could benefit from minor labeling improvements. These are fixable issues that do not block understanding but would improve accessibility.

## Strengths
- The abstract clearly states the main contributions (regime-switching SIR with state-dependent transitions, and recursive distributional analysis) and is self-contained.
- Mathematical notation is introduced systematically: the state space S, the phase process J(t), and the generator Q are all defined before use.
- The paper has a clear three-part structure (model formulation, recursive analysis, application) with explicit section headings and purpose statements.
- Figures 3–11 are well-labeled with units (weeks), legends showing means as dashed lines, and captions that explain what is being compared.
- The distinction between the authors' contribution and prior work (Almaraz & Gómez-Corral 2018) is made explicit in the introduction: 'Rather than using modulation mainly to generate correlated event times, we use it to represent random or epidemic-responsive intervention changes' (page 3).
- The empirical application is transparent about its limitations: 'The switching intensities and intervention effects are specified rather than estimated from the intervention history' (page 4) and repeated in Section 3.1.
- Boundary conditions and special cases are stated clearly (e.g., 'If ψ_p is interpreted as a vaccination intensity, protection is assumed to be immediate and to last until the end of the outbreak' on page 6).

## Weaknesses
- The central methodological claim—that the recursion 'replaces a large global linear system with small phase-level solves'—is stated in the abstract but its practical significance is not quantified until Section 2.4 (page 14), where the computational complexity is given as O(P³N²) for the recursion versus O(|S_T|³) for a dense global solve. A reader cannot judge the contribution's importance until late in the paper. Suggest moving or emphasizing the complexity comparison earlier.
- In Section 2.2, the joint transform Φ_{s,i,p}(z,u) is introduced (equation 4) as 'the joint Laplace–Stieltjes transform–probability generating function of (T, N_I)', but the practical meaning—what questions this object answers—is deferred to Section 2.3 (Corollary 3). A sentence like 'From this transform we can extract the marginal distributions of extinction time and infection count, as well as their dependence' would clarify purpose before derivation.
- Quantitative comparisons in Section 3 sometimes lack precision. For example, 'earlier intervention shifts the distribution of C_I towards smaller values' (page 20) is directional but the next sentence gives means (33.52 vs 21.82). However, the phrase 'shifts the distribution' without stating the shift magnitude (e.g., 'reduces the mean by 35%') makes the claim less concrete. This is SOFT but repeated across scenarios.
- Figure 2 caption states 'Bars represent the observations, and the solid line represents the fitted means' but does not specify the observation model (Poisson) or the time unit (weeks). A reader must cross-reference to Section 3.1 to understand what is plotted. Suggest: 'Observed weekly mpox incidence (bars) and fitted Poisson means (line) under the one-phase baseline SIR model.'
- The notation for mixed moments μ^{(k,r)}_{s,i,p} is introduced in Section 2.3 but its interpretation (E[T^k (N_I)^r]) is given only in the text before Theorem 4. The notation itself does not signal that it is a mixed moment; a brief gloss at first use (e.g., 'mixed moments μ^{(k,r)}_{s,i,p} = E[T^k (N_I)^r]') would help.
- In Section 3.5, the state-dependent escalation rate λ^{sd}_{1,2}(s,i) = 0.01 + 0.40 i²/(i² + 62) is presented without justification for the functional form or the constants. The caption to Figure 9 states 'Rates are expressed in week⁻¹' but does not explain why this particular sigmoid-like function was chosen or how sensitive results are to the choice. This is not a HARD clarity issue (the formula is stated) but it creates friction for a reader trying to understand the design.
- The paper uses 'extinction time' and 'epidemic duration' interchangeably (e.g., page 3: 'the time until extinction are random'; page 26: 'outbreak duration'). While context makes the meaning clear, consistent terminology would reduce friction. Recommend settling on one term throughout.
- In Theorem 2, the recursion (equations 6–7) is stated for Φ_{s,i}(z,u) but the boundary condition Φ_{s,0}(z,u) = 1_P is stated only after the theorem statement. A reader must infer that i=0 is a base case. Suggest: 'with boundary condition Φ_{s,0}(z,u) = 1_P for all s.'

## Questions
- In Section 2.1, you state that 'the phase coordinate alone need not be Markovian; the joint process X is Markovian.' Can you clarify with a concrete example whether the state-dependent switching rates λ_{p,p'}(s,i) in your application (Scenario 4, equation 19) are chosen to ensure the joint process remains Markovian, or is this automatic by construction?
- Section 3.1 fixes the mean infectious period at γ = 1/3 per week 'consistently with the reported two-to-four-week duration' but then states this is 'a pragmatic calibration assumption rather than as a direct estimate.' How sensitive are the scenario comparisons in Section 3 to this choice? Would γ = 1/4 or γ = 1/2 materially change the conclusions?
- In the empirical application, you calibrate only the baseline one-phase model to data and then specify the switching mechanisms exogenously. You acknowledge this ('the switching intensities and intervention effects are specified rather than estimated'). But could you briefly discuss what data or additional information would be needed to estimate the switching rates λ_{p,p'}(s,i) from observed intervention histories?
- Figure 5 and Figure 10 show PMFs of C_I under different scenarios. The x-axis ranges differ between figures (e.g., Figure 5 goes to ~80, Figure 10 to ~60). Was this intentional to show the full support, or would a consistent x-axis range across all infection-count figures aid comparison?
- In Section 2.4, you state the cost is 'O(P³N² + P²N³) for fixed i_0.' Can you clarify whether this is the cost to compute all moments up to a fixed order (k,r), or the cost per (z,u) evaluation? This affects the practical utility claim.
