# Decision Letter

**Decision:** minor

## Summary of Evaluation
The panel converged strongly (weighted 4.03/5; 1 accept, 6 minor, 1 major) and I adopt the modal 'minor revision' verdict. The manuscript's central claims are theorems with proofs supplied in Appendix A, and the empirical claims are already explicitly scaled to conditional model-based comparisons by the authors themselves. No reviewer argued that a stated conclusion is contradicted by the evidence; the surviving criticisms concern verification and framing, both addressable without new science.

Two load-bearing critiques survived the debate: (i) the absence of any independent validation of the recursive numerical implementation underlying every PMF, density and moment in Section 3, including the unvalidated Abate–Whitt inversion with silently zeroed values; and (ii) code and processed data gated behind 'available upon reasonable request'. The authors conceded both fully and proposed concrete, adequate remedies (a validation appendix against sparse global-generator solution and Monte Carlo with error bars; Zenodo/GitHub deposition with DOI, versions and seeds). These are conditions of acceptance, not grounds for escalation to major, because the remedy is bounded and specified.

I weighed the rebuttal on three disputed points. On novelty, the authors' reply that state-dependence of λ_{p,p'}(s,i) changes the generator's block structure — making Q_P depend on level and within-level index and requiring the state-dependent recursion of Theorem 2 — is a structural, not interpretive, distinction from Almaraz & Gómez-Corral (2018), and I accept it; Scenario 4's escalation mechanism is not expressible in a state-independent Markov-modulated framework. On the abstract 'overclaim', the quoted text shows the conditional caveat is the very next sentence, and the authors nonetheless concede a reordering plus retitle; I therefore treat this as a framing fix rather than an unsupported claim, and do not require more than the reordering they propose. On the uniformly high Corr(T,C_I), the authors' argument that invariance of the dependence structure under interventions that shift E[C_I] but not E[T] is itself a substantive finding is credible and needs only to be stated in the paper rather than defended in correspondence.

The methods audit's two HARD gaps (software/library versions; random seeds) are subsumed by the authors' deposition commitment and are listed below as checkable items. The citation audit found no HARD gaps; the future-dated self-citations and access dates are handled as verification items.

## Required Revisions
1. Deposit the processed weekly incidence data, the recursion/solver code, and the calibration scripts in a versioned public repository with a DOI (e.g. Zenodo release tied to a tagged GitHub commit), and cite the DOI in a revised Data and Code Availability section. Replace the 'upon reasonable request' wording entirely.
2. Add a validation subsection (new appendix or Section 3.1 addendum) benchmarking the recursion-derived PMF of C_I, the extinction-time moments, and the inverted density for the fitted N=63, P=3 Luxembourg configuration and for a small synthetic P=2 case against (i) direct sparse solution of the global generator system and (ii) Monte Carlo simulation of the CTMC with reported Monte Carlo error bars. Report maximum absolute/relative discrepancies.
3. Validate the Abate–Whitt inversion against a case with a known or independently simulated extinction-time density (e.g. the one-phase SIR), report the maximum relative inversion error, and state explicitly in each affected figure caption how many grid points were set to zero because of negative or non-finite inversion output.
4. In the revised Methods, state the programming language and library versions (e.g. Python/NumPy/SciPy with version numbers), the hardware or runtime environment, the fixed random seed(s) used for the L-BFGS-B multistart, the number of starts, and whether reported estimates come from a single run or are the best over starts. This closes the two HARD methods-completeness gaps.
5. Add a sensitivity analysis (supplementary table or figure) reporting E[C_I], E[T] and Corr(T,C_I) for γ ∈ {1/4, 1/3, 1/2}, q ∈ {0.02, 0.05, 0.10, 0.35}, ψ ∈ {0.01, 0.05, 0.10}, and the Eq. 19 coefficients perturbed by ±50%, and state in the text whether the qualitative ranking of scenarios is stable under these perturbations.
6. Reframe the title and abstract so the conditional scope precedes the effect language: adopt the proposed retitle (or equivalent wording that removes the historical-evaluation reading) and move the sentence stating that switching mechanisms are specified rather than estimated ahead of the sentence describing what the results show.
7. Verify and update the publication status of Papageorgiou (2025, 2026a, 2026b), supplying DOIs or acceptance details; mark unpublished items as 'manuscript in preparation' and remove any that support load-bearing claims. Also correct or explain the future-dated access dates for Mathieu et al. (2022) and WHO (2024).
8. Add a paragraph in the related-work discussion comparing Feng & Wang (2023) on switching state-space transmission models to the present finite-population CTMC construction, stating the difference in state representation and in distributional versus point-estimate output.
9. Add the robustness check re-fitting the one-phase model with the two partial weeks excluded, reporting (b̂, Ŝ(0), Î(0)) alongside the main estimates, and state the exact partial-week aggregation rule used in the Poisson likelihood.
10. Incorporate into Section 3.6 the argument given in the rebuttal that the near-invariance of Corr(T,C_I) across scenarios is a substantive result — showing that mechanisms which shift E[C_I] without shifting E[T] nonetheless preserve strong positive dependence — so that the value of the joint machinery is stated in the paper rather than left implicit.

## Minor Suggestions
- Surface the O(P^3N^2 + P^2N^3) versus O(|S_T|^3) complexity comparison in the Introduction's contributions paragraph, with a forward pointer to Section 2.4.
- Add the one-sentence gloss on the purpose of Φ_{s,i,p}(z,u) immediately after Eq. (4), and define μ^{(k,r)}_{s,i,p} = E[T^k(N_I)^r | (s,i,p)] at first use rather than only before Theorem 4.
- Revise the Figure 2 caption to name the observation model and the time unit, as proposed.
- Standardise on 'extinction time' throughout, replacing 'epidemic duration' and 'outbreak duration'.
- Consider whether the Papageorgiou (2026a) citation on terrorism radicalization is germane in the scope discussion; if retained, make the relevance to the present construction explicit in one clause.
- State the exact download date and, if available, the file version or snapshot of the Our World in Data mpox series used, so the input series can be reconstructed independently of upstream revisions.
