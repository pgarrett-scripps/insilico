# Methodology Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript pairs a four-fraction TMT human hippocampal study with a two-fraction QUAD mouse aging study, and the underlying measurement approach is sound in both halves. But the paper's central inference – that specific subcellular locations are 'primary determinants' of AD vulnerability – is made from per-fraction significance counts without any test of a disease×fraction interaction, and the QUAD 'degradation' claim cannot be separated from the protein-insolubility shifts the authors themselves describe. Both claims are fixable with additional analysis rather than new experiments, so overall this is a major revision rather than a rejection.

## Weaknesses
- The 'fraction-specific' claim is not tested as a claim. The manuscript states that '78% of significant AD-associated alterations were restricted to a single fraction, demonstrating that subcellular localization is a primary determinant of disease vulnerability.' The supporting analysis is an independent per-fraction ANOVA (Fig. 4A), and then a counting of how many proteins reached p<0.05 in only one fraction. But whether a protein passes the cutoff in fraction A and not fraction B depends on the number of quantified peptides, the measured noise, and the abundance of that protein in each fraction – all of which differ by fraction. A genuinely spatial architecture, by contrast, requires a test of interaction between disease and fraction for proteins measured in multiple fractions (a mixed model where an individual protein is quantified in multiple fractions as a within-subject repeated measure). As it stands, the '78%' number can be produced by power and detection differences alone, and no part of the manuscript's design or analysis presently rules that out.
- QUAD 'degradation' is confounded with insolubilization during the chase. The paper claims degradation rates diverge between fractions and, in the 12-month AD S2, are increased relative to WT, while AD P3 proteins appear more stable. But a labelled protein moving from the soluble S2 fraction into an aggregate/pellet fraction during the chase would produce exactly these measurements without any change in true degradation. This is not a hypothetical: the manuscript puts the 'shift to the insoluble fraction' as one of its central P3/P2 findings in human brain, and the authors explicitly concede in the Discussion that 'proteins with decreased stability in AD under the S2 and increased stability in the AD P3 could be explained by a protein becoming increasingly insoluble during the seven-day chase.' Once that is conceded, the term 'degradation' in the mouse results is not defended. To support 'degradation,' the authors need either (a) an experimental design where vertical protein pooling includes a redistribution-insensitive readout (e.g., entire-cortex day-0/day-7 ratio, compared to the fraction-level ratios), or (b) a re-labeling caveat, that solubility/death is a condition both change while keeping a 'degradation' claim.
- The 'retromer mislocalization' claim requires more than a shift between two fractions. Increased VPS35 in P3, decreased in S2, with unchanged total is consistent with VPS35 becoming less soluble (and therefore pelleting with P3), not necessarily being mislocalized to a different physical compartment. Since the partners construct the conclusion–'retromer complex mislocalization' is a labeled mechanism in the introduction/discussion–the authors should either walk the claim down to 'altered abundance partitioning' or add an orthogonal localization readout (e.g., immunofluorescence or a membrane-based fractionation) to distinguish aggregation from reorganize. The VPS35 immunoblot in Fig. 7D repeats the same fractions and cannot distinguish these.
- Sweep items (each one sentence; listed left to right by what they cost the design):
- The two 16-plex TMT sets per fraction: the manuscript does not state how the AD and ND cases were distributed across the two sets, nor whether the sets can be treated as a single batch; if diagnosis is confounded with the tíde tag set, every fraction comparison inherits a batch effect. Report the design, not a claim in supplementary.
- Fraction purity is only supported by GO-term enrichment on fraction-unique proteins; no marker-enrichment or cross-contamination estimate across fractions is shown, so 'what the four fractions physically are' is not demonstrated. Add a table of marker proteins (e.g., organelle-specific) and their abundance sera across the four fractions.
- The 'pre-pathological' 2-month designation is not substantiated: the A42 ELISA at 2 months is described as giving no signal ('signal was not observed' for wild-type), but no limit-of-detection is given for the sample or dilution; weak or OFF-scale signal would invalidate the word 'pre-pathological'.
- The 2-month QUAD 'initiating features' claim rests on a six-protein overlap, with a nominal n=4 per genotype; this is too small a molecular basis to sustain 'implicating … initiating features of AD'. It may be true, but the word 'initiating' exceeds the assay's temporal resolution.
- Pooled day-0 denominators: the day7/day0 ratio is computed against pooled day0 value, yet the loss of the day-0 replicate structure is not discussed—if AD and WT consumed, and thereby labeled, different AHA, that difference is absorbed into the ratio confounded with degradation.
- WGCNA module significance is thresholded at nominal p<0.05 across many (16–25) modules per fraction without multiple-testing correction; several significant modules are correlated—report (or estimate) the P of the observed number of significant modules under a permutation null.
