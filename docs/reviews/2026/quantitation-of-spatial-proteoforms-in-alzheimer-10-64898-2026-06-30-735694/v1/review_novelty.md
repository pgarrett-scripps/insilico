# Novelty & Contribution Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript's core contribution — a four-fraction TMT analysis of AD human hippocampus combined with QUAD degradation measurements in an AD mouse model across three ages — is real and, on my searches, not preempted. The novelty claim is, however, framed at a higher altitude than the evidence supports: "spatial proteoforms" as a concept and fraction-specific AD proteome changes are not new, and the manuscript's own citations (Bai 2013, Hales 2016, Thul 2017) acknowledge the nearest neighbors without stating the delta over them. The genuinely new elements are the specific four-fraction human dataset and the degradation kinetics in the APPswePS1dE9 model, and these should be positioned as the contribution rather than the framework.

## Strengths
- The combination of a four-fraction human hippocampus dataset with a three-age degradation analysis in the same two fractions is a coherent and, to my search, novel experimental design.
- The cross-dataset human/mouse comparison at the pre-pathological 2-month timepoint is a genuinely useful analytical move that sharpens the contribution.
- The manuscript is candid about the APPswePS1dE9 model's lack of tau pathology and scales its claims accordingly.

## Weaknesses
- The central framing claim — that this "establishes spatial proteoforms as essential units of pathogenic analysis" and reveals "signals invisible to bulk tissue approaches" — outruns what is actually new. Subcellular fractionation proteomics of AD brain is established (the manuscript itself cites Bai 2013 and Hales 2016 on insoluble fractions; Bai 2020 "Deep Multilayer Brain Proteomics" used fractionation), and the "spatial proteoform" concept is standard (Thul 2017, cited). The finding that 78% of AD changes are fraction-specific is a plausible and interesting observation, but it is partly a consequence of the fractionation design itself — a protein moving between fractions will by construction appear as a single-fraction change — so it does not by itself establish that spatial proteoforms are the "essential unit." What would settle this: a head-to-head statement of the delta over Bai 2020's multilayer fractionation and over the insoluble-fraction studies, naming what this four-fraction design detects that those do not. Without that, the contribution is real but its significance is overstated.
- The degradation analysis is the strongest genuinely novel element, but its novelty claim rests on the QUAD method applied to an AD model across ages. My searches (find_away_work and search_preprints on "protein degradation AD mouse azidohomoalanine/QUAD") returned no preempting work, so I cannot flag preemption — but the searches were largely uninformative (the fallback routing returned generic AD papers and empty bioRxiv results), so this is a pass with limited confidence, the verifiable priority. I could not verify the claim that "we have previously overstated with QUAD that proteins are more stable at 12-month compared to 2-month" — that is a citation to the authors' own prior work and is literature's call to check, not mine.
- The novel that most AD changes are fraction-specific (78%) is presented as a discovery, but the manuscript does not compare this against what a bulk tissue analysis of the same samples would show — a direct within-study comparison would make the "invisible to bulk tissue" claim testable rather than asserted.
- The cross-dataset human/mouse correlation (155 measurements) is presented as evidence of shared biology, but the direction-of-change logic (faster degradation ↔ lower abundance) is an assumption that the manuscript does not defend against the absolute value that both reflect independent Abeta-driven processes; this is partly consequence's call, which I flag and defer.
- The six 2-month proteins are called "initiating features," but with N=4 pooled replicates and a single early timepoint, the claim that these are initiating rather than early-responding is not established by the design; this is a significance claim that outruns the evidence.

## Questions
- What specifically does the four-fraction design detect that Bai 2020's multilayer fractionation and the cited insoluble-fraction studies do not — stated as a concrete delta?
- Can the "invisible to bulk tissue" claim be demonstrated within this study by running the same ANOVA on the pooled (unfractionated) samples alongside the fraction-specific results?
- For the 2-month "initiating" claim: is there any independent evidence (e.g., a second early timepoint or a functional assay) that these six proteins precede rather than accompany Abeta accumulation?
