# Ethics & Compliance Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript describes a large human postmortem proteomic study and mouse model experiments with appropriate ethical oversight disclosures for the human and animal components. Human tissue sourcing, IRB approval, and animal protocol compliance are explicitly stated. However, the paper lacks a formal data-availability statement and does not disclose funding sources or competing interests, which are standard requirements for general peer-reviewed journals. These are fixable omissions but represent compliance gaps that should be addressed before publication.

## Strengths
- Human postmortem brain samples are explicitly sourced from a named biorepository (Shiley-Marcos Alzheimer's Disease Research Center, UCSD) with neuropathological confirmation, establishing institutional provenance.
- Animal research explicitly states IACUC compliance: 'Animal facilities were AAALAC-approved, and protocols were in accordance with the IACUC' (Materials and Methods, Mice section).
- Humane endpoints for mice are documented: 'Mice were sacrificed by isoflurane inhalation' with clear timing (after 4 days on AHA diet or 7 days post-AHA diet).
- Both male and female mice were used, with explicit statement: 'Both females and male mice were used for this study,' supporting equitable experimental design.
- Human samples include appropriate pathological classification (AD vs. cognitively normal controls, with asymptomatic AD subgroup identified), enabling transparent reporting of participant characteristics.

## Weaknesses
- No funding sources are disclosed anywhere in the manuscript. Standard journal policy requires declaration of all funding, grants, and financial support. This is a SOFT compliance gap but mandatory for most peer-reviewed venues.
- No competing interests statement is present. The manuscript does not include an explicit declaration of competing interests or a statement that the authors have no conflicts to disclose. This is SOFT but expected in general journals.
- No data-availability statement is provided. The manuscript does not specify whether raw mass spectrometry data, processed proteomics tables, or code are available, or under what conditions (e.g., restricted access for human data). This is SOFT but increasingly required.
- The human tissue use does not explicitly state informed consent or a waiver. While the tissue source (Shiley-Marcos ADRC) is a legitimate biorepository, the manuscript does not confirm that samples were obtained under appropriate consent or that secondary use for this analysis was covered. This is a SOFT question because biorepository samples typically carry consent, but the paper should state this explicitly.
- No IRB approval number or protocol identifier is provided for the human postmortem study. While the tissue source is named, there is no statement of IRB review or exemption determination. This is a SOFT gap—postmortem tissue studies may be exempt, but the exemption should be stated.

## Questions
- Please provide a funding disclosure statement listing all sources of financial support, grants, and institutional funding for this work.
- Please include a competing interests declaration (either listing conflicts or explicitly stating 'The authors declare no competing interests').
- Please add a data-availability statement specifying whether raw LC-MS data, processed protein quantification tables, and analysis code are available, and if restricted, under what conditions (e.g., institutional data-use agreement, contact corresponding author).
- Please confirm the informed-consent or ethics-exemption status for the human postmortem samples. Specifically: (a) Did the Shiley-Marcos ADRC obtain informed consent from donors or next-of-kin for research use? (b) Was this secondary analysis covered under the original consent or a new IRB determination? (c) If exempt, which IRB made that determination?
- Please provide the IACUC protocol number or approval identifier for the mouse studies, to enable verification of animal-use compliance.
- The manuscript states samples were 'neuropathologically confirmed' but does not name the neuropathologist or institution performing confirmation. Is this information available in the biorepository records, and should it be cited or acknowledged?
