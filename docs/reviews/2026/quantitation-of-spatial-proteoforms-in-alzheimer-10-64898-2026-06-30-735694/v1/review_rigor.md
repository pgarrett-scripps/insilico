# Rigor & Overclaiming Reviewer

## Summary
This is a valuable descriptive study: fractionating AD hippocampi reveals that most disease-associated protein changes are fraction-specific, and the VPS35 example (unchanged in total homogenate, redistributed between fractions) is a genuinely compelling demonstration that spatial analysis recovers signals bulk proteomics misses. The QUAD extension across three timepoints is thoughtful, and the authors are commendably candid about the relocalization confound in their degradation interpretation. However, several headline claims in the abstract and discussion outrun the evidence — most seriously the "initiating features of AD" claim, which rests on a six-protein overlap reported without any enrichment statistic and uses causal language from an observational design. The descriptive findings should survive revision; the mechanistic framing needs requalification.

## Strengths
- The VPS35 result — unchanged in total homogenate but redistributed between S2 and P3 — is a clean, well-controlled demonstration of the core thesis that fraction-specific analysis reveals signals invisible to bulk approaches.
- The authors explicitly acknowledge the relocalization confound in the QUAD interpretation ("we may not be observing changes in degradation but changes in protein localization"), which is exactly the kind of candor that makes the rest of their claims credible.
- The multi-fraction design with high protein overlap between fractions (75%) is well-suited to the spatial-proteoform question and avoids the usual pitfall of comparing non-overlapping compartment proteomes.

## Weaknesses
- "Initiating features of AD" claim (HARD). The abstract states the six-protein overlap "implicat[es] vesicle transport and proteostasis disruption as initiating features of AD." Three problems. First, "initiating" is causal language from an observational AD-vs-WT comparison at a single timepoint — association at an early age does not establish initiation. Second, "pre-pathological" is contradicted by the authors' own ELISA, which shows Abeta42 present at 2 months (five-fold less than at 5 months, but present). Third, the six-protein overlap is reported without an enrichment statistic: with ~1,900 human changes and several hundred mouse 2-month changes, six overlaps could be chance. The authors should report the sizes of both compared sets and an overlap test (e.g., hypergeometric), and requalify "initiating" to something like "associated with early pathology."
- "Primary determinant of disease vulnerability" claim (SOFT). The abstract says 78% fraction-specific changes are "demonstrat[ive] that subcellular localization is a primary determinant of disease vulnerability." The descriptive finding is solid, but the mechanistic interpretation is not established. The alternative the authors themselves invoke for P3 — that fraction-specific changes reflect protein aggregation/insolubility shifting proteins between fractions — would not support "localization as a determinant of vulnerability" but rather "aggregation as a determinant." The claim conflates a descriptive observation (changes are fraction-specific) with a mechanistic one (localization determines vulnerability). "Demonstrating" should be softened to "consistent with" or the aggregation alternative explicitly excluded.
- "Revealed retromer complex mislocalization, nuclear transport dysfunction" (SOFT). The VPS35 data (total unchanged + fraction redistribution) supports mislocalization as one explanation, but the aggregation/insolubility alternative is not excluded — the authors invoke this alternative for other non-nuclear P3/S2 discord proteins, and VPS35 is non-nuclear. The NCT claim is indirect: Ran GTPase binding proteins up in P3 and down in S2 are consistent with NCT dysfunction but could reflect other processes, and the body hedges ("may be mislocalized") where the abstract does not. The abstract's "revealed" overstates what the data shows.
- "Protein degradation is altered by Abeta pathology" (SOFT). The QUAD data shows Day7/Day0 differences between genotypes, but the authors acknowledge the relocalization confound. The opposite stability changes between S2 and P3 at 12 months (faster in AD S2, slower in AD P3) are exactly what relocalization of insoluble proteins during the 7-day chase would produce. The abstract's degradation claim should carry the same hedge the body carries.
- "This corresponds to emerging evidence of non-canonical CCT chaperonin structures and indicates they are differentially regulated in AD" — "indicates" is over-strong; different subunits in different fractions with different regulation is consistent with, but does not demonstrate, non-canonical structures.
- "produced by alterations in protein degradation" (discussion) — causal language from a correlational cross-dataset comparison; "associated with" would match the design.
- "invisible to bulk tissue approaches" — the VPS35 example supports this, but "invisible" is absolute; the signal is invisible to *this* bulk approach, not all.
- The "spatial proteoforms" framing conflates localization with aggregation state — a protein sedimenting into P3 because it is aggregated is not a "spatial proteoform" in the functional sense the term implies; this conflation underlies several of the over-claims above.

## Questions
- What are the sizes of the human-change and 2-month mouse-change sets, and does the six-protein overlap exceed chance expectation (hypergeometric or similar)?
- How is "pre-pathological" defined, given that Abeta42 is detectable at 2 months in the authors' own ELISA?
- For the VPS35 "mislocalization" claim, is there any evidence distinguishing redistribution from aggregation/insolubility — e.g., a solubility assay or native fractionation on the S2 and P3 pools?
