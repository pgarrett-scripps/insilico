# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
This is a substantial and well-designed spatial proteomics study whose headline quantitative claims are not yet supported by the statistics as presented. The 78% fraction-specificity claim rests on uncorrected p<0.05 thresholds across ~6,000 proteins in four fractions; the QUAD degradation comparisons treat each protein as an independent observation when the biological unit of replication is four animals per group; and the six "initiating" proteins at 2 months are an overlap of two uncorrected-significance sets never tested against its null expectation. All three are fixable with reanalysis, and the underlying data and design are worth the effort.

## Strengths
- The four-fraction design is a genuine methodological advance over bulk-tissue proteomics, and the authors exploit it to reveal discordant patterns (e.g., VPS35) that bulk analysis would miss.
- The QUAD design is sound in principle, and the Day0 pooling and replicate structure are disclosed rather than hidden.
- The human–mouse cross-dataset comparison is a thoughtful strategy for identifying early drivers, and the authors correctly note the mouse model's lack of tau pathology as a limitation.

## Weaknesses
- Pseudo-replication in the QUAD genotype comparisons (HARD). Figure 8F states "Each circle represents the log2 transformation of the Day7/Day0 ratio for one protein" and reports p-values from one-way ANOVA with Tukey's test. This treats each of the hundreds-to-thousands of proteins as an independent observation, but all proteins in a genotype come from only 4 pooled biological replicates (each pooled from one male and one female mouse). Proteins within an animal are correlated through shared animal-level effects, so the effective sample size for the genotype comparison is 4 per group, not the number of proteins plotted. The "**** < 0.0001" values are almost certainly inflated by this. The same issue affects Figure 8E (proteins as observations in the age comparison). What would settle it: fit a mixed-effects model with animal as a random effect, or compute a per-animal summary (e.g., median log-ratio across proteins) and test with n=4 per group; report whether the direction and significance of the genotype differences survive.
- Uncorrected multiple testing in the human ANOVA undermines the 78% fraction-specificity claim (HARD). The text states "ANOVA was performed in ProteomeDiscoverer 2.5" and the volcano plots (Figure 4A) use a p<0.05 threshold, with no multiple-comparison correction named. With ~6,000 proteins tested per fraction across four fractions, the expected number of false positives at p<0.05 is roughly 300 per fraction. These noise hits would be distributed essentially at random across fractions, inflating the apparent fraction-specificity that is the paper's central human finding. The 2,405 "significant changes" and the 78% figure are computed on this uncorrected set. What would settle it: recompute the significant sets with a named FDR correction (e.g., Benjamini–Hochberg q<0.05) per fraction, and report the 78% fraction-specificity statistic against the corrected set alongside the uncorrected one.
- The six "initiating" proteins at 2 months are not tested against their null expectation (HARD). The overlap of BIN1, NSFL1C, HSPA12A, CACNA2D3, DNAJA4, and PHYHIPL comes from the intersection of two sets each filtered at uncorrected p<0.05. If N proteins were tested in both datasets, the expected number of false overlaps is N × α₁ × α₂; for N≈1,000 and α=0.05 in each, that is ~2.5 expected by chance, and 6 observed is only marginally above that. The claim that these represent "initiating features of AD" outruns this evidence. What would settle it: report N (the number of proteins quantified in both datasets at 2 months), compute the expected overlap under the null, and test the observed 6 against it (e.g., permutation of labels or a hypergeometric test using the corrected hit lists).
- "No difference" claim at 12 months in P3 (HARD). The text states "there was no difference observed between the genotypes at 12 months" in the P3 fraction. With n=4 animals per group, this is an unpowered claim of absence; the non-significant result cannot support "no difference." What would settle it: report the smallest effect detectable with n=4 at the observed variance, or reframe as "no difference was detected."
- Day0 pooling (SOFT). The Day7/Day0 ratio divides each of the 4 Day7 replicates by a single pooled Day0 measurement per genotype and timepoint. If the Day0 pool is atypical for a protein, all four ratios for that protein shift together, adding correlated noise that the protein-as-observation analysis does not model. This does not create a genotype difference (both genotypes have pooled Day0), but it inflates protein-level variance and should be acknowledged.
- WGCNA module testing (SOFT). Sixteen to twenty-five modules per fraction are tested for AD vs. ND differences at p<0.05 with no correction named. With ~20 modules per fraction, ~1 false-positive module per fraction is expected.
- Figure 8E test specification (SOFT). The caption reports "one-way ANOVA with Tukey's multiple comparison test" but does not state the grouping — is this a 2-group paired comparison (2-month vs 12-month ratios for the same proteins) or a 4-group comparison including genotype? For a paired two-group comparison, a paired test on protein-level ratios would be the natural choice; the current description is ambiguous.
- Tau-correlated proteins (SOFT). The r>0.7 threshold for Tau-correlated proteins is stated with no p-value or multiple-comparison correction; with n=13–14 samples, r>0.7 is nominally significant, but the 124 proteins identified across fractions are not corrected for the number of correlations tested.
- CV<50% filter in Figures 8G/H (SOFT). The exclusion of proteins with >50% CV is not justified; if high-CV proteins are preferentially excluded in one genotype or fraction, this could bias the median comparisons. A sensitivity analysis with the filter relaxed would address this.

## Questions
- What is the exact grouping in the Figure 8E ANOVA — is the 2-month vs 12-month comparison paired on protein identity, and is genotype included as a factor?
- How many proteins were quantified in both the human and mouse datasets at the 2-month timepoint (the denominator N for the six-protein overlap)?
- Was any FDR correction applied within ProteomeDiscoverer for the human ANOVA, and if so, what was it and what threshold was used?
