# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
This manuscript presents a large-scale spatial proteomics study of Alzheimer's disease combining human post-mortem hippocampi fractionation with mouse QUAD degradation analysis. The statistical framework is generally sound—appropriate use of ANOVA, WGCNA, and correlation analyses with multiple-comparison corrections applied in most contexts. However, several critical issues undermine confidence in the quantitative claims: (1) multiple statistical tests are performed without consistently disclosed correction thresholds; (2) sample sizes are modest (n=13–14 human subjects) with no power analysis or effect-size reporting; (3) key findings rest on post-hoc comparisons between fractions without pre-specification; (4) the human–mouse concordance analysis (Figure 9E) uses a stipulated mathematical relationship rather than an independent validation; and (5) error bars and raw data points are often absent from figures, obscuring distributional properties. The work is methodologically ambitious and addresses an important question, but the statistical reporting lacks the rigor and transparency needed for confident interpretation of the spatial proteoform hypothesis.

## Strengths
- WGCNA and ANOVA are appropriate unsupervised and supervised methods for the proteomics data; module eigenprotein p-values are reported (Figure 2E, Supplementary Table 4).
- Multiple-comparison correction is applied in GO enrichment analyses (5% FDR filter stated in Methods and figure legends, e.g., Figure 3A, Supplementary Table 5).
- Tau correlation analysis with Braak score (Figure 5C) reports Pearson r and p-values for each fraction separately, with a reasonable threshold (r > 0.7) pre-specified for Tau regulator identification.
- Immunoblot validation of key findings (VPS35, Tau, ubiquitin) provides orthogonal support for LC-MS measurements.
- QUAD degradation analysis includes appropriate controls (Day0 vs Day7 ratios, WT vs AD comparisons across three timepoints) and mitochondrial protein stratification (Figure 8G–H).
- Human–mouse dataset overlap (Figure 9E, Supplementary Table 17) attempts to link mechanistic findings across models, with GO enrichment on correlated proteins.

## Weaknesses
- **HARD: Multiple-comparison correction inconsistently applied or disclosed.** ANOVA in ProteomeDiscoverer (Figure 4A, Supplementary Table 6) reports p < 0.05 without stating whether correction (e.g., Bonferroni, FDR) was applied. The text states 'ANOVA was performed in ProteomeDiscoverer 2.5' but does not specify the multiple-testing adjustment. With 6,123 proteins tested per fraction, uncorrected p < 0.05 would yield ~300 false positives per fraction. The claim that '78% of significant changes were specific to one fraction' (Figure 4C) depends critically on this threshold but the correction status is ambiguous.
- **HARD: No sample-size justification or power analysis.** The human cohort is n=13 AD and n=14 non-AD (Table S1), with 80% of controls being asymptomatic AD (AsymAD). No a priori power calculation is provided. For a typical proteomics effect size (Cohen's d ≈ 0.8–1.0), n=13–14 per group provides ~60–75% power at α=0.05; the study is underpowered for small-to-moderate effects. No post-hoc power or effect-size confidence intervals are reported for key findings (e.g., Tau fold-change in P3, VPS35 fold-change).
- **HARD: Effect sizes and confidence intervals absent.** Volcano plots (Figure 4A) show log2 fold-change on the x-axis but no error bars, CIs, or effect-size estimates are provided in the text or tables. Supplementary Table 6 lists p-values but not fold-changes or SEM/SD. For example, the claim that APP is 'significantly altered only in P3' (Figure 4F) lacks a quantitative effect size or CI.
- **HARD: Post-hoc fraction comparisons without pre-specification.** The discord analysis (Figure 7A–D) identifies proteins with opposite patterns between P3 and S2 post-hoc. No pre-registered hypothesis or Bonferroni correction for the number of fraction-pair comparisons is disclosed. The enrichment of Ran GTPase binding (10-fold, p < 7.7 × 10⁻⁴, FDR < 0.05, Figure 7B) is reported with FDR correction, but the number of GO terms tested is not stated, making the FDR threshold interpretation unclear.
- **HARD: Human–mouse concordance analysis uses stipulated mathematical relationship, not independent validation.** Figure 9E states: 'the correlated proteins should mathematically have opposite trends in the mouse and human analyses.' This is a post-hoc stipulation, not a pre-registered hypothesis. The authors then identify 155 proteins meeting this criterion (27 P3, 128 S2) but do not report how many proteins would be expected by chance under a random model. Without a null distribution, the significance of the overlap is uninterpretable. The claim that six proteins altered at 2-month (pre-pathological) timepoint 'implicat[e] vesicle transport and proteostasis disruption as initiating features' (Abstract) rests on this unvalidated concordance.
- **HARD: Correlation analysis (Tau regulators, Figure 5D) lacks multiple-comparison correction.** The authors identify 124 proteins correlating with Tau (r > 0.7) across fractions and samples. With 6,123 proteins tested, an r > 0.7 threshold at α=0.05 would yield ~300 false positives by chance. No Bonferroni or FDR correction is applied to the correlation p-values. The claim that 15 of 124 are 'previously reported as Tau regulators' is not tested against a null model of random overlap.
- **HARD: QUAD degradation analysis lacks explicit statistical test description for Day7/Day0 ratio comparisons.** The Methods state 'Student t-test determined the significance protein differences between Day7/Day0 WT ratios and Day7/Day0 AD ratios' and 'ratios were log2 transformed prior to statistical analysis.' However, Figure 8F and 9A use ANOVA ('one-way ANOVA with Tukey's multiple comparison test'), not t-tests. The discrepancy between Methods and Results is unresolved. Additionally, Tukey's correction is appropriate for multiple timepoint/fraction comparisons, but the family size (number of comparisons per protein) is not stated.
- **HARD: Figure 8E and 9A volcano plots lack error bars and individual data points.** The y-axis is -log10(p-value) and x-axis is log2 fold-change, but no confidence intervals, SEM, or raw points are shown. For n=4 biological replicates per condition (stated in Methods), individual points should be overlaid to reveal distributional properties and outliers. The absence of raw data obscures whether significance is driven by one or two extreme values.
- **SOFT: Hydrophobicity analysis (Figure 9D, S4A) uses GRAVY score without justification or validation.** The claim that 'proteins in P3 were more hydrophobic' is supported by GRAVY score comparison but no statistical test (t-test, Mann–Whitney U) is reported. The distribution of GRAVY scores is not shown, and it is unclear whether the difference is practically meaningful or driven by a few outliers.
- **SOFT: Ubiquitin immunoblot (Figure S4B) quantified but not statistically tested.** The text states 'we observed a large increase in ubiquitinated proteins in P3 compared to S2' but no p-value, n, or error bar is provided. The figure shows a single representative blot without replication data.
- **SOFT: Braak score correlation (Figure 5C) uses only AD samples (n=13).** The correlation is computed on a small sample with potential non-independence if multiple subjects share the same Braak stage. No adjustment for multiple fractions (3 tested: P3, S3, S2) is disclosed, though the p-values suggest only P3 and S3 are significant.
- **SOFT: Missing exact p-values.** Many results report 'p < 0.05' or 'p < 0.0001' without exact values (e.g., Figure 5C, Figure 7B). Exact p-values improve reproducibility and allow readers to assess practical significance.

## Questions
- In Figure 4A (ANOVA volcano plots), was a multiple-comparison correction (Bonferroni, FDR, or other) applied to the p-values before filtering at p < 0.05? If so, which correction and what was the corrected threshold? If not, how many false positives are expected among the 2,405 reported significant changes?
- For the human cohort (n=13 AD, n=14 non-AD), was a priori power analysis performed? What effect size (Cohen's d or fold-change) was assumed, and what power was achieved? Are post-hoc effect-size estimates and 95% CIs available for key findings (e.g., Tau, VPS35, APP fold-changes)?
- In the QUAD analysis (Figure 9A), the Methods mention 'Student t-test' but Results describe 'one-way ANOVA with Tukey's multiple comparison test.' Which test was actually used? For ANOVA, how many comparisons per protein were made (i.e., what is the family size for Tukey correction)?
- For the human–mouse concordance analysis (Figure 9E), how many proteins would be expected to show opposite trends by chance if proteins were randomly assigned up/down in each dataset? What is the null distribution, and is the observed overlap (155 proteins) significantly greater than expected?
- In Figure 5D (Tau correlators), 124 proteins with r > 0.7 were identified across ~6,123 proteins tested. Was a multiple-comparison correction (Bonferroni or FDR) applied to the correlation p-values? How many false positives are expected at r > 0.7 and α=0.05 without correction?
- For the six proteins altered at 2-month pre-pathological timepoint (BIN1, NSFL1C, HSPA12A, CACNA2D3, DNAJA4, PHYHIPL), what is the statistical support for calling these 'early proteomic perturbations'? Were these proteins pre-specified, or identified post-hoc from the human–mouse overlap? What is the multiple-comparison-corrected p-value threshold?
- In Figure 8F and 9A, why are individual data points (n=4 replicates per condition) not shown? Can the authors provide raw Day7/Day0 ratios for each replicate to assess whether significance is driven by outliers or is robust across replicates?
- For the GO enrichment analyses (e.g., Figure 3A, 7B), how many GO terms were tested in total, and was the 5% FDR threshold applied to the full family or to a pre-filtered subset? Are the reported FDR values adjusted for the total number of terms tested across all fractions?
- In Supplementary Table 9 (Tau correlators), are the reported p-values for r > 0.7 one-sided or two-sided? Were these p-values corrected for multiple comparisons across 6,123 proteins?
- For the immunoblot quantifications (Tau, VPS35, ubiquitin), how many biological replicates were analyzed, and are error bars (SD or SEM) provided? Are the quantifications shown in the figures representative or averaged across replicates?
