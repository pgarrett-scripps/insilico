# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
This is an ambitious and well-designed spatial proteomics study, but the headline quantitative claims are not currently supported by the statistics as reported. The primary human ANOVA and the mouse QUAD comparisons both lack a stated multiple-testing correction, and the mouse degradation analysis treats thousands of proteins as independent observations when the mouse (n=4) is the unit of replication. The 78% fraction-specificity claim and the human–mouse overlap counts are reported without null expectations. The VPS35 immunoblot validation is a genuine strength and shows the discordant-fraction approach can find real biology, but FDR correction, permutation nulls, and mouse-level analysis are needed before the claims can be trusted.

## Strengths
- Orthogonal validation of the key discordant finding (VPS35 immunoblot, Figure 7D) — exactly the check the statistics alone cannot provide.
- The fractionation-before-TMT design is a real advance over unfractionated analyses, and the paper is transparent about its thresholds (p<0.05 stated explicitly), which makes the missing corrections visible.
- The QUAD pulse-chase design is clearly described, including the Day0 pooling and log2 transformation before testing.

## Weaknesses
- (HARD) The primary human ANOVA is thresholded at p<0.05 with no multiple-testing correction stated. With roughly 4,500–6,000 proteins tested per fraction, α=0.05 yields ~225–300 expected false positives per fraction, or ~900–1,200 across the four fractions — up to ~40% of the 2,405 reported changes. The 78% fraction-specificity claim and the discordant P3/S2 enrichment are both built on this hit list. Settle it: apply Benjamini–Hochberg FDR (or state the correction if ProteomeDiscoverer applied one), report how many of the 2,405 changes survive, and recompute the 78% and the discord enrichment on the filtered list.
- (HARD) The mouse degradation comparison (Figure 8F) treats proteins as the unit of replication. Each circle is a protein ratio, but proteins within a mouse are correlated; the effective sample size for "proteins in AD brains degraded significantly faster" is 4 mice per group, not thousands of proteins, so the ANOVA p-values are anti-conservative. Additionally, the Day7/Day0 ratio divides by a single pooled Day0 sample per genotype — a single measured denominator whose error is not propagated, so any bias in the pool shifts every ratio for that genotype. Settle it: fit a mixed model with mouse as a random effect, or compare per-mouse median log2 ratios between genotypes (n=4 per group).
- (HARD) The 78% fraction-specificity claim has no null expectation. A protein quantified in k fractions has k chances to cross the threshold, and abundances across fractions from the same brain are correlated, so the chance rate of single-fraction hits is not simply 0.05. The paper reports that 89% of altered proteins were quantified in multiple fractions but never computes how many single-fraction hits would occur by chance. Settle it: a permutation test (shuffle AD/NA labels, recompute per-fraction ANOVA, count single-fraction hits) or an analytic null based on the observed distribution of k.
- (HARD) The human–mouse overlaps (155 measurements at 12 months; 6 proteins at 2 months) are intersections of two uncorrected p<0.05 filters, reported as counts without a chance expectation. With the marginal hit rates in each dataset, the expected overlap is substantial and is not computed; the "initiating features" interpretation of the 6-protein set requires showing it exceeds chance. The interpretation also assumes steady-state abundance with unchanged synthesis, which is not tested. Settle it: a hypergeometric test or permutation that preserves the marginal hit rates and the opposite-sign constraint.
