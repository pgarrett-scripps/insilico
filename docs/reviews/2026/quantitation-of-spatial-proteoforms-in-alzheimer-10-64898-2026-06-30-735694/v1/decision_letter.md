# Decision Letter

**Decision:** major

## Summary of Evaluation
The panel's numerical signal (3.20/5, 6 major / 2 minor) and the specialist reports converge on a clear diagnosis: this is a substantial, technically ambitious, and genuinely novel dataset whose core descriptive findings are real, but whose headline mechanistic claims outrun the evidence. The strongest fair defense (advocate) is that every HARD issue is fixable by reanalysis or requalification rather than new experiments; the skeptic's counter is that requalification is not cosmetic — it demotes the paper's stated conclusions to descriptive findings. I find the skeptic's point decisive on the load-bearing claims: the 78% fraction-specificity headline may not survive FDR correction (up to ~40% of the 2,405 changes could be false positives at α=0.05), the P3/S2 'mislocalization' claim is confounded by insolubility (P3 is the NP-40-insoluble pellet, and the authors' own hydrophobicity/ubiquitination/insoluble-overlap data favor that reading), and the six-protein 'initiating features' claim is untested against chance in mice carrying mutations from conception. These are not wording changes; they determine what the paper claims. The descriptive contribution — a four-fraction human AD hippocampus TMT dataset plus three-age QUAD degradation in the same two fractions, with orthogonal VPS35 immunoblot validation — stands and is worth publishing after the load-bearing claims are either supported or explicitly demoted. The ethics gaps are documentation-only (funding, COI, ethics approval, data availability) and the citation audits found no HARD gaps and no retracted sources. Because fixing the central claims requires reanalysis (FDR, mixed-model mouse statistics, permutation nulls) whose outcome could change a conclusion, the verdict is major, not minor.

## Required Revisions
1. Apply Benjamini–Hochberg FDR (or state the correction if ProteomeDiscoverer applied one) to the human per-fraction ANOVA; report how many of the 2,405 changes survive; recompute the 78% fraction-specificity figure and the discordant P3/S2 enrichment on the filtered list. If the 78% figure does not survive, demote the 'subcellular localization is a primary determinant of disease vulnerability' claim accordingly.
2. For the QUAD mouse comparisons (Figure 8F, Figure 9A), fit a mixed model with mouse as a random effect, or compare per-mouse median log2 ratios between genotypes (n=4 per group), rather than treating thousands of proteins as independent observations. State the number of mice per Day0 pool and how TMT normalization and missing values were handled.
3. Run permutation or analytic nulls for (a) the 78% fraction-specificity claim, (b) the 155-measurement human–mouse overlap at 12 months, and (c) the six-protein overlap at 2 months, preserving marginal hit rates and the opposite-sign constraint. Report whether each overlap exceeds chance; if not, demote the 'initiating features' interpretation.
4. Requalify the 'mislocalization' (VPS35/retromer) and 'nuclear transport dysfunction' (Ran GTPase-binding proteins) claims to acknowledge that the S2→P3 shift is equally consistent with insolubility/aggregation, which the authors' own hydrophobicity, ubiquitination, and 37-protein insoluble-overlap data favor. The VPS35 immunoblot confirms the abundance pattern, not the mechanism; either add an orthogonal assay that does not depend on pelleting (e.g., immunofluorescence) or state the insolubility alternative as the primary reading.
5. Reconcile the arithmetic in the QUAD results (238 more-stable + 1,323 less-stable = 1,561, which does not match the stated 1,436 altered proteins) and report the correct numbers.
6. Add the required documentation statements: (a) human tissue ethics approval (committee, protocol number, consent basis); (b) IACUC protocol number and approving institution; (c) funding disclosure; (d) competing-interests declaration (or explicit 'none'); (e) data availability statement with raw MS data deposition (PRIDE/MassIVE accession) and confirmation that consent terms permit deposition; (f) code availability for custom analyses.
7. Specify the exact thresholds and filtering criteria for the '155 protein measurements' correlation and the 'six proteins' overlap between human and mouse datasets, and for the Tau correlation analysis (r>0.7 threshold, multiple-testing consideration).
8. Address the AsymAD composition of the control group: 11 of 14 NA samples have AD pathology, so the comparison is a severity gradient (dementia vs. no-dementia, both with plaques/tangles), not a clean contrast; scale the interpretation that findings reflect changes 'underlying AD dementia rather than mechanisms triggered by Abeta plaques or NFT' accordingly.
9. Report whether the 78% fraction-specific changes are concentrated among proteins detected in only one fraction; if so, the claim collapses and must be requalified. Also report, for proteins significant in one fraction but quantified in others, whether the non-significant fraction effect sizes are in the same direction but below significance (measurement artifact) or genuinely absent.
10. State the ANOVA parameters (normalization, correction, threshold) and the mass-spec search parameters (mass tolerances) explicitly; add software versions for MetaNetwork, Webgestalt, PACOM, and Cytoscape; specify the NP-40 buffer volume for the P1 extraction.
11. Confirm the load-bearing citations flagged as unverifiable: (a) that Bai et al. 2021 reanalyzes exactly seven TMT datasets and that VPS35 is among the proteins it reports as unchanged; (b) that the '>50% of proteins in multiple compartments' statistic is in Thul et al. 2017; (c) the correct published year/venue for Samelson et al. (cited as Cell 2026; a 2023 bioRxiv preprint exists); (d) the Hales 2016 entry; (e) the specific Rab2a detail in Samelson et al.; (f) the full bibliography resolves in the deposited preprint.
12. Provide a head-to-head statement of the delta over Bai 2020's multilayer fractionation and the cited insoluble-fraction studies (Bai 2013, Hales 2016), naming what this four-fraction design detects that those do not, to support the 'invisible to bulk tissue approaches' novelty claim.
13. Add a limitations section addressing: the single 7-day chase timepoint and Day0 pooling in QUAD; the untested assumption that degradation determines steady-state abundance (synthesis unmeasured); the unexplained 5-month reversal in degradation patterns; and the lack of cell-type composition correction in the human data.

## Minor Suggestions
- Add a citation for the 'emerging evidence of non-canonical CCT chaperonin structures' claim (Fig. 3D discussion).
- Cite the primary source (Neumann et al. 2006) for the TDP-43 nuclear-depletion observation rather than the Balendra 2025 review.
- Cite Fornasiero 2018 (or other non-QUAD reports) for the claim that protein degradation slows with age.
- Report the sex composition of the human donor cohort and acknowledge any sex imbalance as a potential confound.
- Add antibody details (clone, host, dilution, RRID) for the Tau, VPS35, and Ubiquitin immunoblots.
- Define error bars in all figure legends and state the number of biological replicates for each panel.
- Confirm the final reference list resolves cleanly (several DOIs are garbled in the parse, which is a converter artifact).
- Consider a direct within-study comparison of the pooled (unfractionated) samples against the fraction-specific results to make the 'invisible to bulk tissue' claim testable rather than asserted.
