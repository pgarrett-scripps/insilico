# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The citation record is largely sound: the load-bearing attributions I could check — Thul 2017 for multi-compartment localization, Small & Gandy 2006 and Young et al. 2018 for retromer–AD, Eftekharzadeh et al. 2018 for Tau–NUP98 NCT disruption, Freibaum 2015/Zhang 2015 for C9orf72 NCT, Fornasiero 2018 for long-lived mitochondrial proteins, and the authors' own QUAD method citations — all resolve to real sources that support the claims made. The weaknesses are concentrated in a few specific, verifiable points: the Bai 2021 meta-analysis is asked to carry a central "invisible to unfractionated analysis" claim whose exact numbers I could not confirm, one citation's year is suspect, and the novelty positioning rests on a completeness assumption I could not fully verify. None of these undermines the core findings, but each needs the authors' confirmation or a small addition.

## Strengths
- The load-bearing attributions I could check — Thul 2017 for multi-compartment localization, Small & Gandy 2006 and Young et al. 2018 for retromer–AD, Eftekharzadeh et al. 2018 for Tau–NUP98 NCT disruption, Freibaum 2015/Zhang 2015 for C9orf72 NCT, Fornasiero 2018 for long-lived mitochondrial proteins, and the authors' own QUAD method citations — all resolve to real sources that support the claims made.
- Self-citation is proportionate: the QUAD method and prior stability findings are the authors' own work and are cited appropriately (no issue).

## Weaknesses
- The VPS35 "invisible to unfractionated analysis" claim rests entirely on the Bai 2021 meta-analysis, whose exact contents I could not verify: I could not confirm from the available metadata that this source contains a reanalysis of exactly seven TMT datasets, that the 748/31% figure is correct, or that VPS35 is among the unchanged proteins. This is the single most load-bearing citation in the paper — the VPS35 example is the flagship demonstration that spatial analysis rescues disease-relevant signals — so the authors should confirm the exact source and numbers, or the claim should be softened to what the source actually supports.
- The novelty positioning ("spatial proteoforms... invisible to bulk tissue approaches") rests on a completeness assumption I could not verify: the claim that no directly competing subcellular-fractionation AD proteomics exists is asserted rather than demonstrated. My searches did not surface a directly competing study, so this is a SOFT completeness question rather than a HARD omission — but the authors should confirm they have not omitted prior subcellular or synaptic proteomics of AD brain (e.g., hippocampal proteome studies across AD stages) that would bear on the novelty claim.
- The foundational ">50% of proteins localize to more than one compartment" statistic, the premise for the entire spatial-proteoform framework, could not be verified in Thul 2017: the abstract/metadata of that paper supports multi-compartment localization broadly but I could not confirm the specific ">50%" figure; the authors should confirm the statistic is in that source (or cite the source that actually reports it).
- Samelson et al. is cited as "Cell 189, 1517-1534 e1519 (2026)" (ref 38), but my search surfaced the same work as a 2023 bioRxiv preprint; the published year/venue should be verified (HARD hygiene if the year is wrong).
- Balendra et al. 2025 (Lancet Neurol) is a review cited for the foundational TDP-43 nuclear-depletion observation; the primary source (Neumann et al. 2006) would be more appropriate for a specific empirical claim (SOFT).
- The claim "emerging evidence of non-canonical CCT chaperonin structures" (Fig. 3D discussion) is made without a citation — add one (SOFT).
- The claim that "protein degradation slows with age... many reports using various analytical methods" is made without citing the non-QUAD reports; Fornasiero 2018 is cited elsewhere and should be cited here (SOFT).
- Reference-list hygiene: several DOIs are garbled in the parse (e.g., ref 3, ref 4), which is a converter artifact, but the authors should confirm the final reference list resolves cleanly (SOFT).

## Questions
- Does Bai et al. 2021 reanalyze exactly seven TMT datasets, and is VPS35 among the proteins it reports as unchanged — the "invisible to unfractionated analysis" claim for VPS35 rests on this?
- Is the ">50% of proteins in multiple compartments" statistic actually in Thul et al. 2017, or does it come from another source?
- What is the correct published year/venue for Samelson et al. (cited as Cell 2026; a 2023 bioRxiv preprint of the same work exists)?
