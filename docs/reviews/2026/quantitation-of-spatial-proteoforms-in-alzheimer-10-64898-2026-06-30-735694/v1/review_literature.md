# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The citation record is largely sound — the core methods and AD-proteomics anchors (Bai 2020, Johnson 2020, Wang 2021, Fornasiero 2018, Thul 2017) are real and appropriately attributed, and self-citation is proportionate. The main defects are an unsupported literature claim with no source, and a directly-competing prior work on protein turnover in AD mouse models that is not cited. Both are fixable, but the second bears on the novelty of the QUAD/human-mouse comparison and should be addressed before publication.

## Weaknesses
- **Missing directly-competing prior work on AD protein turnover.** The QUAD section measures degradation in APPswePS1delta9 cortex at 2/5/12 months and cross-compares to human brain. Yarbro et al., *Nature Communications* 2025, "Human and mouse proteomics reveals the shared pathways in Alzheimer's disease and delayed protein turnover in the amyloidome" (PMID 39934151), does precisely this design space — human+mouse AD proteomics with protein-turnover measurements. It is not in the reference list. Because it directly overlaps the degradation and human-mouse claims, this is a must-cite (HARD); the authors should state how their fraction-resolved QUAD results differ from it.
- **Unsupported literature claim with no citation.** In the WGCNA section: "This corresponds to emerging evidence of non-canonical CCT chaperonin structures and indicates they are dierentially regulated in AD." No source is given for the "emerging evidence," so the claim cannot be checked. My searches did not surface a specific "non-canonical CCT" literature; either cite the source or drop the phrase (HARD — a claim about prior work with no traceable origin).
- **Two specific attributions I could not verify from metadata.** (a) "A meta-analysis of seven proteomic TMT datasets… confirmed 748 (31%) AD changes in our dataset" (ref 27, Bai 2021) — the paper exists but the specific 748/31% figure is not checkable from the abstract; (b) "Rab2a was identified as a regulator of Tau oligomers in genome-wide CRISPRi screen in iPSC-derived neurons" (ref 38, Samelson 2026, PMID 41610849) — the paper exists but I could not confirm Rab2a specifically from metadata. Both are plausible; please confirm the specific claims against the cited sources.
