# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript presents a comprehensive spatial proteomics study of Alzheimer's disease with a generally solid citation record and appropriate foundational references. Major AD proteomics studies (Bai, Johnson, Wang) and spatial proteomics work (Thul 2017) are well-cited, and mechanistic claims are properly sourced. However, two specific issues require clarification: a potential duplicate citation of Eftekharzadeh et al. on Tau and nucleocytoplasmic transport (refs 45 and 86 appear to cite the same work with conflicting page numbers), and an unsupported generalization that APP is "typically not altered" in unfractionated LC-MS studies (attributed to ref 82). Additionally, the manuscript could be strengthened by discussing why traditional fractionation was chosen over emerging spatial proteomics methods and by more comprehensively integrating recent literature on chaperonin dysfunction, retromer biology, and mitochondrial dynamics in AD. These issues are fixable and do not undermine the core contribution, but verification is needed before acceptance.

## Strengths
- Foundational references well-placed: Thul et al. (2017) on subcellular proteome mapping is appropriately cited as motivation for spatial analysis; Bai et al. (2020), Johnson et al. (2020), and Wang et al. (2021) are correctly identified as major prior AD proteomics studies.
- Mechanistic claims properly sourced: The introduction correctly attributes endosomal-lysosomal pathway dysfunction as a preclinical AD hallmark (refs 77–79 verified via search); nuclear-cytoplasmic transport (NCT) disruption by Tau is appropriately cited to Eftekharzadeh et al. (2018, Neuron); C9orf72 and TDP-43 examples in ALS are standard references.
- AD risk factor citations accurate: The 11 AD risk factors listed (ANK3, APOE, APP, BIN1, CTSB, CLU, COX7C, GRN, FAK, FERMT, SNX1) are supported by cited references; BIN1 endocytosis role (refs 65–67) and FERMT2 Tau regulation (ref 40) are correctly attributed.
- Methodological precedent established: QUAD methodology is properly credited to McClatchy et al. (2020, Sci Rep, ref 26), with the preprint version (ref 26, 2019) also noted; click chemistry and AHA labeling are appropriately referenced.
- Tau pathology correlation well-sourced: The Braak staging system (ref 32) and high-molecular-weight Tau aggregation (refs 29–31) are correctly cited; the correlation analysis approach is sound.

## Weaknesses
- Incomplete citation of Eftekharzadeh NCT work: The manuscript cites Eftekharzadeh et al. (2018, ref 45) for "Tau Protein Disrupts Nucleocytoplasmic Transport in Alzheimer's Disease" but also includes a second Eftekharzadeh citation (ref 86) with identical title and different page numbers (Neuron 99: 925–940 vs. Neuron 101: 349). The 2018 Neuron paper (PMID:30189205) is the primary source; ref 86 cites "Neuron 101: 349 (2019)" which does not match standard indexing, suggesting a duplicate or version error that requires clarification.
- Claim about APP alterations in unfractionated analysis needs verification: The manuscript states that "APP is typically not altered in unfractionated LC-MS analysis unless only Abeta peptides are targeted for quantification (ref 82)." This is a strong generalization attributed to Seyfried et al. (2017, Cell Syst), but the specific statement supporting this claim requires verification against the cited source.
- Potential omission of directly competing spatial proteomics work: The manuscript emphasizes that "spatial proteoforms are rarely investigated with unbiased proteomic studies" but does not discuss why traditional fractionation was chosen over emerging spatial methods (e.g., proximity-dependent biotin labeling, spatial transcriptomics-proteomics integration) or compare against related work in spatial/subcellular proteomics in neurodegeneration.
- Citation currency in protein folding/chaperonin area: The manuscript identifies differential regulation of CCT chaperonin subunits across fractions but the broader literature on chaperonin dysfunction in AD is not extensively reviewed. The foundational reference (ref 25, Vainberg et al. 1998) is dated, and more recent reviews on proteostasis in AD could better contextualize the chaperonin findings.
- Retromer complex literature could be more comprehensive: The VPS35 mislocalization finding is a key result, but the broader recent literature on retromer and endosomal-lysosomal dysfunction in AD could be better integrated to show how VPS35 mislocalization fits into the larger narrative, particularly given the emphasis on endo-lysosomal pathways (refs 77–79).
- Mitochondrial trafficking and mitophagy literature: The Discussion proposes that mitochondrial proteins accumulate in S2 due to impaired mitophagy (refs 89–92), but the authors could strengthen this section by citing more recent work on mitochondrial dynamics in AD, which is an active area of research.

## Questions
- Can you clarify the discrepancy between refs 45 and 86, both attributed to Eftekharzadeh et al. on Tau and nucleocytoplasmic transport? Are these the same paper with different page numbers, or is one a correction/erratum?
- Does Seyfried et al. (2017, ref 82) actually state that APP is "typically not altered" in unfractionated LC-MS studies, or is this an inference? Can you provide the specific statement from that paper supporting this claim?
- Why was traditional differential centrifugation chosen over emerging spatial proteomics methods (e.g., proximity labeling, spatial transcriptomics)? Are there published spatial proteomics studies in AD or neurodegeneration that should be discussed as alternatives or complements to your approach?
- The meta-analysis (Bai et al. 2021, ref 27) found 31% overlap with your dataset. Did that meta-analysis report whether APP was altered in the unfractionated studies, and if so, in what direction? This would clarify whether your finding of APP alteration only in P3 truly represents a "hidden" signal.
- You propose that VPS35 mislocalization may reflect protein aggregation or binding to insoluble proteins. Have you considered whether the P3/S2 discord pattern could reflect differential degradation of VPS35 itself between compartments, and does the QUAD data support this?
