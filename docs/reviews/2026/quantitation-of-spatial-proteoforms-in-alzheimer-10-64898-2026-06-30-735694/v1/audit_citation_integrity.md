# Citation Integrity Auditor

## Summary
The manuscript uses ~102 numbered references. The PDF converter provided only entries [2]–[25]; entries [1] and [26]–[102] are absent due to truncation. A subset of references was verified via search tools before budget exhaustion. Categories triggered: Reference resolvability, Claim–citation support, Quotation/number fidelity, Self-citation / citation inflation, Retracted / predatory sources. All checked venues (Science, Neuron, PNAS, Nature, Nat Med, etc.) are legitimate. The primary concern is the truncated reference list, which prevents full verification of load-bearing citations for claims in the Results and Discussion sections. No HARD integrity violations were detected. I recommend requesting the complete bibliography from the authors before finalizing the audit. The self-citations (McClatchy et al., Carr et al., Martinez-Bartolome et al.) are all germane to the methodology employed; no padding or non-germane self-citation was detected. Verifiable references (Thul et al. 2017, Eftekharzadeh et al. 2018, Bai et al. 2013) appropriately support their attributed claims. DOI formatting artifacts from PDF conversion were noted as a soft concern. Key questions remain for ref [1] (epidemiological claim of 6.7 million likely from 2025 Alzheimer's Association report) and ref [27] (meta-analysis of seven TMT datasets claiming 748/31% AD changes). The authors should confirm the complete bibliography and correct reference numbering. Supporting the claim with exact figures was plausible but not independently verified from full text. No problems were found with the verified references (Eftekharzadeh et al. 2018, Bai et al. 2013). The self-citation assessment was clean. The meta-analysis claim (748/31%) attributed to ref [27] remains unverifiable from supplied materials. The overall picture is that the reference list completeness is the main barrier to a full citation integrity audit. Soft concerns include DOI formatting issues and the unverifiable exact percentage from Thul et al. The non-verifiable items (refs [1] and [27]) require author confirmation. No HARD gaps were identified.
