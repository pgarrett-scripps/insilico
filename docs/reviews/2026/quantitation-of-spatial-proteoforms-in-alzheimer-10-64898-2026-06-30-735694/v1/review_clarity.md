# Clarity & Presentation Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript is well-organized overall and generally clear in its presentation of a complex spatial proteomics study. The central hypothesis—that subcellular localization is a primary determinant of AD vulnerability—is stated explicitly and the narrative flow from motivation through human data to mouse validation is coherent. However, there are several presentation issues that create friction: inconsistent terminology for sample groups (AD vs. NA vs. ND), some figures lack sufficient detail in legends to stand alone, and quantitative claims occasionally lack specificity. These are largely fixable soft issues that do not block understanding but would benefit from revision.

## Strengths
- The central claim is stated clearly in the abstract and introduction: 'subcellular localization is a primary determinant of disease vulnerability' (Abstract, Results).
- The manuscript follows a logical narrative arc: motivation → human fractionation analysis → mouse degradation study → cross-dataset comparison, with each section's purpose clearly signaled.
- Methods are presented in sufficient order and detail to reconstruct the experimental workflow; the fractionation protocol, TMT labeling, and QUAD strategy are described with step-by-step clarity.
- Figures are generally well-referenced in text and legends define most symbols and abbreviations (e.g., Figure 2D legend explains color coding by fraction).
- Key findings are quantified with actual numbers: '78% of significant changes were specific to one fraction' (Results), '75% of proteins detected in more than one fraction' (Results), enabling precise claims.
- The abstract is intelligible standalone and introduces all key terms before use (spatial proteoforms, TMT-LC-MS, QUAD).
- Supplementary tables are comprehensive and well-indexed to figures and text sections.

## Weaknesses
-  Inconsistent terminology for control samples: the manuscript uses 'non-AD' (NA), 'non-demented' (ND), 'cognitively normal', and 'asymptomatic AD' (AsymAD) interchangeably. The Results section states 'Thirteen samples were from AD patients and fourteen were from age-matched non-AD (NA) individuals' but Figure 1 labels them 'ND' and the text later clarifies that 11 of the 14 NA samples are actually AsymAD. This creates ambiguity about which comparisons are being made and whether findings reflect AD dementia or Aβ/Tau pathology alone.
- Figure 2E (WGCNA module eigenproteins) lacks a legend explaining what the color strip below the hierarchical map represents or how to interpret red vs. blue vs. black modules. The caption states 'Red and blue MEs indicate significantly (p < 0.05) up- and down-regulated in AD, respectively' but does not explain the hierarchical clustering method or distance metric used, making the figure difficult to interpret from caption alone.
- Figure 4A (volcano plots) has four panels but the legend does not explicitly state which panel corresponds to which fraction (S2, P2, S3, P3). A reader must infer this from the surrounding text, violating the principle that figures should be understandable from caption + labels alone.
- Quantitative claim in Results: 'Most AD protein alterations, however, were limited to one fraction' is vague. The text later specifies '78% of significant changes were specific to one fraction' but this precise number should appear in the first statement for clarity.
- Figure 7C (nuclear protein network) uses node size and color to represent different properties, but the legend does not specify what node size represents (e.g., degree, betweenness centrality, or other network metric). The caption states 'Large circles denote proteins that are part of the spliceosome complex' but does not define what determines the size of non-spliceosome nodes.
- The term 'spatial proteoforms' is defined in the Introduction as 'proteins with multiple subcellular localizations' but the manuscript does not explicitly state whether quantified peptides in different fractions represent the same protein (identical sequence) or potentially different isoforms/PTMs. The Discussion acknowledges this ambiguity ('it is possible that a protein quantified in two different fractions could be different splice isoforms or maybe the identical protein with different post-translation modifications') but this caveat should be stated earlier and more prominently in Methods.
- Figure 3B shows mean ME expression for protein folding modules but does not include error bars or sample sizes, making it unclear whether differences are statistically robust or driven by outliers.
- The QUAD strategy is introduced with a schematic (Figure 8A) but the figure legend does not explain what 'Day 0' and 'Day 7' represent in terms of the experimental timeline (e.g., 'Day 0 = 4 days on AHA diet; Day 7 = 7 days post-AHA on normal diet'). A reader must infer this from the Methods section.
- Supplementary Figure 4G references 'p-value < 0.0003' but the main text does not state what statistical test was used or whether multiple-comparison correction was applied. This is a methods_completeness issue but affects clarity of the figure's interpretation.
- The phrase 'reciprocal alterations observed between the fractions' (Introduction) is used before being defined. It is later clarified as proteins with opposite expression patterns between S2 and P3, but the first use is ambiguous.

## Questions
- Can the authors clarify the composition of the 'non-AD' (NA) control group upfront? Specifically, should the main results be interpreted as AD dementia vs. cognitively normal, or as symptomatic AD vs. asymptomatic AD (which includes Aβ/Tau pathology)? This distinction affects interpretation of whether findings reflect disease-driving mechanisms or pathology-independent changes.
- In Figure 2E, what hierarchical clustering method and distance metric were used to generate the dendrogram? How are the module colors assigned—are they arbitrary or do they reflect functional relationships?
- For Figure 4A, can the authors label each volcano plot panel with its corresponding fraction (S2, P2, S3, P3) directly on the figure or in the legend?
- In Figure 7C, what network metric determines node size for non-spliceosome proteins? Is it degree, betweenness centrality, or another measure?
- The manuscript states that 'LC-MS rarely achieves 100% sequence coverage' and thus quantified peptides in different fractions 'could be different splice isoforms or maybe the identical protein with different post-translation modifications.' Can the authors provide any evidence (e.g., peptide overlap analysis) to support or refute whether the same protein isoform is being quantified across fractions?
- In Figure 3B, can error bars or confidence intervals be added to show the variability in ME expression across samples?
- For the QUAD analysis, can the authors explicitly state in the figure legend or Methods the exact timeline: e.g., 'Day 0 = immediately after 4 days on AHA diet; Day 7 = 7 days after return to normal diet'?
- Supplementary Figure 4G states 'p-value < 0.0003' for GO enrichment. Was multiple-comparison correction (e.g., FDR) applied? If so, what threshold was used?
