# Clarity & Presentation Reviewer

## Summary
This is a substantial and well-executed spatial proteomics dataset: TMT-based quantification of four subcellular fractions (P1/P2/S3/P3) from AD and ND human hippocampus, layered with PAL/QUAD protein-stability measurements in the APPswe/PS1dE9 mouse model at multiple ages. The fractionation-based localization of protein changes and the inclusion of the insoluble P3 fraction are valuable, and the data will be a useful community resource. However, the manuscript overinterprets biological implications: the mouse stability experiments appear underpowered (n=3 per group) and stability is inferred from a single 7-day time point; the distinction between translocation and solubility/trapping is not disambiguated; fraction purity and carryover are not assessed; human data are not corrected for cell-type composition; and the stated cross-species convergence aim is never actually executed with a direct human–mouse overlap analysis. Multiple major concerns require revision before publication.

## Weaknesses
- Mouse stability experiments appear underpowered (n=3 per group) and stability is inferred from a single 7-day time point, so changed ratios may reflect synthesis, insolubility, or neomorphic changes rather than degradation.
- The manuscript does not disambiguate translocation from solubility/trapping: less-soluble aggregated proteins may pellet more efficiently in P3 without changing their true subcellular location, and these two models have opposite interpretive implications.
- Fraction purity and cross-fraction carryover are not quantified; QC is limited to immunoblot comparison of a few known markers.
- Human tissue data are not weighted or corrected for cell-type composition, so reported changes may reflect AD/ND differences in cell composition rather than within-fraction protein changes.
- The stated aim of cross-species (human vs mouse) convergence is never executed; no direct overlap analysis between human and mouse datasets is presented.
- Lack of orthogonal validation (e.g., immunoblot, PRM, or microscopy) of fraction-specific TMT hits, especially the central P3/S2 changes.
- Sex of human donors is not reported or acknowledged; apparent female-only or female-enriched samples are a major unaddressed confound.
- No methodological caveats section, no consideration of alternative spatial dynamics models, and thin ethical/participant reporting for human tissue work.
- The WGCNA/centrality conclusion that abundant proteins are most robust or that centrality implies disease relevance is not supported by the data.
