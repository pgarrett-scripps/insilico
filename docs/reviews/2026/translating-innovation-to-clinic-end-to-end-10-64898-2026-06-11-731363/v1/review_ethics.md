# Ethics & Compliance Reviewer

## Summary
This is a bioprocess development and manufacturing report, not a clinical trial report, so most ethics categories are not triggered: no human-subject data are presented, no animal work is reported in this manuscript itself, and the CHO-cell manufacturing raises no dual-use or biosafety concern beyond standard cell-culture practice. Funding is fully disclosed and the first-in-human trial is properly registered and cited. One SOFT issue remains: the competing-interests declaration names only WRS without an explicit statement that all other authors have none.

## Strengths
- Funding sources are fully disclosed for all named funders (NIH CHAVD, Gates Foundation CAVD).
- The claim that clinical material was administered in HVTN144 is backed by a cited trial registration (NCT05217641), so the human-administration claim is verifiable rather than asserted.

## Weaknesses
- For this dimension, the manuscript's compliance claims are supported. The statement that clinical material "has been successfully administered in HVTN144" rests on the cited registration (NCT05217641, reference 10), which is present and checkable — no HARD issue. The manuscript reports no human-subject data, no animal studies of its own (the macaque work in Steichen et al., 2024 is cited context, not this paper's data), and no restricted-consent material, so no approval statements are required in this paper itself. I found no internal contradiction between the human-administration claim and the cited registration.
- The competing-interests section declares only WRS's Moderna employment/shareholding; it does not explicitly state that all other authors declare no competing interests — SOFT, add the explicit sentence.
- The manuscript cites macaque immunization studies but reports none of its own, so no animal-ethics statement is required here.
- The manufacturing process (CHO cells, standard bioprocessing, HIV vaccine immunogen for prevention) does not trigger dual-use or biosafety reporting requirements beyond routine institutional practice.
