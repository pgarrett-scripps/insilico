# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
This is a well-executed bioprocess development manuscript with generally sound experimental design and clear reporting of manufacturing data. The statistical and analytical reporting is largely appropriate for the applied biotech context: descriptive statistics, process optimization studies, and quality control measurements are presented with adequate detail. However, there are several areas where statistical rigor could be strengthened: some comparative claims lack explicit test names and sample sizes, variability measures are sometimes undefined or inconsistently reported, and a few figures present aggregated data without clarity on replication or independence of observations. These are not fatal flaws for a manufacturing/process paper, but they represent opportunities for tighter statistical communication and would benefit from minor revision.

## Strengths
- Process optimization study (Ambr®250, Table 6) clearly reports n=12 bioreactors, peak and Day 14 VCC with viability (%), and growth kinetics across defined conditions—replication and independence are transparent.
- Downstream process robustness evaluations (Tables 7–9) systematically vary operational parameters (column loading, pH, conductivity) and report step yields, SE-HPLC profiles (HMW, Main, LMW %), and residual impurities (ppm/ppb) with centerpoint and worst-case conditions clearly labeled.
- Viral clearance study (Table 12) reports log10 reductions with standard deviations (±) for two model viruses across four unit operations, summed to overall LRV; the comparison to industry benchmarks (≤10⁻⁴ RVLP per dose) is explicit and contextually meaningful.
- Intermediate hold-time stability (Figure 15, Table 4) defines acceptance criteria a priori (≤0.7% change in HMW/LMW by SE-HPLC, ≤15% variation in relative potency) and reports data at multiple timepoints and temperatures, enabling readers to assess stability claims.
- Cell culture scale-up (Figure 14) presents VCC, viability, and BLI titer across three scales (Ambr®250, 50L RCB, 50L MCB, 200L GMP) with consistent metrics and acknowledges reference material differences affecting absolute titer comparisons.

## Weaknesses
- Clone ranking (Figures 9–11, Section 3.1.2–3.1.3): The first round screened 213 clones by BG18_GL0 titer, selected 56 for BG18/PGT145 ratio, then 24 for second round. No statistical test (e.g., ranking criterion, threshold, or cutoff) is named; it is unclear whether selection was deterministic (top N) or threshold-based, and no justification for the 56→24 reduction is provided. This is a SOFT issue for clone selection (common practice is empirical), but the lack of explicit criteria weakens reproducibility.
- Stable pool productivity (Figure 7, Section 3.1.1): BLI titer is reported as 'comparable detectable productivities of BG18 and PGT145' and '350–400 mg/L deemed acceptable,' but no statistical test, n (number of replicates or timepoints?), or error measure is stated. The claim 'comparable' is visual/qualitative; a formal equivalence or paired test would strengthen it. This is a SOFT issue if the intent is descriptive, but the language invites a quantitative interpretation.
- Medium adaptation (Figure 12, Section 3.2.1): Doubling time and viability are plotted across five passages with no error bars, replicates per passage, or variability measure. It is unclear whether each passage is a single culture or the mean of replicates; if single, no statistical inference is possible; if replicates, n and SD/SEM should be shown. The claim 'robust growth kinetics and sustained high viability' rests on visual inspection alone. This is a SOFT issue for a descriptive process study, but error bars would clarify.
- Ambr®250 BLI titer (Figure 13, Section 3.2.2): Twelve conditions are tested, each presumably once (n=1 per condition?). The figure shows titer profiles over time but no error bars or replicates are mentioned. If n=1 per condition, no statistical comparison is valid; if replicates exist, n and variability should be stated. The text claims 'highest Day 14 titers' for three conditions (752, 721, 715 mg/L) but does not test whether differences are significant or meaningful. This is a HARD issue if the selection of 'Control + 7a bolus' is based on a non-significant difference from controls—a justification beyond point estimates is needed.
- Pilot and GMP scale runs (Section 3.2.3, Figure 14): Three runs (50L RCB, 50L MCB, 200L GMP) are compared; each appears to be a single bioreactor (n=1 per scale/bank combination). The text states 'cell growth kinetics were comparable' and 'productivity was consistent,' but no statistical test or confidence interval is provided. Comparability claims from n=1 replicates per group are not statistically defensible. This is a HARD issue if the intent is to claim robustness across scales; at minimum, the single-run nature should be disclosed and caveats stated.
- Downstream process yields (Figure 16, Section 3.4): Step yields are reported as 'average' for pilot-scale and cGMP runs, but the number of replicates per step is not stated. Are these single measurements or means of replicates? If means, where are error bars or SEM? The text notes '>100% yield for 2G12 step' is 'likely attributed to titer assay variability,' but no confidence interval or test of significance is provided. This is a SOFT issue if yields are single measurements (acceptable for process reporting), but the 'average' language suggests aggregation without clarity.
- Glycosylation analysis (Figures 17–18, Section 3.6.1): Two complementary MS methods are used; Figure 17 (DeGlyPHER) shows mean ± SEM for each PNGS, but the number of replicates (n) is not stated. Figure 18 (LC-MS) shows proportions of glycoforms per site but no error bars or replicates are mentioned. The text notes 'good agreement' between methods but does not quantify concordance (e.g., correlation, Bland–Altman plot). This is a SOFT issue; reporting n and replicates per method would strengthen confidence in the glycosylation profile.
- Negative-stain EM (Section 3.6.2): The text states '6,086 particles analyzed' and 'all particles have a similar phenotype' with 'nearly 100% native-like trimers,' but no statistical measure of homogeneity, classification accuracy, or inter-rater reliability is provided. How were particles classified as 'native-like'? Was there a blinded criterion or automated classifier? This is a SOFT issue for a qualitative morphology assessment, but quantitative metrics (e.g., % trimers by automated classification with CI) would be more rigorous.
- Residual impurity clearance (Tables 8–9): Many assays report 'Below LOQ' with LOQ values in parentheses (e.g., '< LOQ (< 11.4 ppm)'). The LOQ is stated per assay, but it is unclear whether these are method-specific or batch-specific, and whether 'Below LOQ' is treated as zero or as a censored value in comparisons. If censored values are present, non-parametric or survival-analysis methods may be more appropriate than simple comparisons. This is a SOFT issue for process QC reporting, but clarity on censoring would help.
- Relative potency (Table 9, Section 3.3.1): BLI relative potency is reported as '100%' for the final pool with no error bar, CI, or replicate count. The text states 'the BDS was considered unstable if relative potency varied by more than 15% over the assessed hold time,' implying a tolerance, but the final potency is presented as a point estimate. This is a SOFT issue; reporting the assay n and variability (e.g., 100% ± X%) would clarify precision.

## Questions
- In Figure 13 (Ambr®250 BLI titer), how many independent replicates were performed per condition? If n=1 per condition, how were the 'highest Day 14 titers' selected—by point estimate alone, or was a statistical test applied? If replicates exist, why are error bars not shown?
- In Section 3.2.3 (pilot and GMP scale runs), were the 50L RCB, 50L MCB, and 200L GMP runs each performed once, or are these means of multiple bioreactors? If single runs, please clarify that comparability claims are observational, not statistical.
- In Figure 12 (medium adaptation), were the doubling time and viability measurements taken from a single culture per passage, or are they means of replicates? If replicates, please provide n and error bars (SD or SEM, defined).
- In Section 3.1.1 (stable pool productivity), what is the sample size (n) for the BLI titer measurements in Figure 7? Are these single timepoint measurements or replicates? How is 'comparable' quantified—by visual inspection or a formal test?
- In Figure 16 (downstream process yields), the text refers to 'average step yields' but does not state the number of replicates per step. Are these single measurements or means? If means, please provide error bars or SEM.
- In Section 3.6.1 (glycosylation analysis), how many independent replicates were analyzed by DeGlyPHER (Figure 17) and LC-MS (Figure 18)? The error bars in Figure 17 are labeled 'standard error of mean'—what is the n per PNGS?
- In Section 3.6.2 (negative-stain EM), how were the 6,086 particles classified as 'native-like trimers'? Was a blinded criterion, automated classifier, or manual inspection used? What is the classification accuracy or inter-rater agreement?
- In Tables 8–9, many residual impurity measurements are reported as 'Below LOQ.' Were these treated as zero, as censored values, or excluded from comparisons? If censored, were non-parametric methods used?
- In Table 9, the final BDS relative potency is reported as '100%' with no error bar or CI. What is the assay n and variability (e.g., SD or SEM) for this measurement?
- In Section 3.1.2–3.1.3 (clone ranking), what explicit criteria were used to select 56 clones from 213 in the first round, and 24 from 56 in the second round? Were these deterministic (top N by titer) or threshold-based? Please state the selection rule.
