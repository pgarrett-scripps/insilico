# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
This paper reports the cell-line development, upstream and downstream process development, and cGMP manufacturing of an HIV-1 Env trimer immunogen. The analytical data used to support process decisions are appropriate for a bioprocess engineering report, but the paper makes a series of quantitative claims — about product quality, impurity clearance, viral clearance, and titer comparability — that cannot be fully evaluated because the supporting statistical information is absent or incomplete. The claims are plausible, the manuscript is well-structured, and the engineering effort is substantial, but a referee cannot verify that the quantitative evidence actually supports the conclusions as stated. A major revision that reports the missing elements would allow a proper evaluation.

## Weaknesses
- Load-bearing weakness 1 — 'comparable' product quality across scales and cell banks is asserted without formal comparison. The paper states that BLI titers differed across runs (562, 355, 390 mg/L) and attributes this to "reference material used" without showing that the reference material explains the variation, citing an untestable change in reference lot. No test of equivalence or comparability (e.g., the 90% confidence interval method for CMC comparability, or even a two-one-sided test) is applied to any quality attribute across the three runs. The reader cannot distinguish process drift, lot-to-lot variation, and artefact.
- Load-bearing weakness 2 — viral clearance LRVs are reported as point estimates with ± standard deviations, but the basis for the uncertainty is not stated and one cannot assess whether the claimed clearance exceeds what the assay can measure. '≥18.14 logs' for XMuLV implies a clearance factor of 10^18; retrovirus spike titers are typically 10^6–10^8 infectious units/mL. Such an LRV can only be reached if clearance is complete at every step and the limit of detection is carried forward. The paper does not state the spike titer, the limit of detection per step, the number of replicates, or whether the standard deviation reflects assay variability, run-to-run variability, or propagation of detection limits. Without these, the headline number is not interpretable as evidence of safety.
- Load-bearing weakness 3 — the '>99% trimeric purity' claim originates from negative-stain EM, described as showing 'nearly 100% native-like trimers' and 'no other oligomeric states.' nsEM is a qualitative structural technique, not a quantitative purity assay. The paper also reports SE-HPLC %Main peak values of 98.6–99.4%, which is a valid quantitative purity metric, but no uncertainty, number of injections, or replicate runs are given. The claim as stated conflates two very different measurement modalities and overstates the quantitative precision of the nsEM result.
- HARD, sweep: No n is given for any SE-HPLC, RP-HPLC, BLI titer, or impurity clearance value in the main results tables (Tables 6–11). Are these single measurements? Means of duplicates? Means of process replicates? The reader cannot assess precision.
- HARD, sweep: The Octet BLI titer values reported for the Ambr250 study and the three 50-L runs are not accompanied by the number of replicate measurements, the intra-assay CV, or the limit of quantification. A difference that a referee would suspect is within assay noise cannot be evaluated.
- HARD, sweep: The relative potency BLI assay (Table 11) reports '116% compared to Reference' for the cGMP batch with no confidence interval or passing criteria. The demonstration run shows a decreasing trend in relative potency over hold time (Figure 15c) but the text says it 'was still within the target potency range' — the range is not stated. The reader cannot tell whether 116% is acceptable.
- SOFT: The intermediate hold-time stability study (Figure 15) used a threshold of 0.7% change in HMW or LMW as the stability criterion. The basis for this threshold (measurement variability, specification, or literature precedent) is not given, and the 0.7% value is never defined as a critical quality attribute or justified.
- SOFT: The HCP ELISA uses a "third-generation" CHO HCP kit. LOQ is reported as ng/mg protein in some places and ppm in others (Table 9 vs Table 10), making cross-comparison unnecessarily difficult; units should be harmonised.
