# Rigor & Overclaiming Reviewer

## Summary
This is a well-executed bioprocess development and cGMP manufacturing paper with strong technical rigor and comprehensive documentation. The claims are generally well-supported by the data presented, and the authors appropriately hedge most statements about process performance and product quality. However, there are several instances of imprecise or over-strong language in the abstract and conclusions that exceed what the evidence directly establishes, particularly around 'scalability,' 'reproducibility,' and the generalizability of the platform. The work is solid and publication-worthy with minor revisions to claim calibration.

## Strengths
- The abstract and results clearly distinguish between small-scale (Ambr250, feasibility), pilot-scale (50L RCB/MCB), and cGMP runs, with explicit data for each stage, supporting claims of process development progression.
- Robustness evaluations (Table 3, 7–9) are thorough, testing worst-case yield and worst-case product quality conditions, and the authors appropriately acknowledge where performance degrades (e.g., Capto adhere yield drop from ~60% to 24% under worst-case conditions) rather than hiding variability.
- Viral clearance data (Table 12) are quantified with log reductions and confidence intervals, and the authors correctly contextualize results against industry benchmarks (≥18 logs for XMuLV vs. ≤10⁻⁴ RVLP/dose standard).
- Glycosylation analysis uses two orthogonal MS methods (DeGlyPHER and LC-MS) with appropriate acknowledgment of method-specific limitations (e.g., 'assessment of occupancy exhibits higher variation between analytical approaches' in Section 3.6.1).
- Hold-time stability data (Table 4, Figure 15) are presented with quantitative thresholds (≤0.7% change in HMW/LMW) and specific recommendations tied to results, avoiding vague 'stability' claims.
- Negative-stain EM confirms native-like trimeric structure with explicit particle counts (6,086 particles analyzed) and comparison to published benchmarks.

## Weaknesses
- {HARD} Abstract claims 'scalable, reproducible manufacturing paradigm' and 'robust, reproducible bioprocessing platforms' but evidence is limited to three runs (50L RCB, 50L MCB, 200L cGMP). While these show consistency, 'reproducibility' typically requires ≥3 independent replicates of the same process under identical conditions; here, the RCB and MCB are different cell banks, and the cGMP run is a single execution. The claim should be reworded to 'demonstrated scalability across two cell banks and vessel scales' or 'consistent performance across three sequential runs' rather than asserting reproducibility as a general property.
- {HARD} Abstract states 'consistent product quality across multiple cGMP batches' but only one cGMP batch is described (Section 3.4, Figure 16). The pilot-scale demonstration run is not a cGMP batch. This is a direct contradiction—the claim must be removed or reworded to 'consistent product quality between pilot-scale demonstration and cGMP batch' (singular).
- {SOFT} Conclusions claim 'process scaled seamlessly from Ambr® 250 to 200 L SUBs' but the Ambr250 optimization identified 'Control + 7a bolus' as optimal, and the 50L RCB run used this condition; however, the 50L MCB run and cGMP run show lower Day 14 BLI titers (355 mg/L and 390.2 mg/L respectively vs. 562 mg/L for RCB), attributed to 'different reference standards' (Section 3.2.3). This explanation is post-hoc and does not establish that the process itself scaled seamlessly—the titer variability is not fully explained. The claim should be hedged: 'process parameters scaled from Ambr250 to pilot and cGMP scales with consistent cell growth and harvest yields, though absolute titers varied due to reference standard differences.'
- {SOFT} Section 3.3.1 states 'the process was able to reduce both HMW and LMW to <1.0% in the final material' based on centerpoint runs (Table 8: 0.1% HMW, 1.0% LMW in final pool). However, the cGMP batch (Table 11) shows 'N/A' for HMW and 0.6% LMW, not <1.0% LMW. The statement is technically correct for the feasibility run but should clarify that the cGMP batch achieved 0.6% LMW, which is still excellent but not <1.0%.
- {SOFT} Section 3.2.3 states 'studies demonstrated robust production and high recovery efficiency under the same feed and control strategy' but the three runs used different reference standards for BLI titer measurement (IA VI/Scripps lot 19Apr0088 for RCB; KBI lot S-20210314-0001-SD2-E-M for MCB; KBI lot P65 for cGMP). This undermines the claim that 'the same feed and control strategy' was used—the analytical reference material changed, which confounds interpretation of whether titer differences reflect process variability or assay differences. The claim should acknowledge this limitation.
- {HARD} Section 3.3.2 evaluates removal of preparative SEC by comparing 'two executions performed in parallel' (Table 10) but provides no statistical analysis or replication. The statement 'the final UF/DF step was able to remove impurities from the product pool to an acceptable level and the overall results were comparable to the control execution' is based on a single side-by-side comparison. 'Comparable' is not quantified—are differences within assay variability? This is a critical decision (SEC step removed from cGMP process) and deserves more rigorous justification or explicit acknowledgment that it is based on a single experiment.
- {SOFT} Abstract claims 'preserved quaternary structure and native-like antigenicity' but antigenicity is assessed only by BLI binding to BG18_GL0 and PGT145 antibodies (Figures 7, 13) and relative potency (Table 9, 11). These are binding assays, not functional immunogenicity assays. The claim should be reworded to 'preserved binding to germline-targeting antibodies and quaternary structure' or 'maintained recognition by design-target antibodies.'
- {SOFT} Section 3.1.1 reports productivity of 'N332-GT5 stable pool' as '350-400 mg/L' (Figure 7 caption) but the actual figure shows BG18 and PGT145 titers in µg/mL, not mg/L. The conversion to mg/L is not shown. This is a unit/magnitude issue that should be clarified or corrected.
- {SOFT} The claim in the abstract that the process 'maintained structural conformity' is supported by nsEM (Figure 19) and glycosylation analysis (Figures 17–18), but 'conformity' is not defined. Conformity to what standard? The nsEM shows native-like trimers but does not compare to a reference structure quantitatively. The glycosylation matches 'design specifications' (abstract) but the specific design specifications are not stated in the manuscript.

## Questions
- In the abstract, 'consistent product quality across multiple cGMP batches'—how many cGMP batches were manufactured? The Results describe only one cGMP run (Section 3.4). If only one batch was made, this claim must be removed or reworded.
- Section 3.2.3 reports different BLI titers across the three runs (562, 355, 390.2 mg/L) attributed to different reference standards. Were the same clarified harvest samples re-assayed with a single reference standard to confirm that the titer differences are assay artifacts and not process differences? Without this, the claim of 'robust production' is weakened.
- Section 3.3.2: The decision to remove preparative SEC from the cGMP process is based on a single parallel comparison (Table 10). Was this decision validated by any additional experiment, or is it based solely on this one side-by-side run? If the latter, this should be explicitly stated as a limitation.
- Figure 17 (DeGlyPHER) shows N625 is ~50% unoccupied, and Figure 18 (LC-MS) shows several sites with high unoccupied fractions (N185e, N611, N625). How do these glycosylation gaps compare to the design specification? Are they acceptable, or do they represent a deviation from the intended glycan profile?
- Section 3.1.1 reports productivity as '350-400 mg/L' for the stable pool, but Figures 7 and 13 show titers in µg/mL. What is the conversion factor, and how was mg/L calculated from the BLI assay data?
- The abstract states the process 'maintained structural conformity'—what is the quantitative criterion for conformity? Is it the nsEM observation of native-like trimers, or is there a comparison to a reference structure (e.g., cryo-EM structure) that defines acceptable conformity?
- Section 3.3.1 states the process reduced HMW and LMW to '<1.0% in the final material' based on feasibility runs, but the cGMP batch (Table 11) shows 0.6% LMW and 'N/A' for HMW. Why is HMW not reported for the cGMP batch? Is this a data gap or a different analytical method?
