# Rigor & Overclaiming Reviewer

## Summary
The process development work is substantial and the core claims about purification yield, trimeric purity, and viral clearance are supported by the presented data. However, several abstract-level claims outrun the evidence: "multiple cGMP batches" is contradicted by the manuscript's own account of a single GMP run; the 60-PD genetic stability claim is asserted with no Results data behind it; and the glycan "matching design specifications" claim is unverifiable as written, since no specification is stated and the two analytical methods disagree on occupancy at several sites. These are concentrated in the abstract and require requalification or re-evidenced support.

## Weaknesses
- HARD — "delivering consistent product quality across multiple cGMP batches" (abstract). The manuscript describes exactly one GMP run: "the same process was successfully executed for the GMP manufacturing using the XDR-200 bioreactor" (Section 3.2.3). The 50-L material supply and 50-L demonstration runs are explicitly pilot-scale (RCB and MCB, non-GMP). "Multiple cGMP batches" is contradicted by the manuscript's own account — and even by its own title, which says "cGMP Manufacturing" in the singular. Fix: change to "across pilot and GMP scale runs," or present data from additional GMP batches.
- HARD — "genetic stability through 60 population doublings" (abstract). Methods 2.3.8 describes a 60-PD study using productivity, transgene copy number, and N332/furin mRNA identity as stability indicators. The Results section never reports any of these outcomes — Section 3.1 covers stable pool development and clone ranking only. The abstract asserts a result the manuscript does not present. Fix: report the PD60 productivity, copy-number, and mRNA-identity data, or remove the claim.
- SOFT — "confirmed site-specific glycan occupancy matching design specifications" (abstract). Two problems. First, no design specification is stated anywhere in the manuscript, so "matching design specifications" is unverifiable as worded. Second, the two methods disagree on occupancy at several sites: DeGlyPHER shows N625 ~50% unoccupied and N185e/N611/N618 10–20% unoccupied, while the LC-MS glycoproteomics shows N185e, N611, and N625 as predominantly unoccupied ("only the non-occupied peptides were prominent"). The body acknowledges this ("assessment of occupancy exhibits higher variation between analytical approaches") but the abstract claim does not. Fix: state the specification, reconcile the two methods' occupancy calls, or hedge.
- SOFT — "Clinical material manufactured through this platform has been successfully administered in HVTN144" (abstract, conclusions). No data in the manuscript supports "successfully administered"; it is an assertion about an external trial, and "successfully" implies safety/tolerability data that are not presented. Fix: remove, or replace with a citation to the trial record.
- SOFT — "preserved quaternary structure and native-like antigenicity" (abstract). The nsEM structural analysis was performed on demonstration-run material ("N332-GT5 from the 50L demonstration run"), not the cGMP batch. The cGMP BDS has BLI (116% relative potency) but no nsEM. The structural claim about the clinical material rests on pilot material.
- SOFT — "robust, cGMP-compliant process" (abstract). The Capto adhere step yield dropped from ~60% to ~24% under worst-case yield conditions (Table 7), and the authors recommend monitoring loading and pH to avoid those conditions. "Robust" is over-strong for a step that requires active avoidance of worst-case conditions, though the body does acknowledge this.
- Sweep: "A streamlined three-step purification strategy" (abstract) is a categorization that obscures the actual eight unit operations (2G12, UF/DF1, Triton VI, Amberlite, MabSelect SuRe, Capto adhere, virus filtration, UF/DF2). The summed viral clearance (≥18.14 XMuLV, ≥11.70 MMV) assumes independence across unit operations; standard practice, but the summed value is an upper-bound estimate, not a measured total. Reproducibility is claimed across three runs while titers varied 355–562 mg/L, attributed to different reference standards; plausible but unverified — a single reference standard across runs would strengthen the claim. The "first-in-human" priority claim in the title is a novelty assertion (novelty reviewer's call), but note the manuscript itself provides no evidence of administration.
