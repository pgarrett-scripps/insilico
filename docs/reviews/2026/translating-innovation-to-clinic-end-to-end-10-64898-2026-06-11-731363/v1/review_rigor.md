# Rigor & Overclaiming Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript reports a careful, well-documented bioprocess development campaign. The central claims — that N332-GT5 gp140 can be manufactured at clinical scale with >99% trimer purity, correct glycosylation, and robust viral clearance — are supported by specific, orthogonal evidence. The main weakness is a pattern of over-strong wording in the abstract and introductory claims that exceeds what the single-product case study can license (generalisability claims, 'established a paradigm'). The paper also has a small but real gap in linking the glycan analysis to the design specification: the claim that glycosylation 'matched design specifications' is asserted verbatim but never shown against a stated target. These are SOFT issues, not fatal, and fixable with requalification. Recommended minor revision.

## Strengths
- The claim ledger is explicitly built: each claim about product quality (trimer purity, glycan occupancy, structural integrity) is matched to a specific orthogonal assay with quantitative results.
- The downstream robustness evaluation tests explicit worst-case yield and worst-case quality conditions rather than only centrepoint runs, and reports where robustness breaks (Capto adhere at low loading).
- Viral clearance is quantified per unit operation and summed, with the resulting RVLP-per-dose calculation allowing the reader to judge safety margins directly.

## Weaknesses
- LOAD-BEARING: The claim that the process 'establishes a scalable, reproducible manufacturing paradigm for structurally complex HIV-1 envelope immunogens' (Abstract) and is 'advancing the field toward rational vaccine design' is a generalisation beyond what a single-product case study supports. The same phrase recurs in the Conclusions: 'provide a blueprint for advancing glycoprotein vaccine candidates'. A single immunogen, one CHO clone, one 200-L scale, and one purification train do not establish a paradigm or a blueprint — they demonstrate feasibility for this specific product under these conditions. The paper would be stronger if requalified as a case study of this immunogen, since the claim a reader would actually test is 'can this particular immunogen be made reproducibly at clinical grade' — which the data do support.
- LOAD-BEARING: The Abstract claims 'site-specific glycan occupancy matching design specifications'. The glycan analysis (Figs 17–18) is thorough, but the manuscript never states what the design specifications were. What proportion of oligomannose vs complex, or what occupancy threshold, was targeted? Without a stated specification, 'matching' is an empty claim. The data may well meet the (unstated) targets, but the reader cannot verify this, and the sentence as written is a SOFT over-claim. The authors should either state the design targets explicitly or requalify to 'consistent with the expected glycosylation profile for this immunogen design'.
- SOFT (Abstract): '>99% trimeric purity with preserved quaternary structure and native-like antigenicity'. The SE-HPLC data (Table 11) show 99.4% Main peak by SE-HPLC, which is excellent. However, SE-HPLC alone does not establish 'preserved quaternary structure' — that claim relies on the nsEM data (Fig. 19), which the text describes as 6,086 particles from a single grid. The sample size is modest; lower your confidence accordingly or note that this is a qualitative check, not a population estimate. 'Native-like antigenicity' is supported by BLI binding to PGT145 and BG18_GL0, both of which are quaternary-structure-dependent antibodies, so the claim is defensible but would benefit from explicit linkage in the Abstract.
- SOFT (Conclusions): 'We established a stable CHO cell-based platform for N332-GT5 gp140 manufacturing using Leap-In transposition'. The word 'platform' is over-strong for a single clone. More precise: 'a stable CHO cell-based process' or 'a cell line'.
- SOFT (Hold-time data): The UF/DF1 retentate shows a clear HMW increase (3–4%) within 1 day at both temperatures (Fig. 15). The manuscript recommends ≤1 day hold for this intermediate, which is appropriate, but does not discuss whether this aggregation is reversible, whether it correlates with a potency loss, or whether earlier loading onto the next column would mitigate the risk. A brief note on whether this is considered a quality-critical bottleneck would address a practical question a manufacturer would have.

## Questions
- Table 9 reports Capto adhere flowthrough resHCP ≈ 300 ppm for the WCPQ cycle but does not report a centrepoint value; only the WCY cycle has a centrepoint. Was the centrepoint run assayed and, if so, what was the value?
- The preparative SEC removal study (Table 10) uses two different LOQ thresholds (<157 ng/mg vs <34 ng/mg). Is that a genuine difference in LOQ between the two assays or a reporting inconsistency?
- Hold-time stability (Fig. 15) — the UF/DF1 retentate showed a 3–4% HMW increase within 1 day. Did the authors assess whether this increase is reversible or stabilised by formulation additives, or is 1 day the recommended maximum regardless?
