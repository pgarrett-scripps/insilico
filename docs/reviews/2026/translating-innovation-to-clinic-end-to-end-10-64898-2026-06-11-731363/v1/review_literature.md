# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript is well-grounded in its citation of the directly relevant prior work: the foundational HIV Env trimer design and germline-targeting immunogen papers (Sanders et al. 2013, 2015; Steichen et al. 2016, 2019, 2024), the most comparable cGMP manufacturing precedent for a trimeric Env (Dey et al. 2018), and the platform-based gp120 production work (Wolfe et al. 2021). I found no HARD errors—no fabricated or unresolvable references, no omitted directly competing manufacturing process, and no straw-manned characterization of prior work. The issues are all minor and fixable: a typo in a reference author name, a small set of references in the list that appear not to be cited in the text, and one citation whose existence I could not independently verify from the available metadata.

## Strengths
- The most directly comparable prior work (Dey et al. 2018 on BG505 SOSIP.664 cGMP manufacturing) is transparently cited as the basis for the downstream process, which is the correct and useful attribution.
- The chain of references for the immunogen design (Steichen 2016 → 2019 → 2024) is accurate and lets a reader trace the full design lineage of N332-GT5.
- Self-citations (Pallerla et al. 2025 for the SMNP adjuvant; Steichen et al. papers for the immunogen) are proportionate and justified by direct relevance to the work.

## Weaknesses
- The paper states that N332-GT5 gp140 was 'successfully administered in HVTN144' and cites Parks et al. 2025 as evidence that 'clinical evaluations have demonstrated the safety and immunogenicity of native-like trimer vaccines in humans.' I was unable to verify the Parks et al. 2025 reference (Science Translational Medicine, Vol 17, Issue 809) through PubMed or bioRxiv searches—the DOI may be too recent to have been indexed. If the Parks paper has not yet been published or does not actually report safety and immunogenicity data from a clinical trial, then the claim that prior clinical evaluations support the safety/immunogenicity of trimer vaccines is unattributed (and the clinical-trial description of HVTN144 itself would bear more of the evidentiary weight). Please confirm that the Parks et al. 2025 reference is published, indexed, and supports the specific claim for which it is cited.
- Several references in the list appear not to be cited in the manuscript text. Specifically: Ref [9] (Hahn et al. 2024, JEM), Ref [15] (Pauthner et al. 2017, Immunity), and Ref [18] (Ramezani-Rad et al. 2025, Science Translational Medicine) are present in the bibliography but I could not locate any in-text citation for them. One of these (Ref [18]) is a different paper from Ref [32] (Parks et al. 2025), both in the same journal and year—so the distinction matters. The reference list should map cleanly to the text; uncited references should be removed, and if I missed an in-text citation, the authors should confirm which sentence references which paper.
- Ref [33] lists the author as 'Sander et al.'; the correct name for this Science 2015 paper on native-like trimers is 'Sanders et al.'—a minor typo, likely from OCR, but it needs correction.
- The 2G12 antibody used as the affinity capture ligand is referenced only as a commercial supplier (Polymun Scientific); the original characterization of 2G12 (Trkola et al., J Virol 1996) is not cited. This is not a load-bearing omission, but it would be good practice to cite the primary source for a key process reagent.
- The Leap-In transposon system is described as ATUM's proprietary technology but no independent citation for the transposase method is provided. If a peer-reviewed description exists, it should be cited; if not, a statement that the system is proprietary would avoid the appearance of an omitted reference.
