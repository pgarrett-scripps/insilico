# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

SCORE: 4
CONFIDENCE: 4

## Summary

This is a bioprocess development and cGMP manufacturing manuscript describing the production of the N332-GT5 gp140 HIV vaccine candidate for the HVTN144 trial. My remit is the citation record: whether claims about prior work are accurate, whether foundational or directly-competing work is missing, and whether the reference list is sound. The manuscript's load-bearing claims rest on (1) the claim that N332-GT5 is a germline-targeting immunogen designed to prime BG18-class bnAb precursors, (2) the claim that the manufacturing process preserves native-like trimeric structure and antigenicity, and (3) the claim that this represents a novel or at least non-duplicative manufacturing platform for HIV Env trimers. The citation record is generally sound, with a few specific issues detailed below.

## Load-Bearing Claims

**Claim 1: N332-GT5 gp140 is a germline-targeting immunogen for BG18-class bnAb precursors.** The manuscript cites Steichen et al. 2019 (Science) and Steichen et al. 2024 (Science) for the design and preclinical validation of N332-GT5. I verified these citations: Steichen et al. 2019 (Science 366:eaax4380) does describe the generalized HIV vaccine design strategy including the N332-GT series, and Steichen et al. 2024 (Science 384:adj8321) does report the nonhuman primate priming data with N332-GT5/SMNP. The attribution is accurate. The claim that N332-GT5 binds "20 of 27 potential BG18 precursor antibodies with Kd ≤ 1 μM" is attributed to Steichen et al. 2019 — I could not verify this specific number from the abstract alone, and the manuscript does not provide a supplementary table or figure reference for this value. This is a SOFT issue: the authors should confirm the specific count and threshold against the cited source, or provide the supporting data.

**Claim 2: The manufacturing process preserves native-like trimeric structure and antigenicity.** The manuscript cites Dey et al. 2018 (Biotechnology and Bioengineering) as the basis for the downstream purification process, and Bale et al. 2025 (npj Vaccines) for comparison of nsEM results. Both are appropriate. The nsEM data (Figure 19) showing native-like trimers is presented as evidence, but I cannot inspect the micrographs or class averages from the text alone. The claim that "no other oligomeric states (dimers, monomers) were observed" rests on the figure, which I cannot verify. This is a confidence-limiting factor rather than a citation defect. The SE-HPLC data showing >99% trimeric purity is internally consistent with the claim.

**Claim 3: This is a novel or non-duplicative manufacturing platform for HIV Env trimers.** The manuscript cites Dey et al. 2018 for the BG505 SOSIP.664 cGMP production and Wolfe et al. 2021 for platform-based approaches to HIV gp120 production. These are the directly-competing prior manufacturing efforts. I ran a related-work search to check for other cGMP manufacturing reports of HIV Env trimers that might be missing.

## Sweep

1. **HARD — Missing citation for the clinical trial itself.** The manuscript repeatedly references "HVTN 144" and its design (doses, routes, schedules) but cites only ClinicalTrials.gov identifier NCT05217641 (ref [10]). If the trial protocol or a published study design paper exists, it should be cited; if not, the ClinicalTrials.gov entry is acceptable but the manuscript should state that no protocol paper exists. The claim that HVTN144 is "the first human trial to test a generalizable strategy for priming HCDR3-dominant bnAbs" is a strong novelty claim that should be checked against the trial record.

2. **SOFT — The Parks et al. 2025 citation (ref [32]) is garbled.** The DOI reads "0. 1 1 26/scitranslmed.ady683 1" with spacing errors, and the reference list entry has inconsistent formatting. This is a resolvability concern — the DOI as printed will not resolve. The authors should correct the DOI to 10.1126/scitranslmed.ady6831. I verified this is a real paper (Science Translational Medicine, 2025, reporting phase 1 clinical results with mRNA-encoded membrane-anchored HIV envelope trimers).

3. **SOFT — Reference [33] "Sander et al." is misattributed.** The entry reads "Sander et al. HIV-1 neutralizing antibodies induced by native-like envelope trimers. Science, Vol 349, Issue 6244. DOI: 10.1126/science.aac4223." The correct first author is Sanders (Sanders RW, et al., Science 2015;349:6244). This is a typographical error in the reference list that will impede resolution.

4. **SOFT — The manuscript cites ref [21] (Silva et al., Science Immunology 2021) for the SMNP adjuvant** but the more directly relevant citation for the adjuvant used in HVTN144 may be the authors' own Pallerla et al. 2025 (ref [13]) describing SMNP scale-up and cGMP manufacturing. The manuscript does cite both, so this is not a missing citation but a question of whether the SMNP description in the introduction is adequately sourced. The claim that SMNP is a "saponin/MPLA nanoparticle" adjuvant is supported by both citations.

5. **SOFT — Currency check.** The reference list spans 2013–2025, which is appropriate for a fast-moving field. The most recent manufacturing-relevant citations (Bale et al. 2025, Parks et al. 2025, Ramezani-Rad et al. 2025) are current. No anchoring to old work only.

6. **SOFT — Self-citation is proportionate.** The authors cite their own work (Steichen et al. 2019, 2024; Pallerla et al. 2025) where it is genuinely load-bearing. No evidence of citation padding.

7. **HARD — The claim "Preclinical studies in rhesus macaques subsequently demonstrated the remarkable efficacy of N332-GT5 when formulated with the saponin/MPLA nanoparticle (SMNP) adjuvant"** is attributed to Steichen et al. 2024. I verified this paper reports the NHP priming data. However, the word "efficacy" is potentially overclaiming — the paper reports priming of BG18-class precursors and affinity maturation, not protective efficacy against challenge. The authors should either soften "efficacy" to "immunogenicity" or confirm that the cited paper reports challenge data. This is a HARD attribution issue because the cited source may not support the specific claim as worded.

8. **SOFT — The manuscript does not cite the original SOSIP design paper (Sanders et al. 2013, PLoS Pathogens, ref [19]) in the introduction** where it discusses native-like Env trimers, but does cite it in the methods section. This is adequate coverage.

## Questions

1. Can the authors confirm that the "20 of 27 potential BG18 precursor antibodies with Kd ≤ 1 μM" figure is directly reported in Steichen et al. 2019, and if so, provide the specific table/figure reference? If it is from a different source or from unpublished data, that should be stated.

2. Does Steichen et al. 2024 report challenge data (protective efficacy), or only immunogenicity/priming data? The word "efficacy" in the manuscript should be aligned with what the cited source actually reports.

3. Is there a published HVTN144 protocol paper, or is the ClinicalTrials.gov entry the only public record of the trial design?

4. Can the authors confirm the correct DOI for Parks et al. 2025 (ref [32]) and correct the first author of ref [33] from "Sander" to "Sanders"?

## Related-Work Verification

I ran a search for cGMP manufacturing of HIV Env trimers and related immunogen production work to check for omissions. The search returned the expected prior work (Dey et al. 2018 on BG505 SOSIP.664 cGMP production; Wolfe et al. 2021 on platform approaches for gp120). I found no directly-competing cGMP manufacturing report for N332-GT5 or other germline-targeting Env trimers that is missing from the reference list. The novelty claim regarding the manufacturing platform appears adequately supported by the citation record, though the novelty verdict itself belongs to the novelty reviewer.

## Overall

The citation record is largely sound: the load-bearing claims about N332-GT5 design and preclinical validation are accurately attributed to the correct primary sources, the manufacturing process appropriately cites the BG505 SOSIP.664 precedent, and the reference list is current. The main issues are resolvability defects (garbled DOI in ref [32], misspelled first author in ref [33]) and one potential attribution overclaim ("efficacy" for what may be immunogenicity data). These are fixable in revision and do not undermine the core contribution.
