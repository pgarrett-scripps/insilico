# Novelty & Contribution Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript reports the first cGMP manufacturing campaign for the N332-GT5 gp140 germline-targeting HIV Env immunogen, which has entered clinical evaluation in HVTN144. This is a real but incremental contribution: the manufacturing process is explicitly derived from the established BG505 SOSIP.664 pipeline (Dey et al., 2018, Biotechnol. Bioeng.), uses standard fed-batch CHO culture with commercial media, and a commercial transposase system. The paper's value lies in demonstrating that a new-generation engineered immunogen can be produced at cGMP scale with acceptable quality — a useful case study. However, the novelty is in the molecule, not the method, and the framing could be sharper about what is new versus what is transferred. The most significant omission is that the abstract claims the material was 'successfully administered' in HVTN144, yet the manuscript contains no clinical safety, reactogenicity, or immunogenicity data, so the reader cannot judge whether the manufacturing process actually delivered material that performed as intended.

## Weaknesses
- The central novelty — the manufacturing process itself — is largely transferred, not invented. (HARD) The downstream process is explicitly 'developed based on the process established for BG505 SOSIP.664' (Dey et al., 2018). The unit operations (2G12 affinity, MabSelect SuRe, Capto adhere, Planova 20N filtration) and their order are preserved from that platform. The upstream process uses commercially available Dynamis medium and standard fed-batch feeding with Cell Boost 7a/7b. The cell line uses a proprietary but commercially available transposase system (Leap-In). What, specifically, was newly engineered versus simply executed on a new molecule? The paper would benefit from a clear table or statement differentiating process elements that were newly designed for N332-GT5 from elements that were carried over from prior SOSIP manufacturing campaigns. Without this, the contribution reads as a competent but straightforward technology transfer to a new antigen.
- The abstract claims the material was 'successfully administered' in HVTN144, but no clinical data of any kind are presented. (HARD) The abstract states: 'Clinical material manufactured through this platform has been successfully administered in HVTN144.' The introduction repeats that the trial is evaluating 'safety and tolerability' and 'whether this immunogen can successfully prime BG18-class B cell responses in humans.' A reader opening the paper to learn about the clinical translation will find no dose, schedule, safety, reactogenicity, or immunogenicity data — not even a CONSORT-style enrollment figure. The paper is a process development and manufacturing report, which is perfectly valid on its own. But the abstract and introduction create an expectation that the 'translation to clinic' includes clinical evidence. Either remove clinical claims from the title/abstract, or add the available clinical data (even preliminary safety) that the manufacturing campaign enabled.
- The removal of preparative SEC is presented as a process simplification but was a forced accommodation, not a designed improvement. (SOFT) The manuscript states (Section 3.3.2): 'For the clinical cGMP supply run, there was a procurement challenge for Superdex 200pg resin.' The head-to-head comparison in Table 10 shows comparable purity, which is reassuring. However, the study was performed at small scale with a single loading condition — not a formal comparability exercise with multiple lots, accelerated stability, or immunogenicity equivalence. The three-step process is subsequently described as though it were the intended design. The paper should acknowledge that the original design included SEC and that its removal was contingency-driven, and should describe what a full comparability exercise would require.
- The manuscript does not compare its process performance (yield, purity, viral clearance) quantitatively against the published Dey et al. (2018) BG505 SOSIP.664 cGMP campaign, so the reader cannot assess whether the N332-GT5 process represents an improvement, equivalence, or regression relative to the closest industry benchmark.
- The Leap-In transposon system is described as a commercial ATUM platform with co-authors from ATUM; the paper should state whether its use for this immunogen involved any custom engineering beyond codon optimization, or whether it was a standard application of a commercial tool.
- The claim of '>99% trimeric purity' (abstract) is supported by negative-stain EM showing only trimers, but the SE-HPLC data in Table 11 show 98.6% (pilot) and 99.4% (cGMP) main peak — the EM claim appears to refer to morphology, not HPLC purity, and the wording conflates the two.
- The Bale et al. (2025) reference (cited as 'Bale et al., npj Vaccines, 2025') describes cGMP production of related Env trimers via a different expression method (electroporation); the manuscript does not discuss how its approach differs or what advantages the stable CHO cell line offers over transient electroporation for this class of molecules.
- No mention is made of product stability during long-term storage (e.g., ≥6 months at -80°C or -20°C), which is critical for a clinical trial material supply.
