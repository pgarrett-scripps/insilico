# Methodology Reviewer

## Summary
This is a solid, honestly reported process development paper whose core claims are mostly supported by appropriate designs — the hold-time stability study with pre-defined criteria, the robustness evaluations with explicit worst-case conditions, and the use of two orthogonal glycosylation methods are all well-constructed. However, several load-bearing claims outrun the evidence: the titer comparability across runs is confounded by different reference standards, the viral clearance scale-down model's comparability to manufacturing scale is asserted but never demonstrated, and the 'matching design specifications' glycan claim has no stated specification. These are fixable with additional data or narrowed claims, but as written they are not earned.

## Strengths
- The robustness evaluations with explicit worst-case yield and worst-case product quality conditions are a strong design feature, and the authors honestly report the Capto adhere yield sensitivity rather than hiding it.
- The hold-time stability study with pre-defined stability criteria (0.7% HMW/LMW change, 15% relative potency) is a well-designed approach with clear decision rules.
- The use of two orthogonal methods for glycosylation analysis, with the discrepancy between them acknowledged rather than papered over, is methodologically sound.

## Weaknesses
- Titer comparability across the three production runs is confounded by the use of different reference standards. The authors state "The difference in titer was mainly due to the reference material used" — three different standards were used across the three runs — yet conclude the process is "scalable and reproducible" citing "similar... BLI titers." Since BLI titer is a relative measurement calibrated against a reference standard, the productivity comparison cannot support the reproducibility claim; only the cell growth and product quality comparisons (absolute measurements) do. Resolve by re-assaying archived samples from all runs against a single reference standard, or narrow the claim to growth and product quality.
- The viral clearance claims (≥18.14 log XMuLV, ≥11.70 log MMV) rest on a scale-down model whose comparability to manufacturing scale is asserted but never demonstrated. The text says "A scale-down model of the purification process was developed to ensure comparability with the manufacturing-scale process" but presents no comparability data — step yields, product quality, or column performance at scale-down versus full scale. Without this, the clearance figures cannot be assumed to transfer to the manufacturing process. Resolve by presenting a comparability table for each unit operation included in the viral clearance study.
- The abstract claims "site-specific glycan occupancy matching design specifications," but no design specification is stated anywhere in the manuscript. The claim is unevaluable as written. Moreover, the two orthogonal methods disagree on occupancy at N185e, N611, and N625 — DeGlyPHER shows 50–90% occupancy while LC-MS shows prominent unoccupied peptides — and the manuscript acknowledges but does not resolve this. Resolve by stating the specification and reconciling the method discrepancy at these three sites.
- "Multiple cGMP batches" (abstract) is not supported by the evidence presented. The manuscript describes one GMP batch (the 50L GMP run); the 50L material supply and demonstration runs are non-GMP pilot-plant runs. The consistency claim rests on a single GMP batch plus one non-GMP pilot batch.

## Questions
- What is the stated design specification for site-specific glycan occupancy, and how was it derived?
- Can archived samples from all three production runs be re-assayed against a single reference standard to support the titer comparability claim?
- What comparability data demonstrate that the scale-down model used for viral clearance is representative of manufacturing scale?
