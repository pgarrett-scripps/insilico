# Methodology Reviewer

## Summary
This manuscript describes the cell-line development, upstream and downstream process design, and cGMP manufacturing of the N332-GT5 gp140 HIV vaccine candidate. The design is thorough for a process-engineering report: the authors established a stable CHO clone, optimized conditions in Ambr®250 bioreactors, scaled to 200-L single-use systems, and applied a three-step purification train with formal robustness and viral-clearance evaluations. The central design limitation is that critical process-performance baselines (clone ranking, purification yield, titer comparisons) rely on BLI titer values calibrated against different reference standards across experiments — the shifts in absolute titer between the RCB and MCB runs could reflect either genuine process differences or reference standard drift, and the design does not distinguish them. A softer concern is that the decision to remove preparative SEC was based on a single small-scale side-by-side comparison (n=1), which is not a formal process-characterization study. The evidence supports the claim that the process produced clinical-grade material, but a few quantitative comparisons in the paper (titer fold-change between runs, removal efficiencies) rest on measurement baselines that were not designed to be comparable.

## Strengths
- The upstream process optimization used a high-throughput Ambr®250 system across multiple feeding, inoculum, pH, and temperature conditions, enabling systematic identification of a lead condition before committing to pilot scale.
- The three-step purification train was evaluated at small scale with worst-case yield and worst-case quality conditions for each chromatography step, providing formal robustness data rather than only center-point performance.
- The viral clearance study used two orthogonal model viruses (XMuLV, MMV) across four unit operations and reported LRV values with standard deviations, which is standard practice for process validation.

## Weaknesses
- The claim that the process "scaled efficiently" from Ambr®250 to 200-L rests on BLI titer as the primary comparability metric, but the three production runs (50-L RCB, demonstration, GMP) each used a different reference standard for the BLI assay: Lot 19Apr0088 for the RCB run, KBI S-20210314-0001-SD2-E-M for the demonstration run, and KBI P65 for the GMP run. The reported Day-14 bioreactor titers — 562, 355 and 390 mg/L — cannot be meaningfully compared across runs because a change in reference standard is a change in the measurement scale. This is a HARD weakness for any comparative claim about titer across runs, and the scale-up conclusion cannot rely on these numbers. The authors note this briefly in the text ("mainly due to reference material") but then draw the conclusion anyway; the design does not include a cross-run bridging study that would allow the comparison to be made.
