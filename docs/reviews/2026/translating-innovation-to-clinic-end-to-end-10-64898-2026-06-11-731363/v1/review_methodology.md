# Methodology Reviewer

## Summary
This is a comprehensive bioprocess development and cGMP manufacturing study for N332-GT5 gp140 HIV vaccine candidate. From a methodology perspective, the work demonstrates sound experimental design across cell line development, upstream process optimization, and downstream purification. The study includes appropriate controls, robustness evaluations, and scale-up demonstrations that support the main claims of process scalability and reproducibility. However, there are some design limitations that prevent a higher score: the lack of independent replication of the final cGMP batch, limited discussion of potential confounds in the Ambr250 optimization, and some ambiguity in how reference standards were selected and validated. The work is methodologically rigorous for a manufacturing/process development context but has minor gaps in experimental design that warrant clarification.

## Strengths
- Cell line development includes two rounds of clone ranking with explicit selection criteria (BG18/PGT145 ratio, furin cleavage efficiency, growth characteristics), reducing selection bias and supporting the choice of clone C235 as representative.
- Genetic stability is directly tested via 60 population doubling cultures in presence and absence of glutamine, with multiple stability indicators (productivity, transgene copy number, mRNA identity), ruling out the confound that apparent stability is merely transient.
- Upstream process optimization uses a factorial design (12 Ambr250 conditions, Table 1) with explicit control arms (Control #1, Control #2) and multiple test parameters (feed concentration, bolus vs. slow addition, temperature shift timing, pH), enabling isolation of individual variable effects.
- Scale-up is demonstrated across three independent runs (50L RCB, 50L MCB, 50L GMP) using matched process parameters, with comparable cell growth kinetics and harvest yields (91.4%, 90.8%, 91%) across scales, supporting scalability claims.
- Downstream process robustness is explicitly evaluated under theoretical worst-case yield and worst-case product quality conditions (Table 3, Table 7), with results showing acceptable performance even at extremes, strengthening claims about process robustness.
- Viral clearance study uses two orthogonal model viruses (XMuLV, MMV) representing enveloped and non-enveloped classes, with individual unit operation clearance measured separately and summed, providing independent validation of each step's contribution.
- Intermediate hold-time stability is systematically evaluated across two temperatures and multiple time points (Table 4, Figure 15), with explicit acceptance criteria (≤0.7% change in HMW/LMW), enabling operationally practical manufacturing windows to be defined.
- Product characterization uses two complementary mass spectrometry methods (DeGlyPHER and LC-MS glycoproteomics) for glycosylation analysis, providing orthogonal validation of site-specific occupancy and glycan processing.

## Weaknesses
- The final cGMP batch (50L GMP run) is presented as a single execution without independent replication; while pilot-scale and RCB runs provide some support for reproducibility, the actual clinical material rests on one manufacturing run, limiting confidence in batch-to-batch consistency claims.
- Ambr250 optimization (Section 2.4.2, Table 1) tests 12 conditions but does not explicitly randomize or balance the order of execution, raising the possibility of time-dependent confounds (e.g., media degradation, instrument drift) that could bias titer or growth measurements across conditions.
- Reference standards used for BLI titer measurement differ across the three scale-up runs (IA VI/Scripps lot 19Apr0088 for RCB; KBI lot S-20210314-0001-SD2-E-M for MCB; KBI lot P65 for GMP), and the manuscript acknowledges this explains titer differences (Section 3.2.3: 'The difference in titer was mainly due to the reference material used'). However, no validation is provided that these reference standards are equivalent or traceable to a common standard, making absolute titer comparisons across runs difficult to interpret.
- The choice to remove the preparative SEC step from the cGMP process (Section 3.3.2) is justified by a small-scale comparison (Table 10) showing comparable product quality, but this evaluation uses only pilot-scale material as the control. No independent replication of the SEC-removal decision using fresh material or a second operator is provided.
- Clone C235 is selected based on performance in 14-day fed-batch runs (Section 3.1.3), but no direct comparison is made between the top-ranked clone and the next-best alternative under identical conditions to quantify the performance margin and rule out the possibility that a different clone would have performed similarly.
- Negative-stain EM analysis (Section 2.7.2) reports 6,086 particles analyzed and states 'nearly 100% native-like trimers' but does not provide the actual percentage or a statistical confidence interval, making it unclear whether the claim is based on all particles or a subset, and whether the measurement uncertainty is acceptable for the conclusion.

## Questions
- Section 3.2.3 states that BLI titers differed across the three scale-up runs 'mainly due to the reference material used,' but were the three reference standards (lots 19Apr0088, S-20210314-0001-SD2-E-M, and P65) validated for equivalence or traceability to a primary standard? If not, how can the absolute titer values be compared across runs to support the scalability claim?
- In the Ambr250 optimization (Table 1), what was the order of execution of the 12 conditions, and were they randomized or balanced to control for time-dependent confounds such as media lot changes or instrument calibration drift?
- For the preparative SEC removal study (Table 10), was the comparison performed with material from a single pilot-scale run, or were multiple independent pilot runs used to generate the control and test materials? If a single run, does this limit the generalizability of the decision to remove SEC from the cGMP process?
- The nsEM analysis (Section 3.6.2) states that 6,086 particles were analyzed and that 'all particles have a similar phenotype' and 'nearly 100% native-like trimers' were observed. What is the exact percentage of particles classified as native-like trimers, and what is the confidence interval or measurement uncertainty?
- Clone C235 was selected from 24 clones based on growth, productivity, and product quality (Section 3.1.3). Was a direct head-to-head comparison performed between clone C235 and the next-best clone under identical 14-day fed-batch conditions to quantify the performance margin and rule out the possibility that alternative clones would have been equally suitable?
- In the downstream robustness evaluations (Table 3, Table 7), were the theoretical worst-case yield and worst-case product quality conditions based on prior data, statistical modeling, or expert judgment? If the latter, how were the parameter ranges selected to ensure they represent realistic manufacturing extremes?
- The manuscript states that the GMP run achieved 91% harvest yield and 22.6 g total output (Section 3.2.3), but does not report the total amount of clinical material produced or the number of doses manufactured. Can the authors clarify the total clinical supply and whether it was sufficient for the HVTN144 trial?
