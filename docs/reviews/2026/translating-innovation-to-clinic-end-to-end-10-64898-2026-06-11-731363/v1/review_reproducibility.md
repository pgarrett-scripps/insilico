# Reproducibility Reviewer

## Summary
The process is described in unusual operational detail — hold-time studies with explicit stability thresholds, robustness conditions per unit operation, and stepwise impurity-clearance tracking go well beyond the typical manufacturing paper. But the two most load-bearing quantitative claims — ≥18-log viral clearance and site-specific glycan occupancy matching design — are not reproducible from this manuscript: the viral-clearance scale-down model and virus assays are entirely undescribed, and the mass spectrometry data behind the glycan calls has no deposition. The three production runs' titers are also not anchored to a common standard, and the harvest-yield percentages do not trace to the stated numbers. With a proper data-availability section, the viral-clearance study details, and cross-standard calibration, this becomes reproducible; as written, an outside group cannot regenerate the headline numbers. I review from text only; I could not inspect the figures themselves, and I have lowered confidence on claims that rest on them.

## Weaknesses
- Viral clearance claim is unreproducible as written. The abstract's headline "robust viral clearance exceeding 18-log" rests on Table 12, which reports mean ± SD LRV for XMuLV and MMV across four unit operations. But neither the scale-down model used (column dimensions, bed heights, linear flow rates, load ratios, and the comparability criteria against manufacturing scale) nor the virus assays (quantification method, spike titer, cytotoxicity/interference controls, replicate structure, assay LOQ/LOD) is described anywhere. "A scale-down model of the purification process was developed to ensure comparability" is an assertion, not a procedure — a reader cannot tell whether the ≥18-log figure is a measured value or an assay-limited lower bound, and an independent group cannot recover the LRV numbers end-to-end. What would settle it: a per-unit-operation table of scale-down versus manufacturing parameters, plus the virus-assay protocol with per-run LRV values and assay limits.
- Glycan occupancy claims rest on inaccessible data, and the two methods disagree on the same sites. The claim of "high glycan occupancy at most sites" (with N625 ~50% unoccupied, per DeGlyPHER) rests on mass spectrometry data with no repository accession (no MassIVE/PRIDE/ProteomeXchange). More seriously, the manuscript's own text reports that at N185e, N611, and N625, the LC-MS method detected "only non-occupied peptides," while DeGlyPHER shows 50–90% occupancy at those same sites (Figure 17 vs Figure 18). The occupancy answer is method-dependent, so it is undefined which result constitutes the "design specifications" match. Settle it: deposit raw MS data for both methods with accessions, report a reconciled per-site occupancy table with replicate counts, and state how the disagreement was adjudicated for the design-match claim.
- Titer comparability across production runs is not anchored to a common standard, and harvest yields don't compute. The three production runs used three different BLI reference standards (IAVI Lot 19Apr0088; KBI S-20210314-0001-SD2-E-M; KBI P65), and the authors attribute the titer differences to this — but no cross-calibration between standards is reported, so Figure 14c plots three runs in incomparable units and the "consistent product quality across multiple cGMP batches" claim cannot be checked quantitatively for titer. Separately, the "harvest yield" percentages (91.4%, 90.8%, 91%) do not trace to the stated numbers: 399 mg/L clarified harvest over 562 mg/L bioreactor titer is 71%, not 91.4%, and the denominators used are undefined. Settle it: re-measure all three runs' material against one common standard, and give the harvest-yield formula with volumes.
