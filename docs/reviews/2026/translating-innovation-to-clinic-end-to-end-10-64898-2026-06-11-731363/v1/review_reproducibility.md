## Summary
This is a candid, workmanlike account of taking a difficult glycoprotein immunogen from cell line to cGMP drug substance. But from the reproducibility lane the manuscript currently fails on its two central comparative claims: the run-to-run titer comparison is calibrated against three different internal reference standards, and the headline genetic-stability result is never actually shown. Several scale-critical parameters (feed volume definitions, Amberlite resin ratio, 2G12 column geometry, viral-clearance scale-down qualification) are missing or unreadable. The fixes are mostly re-analysis and reporting of existing data rather than new experiments, but as written an independent group could not reproduce the outputs. I could not inspect the figures themselves; the nsEM and glycomics conclusions reach me only through text, and my confidence on those specific artifacts is correspondingly lower.

## Weaknesses
- The central claim — "the fed-batch process … is scalable and reproducible across both cell bank sources and vessel scales" — is supported only by titers read on three different reference standards. The manuscript reports Day-14 titers of 562 mg/L (RCB, XDR-50), 355 mg/L (MCB, XDR-200) and 390.2 mg/L (GMP, MCB, XDR-200), and states "the difference in titer was mainly due to the reference material used," citing three lots (IAVI/Scripps 19Apr0088; KBI S-20210314-0001-SD2-E-M; KBI P65). The alternative the authors did not exclude: the MCB-based cultures genuinely produce ~35–40% less than the RCB-based run, and the reference-standard explanation is post-hoc. This is unfalsifiable as presented because no two runs were ever read on a common scale. What would settle it: re-assay stored harvest samples from all three runs against a single reference standard and report the adjusted titers. If they converge within the assay's accepted variability, the claim holds; if the RCB/MCB split persists, the process differs between cell banks. The authors' own >100% step yield for 2G12, attributed to "titer assay variability," reinforces that the assay's comparator problem is real and acknowledged.
- The abstract's claim of "genetic stability through 60 population doublings" has no corresponding results. Section 2.3.8 describes the design (60-PD cultures ±5 mM glutamine; readouts of productivity, transgene copy number, N332/furin mRNA identity), but Section 3 contains no PD0-vs-PD60 data anywhere — no productivity comparison, no copy-number or mRNA-identity table. The RCB/MCB qualification and the entire 200-L campaign rest on this result, and a reader cannot check it. The missing artifact is a single figure/table of the 60-PD metrics; note also that the transgene copy-number assay itself is never described (method, instrument, primers).
- Both quantitative axes — titer and relative potency — are calibrated against internal reference materials that are not deposited or characterized beyond a lot number. The GMP BDS "percent trimer" is reported as "116% compared to Reference" (an unidentified KBI lot), and all Octet titers trace to one of three proprietary lots. An independent lab cannot recover an absolute titer or potency from this manuscript. Similarly, the expression construct is "codon-optimized using ATUM's proprietary algorithm" with no sequence given, and the C235 RCB/MCB is an ATUM-internal line with no accession or access path. For a process paper, the exact transgene sequence is a load-bearing input; any reproduction must re-synthesize the gene, and the sequence is not available.
- The Ambr250 optimization that selected the final process cannot be reconstructed from the text. Table 1 is garbled — rows 8–9 lack condition names entirely (only stray ">" and pH fragments), so the 12-condition design is not fully recoverable. Base DO and pH control setpoints for the Ambr250 runs are never stated (only the pH test arms), and the feed schedule is given as "27% CB7a slow, 2.7% CB7b bolus" without defining the base — percent of what, delivered over what interval, from which days? The same gap affects the 50-L runs, which use "boluses according to predefined feed schedules" but never tabulate the schedule. A replicator cannot compute a single feed volume.
- The 2G12 capture step omits the column bed height, load pH/conductivity, residence time, and the resin/ligand coupling chemistry; only the equilibration and elution buffers are given, so the step cannot be sized.
- The Amberlite XAD-2 step specifies circulation time (120–150 min) but not the resin-to-product volume ratio — the scale-critical parameter needed to reproduce the <0.002% residual Triton X-100 result.
- The viral-clearance scale-down model is asserted to "ensure comparability with the manufacturing-scale process," but no qualification data (LRV agreement with manufacturing scale, load-matrix comparability, virus spike-to-load ratio, which should be ≤5% to avoid altering the load) are shown; without this, the ≥18.14/≥11.70 total LRVs are not tied to the GMP process.
- No data availability statement exists: raw MS data behind the Fig. 17/18 site-specific glycan occupancy percentages are not deposited (no accession or DOI), so those percentages cannot be recomputed.
- Table 2's column headers are misaligned with its values — the GMP XDR-200 column appears to show a Day-0 weight of 34.0 ± 0.5 kg, which is implausible given the 87.6 kg harvest recovered; initial working volumes cannot be reliably attributed to each run.
- Bed heights are given only for MabSelect SuRe and Capto adhere; the "10–20 cm ID" pilot and "14–45 cm ID" cGMP columns lack a per-step bed-height/residence-time comparison, so scale comparability of the chromatography train cannot be assessed.
- The contributions section credits the authors with cryo-EM structure determination, but no cryo-EM data appear anywhere in the manuscript — readers cannot tell which structural claims rest on the 6,086-particle nsEM dataset alone.

## Questions
- What was the measured inter-assay variability (%CV) of the Octet titer method on identical samples, and can stored harvests from all three runs be re-assayed on a single reference standard to produce a common-scale titer comparison?
- What is the Amberlite XAD-2 resin-to-product volume ratio used in section 2.5.4.4?
- Was the viral-clearance scale-down model qualified against manufacturing scale (LRV agreement, load composition, pressure/flow), and what was the virus spike-to-load ratio in each study?
- Does the 60-PD genetic stability dataset exist in analyzable form and can it be included as a results figure/table?
