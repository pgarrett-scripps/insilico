# Clarity & Presentation Reviewer

## Summary
This is a dense, generally well-organized development report whose essential narrative — cell line → media adaptation → scale-up → purification → characterization → viral clearance — is recoverable on a first read. The main argument, however, rests on several values the paper itself makes impossible to interpret: condition identity, reference-standard comparability, and conflicting occupancy measurements between two mass-spectrometry methods. Several figure captions lack axis definitions, and there are outright textual contradictions in the chromatography descriptions. These are presentation defects in the sense that a competent reader cannot determine exactly what was done, not merely that the prose is rough.

## Strengths
- The development narrative is genuinely end-to-end and chronological, so the reader can reconstruct the sequence of decisions (media adaptation → Ambr 250 → pilot → GMP) from the text alone.
- The figure chart for Table 1/2 gives explicit comparable parameters: the deliberate identification of differences between scales is the right way to present scale-up reproducibility.
- Where the authors were uncertain — e.g., DeGly vs. LyMS disagreement on occupancy, the removal of preparative SEC — they flag it rather than burying it.

## Weaknesses
- HARD — Three titer values compared against three different reference standards make the reproducibility claim uncheckable. Section 3.2.3 reports the 50-L RCB run at 562 mg/L (IAVI reference, Lot 19Apr0088), the MCB run at 355 mg/L (KBI reference), and the GMP run at 390 mg/L (KBI reference P65): "The difference in titer was mainly sagged by the reference material used." No normalization data, cross-calibrated, or dilution curves are shown. A reader cannot determine whether "355 vs. 562" reflects the product or the artifact. Resolution: either state the yield assay as the replication, report all three runs relative to a single calibrated reference standard (normalized values and curves), or provide the cross-referencing of the three standards with ED50 or Kd values per standard.
- HARD: The site-occupancy results of the two mass-spectrometry measures are presented but never numerically reconciled; the textual claim, "high glycan occupancy at most sites" (3.6.1), outruns one of the figures. DeGlyphoR is reported showing N625 ~50% unoccupied and N185e/N611/N618 10–20% unoccupied; the second method "only non-occupied peptides were prominent" at N185e, N611, N625. The text acknowledges with "revealing that assessment of occupancy exhibits higher variation between methods" — but never gives numbers for both methods per site. As written, a reader cannot decide whether the product is mostly occupied (as claimed) or half-empty (as suggested by the LC-MS peptide pool). Resolution: a small table with per-site occupancy (DeGlyPHER %) and (LC-MS %, with peptide- and glycan-detection thresholds) for all 27 sites.
- HARD: The peak-collection window in the protein-A flowthrough step is stated as contradictory. Section 2.5.4.5: "product collection was defined between ≥ 0.05 AU/cm and ≤ 0.05 AU/cm," both thresholds being equal is an empty window. Even if the reader guesses the intent (from rising to falling 0.05 AU/cm), the written condition leaves the step irreproducible. One line — or a revised sentence stating the collection window DURING rise and fall.
- HARD (and the title case of ambiguity): a central claim is made in the abstract but entirely absent from the body. The abstract ends with "Clinical material manufactured through this platform has been successfully administered in HVTN144." The body never describes dose, route, number of participants, trial status, or where the reader might check this. A claim that "the vaccine has been administered in humans" is precisely the type of sentence a reviewer must not have to take on faith. If this is correct, the body needs a clinical-trial-data statement (trial ID, dates, progress status). If true but unverifiable here, the sentence should be removed from the abstract.

## Questions
- For §3.2.3: Run all three BLI titers against one common reference and report normalized values; likewise, list the detection limits and normalization curves for both references, neither of which is provided.
- For §3.2.2 / Table 1: The conditions "Control #1" and "Control #2" are listed with identical parameters (27% CB7a slow, 2.7% CB7b bolus, 34°C Day 4). What differentiates them? (If it is the inoculum density or another parameter, that must be in the table.)
- For §3.6.1: Provide the per-site occupancy table for both methods (as in weakness 2); in particular, specify the assigned glycoman count for N625 for that position 50% vs. "mostly non-occupied."
- For §2.5.4.5: Is the assignment window "≥ 0.05 AU/cm on the ascending shoulder to ≤ 0.05 AU/cm on the descending shoulder"? State the actual protein load per L of column and the collection start/end definitions.
- For §2.1 (parent cell line derivation): "HD BIOP3 is a GS-null cell line derived from ECACC CHOK1 established by Horizon Discovery" — does "established by Horizon Discovery" refer to HD BIOP3 or CHOK1? If the former, the "HD" abbreviation should be removed to avoid ambiguity.
