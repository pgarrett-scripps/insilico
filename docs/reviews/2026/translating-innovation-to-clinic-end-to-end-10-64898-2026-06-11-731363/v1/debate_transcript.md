# Debate Transcript

## Advocate — round 1

This manuscript should be accepted with minor revisions because it delivers exactly what it promises: a rigorous, transparent account of translating a rationally designed HIV immunogen into clinical material now dosed in HVTN144. Every reviewer scored it 4/5 or higher, and the panel's own strengths lists confirm the core claims are well-supported: clone selection with explicit BG18/PGT145 ratio criteria, genetic stability through 60 PDs with three orthogonal indicators, viral clearance of ≥18.14 and ≥11.70 logs against industry benchmarks (Table 12), robustness testing under theoretical worst-case yield/quality conditions (Table 3, 7-9), and orthogonal glycan characterization (DeGlyPHER plus intact-glycopeptide LC-MS, Figures 17-18) showing agreement on occupancy trends. This is a translational bioprocess paper, and the rigor reviewer confirms the authors 'appropriately acknowledge where performance degrades' rather than concealing variability (e.g., Capto adhere yield dropping to 24% under worst-case conditions).//Most identified weaknesses are genuinely fixable in revision rather than fatal: defining WCPQ/WCY at first use, clarifying Figure 7/14/18 legends, stating replicate numbers for hold-time and EM classification, correcting the malformed DOI, adding Gulla et al. as a comparator, and softening 'reproducible'/'multiple cGMP batches' language to match the single-batch reality. None of these undermine the underlying data—they are presentation and calibration issues, exactly the kind of gap peer review exists to catch before final publication. The methodology reviewer's harshest concerns (single cGMP batch, differing reference standards) are already partially disclosed by the authors themselves ('The difference in titer was mainly due to the reference material used'), showing good-faith transparency rather than concealment—a genuine strength, not merely a fixable flaw. The ethics reviewer found no gaps beyond routine data-availability disclosure. Given unanimous 4-5 scores and no reviewer recommending rejection, this paper merits acceptance conditional on the specific, enumerated textual corrections.

## Skeptic — round 1

The advocate's strongest concession—that authors 'disclosed' the reference-standard confound—actually proves my point rather than resolving it. Self-disclosure that titers differ 'mainly due to the reference material used' (Sec 3.2.3) does not fix the confound; it admits that the paper cannot distinguish process variability from assay artifact across the three scale-up runs. That matters because the abstract's central claim—'consistent product quality across multiple cGMP batches'—is factually false on the manuscript's own data: only one cGMP batch exists (Sec 3.4). The rigor reviewer flags this as HARD, not stylistic. This isn't a wording tweak; it's the paper asserting reproducibility it hasn't demonstrated, from n=1 GMP runs with different reference standards, which the data_analysis reviewer independently calls statistically indefensible.

Compounding this, the removal of preparative SEC—a real change to the clinical manufacturing process—rests on a single unreplicated parallel comparison (Table 10), justified by supply-chain pressure rather than data sufficiency (novelty reviewer: 'pragmatic compromise, not scientific advance'; rigor reviewer: HARD, 'deserves more rigorous justification'). For material already dosed in a first-in-human trial, a critical unit-operation decision resting on one side-by-side run is a substantive gap, not a caption fix.

I concede the DOI typo, WCPQ/WCY definitions, figure legend clarity, and Gulla et al. omission are genuinely fixable. But the reproducibility and SEC-removal issues require the authors to either soften core claims substantially—undercutting the very 'scalable, reproducible paradigm' framing the advocate leans on—or provide data they don't currently have.
