the Venue Recommendations

## as_is

**Biotechnology Progress** — This is the most natural home for this work at its current quality. The manuscript is a process development and scale-up report for a biopharmaceutical product, which is squarely within this journal's scope. The candid treatment of process decisions (e.g., the preparative SEC removal study, the hold-time stability data) is exactly the kind of practical manufacturing insight this journal publishes. The panel's major revisions are mostly about claim precision and missing assay details rather than fundamental flaws; a journal with a strong process-engineering readership will find the work valuable even before those revisions are fully addressed. Acceptance odds: moderate — the topic is a strong fit, but the journal may also request the same clarifications.

**Biotechnology and Bioengineering** — Similar scope to Biotechnology Progress but with a slightly broader biotechnology readership. This journal has published related work (Dey et al. 2018, cited in the manuscript, appeared in a comparable venue). The viral clearance and scale-down model data are particularly well-aligned with this journal's interests. Acceptance odds: moderate.

## after_revision

**Biotechnology Progress** — If the authors address the required revisions (especially the titer comparability, preparative SEC justification, and relative potency assay description), this becomes a strong submission. The journal's readership will value the practical lessons on cGMP manufacturing of a complex glycoprotein immunogen. Acceptance odds: good.

**Biotechnology and Bioengineering** — With the revisions complete, the manuscript would have the rigor this journal expects. The preparative SEC removal study, if properly qualified with assay variability data, would be a useful contribution to the bioprocess literature. Acceptance odds: moderate-to-good.

**Vaccine** — If the authors add the requested context on HVTN144 (even a sentence on whether any immunogenicity data are available), this journal becomes viable. The translational narrative — from cell line development through cGMP manufacture to first-in-human trial — fits Vaccine's scope well. The viral clearance data and the discussion of manufacturing challenges for complex immunogens would appeal to this readership. Acceptance odds: moderate; the journal may want more emphasis on the clinical context.

## alternative

**PDA Journal of Pharmaceutical Science and Technology** — A solid fallback if the authors cannot fully resolve the titer comparability question or the preparative SEC justification. This journal publishes detailed process development and manufacturing science with less emphasis on novelty and more on practical utility. The viral clearance data alone would be publishable here. Acceptance odds: good.

**Journal of Pharmaceutical Sciences** — Another reasonable fallback, particularly if the authors want to emphasize the analytical characterization and product quality aspects. The glycan analysis and the comparison of two mass-spec methods could be positioned as a methods contribution. Acceptance odds: moderate-to-good.

**bioRxiv** (preprint) — If the authors want to establish priority and share the work quickly while pursuing a journal submission, posting to bioRxiv (perhaps in the "Biotechnology" or "Synthetic Biology" section) would make the work citable and discoverable. This is not a substitute for peer review but a complement to it.

---

**Notes on fit uncertainty:** I have moderate-to-high confidence in the fit for Biotechnology Progress and Biotechnology and Bioengineering, as both regularly publish bioprocess development and manufacturing science. Vaccine is a reasonable fit but the journal's scope leans toward immunology and clinical outcomes; the authors would need to emphasize the translational aspects. The PDA Journal and J. Pharm. Sci. are well-established but I am less certain about their current editorial priorities; the authors should check recent issues for similar process-development content.
