# Decision Letter

VERDICT: major

## Summary of Evaluation

This manuscript reports a substantial and genuinely useful achievement: the end-to-end development and cGMP manufacture of the N332-GT5 gp140 HIV Env trimer for the HVTN144 first-in-human trial. The work is technically demanding, the process narrative is unusually candid about its own limitations, and the viral clearance data (≥18 logs XMuLV, ≥11.7 logs MMV) substantially exceed industry benchmarks. The ethics and literature reviews are clean; the citation record is sound with only minor resolvability defects; and the novelty claim — first cGMP manufacture of this specific germline-targeting immunogen — is not preempted by prior work.

The panel's verdict distribution (1 accept, 2 minor, 5 major) reflects a consistent set of concerns rather than disagreement about the core science. The central claim — that N332-GT5 gp140 can be manufactured at clinical grade with preserved trimeric structure and antigenicity — is supported by the evidence. The problems are that several load-bearing claims are stated more strongly than the data warrant, and a few process decisions rest on evidence that is either incompletely reported or not statistically framed. These are fixable in revision, but they require more than textual polish: the authors must either supply missing data or requalify claims to match what is actually shown.

The most serious issues, in order:

1. **The preparative SEC removal is justified by a single, non-replicated comparison** (Table 10) with no stated acceptance criteria, no assay variability data, and no statistical analysis. The decision to delete a unit operation from a cGMP process — one that contributed to aggregate control — cannot rest on n=1 per condition without at least reporting assay precision so the reader can judge whether the observed differences (1.0% vs 0.8% LMW; 0.8% vs 0.5% pre-Main) exceed noise.

2. **The titer comparison across scales is not interpretable as written.** Three different reference standards were used for the three production runs (562, 355, and 390 mg/L), and the authors attribute the differences to this. That may be correct, but the "consistent product quality across scales" claim cannot be evaluated without either re-assaying all three harvests against a single standard or explicitly restricting the reproducibility claim to quality attributes (SE-HPLC, residual impurities) and excluding titer.

3. **The ">99% trimeric purity" claim in the abstract is not traceable to a single measurement.** SE-HPLC %Main Peak (98.6–99.4%) is not the same as trimer content; the nsEM "nearly 100% native-like trimers" is a qualitative 2D-classification assessment of 6,086 particles with no report of excluded particles. The abstract must be aligned with the specific assay supporting the claim.

4. **The relative potency assay is a listed quality attribute but is not described.** The BLI-based relative potency (116% vs reference for the cGMP batch) has no method description, no reference-standard qualification, no acceptance criteria, and no replicate information. For a product where this is the only functional readout, this is a significant gap.

5. **The glycan occupancy claim "matching design specifications" is unsupported.** No design specification for glycan occupancy is stated anywhere. The two mass-spec methods disagree at several sites (e.g., N625 ~50% unoccupied by DeGlyPHER vs predominantly occupied by LC-MS). The claim should be requalified to "characterized and consistent with a native-like trimer profile," with the discrepancies discussed.

6. **The "three-step purification" framing is misleading.** The actual process has eight unit operations (2G12 capture, UF/DF1, detergent inactivation, Amberlite adsorption, MabSelect SuRe, Capto adhere, virus filtration, UF/DF2). The abstract and conclusions should describe the process accurately.

7. **The "efficacy" claim for the NHP study is potentially over-attributed.** The manuscript states Steichen et al. 2024 demonstrated "remarkable efficacy" of N332-GT5/SMNP, but that paper reports priming and affinity maturation, not protective efficacy against challenge. The wording should be softened to "immunogenicity" unless the cited paper reports challenge data.

The manuscript is a sound, useful process-development report. The weaknesses are precision issues in claims, missing assay details, and statistical framing — all addressable in revision without new experiments. The authors should either supply the missing data (assay variability, replicate counts, cross-calibration) or explicitly requalify the claims to match the evidence presented.

## Required Revisions

1. **Resolve the titer comparability question.** Either re-assay the three production-run harvests (RCB/XDR-50, MCB/XDR-200, GMP) against a single common reference standard and report the corrected titers, or state explicitly that absolute titer comparability across scales was not assessed and restrict the reproducibility claim to growth, viability, and product quality attributes. Report the BLI assay precision (replicate measurements, assay CV) so the reader can judge the significance of the observed differences.

2. **Justify or requalify the preparative SEC removal.** Provide the number of replicate runs per arm of the Table 10 study, the pre-defined acceptance criteria for "comparable," and the assay repeatability (%RSD) for SE-HPLC and RP-HPLC at the observed impurity levels. If the study was n=1 per condition, state this explicitly and requalify the claim from "comparable" to "within the limits of a single non-replicated comparison."

3. **Align the abstract and conclusions with the actual measurements.** Replace ">99% trimeric purity" with the specific assay supporting it (e.g., ">99% main peak by SE-HPLC" or "native-like trimers confirmed by negative-stain EM"). Correct the "three-step purification" description to reflect the actual eight unit operations. Requalify "matching design specifications" for glycan occupancy to "characterized and consistent with a native-like trimer profile," and discuss the N625 discrepancy between the two mass-spec methods.

4. **Describe the relative potency assay.** Provide the full method for the BLI-based relative potency: capture antibody, reference standard identity and qualification, dilution scheme, acceptance criteria, and replicate information. State whether the reference standard was bridged to any characterized material and how its stability was assessed.

5. **Correct the "efficacy" attribution.** Soften "remarkable efficacy" to "immunogenicity" or "priming of BG18-class precursors" for the Steichen et al. 2024 citation, unless that paper reports challenge data. Confirm the "20 of 27 antibodies with Kd ≤ 1 μM" figure against the published supplementary data of Steichen et al. 2019.

6. **Report the nsEM particle accounting.** State what fraction of the 6,086 particles was excluded during 2D classification and whether any non-trimeric classes (dimers, monomers, aggregates) were observed.

7. **Provide the viral clearance scale-down qualification data.** The manuscript states a scale-down model was developed but shows no comparability data (step yields, product quality at both scales). Provide these data or state explicitly that the scale-down model was not qualified and the LRV values are from a single study.

8. **Fix the citation defects.** Correct the corrupted DOI for Parks et al. 2025 (ref [32]) to 10.1126/scitranslmed.ady6831. Correct "Sander et al." to "Sanders et al." (ref [33]). Provide the full citation for Bale et al. 2025 (ref [3]) including volume, article number, and DOI. Correct "Pujani" to "Punjani" for the CryoSPARC citation. Confirm whether refs [9] and [31] are cited in the text or should be removed from the bibliography.

9. **Add the missing methods and reagent details.** Provide: (a) a statement of whether "±" values in Tables 7–9 and 12 are SD or SEM; (b) replicate counts for the Ambr250 study (n=1 per condition appears to be the case but is not stated), pilot-scale runs, and mass-spec analyses; (c) vendor/catalog/RRID information for PGT145, BG18_GL0, and DEN3 antibodies; (d) a data-availability statement and mass-spec repository accession (PRIDE/MassIVE) for the glycomics data; (e) software versions for CryoSPARC, Leginon, Byos, ProLuCID, and GlycoMSQuant; (f) a mycoplasma testing statement for the CHO cell line.

## Minor Suggestions

1. Define "WCPQ" and "WCY" at first use in Table 3 (worst-case product quality and worst-case yield, presumably).
2. Define "detectable productivity" at first use in Section 3.1.
3. State explicitly that the overall viral clearance LRV values are a sum across orthogonal steps, not a single-step measurement.
4. Clarify whether the "clinical material administered in HVTN144" refers to the specific batch described here or to material from the same process, and whether any immunogenicity or safety data from that trial are available to cite.
5. Report the actual Day 0 and Day 1 %HMW values for the UF/DF1 retentate in the hold-time stability study that led to the ≤1-day recommendation.
6. State the n for the DeGlyPHER glycan occupancy measurements in Figure 17.
7. Provide a biosafety or institutional oversight statement for the cell culture and manufacturing work.
8. Consider adding a table comparing step yields and residual impurity profiles against the BG505 SOSIP.664 platform (Dey et al. 2018) to sharpen the differentiation of this work.
