# Decision Letter

**Decision:** minor

## Summary of Evaluation
Eight reviewers converged on a positive assessment (1 accept, 7 minor; confidence-weighted 4.15/5), and I concur with the draft recommendation of minor revision. The manuscript reports a complete, honestly documented bioprocess translation — cell line development through cGMP manufacture of N332-GT5 gp140 for HVTN144 — with a technically sound core: 60-PD genetic stability assessed by three orthogonal indicators, worst-case robustness studies, large viral clearance margins, orthogonal DeGlyPHER and LC-MS glycan characterization, and nsEM confirmation of native-like trimers. Transparency about degraded performance under worst-case conditions (e.g., Capto adhere yield at 24%) is a genuine strength.

The substantive concerns raised by the rigor and data_analysis reviewers are claim-calibration problems rather than data problems. The abstract's reference to 'multiple cGMP batches' is a factual error given a single cGMP run; the 'scalable, reproducible manufacturing paradigm' framing outruns three n=1 runs measured against three non-cross-validated reference standards; the SEC removal rests on one unreplicated side-by-side comparison made under supply-chain constraint. The authors concede all of these and propose precise, adequate textual remedies. Because material from this batch is already in humans, I am converting those concessions into non-negotiable conditions rather than suggestions — but none of them requires new experiments, and none overturns a reported result.

I weighed the rebuttal on the three disagreements. On the n=1 comparability point, the authors' argument that single-lot GMP tech transfer is conventionally assessed by comparability of process parameters and quality attributes rather than replicate hypothesis testing is credible and consistent with regulatory practice; I therefore do not require statistical testing, only the softened language and the explicit reference-standard confound disclosure they offer. On the Ambr250 randomization concern, the authors' point that all 12 vessels ran in parallel within one campaign on identical media lots defeats the time-drift confound; no action required beyond stating the parallel design plainly in Section 2.4.2. On novelty, the authors are correct that the paper is framed as a translational case study, though the abstract sentence quoted in their own rebuttal is precisely the one that overreaches, so the reframing required below resolves this too. The AI-disclosure point is satisfied by their proposed one-line statement.

The methods audit returned 11 HARD gaps. Most are additive reporting items (mycoplasma testing status, MS data accession, viral clearance quantification method and virus sourcing, n and error-bar definitions, software versions, transgene copy-number method). None prevents evaluation of the central claims — the viral clearance and characterization conclusions are legible from what is reported — but they must be supplied. The IACUC item concerns a study conducted and reported elsewhere (Steichen et al. 2024) and needs only an explicit statement of delegation. The citation audit found no HARD gaps; the 30 unverifiable entries are an artifact of tooling and are handled as verification requests.

## Required Revisions
1. Correct the factual error in the abstract and Section 3.4: replace 'consistent product quality across multiple cGMP batches' with language reflecting a single cGMP batch (e.g., 'consistent product quality between the pilot-scale demonstration run and the single cGMP batch manufactured for HVTN144'), and remove plural 'batches' phrasing throughout the manuscript.
2. Downgrade the 'scalable, reproducible manufacturing paradigm' framing in the abstract and Conclusions to what the data support: consistent process performance across two cell banks and three sequential runs at two vessel scales. Add an explicit sentence in Section 3.2.3 stating that absolute BLI titer comparisons across runs are confounded by the use of three non-cross-validated reference standards (lots 19Apr0088, S-20210314-0001-SD2-E-M, P65).
3. Revise Section 3.3.2 to state plainly that removal of preparative SEC (Superdex 200pg) was a supply-chain-driven contingency supported by a single unreplicated side-by-side comparison (Table 10), and add an explicit limitations sentence on the residual risk that impurity classes uniquely cleared by SEC were not exhaustively characterized.
4. Narrow the abstract claim of 'preserved native-like antigenicity' to 'preserved recognition by germline-targeting and quaternary-structure-specific antibodies (BG18_GL0, PGT145) and native-like trimeric architecture by nsEM', since no functional immunogenicity assay is reported here.
5. Replace the qualitative nsEM claim of 'nearly 100% native-like trimers' in Section 3.6.2 and the Figure 19 legend with the exact percentage of particles classified as trimeric in 2D class averages and the explicit classification criterion (e.g., 3-lobed compact morphology consistent with reference SOSIP structures).
6. Complete the viral clearance methods (Section 2.6): specify the virus quantification method (TCID50, plaque assay, qPCR, etc.), the spike titers/concentrations with units, the source, strain and lot for XMuLV and MMV, and the number of independent determinations (n) underlying the mean ± SD values in Table 12. Add the key scale-down model parameters (column dimensions, flow rates, load ratios) and the comparability criteria used.
7. State n and the variability measure (SD, SEM, or CI) explicitly for every figure and table displaying error bars or averaged values, including Figures 7, 9, 10, 12–17, the relative-potency data, and hold-time data; state whether replicates are biological or technical, and whether any significance testing was performed (and if not, say so).
8. Add a Data and Software Availability statement specifying access conditions for raw chromatography, BLI, MS, and EM data given proprietary/regulatory constraints, and deposit or explain the non-deposition of the DeGlyPHER/LC-MS glycoproteomics data (PRIDE/MassIVE accession where possible). List version numbers for Byos, IP2, ProLuCID, DTASelect2, Census2, GlycoMSQuant, CFX Manager, Leginon, and CryoSPARC.
9. Report mycoplasma testing status for the HD BIOP3 parental line, the stable pool, and clone C235/the banked cells, and state the method used to determine transgene copy number in Section 2.3.8 (qPCR, ddPCR, Southern blot, etc.), along with the definition and calculation of population doubling.
10. Cite and discuss Gulla et al. (2021) in the Discussion, contrasting affinity-capture with non-affinity cGMP purification strategies for Env trimers; correct the Parks et al. (2025) DOI to 10.1126/scitranslmed.ady6831; complete the Bale et al. (2025) reference with volume/issue/DOI; add the missing CryoSPARC reference (Punjani et al., 2017 — note the spelling) to the reference list; and reconcile the Sanders et al. year discrepancy (2015 in text vs. 2013 in the reference list).
11. Define 'worst-case product quality (WCPQ)' and 'worst-case yield (WCY)' at first use in Section 2.5.1 and repeat the definitions in the Table 3 legend.
12. Make Figures 7, 14, 16, and 18 self-contained: state axis units explicitly (e.g., mg/L for BLI titer), label panels (a)/(b)/(c) against their content, define error bars and n, and add a glycan category color key directly to Figure 18.
13. State the clone-selection rules for the 213→56→24 reductions in Section 3.1.2 — whether top-N rank-ordered or threshold-based, the specific metrics, and any tie-breaking criteria.
14. Add a short paragraph in Section 3.2.3 clarifying the lineage, derivation, and qualification of the three reference materials, and state explicitly whether any cross-calibration between lots was performed.
15. State in Section 2.5 which elements of the downstream process were adopted unchanged from Dey et al. (2018) and which were modified, so that the process is reproducible from this manuscript without recourse to the prior work.
16. Add an explicit statement that the rhesus macaque preclinical work referenced in the Introduction was conducted and reported under Steichen et al. (2024), with ethical approval covered in that publication and no animal work performed for the present study.

## Minor Suggestions
- Add the proposed one-line statement confirming that no generative AI tools were used in data analysis, figure generation, or manuscript drafting; this satisfies the ethics reviewer's point.
- State in Section 2.4.2 that all 12 Ambr250 vessels were run in parallel within a single campaign on identical media lots — this makes the rebuttal's (persuasive) answer to the randomization concern visible to readers.
- Provide vendor/catalog or in-house production details and, where available, RRIDs for PGT145, BG18_GL0, and DEN3, rather than delegating solely to Steichen et al. (2019).
- Provide an availability statement or accession for the N332-GT5 gp140 and furin expression constructs (repository deposit or MTA route), even if commercial constraints prevent public deposition.
- Annotate the SDS-PAGE panels in Figures 8 and 11 with size markers and expected molecular weights for cleaved versus uncleaved species to support the furin cleavage conclusion.
- Supply DOIs or PMIDs for Dey et al. (2018), Rameez et al. (2014), and Shukla & Aranha (2015), which the citation audit could not resolve and which support load-bearing methodological and acceptance-criteria claims.
- Add digestion conditions (enzyme:substrate ratio, time, temperature) for the trypsin/chymotrypsin/alpha-lytic protease workflow rather than delegating entirely to Watanabe et al. (2020).
- Consider a brief code-availability note for any custom analysis scripts used with Census2 or GlycoMSQuant.
