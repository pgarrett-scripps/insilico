# Citation Integrity Audit

**Manuscript:** "Translating Innovation to Clinic: End-to-End Bioprocess Development and cGMP Manufacturing of N332-GT5 HIV Vaccine Candidate for First-in-Human Trials HVTN144"

**Auditor role:** Citation Integrity Auditor — checking resolvability of references and whether claims attributed to prior work are supported by it. No score assigned; no accept/reject judgment.

---

## Categories in play

The following checklist categories are triggered by the manuscript content:

1. **Reference resolvability** — the manuscript makes numerous load-bearing citations to prior work (immunogen design, preclinical immunogenicity, prior cGMP production of Env trimers, analytical methods).
2. **Claim–citation support** — several specific quantitative/mechanistic claims are attributed to specific references.
3. **Quotation/number fidelity** — specific values (e.g., Kd thresholds, fold-improvements, log-reduction benchmarks) are attributed to sources.
4. **Self-citation / citation inflation** — the manuscript cites its own companion work (Pallerla et al., 2025) and prior work from the same groups.
5. **Retracted / predatory sources** — checked; no evidence found (see below).

Categories **not** in play: no "(data not shown)", "(unpublished)", or "(in preparation)" citations appear in the manuscript text.

## 1. Reference resolvability

**R1 — Reference [10] (HVTN 144 trial registration).** The manuscript cites "HIV Vaccine Trials Network. HVTN 144: A Phase 1 clinical trial... ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT05217641." The identifier is specific and resolvable. I verified the identifier exists via the clinical trials registry. **Status: present.** (The manuscript's own text states the trial is "now undergoing first-in-human evaluation," which is consistent with a registered trial.)

The reference is a published Science paper (2024;384:adj8321). The DOI is not printed in the reference list entry, but the full citation (journal, volume, article ID) is sufficient to resolve it. **Status: present** (resolvable via full citation).

**R3 — Reference [24] (Steichen et al., Science 2019).** Cited for the N332-GT series design and the "Kd ≤ 1 μM" threshold for 20 of 27 BG18 precursor antibodies. Full citation present (2019;366:eaax4380). **Status: present.**

**R4 — Reference [7] (Dey et al., Biotechnology and Bioengineering 2018).** Cited as the basis for the downstream purification process ("developed based on the process established for BG505 SOSIP.664"). Full citation with DOI present. **Status: present.**

**R5 — Reference [1] (Baboo et al., Analytical Chemistry 2021).** Cited for the DeGlyPHER method. Full citation with DOI present. **Status: present.**

The entry is incomplete: "Bale S, et al. This is a load-bearing comparison (the nsEM claim "similar to published cGMP production runs" rests on it). ## 2. Claim–citation support

### Findings

**C1 — Reference [23] (Steichen et al., Science 2024) supports the NHP priming claim.** The manuscript states: "Immunization with N332-GT5/SMNP successfully primed BG18-class precursor B cells in all eight animals tested — the first demonstration of priming of HCDR3-dominant bnAb precursors in outbred animals." The reference is the published Science paper on N332-GT5 vaccination in nonhuman primates. The claim is specific and the reference is the primary source for it. I could not independently verify the "all eight animals" figure from the tools available, but the citation is the correct primary source for this claim. **Status: unverifiable** (content check beyond tool reach), but the attribution is appropriate.

**C2 — Reference [24] (Steichen et al., Science 2019) supports the Kd threshold claim.** The manuscript states: "N332-GT5 emerged as a highly refined immunogen that binds 20 of 27 potential BG18 precursor antibodies with Kd ≤ 1 μM." This is a specific quantitative claim attributed to the 2019 Science paper (the N332-GT series design paper). The claim is plausible and the citation is the correct primary source. **Status: unverifiable** (specific numbers not checkable from tools), but the attribution is appropriate.

**C3 — Reference [7] (Dey et al., 2018) as process basis.** The manuscript states the downstream process was "developed based on the process established for BG505 SOSIP.664 (Dey et al., 2018)." The Dey 2018 paper is the published cGMP production and analysis of BG505 SOSIP.664, which is precisely the process being referenced. **Status: present** (attribution appropriate).

**C4 — Reference [1] (Baboo et al., 2021) supports the DeGlyPHER method description.** The manuscript describes DeGlyPHER as "an ultrasensitive method for the analysis of viral spike N-glycoforms" and cites [1]. The reference title matches the method description. **Status: present.**

**C5 — Reference [17] (Rameez et al., 2014) supports the Ambr250 scalability claim.** The claim "Ambr®250 vessels have been shown to provide scalable data for fed batch production processes" is attributed to Rameez et al. 2014. The paper's title ("High-throughput miniaturized bioreactors for cell culture process development: reproducibility, scalability, and control") is consistent with the claim. **Status: present** (attribution plausible).

**C6 — Reference [20] (Shukla & Aranha, 2015) supports the viral clearance benchmark.** The manuscript states "a common industry standard for retroviral log reduction is to achieve a reduction of at least four logs beyond the estimated viral load per therapeutic dose, which is determined using retrovirus-like particle (RVLP) counts." This is attributed to Shukla & Aranha 2015, a review on viral clearance for biopharmaceutical downstream processes. The claim is consistent with the topic of that review. **Status: unverifiable** (specific benchmark text not checkable from tools), but the attribution is plausible.

**C7 — Reference [5] (Burton & Hangartner, 2016) supports the "only target" claim.** The claim "The HIV Env trimer is the only target for neutralizing antibodies on the virus surface" is a well-established statement in the HIV vaccine field, and Burton & Hangartner's Annual Review of Immunology article on broadly neutralizing antibodies is an appropriate source. **Status: present** (attribution appropriate).

**C8 — Reference [33] (Sanders et al., Science 2015) supports the "native-like trimers induce neutralizing responses" claim.** The manuscript states "Native-like soluble Env trimers like BG505 SOSIP.664 have enabled the induction of neutralizing antibody responses in preclinical models (Sanders et al., 2015)." The Sanders 2015 Science paper ("HIV-1 neutralizing antibodies induced by native-like envelope trimers") is precisely the source for this claim. **Status: present** (attribution appropriate).

**C9 — Reference [32] (Parks et al., Sci. Transl. Med. 2025) supports the "clinical evaluations in humans" claim.** The manuscript states "clinical evaluations have demonstrated the safety and immunogenicity of native-like trimer vaccines in humans (Parks et al., 2025)." The Parks 2025 paper (mRNA-encoded membrane-anchored HIV envelope trimers, phase 1 clinical trial) is the correct source for this claim. **Status: unverifiable** (content not checkable from tools), but the attribution is appropriate.

**C10 — Reference [22] (Socal et al., 2021) supports the supply-chain disruption claim.** The claim "the biopharmaceutical industry, in general, faced major supply chain disruption at the time of process development" is attributed to Socal et al. 2021 (Am. J. Public Health, "The pandemic and the supply chain"). The reference title matches the claim. **Status: present** (attribution appropriate).

**C11 — Reference [29] (Wolfe et al., 2021) supports the mixed-mode resin claim.** The claim "Capto adhere mixed-mode chromatography achieves substantial reduction of residual impurities... as reported by Wolfe et al., 2021, for complex glycoproteins" is attributed to Wolfe et al. Vaccine 2021 ("Development of a platform-based approach for the clinical production of HIV gp120 envelope glycoprotein vaccine candidates"). The reference is on-topic. **Status: present** (attribution plausible).

**C12 — Reference [3] (Bale et al., npj Vaccines 2025) supports the nsEM comparison.** The claim "confirming the presence of nearly 100% native-like trimers, similar to published cGMP production runs of other engineered Env trimers (Dey et al., 2018; Bale et al., 2025)" cites both [7] and [3]. Dey 2018 is verifiable; Bale 2025 is not fully resolvable (see R6). **Status: unverifiable** for the Bale component.

## 4. Self-citation / citation inflation

### Findings

**S1 — Reference [13] (Pallerla et al., J. Pharm. Sci. 2025).** This is a companion paper from the same group on the SMNP adjuvant used in the same HVTN144 trial. The citation is germane — the manuscript describes the SMNP-adjuvanted formulation and the companion paper is the manufacturing report for that adjuvant. This is a legitimate cross-reference, not inflation. **Status: present, germane.**

**S2 — References [23], [24], [31] (Steichen et al.).** Multiple citations to the same group's prior work on the N332-GT series. These are the primary sources for the immunogen design and preclinical data, so the citations are germane and load-bearing, not padding. **Status: present, germane.**

**S3 — Reference [7] (Dey et al., 2018) and [3] (Bale et al., 2025).** Both are prior cGMP production reports for HIV Env trimers from overlapping groups. The citations are germane (the manuscript's process is explicitly based on [7]). **Status: present, germane.**

No conspicuous citation inflation or non-germane self-citation detected.
