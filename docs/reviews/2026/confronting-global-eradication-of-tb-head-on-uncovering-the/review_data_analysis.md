# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
This manuscript presents a large-scale bioinformatics analysis of 821 TB drug-resistance mutations across 31,073 isolates from 149 studies. While the data-collection effort is substantial and the hypothesis-driven framework is conceptually sound, the statistical and analytical rigor is severely compromised. Critical issues include: (1) no multiple-comparison correction despite testing hundreds of mutations across multiple tools and regions; (2) undefined or unsupported statistical claims throughout (e.g., 'frequency' reported without sample sizes, confidence intervals, or tests); (3) mutation ranking into four categories (lethal, moderate, mild, neutral) based on ad-hoc concordance of multiple prediction tools with no validation, no inter-rater reliability, and no disclosure of how ties or disagreements were resolved; (4) no cross-validation, held-out test set, or external validation of the ranking scheme; (5) geographic comparisons (Fig. 6) presented without statistical tests, confidence intervals, or adjustment for multiple regions; (6) no power analysis or sample-size justification for the claim that 30-year data reveal 'globally realistic patterns'; (7) critical data-handling decisions (e.g., how synonymous mutations were excluded, how duplicates across studies were handled) not disclosed. The work reads as a large descriptive catalog with post-hoc pattern-finding rather than a hypothesis-testing study, yet it is framed as uncovering 'universal strategies' and 'mechanisms.' This gap between claim and evidence is the core statistical problem.

## Strengths
- Large, systematically collected dataset of 31,073 isolates from 149 publications spanning 30 years provides substantial raw material for pattern discovery.
- Clear, multi-step bioinformatics framework (Fig. 3) with explicit use of multiple tools (ConSurf, SIFT, PROVEAN, PolyPhen-2, I-MUTANT 3.0, mCSM, AutoDock) for consensus prediction is methodologically transparent.
- Explicit hypothesis stated upfront (bacteria favour mutations that protect them while disarming drugs) provides a testable framework.
- Detailed supplementary tables (S1, S3, S5–S11) document individual mutations, frequencies, and tool outputs, supporting reproducibility of the raw predictions.
- Geographic stratification by WHO region (Fig. 5–6) attempts to contextualize findings globally.

## Weaknesses
- **HARD: No multiple-comparison correction.** The manuscript reports 821 mutations tested across 6 tools (ConSurf, SIFT, PROVEAN, PolyPhen-2, I-MUTANT 3.0, mCSM) and 6 WHO regions (Fig. 6), yielding hundreds of statistical comparisons. No Bonferroni, FDR, or other correction is applied or even mentioned. The family-wise error rate is uncontrolled. For example, Fig. 10 reports 507 mutations as 'deleterious' by all three tools, but no correction threshold or adjusted p-value is stated.
- **HARD: 'Frequency' reported without sample sizes or confidence intervals.** Throughout (e.g., 'S315T was the most common AAS with 60.57% single mutation frequency'), percentages are given without the denominator (n) clearly tied to each claim, without 95% CIs, and without a statistical test of whether differences between frequencies are significant. The formula in Methods states 'Total number of a specific mutation / Total number of resistant isolates,' but it is unclear whether this is per-drug, per-region, or global; denominators vary across figures and text.
- **HARD: Mutation ranking (lethal/moderate/mild/neutral) is not validated.** The four-category ranking (Table 2, Section 3.6.2.5) is based on 'concordance analysis' of multiple tools, but: (i) no inter-tool agreement metric (e.g., Cohen's kappa, Fleiss' kappa) is reported; (ii) no definition of 'concordance' is given (how many tools must agree? what if 2 of 3 agree?); (iii) no external validation (e.g., against experimental fitness data, clinical outcomes, or a held-out test set) is provided; (iv) the ranking is never tested against the hypothesis (do mild mutations actually occur more frequently than lethal ones in the data?). This is a post-hoc categorization without statistical support.
- **HARD: No test of the central hypothesis.** The hypothesis is that 'bacteria favour mutations that protect them while making the drug ineffective.' The prediction would be: mild/moderate mutations should be significantly more prevalent than lethal mutations. This is never formally tested. Fig. 10 and Table 2 show counts, but no chi-square test, Fisher's exact test, or logistic regression comparing prevalence by category is reported. The claim 'Mtb prefers mild mutations in targets essential for its survival' (Discussion) is stated without a statistical test.
- **HARD: Geographic comparisons lack statistical tests.** Figure 6 shows mutation frequency across WHO regions, but no confidence intervals, no test of heterogeneity (e.g., chi-square test for independence), and no correction for multiple regional comparisons are provided. The text states 'higher mutational burden in the Western Pacific and South-East Asia' but does not report a test statistic, p-value, or CI. Are these differences statistically significant or within sampling variation?
- **HARD: Bioinformatics tool outputs treated as ground truth without validation.** The manuscript relies on SIFT, PROVEAN, PolyPhen-2, I-MUTANT 3.0, and mCSM to predict functional impact and stability, but: (i) no comparison to experimental data (e.g., in vitro fitness assays, MIC measurements) is provided; (ii) no sensitivity/specificity or ROC curve is reported; (iii) disagreements between tools are not analyzed (e.g., when SIFT says 'deleterious' but PolyPhen-2 says 'benign,' how is this resolved?); (iv) the claim that using 'many tools reduces error' (Discussion) is unsupported by any validation metric.
- **HARD: No sample-size justification or power analysis.** The claim that '30-year mutation data could reveal globally realistic patterns' (Section 3.3) is not supported by a power calculation. How many isolates per region are needed to detect a true difference in mutation frequency with 80% power? Are some regions underpowered? The text does not address this.
- **HARD: Data-handling decisions not disclosed.** (i) How were synonymous mutations identified and excluded? (ii) How were duplicate isolates across studies handled (e.g., if the same strain was sequenced in two papers)? (iii) Were isolates weighted equally or by study size? (iv) How were missing data (e.g., studies that did not report all four drug targets) handled? These are critical for reproducibility and bias assessment.
- **HARD: Binding affinity (AutoDock) not validated.** Figure 12 reports binding energies (kcal/mol) for mutant vs. wild-type proteins, but: (i) no experimental validation (e.g., against measured Kd values) is provided; (ii) no error bars or confidence intervals are shown; (iii) the docking protocol (grid size, number of runs, clustering threshold) is described in Methods but no validation metric (RMSD to crystal structure, if available) is reported; (iv) the claim 'All mutations reduced drug binding affinity' (Abstract) is not tested statistically—are the differences significant or within noise?
- **SOFT: Concordance analysis methodology unclear.** Section 3.6.2.5 states 'concordance analysis was done for integrating results from the previous mutation analysis,' but the exact algorithm is not described. How were the four categories assigned? If a mutation was predicted 'deleterious' by SIFT and PolyPhen-2 but 'neutral' by PROVEAN, which category does it fall into? Were weights assigned to tools? This must be explicit.
- **SOFT: No sensitivity analysis.** The ranking depends on thresholds for conservation (ConSurf 1–9 scale), functional impact (SIFT ≤0.05, PROVEAN ≤−2.5), and stability (I-MUTANT/mCSM categories). How sensitive are the results to these thresholds? What if the ConSurf cutoff for 'highly conserved' were 6 instead of 7? This is not explored.
- **SOFT: Figure 6 lacks error bars and statistical annotation.** The bar chart shows mutation frequency by region but no 95% CIs, no sample sizes per region, and no significance markers. Readers cannot assess whether differences are real or sampling noise.
- **SOFT: Hotspot analysis (Section 3.4) not formally tested.** The claim that 'frequently mutated positions also have a larger number of amino acid substitutions' is illustrated in Fig. 7 but not tested (e.g., Spearman correlation between position frequency and number of AAS variants). A correlation coefficient and p-value would strengthen this.
- **SOFT: No discussion of confounding in geographic patterns.** The text notes that 'prolonged exposure to drugs, weather conditions, poor performance of diagnostic testing' may explain regional differences (Section 3.3) but does not adjust for these or quantify their contribution. This is acknowledged but not addressed analytically.

## Questions
- How exactly was 'concordance' defined when ranking mutations into four categories? If three tools disagreed (e.g., one said 'deleterious,' two said 'neutral'), which category was assigned, and why?
- For the central hypothesis ('bacteria favour mild mutations'), what is the statistical test? Is it a chi-square test comparing the observed distribution of mutations across the four categories to a null distribution (e.g., uniform)? Please provide the test statistic, degrees of freedom, and p-value.
- In Figure 6, are the differences in mutation frequency across WHO regions statistically significant? Please report a chi-square test for independence (or equivalent) with p-value and 95% CIs for each region.
- How were sample sizes (n) determined for each frequency estimate? For example, when reporting 'S315T with 60.57% frequency,' what is the denominator (total INH-resistant isolates, total isolates sequenced for katG, or something else)? Please clarify for each drug target.
- Were any isolates or studies excluded after seeing the outcome data (e.g., removing outliers, removing studies with 'inconsistent' results)? If so, please disclose the exclusion criteria and the number excluded.
- How were the AutoDock docking parameters (grid size, number of runs, clustering threshold) chosen? Was a validation set used to optimize these, and if so, was the final binding-energy analysis done on a held-out test set?
- The manuscript states that 'using many tools reduces error,' but provides no empirical support. Can you report the agreement (e.g., Fleiss' kappa) between SIFT, PROVEAN, and PolyPhen-2 on the 821 mutations? What fraction of mutations were predicted consistently by all three?
- For the claim that 'most frequent mutations occur in non-conserved areas' (Abstract), is this tested statistically? Please report a chi-square test comparing the distribution of conserved vs. non-conserved mutations in the high-frequency group vs. low-frequency group.
- How were ties or disagreements between I-MUTANT 3.0 and mCSM resolved when predicting stability? The text says 'both tools' were used, but does not explain how conflicting predictions were handled.
- Were any interim analyses or optional stopping rules applied during the study? If the analysis plan was modified after seeing preliminary results, please disclose this.
