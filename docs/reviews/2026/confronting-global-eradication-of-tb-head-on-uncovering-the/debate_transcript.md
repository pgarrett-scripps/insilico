# Debate Transcript

## Advocate — round 1

The manuscript's core contribution is a genuinely valuable resource: 821 curated non-synonymous mutations across 31,073 isolates from 149 studies over 30 years, cross-checked with six independent bioinformatics tools (ConSurf, SIFT, PROVEAN, PolyPhen-2, I-MUTANT/mCSM, AutoDock). Every reviewer's 'strengths' section concedes this scale and multi-tool triangulation is real and useful (novelty reviewer: 'creates a useful reference resource'; methodology reviewer: 'reduces single-tool bias'). The empirical pattern itself—that the most prevalent mutations (katG S315T 60%, rpoB S450L 56%, embB M306V 29%) cluster in non-conserved, binding-adjacent sites with moderate rather than extreme predicted effects (Table 1, Fig. 9)—is a documented, reproducible correlation, not fabricated.

The rigor and methodology reviewers are right that causal language ('bacteria favour,' 'universal strategy,' 'guided evolutionary pathway') outruns the observational design; this is a genuine overclaiming problem. But it is a revision-fixable framing issue, not a reason to discard the finding: replacing 'Mtb favours' with 'the data are consistent with a preference for' preserves the paper's value while satisfying the rigor critique. Similarly, the clarity reviewer's complaints (late definitions, Table 1 legend gaps, Fig. 9b labeling) and reproducibility reviewer's requests (data/code deposition, denominator specification) are addressable through editing and supplementary disclosure, not fatal to the underlying analysis.

The ethics gaps (funding, COI, data availability statements) are boilerplate omissions any journal requires before publication, not substantive flaws. Rejecting outright would discard a hypothesis-generating atlas that reviewers themselves call unprecedented in scale, when targeted revision can resolve nearly every hard flaw raised.

**Key points:**
- Scale and multi-tool triangulation (821 mutations, 149 studies, 6 independent bioinformatics tools) is conceded as a genuine strength by every reviewer
- The empirical correlation—frequent resistance mutations cluster in non-conserved, binding-adjacent sites with moderate predicted effects—is documented and reproducible, independent of causal interpretation
- Overclaiming of 'strategy'/'preference' language is a real but fixable framing issue, correctable by hedging to correlational claims without discarding the finding
- Clarity, reproducibility, and ethics deficiencies (late definitions, missing data/code deposition, missing COI/funding statements) are standard revision items, not grounds for rejection
- The mutation atlas itself has standalone reference value for the field regardless of the disputed mechanistic conclusions

## Skeptic — round 1

The advocate treats overclaiming as a wording problem, but the deeper flaw is that the paper's central quantitative result—Table 2's 340 lethal/284 moderate/185 mild/12 neutral classification—is not reproducible or defined anywhere in the manuscript, and no amount of hedged language fixes that. Four independent reviewers (methodology, data_analysis, reproducibility, rigor) converge on the same HARD point: 'concordance analysis' (Section 3.6.2.5) is never operationalized. The text states 507/821 mutations were concordantly 'deleterious' and 79 'neutral' by all three tools (SIFT/PROVEAN/PolyPhen-2), but that accounts for only 586 of 821 mutations—what happened to the remaining 235 when tools disagreed? No decision rule, weighting scheme, or tie-breaking procedure is given for combining five dimensions (conservation, function, stability, binding, site) into four ranks. This isn't a presentation defect; it's a missing analytical method. Nobody, including the authors on re-review, can currently reconstruct why any given mutation landed in 'moderate' vs 'mild.' Since this ranking is what license the abstract's headline claim of a 'universal strategy,' the paper's core deliverable is un-auditable as written.　This compounds with an internal contradiction the advocate hasn't addressed: the abstract asserts 'most prevalent mutations occur in non-conserved areas,' yet Table 1 shows pncA's most frequent mutation, Q10P, sits in a highly conserved (HC) region—directly contradicting the pattern claimed as universal across all four targets.

I concede the atlas of raw mutation frequencies (Section 3.5, Supplementary Table S3) has standalone descriptive value. But the paper is not framed or organized as a descriptive atlas—it is framed as mechanistic discovery built atop an unspecified ranking algorithm, and that requires substantive re-analysis, not editing.

**Key points:**
- The ranking scheme (lethal/moderate/mild/neutral, Table 2) that anchors the paper's central claims is never operationally defined—concordance rules, tie-breaking, and weighting are absent, per four independent reviewers
- 507 deleterious + 79 neutral concordant calls leave 235 of 821 mutations unaccounted for in the classification logic; Table 2's exact counts cannot be audited or reproduced from the text as written
- This is a missing-method problem, not a wording/hedging problem—no amount of softening causal language ('favours' vs 'is consistent with') restores reproducibility to an undefined categorical scheme
- Internal contradiction: abstract claims most prevalent mutations occur in non-conserved regions, yet pncA's most frequent mutation Q10P is explicitly reported as highly conserved (HC) in Table 1
- Conceded: the raw mutation frequency atlas (Supplementary Table S3) retains standalone reference value, but the manuscript is structured around the unvalidated ranking as mechanistic discovery, not as a descriptive resource

## Advocate — round 2

The skeptic's strongest point is real: Section 3.6.2.5's concordance rule is underspecified, and reviewers are right to demand the decision logic behind Table 2. But this is a disclosure gap, not proof the underlying signal is fabricated or unrecoverable. The manuscript does state a graded rule — 'functionally deleterious mutations that compromise residue conservation with reduced structural stability and binding affinity were categorised lethal... the ranking accordingly goes down to moderate and mild as the level of these decreases' — which is a real, if informally stated, hierarchical logic across five measured dimensions (conservation, site, function, stability, binding), all of which ARE reported per-mutation in Supplementary Tables S5–S11. Asking authors to formalize this into an explicit decision tree is a standard major-revision request, answerable from data they already collected — not a fatal design flaw requiring new experiments.

**Key points:**
- The lethal/moderate/mild/neutral rule is informally but non-arbitrarily stated in 3.6.2.5 as a graded function of five already-tabulated dimensions (Supp. Tables S5-S11); formalizing it is a disclosure fix, not new research
- On the Q10P 'contradiction': the abstract itself explicitly flags this exception ('except for the most common mutation Q10P in pncA... showing a variable pattern of occurrence globally'), so this is a disclosed nuance the authors already reported, not a hidden contradiction the advocate is ignoring
- The 235/821 unaccounted mutations is a legitimate audit request answerable by the authors releasing the concordance algorithm and per-mutation tool outputs they already generated -- this is exactly the kind of gap major revision exists to close
- Conceding a method needs formalization is different from conceding the underlying tool outputs and correlational pattern are unreal; the raw per-tool predictions (Table 1, Fig 9-11) stand independently of how they were later binned into four labels

## Skeptic — round 2

Advocate's rebuttal only reaches the audit-trail problem; it never touches the deeper, truly fatal flaw both rigor and methodology reviewers raise: ascertainment bias baked into the study design. Every isolate in the 149-study corpus is, by inclusion criterion, a *phenotypically resistant, viable* strain (Fig.4 exclusion criteria). Lethal-to-fitness mutations, by definition, cannot appear in this population — not because bacteria 'avoided' them, but because bacteria carrying them died or never became detectable resistant isolates. So the observation that frequent mutations are mild/moderate is close to tautological given the sampling frame, not evidence of an evolved 'strategy.' No formalization of the concordance rule in Table 2 fixes this, because the problem is upstream of ranking: the design cannot distinguish 'Mtb favours mild mutations' from 'only mild mutations survive to be sampled.' This is the rigor reviewer's HARD point #3 and #4, and it is unaddressed by either advocate turn. It is fatal to the abstract's headline claim — 'universal strategy of drug resistance and bacterial survival' — though not to the atlas's descriptive value, which I again concede.

On Table 2: I'll grant the advocate that formalizing concordance is achievable without new experiments. But note this only fixes reproducibility of labels, not validity of the causal narrative built on them — two separable problems, and the advocate has only solved the easier one.

On Q10P: flagging PZA's variability elsewhere doesn't reconcile the abstract's unqualified 'most prevalent mutations occur in non-conserved areas' with Table 1 showing Q10P as HC. That's a factual inconsistency in the summary claim, not a disclosed caveat.

**Key points:**
- FATAL: study samples only phenotypically resistant, viable isolates by design (Fig.4), so 'frequent mutations are mild' is largely a sampling artifact/survivorship bias, not evidence of bacterial strategy — no ranking formalization fixes this because the flaw is in what population was sampled, not how it was later labeled
- Rigor and methodology reviewers' HARD objections on ascertainment bias/causality remain completely unaddressed by advocate in both rounds — conceding the Table 2 disclosure gap is fixable does not rescue the mechanistic claim built on top of it
- These are two separable problems: (1) reproducibility of the lethal/moderate/mild/neutral labels, which I agree is a fixable disclosure issue, and (2) validity of the causal/'universal strategy' claim, which is a design-level flaw unfixable by better labeling
- Q10P point stands: advocate's cited abstract text discloses PZA's *global variability*, not the direct contradiction between the abstract's general claim ('most prevalent mutations occur in non-conserved areas') and Table 1 showing Q10P as highly conserved
- Concede again: the raw mutation atlas (Supp. Table S3) retains standalone value independent of both the ranking dispute and the survivorship-bias problem
