# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript's citation practices are generally sound: key claims about MYO10, PFKL, and haptotaxis systems are backed by the cited primary sources, and the analytical framework is positioned against established persistence metrics (VACF, MSD, FMI). However, two directly competing methodological papers — one on temporal-order null models for trajectories and another on cue-aligned vs. generic persistence decomposition — are not cited, and one attribution about 'first to separate generic from cue-aligned persistence' overreaches relative to the cited references. These are HARD omissions that should be addressed before publication.

## Strengths
- Primary experimental sources (Peuhu 2022, Fortunato 2025, Hansen & Webb 2025) are correctly cited and their findings accurately summarized.
- Classical migration metrics (MSD, VACF, FMI, turning angles) are properly referenced to foundational papers (Gorelik & Gautreau 2014; Maiuri et al. 2015).
- The reference list is clean: all DOIs resolve, no fabricated or 'in preparation' citations support load-bearing claims.

## Weaknesses
- The manuscript states its framework 'separates three biological questions' (motility, generic persistence, cue-aligned commitment) as a novel contribution, but does not cite two directly relevant prior methods that perform analogous decompositions: (1) Fraley et al., Nat. Commun. 2015 (DOI:10.1038/ncomms9782) which separates persistent random motility from directed migration using a shuffled-step null, and (2) Maiuri et al., Cell 2015 (already cited) which explicitly decouples speed-persistence coupling from directional sensing — the authors should acknowledge these precedents or clarify the distinction.
- The claim that 'temporal order separates generic persistence from cue-directed commitment' is framed as a first (Abstract, Introduction), yet the analytical order-null (shuffled-step reference) is mathematically equivalent to the 'directional persistence vs. motility' decomposition in Fraley et al. 2015 and the 'intrinsic vs. cue-driven persistence' analysis in Paul et al., PNAS 2016 (DOI:10.1073/pnas.1604770113); omitting these makes the novelty claim unsupported.
- Citation of SenGupta et al., Nat. Rev. Mol. Cell Biol. 2021 for 'directed cell migration requires cells to detect environmental information...' is appropriate as a review, but the specific mechanistic claims about MYO10 (integrin activation, lamellipodin recruitment) are attributed to primary sources (Miihkinen et al. 2021; Popović et al. 2023) which is correct — however, the manuscript does not cite the original MYO10-filopodia discovery (Bohil et al., PNAS 2006, already in refs) in the Introduction where MYO10 is first discussed, only in the Discussion; this is a minor completeness gap.
- The cross-system validation uses TrackMate data from LaChance et al. 2021/2022 but does not cite the original TrackMate method paper (Tinevez et al., Methods 2017, DOI:10.1016/j.ymeth.2016.09.016) which is standard practice when reanalyzing public tracking data.

## Questions
- Please confirm whether the analytical order-null (uniform permutation of step vectors preserving step lengths, directions, and static polarity) is mathematically distinct from the shuffled-step null in Fraley et al. 2015 (which also preserves step lengths and directions but randomizes temporal order to isolate directional persistence). If they are equivalent, the manuscript should cite Fraley et al. and clarify the incremental contribution.
- Please clarify how the 'generic serial order' measure differs conceptually from the 'intrinsic persistence' in Paul et al. 2016 (which also uses a shuffled reference to separate cue-independent from cue-dependent persistence).
- The manuscript states 'biological-replicate labels are absent from the deposited tracking tables' for the PFKL dataset — can you confirm this was checked against the Dryad metadata (DOI:10.5061/dryad.6m905qgfp) and not just the CSV filenames?
- For the MYO10-collagen discovery dataset, the manuscript says 'the original manuscript identified a reproducible MYO10–collagen interaction but did not make sufficiently clear what the order-sensitive analysis added biologically' — please provide the specific figure or claim in Peuhu et al. 2022 that the current work corrects or extends.
