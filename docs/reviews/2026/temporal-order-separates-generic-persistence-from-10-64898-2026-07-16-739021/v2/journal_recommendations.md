## After required revisions
- **PLOS Computational Biology**
  - Fit: Strong match for a novel computational method (exact analytical order-null with algebraic closure) demonstrated on multiple cell migration datasets. The journal regularly publishes methodological advances in cell tracking and motility analysis with rigorous statistical safeguards. After the required revisions — scaling biological claims to track-level evidence, adding stationarity caveats, justifying non-inferiority margin, and depositing versioned code — the paper meets the journal's standards for methodological novelty and reproducibility. Acceptance odds: moderate (30-40%) given competition but strong method core.
  - Realism: Moderate; the method is novel and transportable, but limited biological replication (n=3 repeats for MYO10, track-level only for PFKL) may concern reviewers even with qualified claims.
- **Bioinformatics**
  - Fit: Excellent fit for a new algorithmic framework (analytical order-null, exact decomposition identities, hierarchical bootstrap with simultaneous bands) applied to trajectory data. The journal specializes in computational methods with biological applications and values exact analytical solutions over Monte Carlo approximations. The required revisions directly address the journal's emphasis on method validation and availability. Acceptance odds: moderate-high (40-50%) if code deposition and claim qualification are completed.
  - Realism: Good; the method's exact analytical nature and algebraic closure are distinctive strengths for this venue.
- **Cell Reports Methods**
  - Fit: Designed for methodological advances with biological demonstrations. The paper's three-question framework (motility, generic persistence, cue-aligned commitment) and cross-system transportability demonstration align with the journal's scope. The required revisions (limitations paragraph, claim scaling, code deposition) match the journal's expectations for transparency. Acceptance odds: moderate (35-45%) as a methods-focused Cell Press journal with growing reputation.
  - Realism: Moderate; the journal values methodological rigor and open science practices, which the revised manuscript will satisfy.

## Notes
The manuscript's core contribution is a novel, exact analytical method for decomposing cell migration persistence — not the specific biological generalizations. After revision, it should target computational/methods journals that value analytical rigor and transportable frameworks over biological replication depth. The required revisions (claim qualification, stationarity caveat, non-inferiority justification, code deposition) are textual and depositional, not experimental, making the paper viable for strong methods venues. In Silico's 'major revision' verdict reflects claim–evidence mismatch, not method invalidity. Do not submit to top-tier general journals (Nature, Science, Cell) — the biological evidence is insufficient. Avoid journals requiring prospective experimental validation (e.g., JCB, Dev Cell) unless new data are generated.
