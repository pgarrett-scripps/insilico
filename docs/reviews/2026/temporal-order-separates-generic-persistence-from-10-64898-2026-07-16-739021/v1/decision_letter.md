# Decision Letter

**Decision:** major

## Summary of Evaluation
The panel agrees this is a genuine methodological advance: an exact analytical order-null (Eqs. 8–12) with algebraic closure to machine precision, clean separation of motility/generic persistence/cue-aligned commitment, and statistical safeguards exceeding field standards. The split centers on whether biological claims in the Abstract/main text exceed replication evidence. The meta-review sides with the skeptic on claim level but with the advocate on contribution level: the method stands; the biological generalizations must be scaled to the evidence (track-level, this dataset). The authors' rebuttal credibly addresses every load-bearing critique by conceding the overclaim and proposing precise textual qualifications — not new experiments. Specifically: (1) MYO10–collagen claims will be qualified to 'repeat-consistent in this dataset' with a prominent Limitations paragraph; (2) PFKL claims will be scaled to 'track-level statistical statement in this dataset'; (3) stationarity assumption of the analytical order-null will be explicitly caveated; (4) non-inferiority margin (0.10 SD) will be justified in main text; (5) Fraley 2015 and Paul 2016 citations will be added with clear distinction of exact vs. Monte Carlo null; (6) terminology will be harmonized ('sequence-dependent persistence' as biological term, 'sequence excess' as mathematical label); (7) cross-system comparisons will be framed as descriptive transportability. The Methods Completeness audit identifies 5 HARD gaps (optimizer details, library versions, code availability, hyperparameters, software versions) that map directly to the reproducibility concessions (#19–23). These are depositional requirements, not methodological flaws. The Citation Integrity audit finds no HARD gaps; the single unverifiable item (2025 paper content) is noted but does not affect the manuscript's central claims. The confidence-weighted score (4.03) reflects method strength; major revision aligns with data_analysis (3/5) and reproducibility (2/5) flags on claim–evidence match and checkability. In Silico's first criterion — 'Are the conclusions supported by the evidence?' — requires the biological claims as written to be revised. The methodological core survives all critiques. Acceptance is conditional on deposition of versioned workflow, environment capture, and figure–script mapping to Zenodo before revision submission.

## Required Revisions
1. Rewrite Abstract and main-text claims to match replication level: change 'reproducible MYO10–collagen interaction' to 'repeat-consistent MYO10–collagen interaction in this dataset'; change 'PFKL-N702T produced fewer up-gradient steps...' to 'In this track-level analysis, PFKL-N702T tracks showed fewer up-gradient steps...'.
2. Add explicit stationarity caveat for the analytical order-null in Results (MYO10–collagen section) and Methods (Analytical temporal-order null subsection): state that the null assumes step exchangeability; non-stationary trends would be destroyed by uniform permutation and inflate sequence-excess; the early-vs-late test (Appendix 1–Fig. 5) checks interaction strengthening, not stationarity. Qualify the ~70% sequence-excess fraction as conditional on exchangeability. Add a sentence in the Discussion Limitations paragraph.
3. Justify the non-inferiority margin (0.10 SD) in Results (PFKL section) and Methods (PFKL chemotaxis dataset subsection): explain it corresponds to ~1/10 of observed track-level SD of cue-axis FMI in WT (SD ≈ 0.28), smaller than minimal biologically relevant effect size in prior literature (e.g., Hansen & Webb 2025, Fig. 3), and was fixed before final Stage 3 execution.
4. Add Fraley et al. (Nat. Commun. 2015) and Paul et al. (PNAS 2016) citations in Introduction and Discussion with clear distinction: the analytical order-null provides exact expectation, algebraic decomposition closure (Eqs. 10–12), exact speed–direction separation (Eq. 15), and exact pair-separation bridge (Eqs. 13–14) — not just Monte Carlo shuffling.
5. Add Bohil et al. (PNAS 2006) citation in Introduction (MYO10 paragraph) and Tinevez et al. (Methods 2017) citation in Methods (Cross-system TrackMate validation subsection).
6. Harmonize terminology: standardize on 'sequence-dependent persistence' as primary biological term and 'sequence excess' as mathematical label (Eq. 9); update Abstract, Introduction, Results, Figure legends, and Table 1 footnote. Change Abstract 'stationary angular hidden-state model' to 'nonlinear angular hidden-state model'. Replace 'positive factorial interaction' with 'buffering interaction' throughout main text after definition.
7. Add one-sentence summary of generative model target 'passing' criteria in Results (Stationary local models section): ≥80% lag-point coverage by 95% predictive interval, observed frozen-window mean within interval, sign probability ≥0.90.
8. Define 'lag-invariant cohort' at first use in Results (Corrected trajectory identities section).
9. List all five PFKL perturbation series in Results (PFKL section): acute depletion (siPFKL), stable depletion (shPFKL), catalytic inactivation (PFKL-H199Y), pharmacological inhibition (PFK15), filament disruption (N702T).
10. Strengthen cross-system validation framing in Results and Abstract: add 'descriptive transportability' qualifier; add to Figure 6 legend that comparisons are descriptive because species, substrate, acquisition, and laboratory differ; identical axes facilitate visual comparison of curve shapes, not quantitative equivalence.
11. Add haptotaxis block details in Methods (Single-cell haptotaxis dataset): 131 complete trajectories across five geometry conditions, block labels = independent microfluidic devices (n=5 blocks, one per geometry, separate imaging sessions); hierarchical bootstrap resampled blocks with replacement. In Results, state hierarchical bootstrap used 5 independent blocks.
12. Add prominent Limitations paragraph in Discussion covering: MYO10–collagen n=3 repeats (R3 anomalous), PFKL track-level inference (zero replicate labels), stationarity assumption, and descriptive nature of cross-system comparisons.
13. Deposit versioned computational workflow (Python/SLURM scripts, conda environment.yml, Dockerfile, conda-lock.yml) to Zenodo with DOI before revision submission. Include explicit mapping from every figure panel and table to generating script, input files, and key parameters (README / Figure–Table reproduction guide). Include generative model fitting scripts (linear active-memory and angular hidden-state) with documentation. Reference Zenodo DOI in revised manuscript Data Availability and Methods (Software, computation, and reproducibility). Clarify in Methods (FOV-aware trajectory reconstruction) that Unique_ID column is present in Zenodo deposit (doi:10.5281/zenodo.11282716) as provided by CellTracksColab compilation.

## Minor Suggestions
- Consider adding a brief note in Methods (Generative modelling) specifying the optimizer (e.g., Adam), learning rate, number of epochs, and early-stopping criteria used for the 16 FOV-bootstrap models, even though the Zenodo deposit will contain the full code.
- In the haptotaxis reversal analysis, the matched pseudo-event control is appropriate; consider a brief comment in Results that this controls for selection bias by comparing true reversals to time-matched non-reversal windows from the same tracks.
