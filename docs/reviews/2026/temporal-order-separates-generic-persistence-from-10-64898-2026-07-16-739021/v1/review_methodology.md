# Methodology Reviewer

## Summary
The analytical order-null framework is a valuable methodological advance that cleanly separates sequence-dependent persistence from static polarity, and triangulation across three migration systems supports the core claim that temporal order distinguishes generic persistence from cue-aligned commitment. However, the PFKL chemotaxis conclusion lacks biological replication (track-level only), the MYO10-collagen discovery rests on only three biological repeats with one anomalous, and the analytical order-null assumes stationarity without verification. These limitations require qualification but do not invalidate the primary contribution.

## Strengths
- The analytical order-null provides an exact, computationally efficient reference that isolates temporal order from step content and static polarity, enabling clean decomposition of persistence into sequence-dependent and order-null components.
- Hierarchical bootstrap with simultaneous confidence bands, lag-invariant cohorts, and leave-one-FOV-out sensitivity analyses rigorously address the nested data structure and multiple comparisons across lags.
- Triangulation across matrix (MYO10-collagen), geometric (haptotaxis), and metabolic (PFKL) perturbations demonstrates the framework's ability to dissociate motility, generic persistence, and cue commitment in biologically distinct contexts.

## Weaknesses
- The PFKL chemotaxis conclusion that "PFKL filamentation supports cue-aligned commitment without reducing generic serial order" rests on track-level non-inferiority testing because biological-replicate identity was lost in the public trajectory labels. Track-level intervals cannot substitute for biological replication; the design therefore does not support the biological generalization at the replicate level, only the track-level statistical statement. The manuscript acknowledges this boundary but still draws the biological conclusion.
- The MYO10-collagen discovery experiment has only three biological repeats (R1-R3), with R3 identified as disproportionately large and phenotypically distinct. While equal-repeat weighting and leave-one-repeat-out analyses are performed, three repeats provide minimal basis for estimating between-repeat variance and the simultaneous confidence bands' coverage properties. The claim of a "reproducible" interaction across repeats is supported by sign consistency in all three, but the precision of the interaction estimate is fundamentally limited by n=3 at the highest level.
- The analytical order-null assumes uniform permutation of steps is the appropriate null for "no temporal order beyond static polarity." However, if migration exhibits non-stationarities (e.g., systematic speed or direction changes over the 14-hour movies), uniform permutation destroys not only serial correlations but also temporal trends, making the order-null expectation potentially mismatched to a null of "no serial order but preserved non-stationarity." The manuscript does not test or discuss stationarity of step statistics within tracks, which could inflate sequence-excess estimates if trends exist.
- The haptotaxis analysis relies on deposited block labels for hierarchical bootstrap but the number and nature of biological replicates are not clearly specified.
- The cross-system validation compares different cell types, species, substrates, and imaging conditions simultaneously, so descriptive transportability is the only warranted inference.
- The non-inferiority margin of 0.10 SD for generic serial-order measures in PFKL analysis is arbitrary though pre-specified; no justification for this specific threshold is provided.
- The generative modeling section fits models only to local (1-2 step) transitions, so failure on long-lag targets is expected and does not strongly discriminate between model classes.
- The factorial interaction interpretation as "buffering" rather than "synergy" depends on the choice of scale (log for MSD, linear for VACF); the manuscript does not justify why these scales are biologically privileged.

## Questions
- For the MYO10-collagen data, were step statistics (mean speed, direction distribution) stationary within tracks over the 14-hour movies, and if not, how might non-stationarity affect the analytical order-null expectation?
- In the PFKL dataset, what was the original biological replicate structure reported by Hansen and Webb (2025), and is there any way to recover or approximate replicate-level inference from the deposited data?
- For the haptotaxis data, how many independent biological replicates (e.g., separate experiments/dishes) underlie the 131 trajectories across the five geometry conditions?
