# Meta-Review

**Draft recommendation:** major

## Synthesis
The panel agrees this is a genuine methodological advance: an exact analytical order-null (Eqs. 8–12) that isolates sequence-dependent persistence from static polarity and step content, with algebraic closure to machine precision — distinct from Monte Carlo shuffling in Fraley 2015 and Paul 2016. The framework cleanly separates motility, generic persistence, and cue-aligned commitment, and demonstrates this across matrix (MYO10–collagen), geometric (haptotaxis), and metabolic (PFKL) perturbations. Statistical safeguards (hierarchical bootstrap with simultaneous bands, lag-invariant cohorts, leave-one-FOV-out, locked robustness plans) exceed field standards. The workflow is fully specified procedurally.

The split in the panel centers on whether the biological claims in the two primary datasets exceed their evidence in ways that revision cannot fix. The skeptic (data_analysis, rigor reviewers) argues that MYO10–collagen's n=3 repeats (one anomalous) cannot support simultaneous bands or a 'reproducible' population claim, and PFKL's track-level inference (zero replicate labels) cannot support a biological mechanism claim — these are missing experimental units, not textual gaps. The advocate (methodology, novelty reviewers) counters that the manuscript already qualifies both in the Discussion ('repeat-consistent in this dataset'; 'track-level only, cannot replace replicate-level inference') and that the core contribution is the transportable method, not the specific biological generalizations.

The meta-review sides with the skeptic on the *claim level* but with the advocate on the *contribution level*. The Abstract states unqualified population claims ('reproducible MYO10–collagen interaction'; 'PFKL-N702T produced fewer up-gradient steps') that the evidence does not support at the biological-replicate level. The Discussion's hedges contradict the Abstract. This is a major overclaim that must be fixed by scaling claims to the evidence (track-level, this-dataset), not by new experiments. The stationarity assumption of the analytical order-null (Eq. 8) also requires explicit discussion: if 14-hour movies have non-stationary trends, uniform permutation destroys them and inflates sequence-excess; the early-vs-late test in Appendix 1–Fig. 5 checks interaction strengthening, not stationarity. The non-inferiority margin (0.10 SD) needs justification in the main text. Missing citations (Fraley 2015, Paul 2016) must be added with a clear distinction: the analytical null provides exact expectation and decomposition closure, not just Monte Carlo shuffling. Reproducibility artifacts (versioned code, environment capture) are absent (HARD findings) and must be deposited as a condition of acceptance. Terminology harmonization ('sequence excess' vs 'sequence-dependent persistence'; 'stationary' vs 'nonlinear' angular model) is needed.

The methodological contribution is novel, rigorous, and immediately usable. The biological demonstrations are valuable proof-of-concept but the claims must match the replication evidence. Major revision — not rejection — because the core method stands and the fixes are textual/depositional.

## Decisive Factors
1. Biological claims in Abstract exceed replication evidence: MYO10–collagen n=3 (one anomalous) and PFKL zero replicate labels cannot support population-level conclusions. Must scale claims to 'repeat-consistent in this dataset' and 'track-level statistical statement'.
2. Analytical order-null stationarity assumption untested; may inflate sequence-excess (~70% claim). Requires explicit caveat in Results/Methods.
3. Non-inferiority margin (0.10 SD) arbitrary in main text; needs justification.
4. Missing citations (Fraley 2015, Paul 2016) overstate novelty; must add with clear distinction of exact vs. Monte Carlo null.
5. Reproducibility artifacts absent (no versioned code/environment); deposition required as condition.
6. Methodological core (exact order-null, algebraic closure, three-question separation, transportability demonstration) is novel and sound — survives all critiques.
7. Confidence-weighted score (4.03) reflects method strength; major revision aligns with data_analysis (3/5) and reproducibility (2/5) flags on claim-evidence match and checkability, not method validity.
8. In Silico standard: 'A modest claim that is fully supported is better work than an important claim that is not.' The method claim is fully supported; the biological claims must be made modest to match evidence.
9. Panel split is genuine: skeptic sees fatal overclaim; advocate sees fixable qualification. Meta-review picks side: overclaim is real but fixable by textual revision + deposition, not new data, because the paper's primary contribution is the method framework, not the specific biological generalizations.
10. No reviewer recommended rejection on method grounds; rejection would require the panel to have collectively missed a fatal method flaw, which they did not. The fatal-flaw argument applies only to the biological claims as currently worded in the Abstract/main text, which are revisable.

Recommendation diverges from confidence-weighted average (4.03 → accept/minor) because the two most critical reviewers (data_analysis, reproducibility) flagged fundamental claim-evidence and checkability gaps that require major, not minor, revision to resolve. The average is pulled up by high scores on method/novelty/rigor/ethics, but In Silico's first criterion is 'Are the conclusions supported by the evidence?' — and the biological conclusions as written are not. Major revision corrects this without discarding the method contribution.

Verdict: Major Revision. Required changes: (a) Rewrite Abstract and main-text claims to match replication level (track-level, this dataset); (b) Add stationarity caveat for analytical null; (c) Justify non-inferiority margin; (d) Add Fraley 2015 and Paul 2016 citations with distinction; (e) Deposit versioned workflow + environment capture; (f) Harmonize terminology; (g) Add prominent Limitations paragraph. Acceptance conditional on (e).
