# Citation Integrity Auditor

## Summary
This qualitative study of 13 semi-structured interviews with CS instructors cites 79 references spanning empirical studies, pedagogical frameworks, and prior work on AI in education. The audit detected five active categories: Reference Resolvability, Claim–Citation Support, Quotation/Number Fidelity, Self-Citation/Citation Inflation, and Retracted/Predatory Sources. Most citations are resolvable with complete bibliographic information; however, several load-bearing claims rest on very recent works (2025–2026) that cannot be independently verified. A few citations lack complete bibliographic information (DOI, venue, or page numbers), and some claims represent the authors' own theoretical applications rather than direct empirical findings from cited works. No HARD violations, retracted sources, or predatory venues were detected. Overall citation practice is sound for qualitative education research.

## Unverifiable (raise as questions)
- **[Claim–Citation Support] Cognitive offloading effects on learning (code reading, debugging, critical thinking, self-efficacy)** — Section 2.1 and 4.1.1 claim 'Studies show that relegating cognitive load of programming tasks to AI can reduce code reading and debugging skills [68], reduce critical thinking skills [29], reduce self-efficacy [45], and lead to less perceived ownership of ideas and depth of thought [6].' Citations [6], [29], [45], [68] are all dated 2025–2026 and cannot be accessed to verify the specific empirical findings attributed to them.
- **[Quotation/Number Fidelity] Interview quotations from primary data (I1–I13)** — Sections 4 and 5 extensively quote interview participants (I1–I13) throughout the manuscript. These are primary data collected by the research team and cannot be verified from external sources without access to original interview transcripts. This is standard and expected for qualitative research; IRB exemption mentioned in Section 3.1 indicates ethical review.

## Documented (for the record)
- **[Reference Resolvability] Lau & Guo 2023 on instructor attitudes and curricular adaptation** — Section 2.2 cites [44] 'Sam Lau and Philip Guo. 2023. From 'Ban It Till We Understand It' to 'Resistance is Futile': How University Programming Instructors Plan to Adapt as More Students Use AI Code Generation and Explanation Tools such as ChatGPT and GitHub Copilot. In Proceedings of the 2023 ACM Conference on International Computing Education Research V.1. ACM, Chicago IL USA, 106–121.' Reference is complete and resolvable; claim attribution is plausible but unverifiable without access to full text.
- **[Claim–Citation Support] AI eroding social interactions and student learning communities** — Section 4.1.3 and Discussion cite [33] 'Irene Hou, Owen Man, Kate Hamilton, Srishty Muthusekaran, Jeffin Johnykutty, Leili Zadeh, and Stephen MacNeil. 2025. 'All Roads Lead to ChatGPT': How Generative AI is Eroding Social Interactions and Student Learning Communities.' The paper's title directly supports the manuscript's claim about social interaction erosion; citation is resolvable.
- **[Reference Resolvability] Reflexive thematic analysis methodology (Braun & Clarke)** — Section 3.3 cites [13], [14], [15]: Braun & Clarke 2019, 2006, and 2023 papers on thematic analysis. All citations include complete bibliographic information with DOIs, journal names, volume/issue numbers, and page ranges. Citations are resolvable and appropriately support the methodological approach.
- **[Claim–Citation Support] Abstinence-only education as analogy for ineffective restrictive AI policies** — Section 5.1 states 'Disallowing AI misuse without corresponding positive intervention is akin to abstinence-only sex education, which has been demonstrated to be ineffective [69].' Citation [69] is Stanger-Hall & Hall 2011, a resolvable work on abstinence-only education ineffectiveness. However, the analogy to AI policy effectiveness is the authors' own reasoning, not a direct claim from the cited work.
- **[Self-Citation/Citation Inflation] Self-citation on AI literacy (Gu & Ericson 2025)** — Section 2.1 includes [31] 'Xingjian (Lance) Gu and Barbara J. Ericson. 2025. AI Literacy in K-12 and Higher Education in the Wake of Generative AI: An Integrative Review.' Self-citation is germane to the topic (AI literacy in education) and used to reference a review article, not to inflate empirical findings. This is appropriate scholarly practice.
- **[Reference Resolvability] AI performance on programming assignments and tests** — Section 2.2 cites [23], [26], [27] including Finnie-Ansley et al. 2022 and 2023 papers testing OpenAI Codex on introductory and CS2 programming exercises. All citations are complete with venue names and years. Titles directly support the claim that 'models since 2022 have been demonstrated to perform well on many programming assignments and tests.'
- **[Reference Resolvability] Vygotsky foundational work on social interactions and learning** — Section 4.3.3 cites [74] 'L. S. Vygotsky and Michael Cole. 1978. Mind in Society: Development of Higher Psychological Processes. Harvard University Press.' Citation is resolvable but lacks page numbers or specific chapter information. For a foundational work, better practice would include page range or chapter specificity.
- **[Reference Resolvability] Backward design pedagogical framework (Wiggins & McTighe)** — Section 5.2 cites [75] 'Grant P. Wiggins and Jay McTighe. 2005. Understanding by Design. ASCD.' Citation is complete with authors, year, title, and publisher. The claim that backward design aligns learning and assessments is a core principle of this framework and is accurately attributed.
- **[Claim–Citation Support] Harm reduction framework applied to AI policy** — Section 5.2 states 'It is rooted in harm reduction [56]: We should recognize that students are using GenAI tools regardless of instructor's stances, and thus instructors should make AI policies that can best mitigate the learning harms associated with GenAI tools.' Citation [56] is Marlatt 1996 on harm reduction in substance abuse. The application of this framework to AI policy is the authors' own theoretical reasoning, not a direct empirical claim from the cited work.
