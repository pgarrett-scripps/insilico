# Decision Letter

**Decision:** minor

## Summary of Evaluation
The panel converges (weighted 4.17/5; 2 accept, 5 minor, 1 major) on a competently executed qualitative interview study (n=13 US CS instructors, reflexive thematic analysis) whose ethics and analysis were scored at maximum by the two highest-confidence specialist reviewers. The single genuinely load-bearing critique is a framing problem, not a design problem: the title, abstract and Section 5.2 assert directional effects on student learning and instructor-student relationships that a self-report, no-student-data design cannot establish, even though the body text is consistently hedged. The authors concede this fully and propose a specific, checkable rewrite; I therefore treat it as an alignment task rather than grounds for major revision.

I weighed the rebuttal on the two contested points. On methodology's strong form ('the design cannot support any of its claims'), the authors' response is credible and quotes Section 4.2: the load-bearing finding is about instructor cognition and behaviour \u2014 that instructors who diagnose learning harms nonetheless overwhelmingly enact assessment-integrity policies (9/13 adopting paper exams) and report substantial second-order burden \u2014 which semi-structured interviews are the appropriate instrument to establish. That finding survives the required rewording intact and requires no new data. On reproducibility's suggestion that the learning/assessment framework may be an artifact of researcher priors, the Section 3.3 quote about three researchers with divergent GenAI stances converging from independent notes is a partial but real answer; it does not remove the need for the codebook, and I have kept the codebook and protocol deposition as required. The authors' flag that full transcript deposition may be irreconcilable with re-identification risk in a small, describable population is accepted: In Silico does not require raw transcript deposition where a documented consent and re-identification justification is given, provided the protocol and codebook are released and a conditional-access statement is added.

The compliance audits raise no blocking citation problems (0 HARD; the unverifiable interview quotations are inherent to the design). The methods audit's two HARD gaps \u2014 absent informed-consent documentation and absent participant demographics \u2014 are additive, not fatal: the demographic omission is an explicit, justified de-identification choice that need only be stated as such in the methods rather than only in Section 3.2, and the consent statement is a one-paragraph addition. Novelty's 3/5 identifies a real positioning weakness against Luo (2025), Ali et al. (2025) and Tom Tong et al. (2025); the authors' proposed contrast paragraph (enforcement/reasoning layer vs. survey and syllabus-text analyses) is an adequate remedy, and unacknowledged duplication was not demonstrated.

Decision: minor revision, at the demanding end of that label. Every required item is a documentable change to text or supplementary materials that the authors have already committed to; none requires further data collection. Acceptance is contingent on all items below being verifiably present in the revision.

## Required Revisions
1. Retitle and reframe all causal/directional claims. Revise the title, abstract, introduction and Section 5.2 so that no sentence asserts that policies worsen relationships, hinder learning, or reshape dynamics as an established effect. Every such statement must be explicitly attributed to instructor perception or report (e.g. 'instructors describe more adversarial dynamics'), matching the hedging already used in the body and Limitations. Section 5.2 recommendations must be labelled as exploratory suggestions warranting empirical validation, consistent with the p.18 admission that effectiveness evidence is lacking.
2. Rewrite the abstract to state the paper's central contribution and the learning-harms vs. assessment-harms distinction that structures the analysis, including the finding that assessment-oriented policies predominate in this sample. Replace all vague quantifiers ('most', 'surprisingly common', 'several') throughout the manuscript with explicit counts out of 13.
3. Deposit, as a supplementary appendix or in a public repository linked from the paper, (a) the full interview protocol and (b) the final codebook, giving code names, definitions and at least one example excerpt per code, with the mapping from codes to the reported themes. A citable, permanent link must appear in the manuscript.
4. Add a Data Availability statement that explicitly addresses transcripts: state that they cannot be deposited, give the consent and re-identification justification (small describable population of US CS instructors), and specify the conditions under which de-identified coded excerpts can be obtained. Non-deposition is acceptable here only if the justification is stated in the paper rather than left implicit.
5. Add explicit informed-consent documentation to Section 3.1: state that consent was obtained, how (written/verbal, recorded), what participants consented to regarding recording and quotation, and the data storage/retention practice for recordings and transcripts. Add the IRB committee name and protocol/exemption number.
6. Move the demographic non-reporting decision into the Methods as an explicit statement (currently only in Section 3.2), naming what was collected, what is withheld, and why, so a reader can assess sample composition limits without inferring the rationale.
7. Add the alternative-explanations paragraph to Sections 4.1.3/4.2.2 as proposed: state that reduced office-hour attendance and paper-exam learning effects are instructor-attributed observations with plausible competing explanations (scheduling, general help-seeking trends) that this design cannot adjudicate.
8. Add a positioning paragraph in Section 2.2 explicitly contrasting this study's enforcement/implementation-level interview data with Luo (2025) survey-based trust findings and the syllabus-text analyses of Ali et al. (2025) and Tom Tong et al. (2025), stating precisely what layer of the phenomenon this study captures that those do not.
9. Re-check the Baldeo (2026) and Shen & Tamkin (2026) citations in Section 2.1 against the specific programming-skill claims attributed to them. If the sources address executive function or general skill formation rather than programming/debugging, narrow the in-text claim to the sources' actual scope or substitute a directly relevant citation. State in the response letter which option was taken for each.
10. Add the missing conceptual citations: restorative-justice-in-academic-integrity literature (Section 4.4.1), test-anxiety/assessment-validity literature (Section 4.2.2), and Deci & Ryan on self-determination theory (Section 4.3.3).
11. Define 'AI-proof' at first use (Section 1) and 'second-order costs' at first use (Section 4.2) in one sentence each.

## Minor Suggestions
- State the stopping criterion for recruitment at n=13 \u2014 whether saturation was assessed, and if the authors reject saturation as inconsistent with reflexive TA (a defensible position), say so and explain what determined the sample size instead.
- Name the analysis tool, or state that coding was done manually.
- Briefly document the concrete reflexive safeguards used (memo-writing, checking codes against raw quotes, the independent-notes-before-group-discussion sequence described in the rebuttal). The rebuttal's account of three researchers with divergent GenAI stances converging independently is a stronger reflexivity claim than the manuscript currently makes \u2014 it is worth putting in Section 3.3.
- Consider stating explicitly in the Limitations that participant quotations cannot be independently verified by readers given the non-deposition of transcripts; candour here costs nothing and pre-empts the obvious reader question.
