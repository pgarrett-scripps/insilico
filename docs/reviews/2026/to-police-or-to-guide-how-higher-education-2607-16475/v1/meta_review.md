# Meta-Review

**Draft recommendation:** minor

## Synthesis
The panel is broadly aligned that this is a competently executed qualitative interview study whose methods and ethics are sound. The data-analysis reviewer (5/5, conf 5) confirms there are no quantitative claims requiring statistical validation and that reflexive TA is appropriate; the ethics reviewer (5/5, conf 5) finds IRB exemption, consent, compensation, and above-baseline de-identification all in order. Clarity (4/5, conf 5) finds the paper readable with one hard issue 	he abthe abstract failing to state the central learning-harms/assessment-harms distinction) and a set of soft fixes (undefined 'AI-proof', 'second-order costs', vague quantifiers, pronoun ambiguity).

The genuine split is between the advocate's 'wording fix' reading and the skeptic's 'structural overclaim' reading, anchored in the rigor (4/5) and novelty (3/5) reports. Rigor flags as HARD the title/abstract causal framing ('reshaping', 'worsening relationships', 'hinder student learning') given that the design contains no student data, no relationship measures, and no outcome comparison; methodology independently reaches the same conclusion, calling the absence of any measurement of student stress or learning a HARD design limitation and noting that Section 5.2's recommendations outrun what the design supports. Novelty adds that once the causal framing is trimmed, differentiation from Luo (2025), Ali et al. (2025) and Tom Tong et al. (2025) is not articulated, and the recommended learning-oriented practices are largely standard pedagogy applied to a new context. Reproducibility (4/5) raises three HARD artifact gaps: no deposited transcripts, no codebook, no full interview protocol 	abla for a study whose entire contribution is an interpretive analysis.

The critique that survives contact with the advocate is the skeptic's compounding argument: the overclaim and the unverifiable analysis interact. The advocate is right that the body text is consistently hedged ('instructors observe', 'instructors report') 	abla rigor concedes this explicitly 	abla and that the 9/13 paper-exam finding alongside instructors' own diagnosis of learning harms is a real, quotable empirical puzzle, not a relabeling. But the advocate understates the scope of the fix: the title, abstract framing and Section 5.2 recommendations all currently assert effects the design cannot establish, and repairing them touches the paper's positioning, not only its prose.

What the skeptic overstates: the underlying contribution does not evaporate when the causal language is removed. 'Instructors diagnose learning harms but overwhelmingly enact assessment-integrity policies, and report substantial second-order burden in doing so' is a fully supported, non-trivial descriptive finding at the sample level. That is a defensible paper. The literature reviewer's concerns (Baldeo 2026 and Shen & Tamkin 2026 attributions possibly broader than the programming-specific claims made; missing citations for restorative justice, test anxiety, SDT) are targeted and checkable.

## Decisive Factors
The confidence-weighted signal (4.17, 5 of 8 votes for minor) matches my recommendation, so no divergence to justify 	abla but the 'minor' label here is at its demanding end and should not be read as near-acceptance. Three things are required before this is publishable as written: (1) retitle/rewrite the abstract, introduction and Section 5.2 so that every claim about policy effects on relationships and learning is explicitly attributed to instructor perception, and so the recommendations are framed as exploratory suggestions warranting empirical validation 	abla the authors already concede on p.18 that they lack effectiveness evidence, so this is an alignment task, not a retreat; (2) state the abstract's core contribution and the learning/assessment harm distinction, with a precise denominator for quantifiers such as 'most' and '9 interviewees'; (3) address the reproducibility artifacts 	abla deposit the full interview protocol and codebook at minimum, and state explicitly whether consent conditions permit transcript deposition, with a clear justification if they do not. I weight the reproducibility HARD flags heavily because, as the skeptic argued and the advocate did not rebut, an interpretive analysis with no inspectable codebook cannot be checked by a reader, which is precisely the standard this venue applies. I do not escalate to major because the ethics and data-analysis reviewers are both at maximum score and confidence, the methodology reviewer confirms the analysis itself is competently executed, and the load-bearing descriptive finding survives the required rewording intact 	abla no new data collection is needed. The novelty reviewer's 3/5 is the panel's low outlier at conf 4; it identifies a real positioning weakness (undifferentiated from Luo 2025 and the concurrent syllabus studies) that should be fixed with an explicit comparison paragraph, but 'incremental' is not disqualifying at this venue and unacknowledged duplication has not been demonstrated.
