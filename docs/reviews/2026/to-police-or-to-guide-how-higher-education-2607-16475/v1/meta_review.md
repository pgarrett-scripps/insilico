# Meta-Review

**Draft recommendation:** major

## Synthesis
The panel agrees on the shape of this manuscript: a competently executed, timely qualitative interview study (n=13 CS instructors, reflexive thematic analysis) whose analytical framing — learning harms vs. assessment harms, and policy as a mediator of instructor-student relationships — is a real if incremental contribution. Clarity (4@5), ethics (4@5), literature (4@4) and novelty (4@4) all judge the work sound within its scope, with only refinement-level asks (terminology consistency around 'assessment harms'/'second-order costs', vague quantifiers, funding/COI and data-availability statements, sharper positioning against Lau & Guo 2023 and Luo 2024/2025). The data-analysis dimension was correctly declared inapplicable.

The split is between those four and the three critical reviews: methodology (3@4), rigor (3@5) and reproducibility (2@5). Two distinct concerns survive the debate. First, a construct-validity/overclaiming problem: the title and abstract present 'worsening relationships' and AI 'harm[s] student learning' as findings, when the evidence is instructor perception with no student data — a point the methodology reviewer marks HARD and the rigor reviewer documents claim by claim, including the paper's own quote from I5 that attribution to GenAI is difficult. Relatedly, the abstract 'make[s] recommendations' for learning-oriented policies while Section 5.3 concedes there is no evidence they work. Second, reproducibility: no deposited transcripts, no codebook, no code-to-quote mapping, no audit trail for the three-coder process.

The advocate's defence — that the authors are candid about limitations and that these are revision-level fixes — is half right, and the skeptic conceded as much: each item is fixable. But the skeptic's rebuttal lands where it matters. Candour buried in Section 5.3 does not neutralise causal framing in the title and abstract, which is what most readers of a preprint will actually consume. The novelty reviewer independently flags the same mismatch ('reading as advocacy without evidence'). On reproducibility, the advocate offered no counter beyond 'fixable'; the reviewer's specific point — that reflexive TA's rejection of inter-rater reliability does not excuse withholding a codebook or audit trail — stands unanswered, and confidentiality alone does not explain why de-identified materials cannot be deposited.

On the tension between the 4s and the 2/3s: I side with the critical reviewers on the framing question and with the majority on the underlying value of the study. The design is defensible for the question asked; what is not defensible is the gap between what the design can license and what the abstract and title assert.

## Decisive Factors
The confidence-weighted mean (3.41) sits between minor and major, and the modal verdict is 'minor'. I land on major rather than minor because the two surviving criticisms are both high-confidence (rigor 5, reproducibility 5) and both touch load-bearing, front-matter claims rather than internal detail. Retitling and rewriting the abstract so that perception-based findings are stated as perceptions, and recasting Section 5.2 recommendations as hypotheses for future work, changes what the paper claims — that is a substantive revision, not a copy-edit. Depositing a codebook, code-to-quote mapping, per-theme participant counts, and a documented disagreement-resolution procedure is likewise new material. Against that, I do not treat the absence of student outcome data as grounds for rejection: an instructor-perspective study is a legitimate design, and the flaw is in how its results are worded, not in the design itself. The single 'reject' (reproducibility) is therefore weighted as a strong demand for deposited materials rather than as a verdict on the work's soundness.

A revision that (i) removes causal language from title/abstract, (ii) provides counts for 'most'/'several' claims across the 13 participants, (iii) reframes learning-oriented policies as untested but promising, (iv) deposits codebook and audit trail or states explicitly why it cannot, (v) reports negative/disconfirming cases, and (vi) adds funding and COI statements, would move this to acceptance without further experiments.
