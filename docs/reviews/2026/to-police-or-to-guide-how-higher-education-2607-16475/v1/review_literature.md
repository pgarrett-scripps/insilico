# Related-Work & Citations Reviewer

## Summary
The manuscript demonstrates solid citation practices overall, with appropriate grounding in foundational literature (Vygotsky, Bandura, Wiggins & McTighe, Braun & Clarke) and good currency with recent work in AI and education (2024-2026). The bibliography is comprehensive (79 references) and well-formatted. However, two specific claims about cognitive offloading effects (Baldeo 2026 and Shen & Tamkin 2026) require verification, as the cited titles are broader than the specific programming-skill claims attributed to them. Additionally, several conceptual frameworks discussed in the findings (restorative justice, test anxiety, intrinsic motivation) could be strengthened with explicit citations to relevant literatures. The work fairly represents prior findings and does not appear to strawman competing approaches. No fabricated or unresolvable references were detected.

## Strengths
- Currency with fast-moving field: bibliography includes substantial 2024-2026 work on AI in education, showing awareness of recent developments
- Proper attribution of key prior work: Lau & Guo (2023) and Lau et al. (2026) on instructor adaptation, Luo (2024-2025) on trust and transparency are appropriately cited and accurately characterized
- Foundational grounding: canonical references for backward design (Wiggins & McTighe 2005), social learning (Vygotsky, Bandura), and methodology (Braun & Clarke) are present
- Cross-disciplinary reasoning: thoughtful use of harm reduction framework (Marlatt 1996) and abstinence-only education analogy (Stanger-Hall & Hall 2011) with proper attribution
- Reference hygiene: most citations include DOIs/URLs; list is well-formatted and comprehensive
- Fair representation: prior work is not strawmanned; mixed findings on AI effects are acknowledged rather than selectively cited

## Weaknesses
- Baldeo (2026) [ref 6] claim requires verification: manuscript attributes 'reduce[s] code reading and debugging skills' to a work titled 'Generative artificial intelligence reliance and executive function attenuation,' which appears to make a broader claim about executive function rather than specifically programming skills
- Shen & Tamkin (2026) [ref 68] claim requires verification: the claim that this work shows AI reduces 'code reading and debugging skills' should be checked against actual content, as the title 'How AI Impacts Skill Formation' is general and may not specifically address programming skills
- Restorative justice framework discussed but not cited: Section 4.4.1 quotes I8 on 'rehabilitative or restorative justice' but does not cite literature on restorative justice in academic integrity contexts
- Test anxiety mentioned but not grounded: I5's discussion of test anxiety (Section 4.2.2) is not supported by citations to educational psychology literature on test anxiety and assessment validity
- Self-determination theory missing: Section 4.3.3 discusses intrinsic motivation and interest but cites only Elliot (1999) on achievement goals; Deci & Ryan's self-determination theory would be more directly relevant
- Prather et al. (2023) [ref 61] genre unclear: cited as source for instructor adoption of AI tools, but the reference is a working group report, not a primary empirical study
- Albluwi (2019) [ref 2] scope unclear: claim about 'mixed results' in cheating deterrence cites a 2019 systematic review on plagiarism detection in the pre-AI era; generalizability to broader cheating-deterrence literature should be verified

## Questions
- Can the authors confirm that Baldeo (2026) specifically addresses code reading and debugging skills, or should a more targeted reference be used for this claim?
- Can the authors confirm that Shen & Tamkin (2026) specifically addresses programming skill formation, or is the title's broader framing more accurate?
- Would the manuscript benefit from citing literature on restorative justice in academic integrity to ground the shift from 'policing' to 'guiding' discussed in Section 4.4.1?
- Is Prather et al. (2023) a working group report or a primary empirical study, and should this be clarified when citing it for specific claims about instructor adoption?
- For the claim that Albluwi (2019) shows 'mixed results' in cheating deterrence, does this conclusion apply to the broader literature or specifically to pre-AI plagiarism detection methods?
- Would citing Deci & Ryan's self-determination theory strengthen the discussion of intrinsic motivation in Section 4.3.3?
