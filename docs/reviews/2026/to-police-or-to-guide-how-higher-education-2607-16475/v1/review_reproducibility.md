# Reproducibility Reviewer

## Summary
This is a qualitative interview study on CS instructor AI policies. From a reproducibility standpoint, the work is reasonably transparent about its qualitative methodology and data analysis approach. The authors document their reflexive thematic analysis process in detail (Section 3.3), acknowledge their positionality, and describe the interview protocol structure. However, critical load-bearing artifacts—the interview transcripts and the coded data—are not deposited or made available, which is a HARD flag for reproducibility of the thematic analysis. The interview protocol itself is only partially shown (Table 1 with example questions), and the full protocol is not provided. Without access to the raw transcripts and the detailed coding scheme/codebook, an independent group cannot verify the thematic analysis, trace how quotes were selected, or audit the interpretation. For a qualitative study where the analysis is the primary contribution, this is a significant reproducibility gap. The work is otherwise well-documented in its methodology and reasoning, making it suitable for minor revision to address data availability.

## Strengths
- The authors provide explicit documentation of their reflexive thematic analysis process across six phases (Section 3.3), including how codes were developed, compared across researchers, and consolidated into themes.
- Positionality is clearly stated: all three researchers have programming teaching experience, varying AI tool familiarity, and explicit motivation for the research, which supports reflexive methodology.
- The interview protocol structure is described with four sections (Table 1), and example questions are provided, giving readers a sense of the inquiry design.
- The authors acknowledge limitations of their approach (Section 5.3), including that findings are self-reported instructor accounts without independent empirical validation, and that the study is limited to US undergraduate CS contexts.
- Participant teaching contexts are documented in Table 2, providing transparency about the diversity of institutions, course types, and class sizes represented.

## Weaknesses
- The full interview protocol is not provided in the manuscript or referenced as a supplementary document or deposited resource. Only Table 1 shows example questions; the complete protocol needed to assess question design and potential bias is absent (HARD).
- Interview transcripts are not deposited in any repository, not made available as supplementary materials, and no statement indicates they will be deposited upon publication or are available on request with access conditions specified (HARD for load-bearing data).
- The coding scheme or codebook is not provided, deposited, or referenced. The manuscript describes codes like 'time and human cost of detecting AI use' and 'responsibility rhetoric' (Section 3.3) but does not provide the full codebook that would allow verification of how quotes were assigned to themes (HARD).
- No information is provided about how the three researchers' individual coding was compared or reconciled. The manuscript states 'we met over several meetings to compare our notes and discuss our interpretations' but does not specify the reconciliation procedure, inter-rater agreement metrics, or how disagreements were resolved (SOFT).
- The mapping between raw data (interview segments) and reported themes is opaque. While representative quotes are provided, it is unclear how many interview segments support each theme, whether all themes are equally represented across participants, or how the selection of quotes for inclusion was made (HARD for results-to-artifact mapping).
- No audio recordings, transcription protocols, or transcription software/version are mentioned. It is unclear whether interviews were transcribed verbatim, by whom, or using what tool, which affects reproducibility of the text data (SOFT).
- The manuscript does not specify the software or tool used for qualitative data analysis (e.g., NVivo, Atlas.ti, MAXQDA, or manual coding). This is relevant for reproducibility of the coding and theme-building process (SOFT).

## Questions
- Will the interview transcripts be deposited in a qualitative data repository (e.g., OSF, Zenodo, or a university institutional repository) with appropriate consent and anonymization? If so, what is the accession number or DOI?
- Can the authors provide the full interview protocol as a supplementary document or deposited resource?
- Can the authors provide the complete coding scheme or codebook used in the thematic analysis, showing the definitions and hierarchies of codes and how they map to final themes?
- How were disagreements between the three researchers' initial codes resolved? Was there a formal reconciliation procedure, and if so, what was it?
- What qualitative data analysis software, if any, was used to manage and code the interview transcripts? If manual coding was used, how was consistency maintained across the three researchers?
- How were the representative quotes selected for inclusion in the manuscript? Was this a systematic sample, or were quotes chosen based on illustrative power? How many interview segments supported each major theme?
- Were the interviews transcribed verbatim? By whom, and using what tool or process? Are transcription quality checks documented?
- Can the authors clarify the consent conditions for the interviews? Were participants informed that interview data might be deposited in a repository, or do consent limitations restrict data sharing?
