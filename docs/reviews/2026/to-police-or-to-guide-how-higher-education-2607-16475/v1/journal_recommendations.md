# Journal Recommendations

## As-is (current quality)
- **arXiv cs.CY**
  - Fit: The manuscript can be posted as a preprint server deposit without modification; this preserves archival record and allows broad circulation while the authors pursue peer-reviewed revision. arXiv cs.CY (Computers and Society) is the natural home for policy-focused CS research on societal impact.
  - Realism: Guaranteed (no peer review); preprint servers accept all technically sound work. However, this is a staging step, not an endpoint—most venues still expect publication in a peer-reviewed outlet.
- **EdArXiv**
  - Fit: Education-focused preprint server, explicitly designed for working papers and open-access dissemination in education research. Accepts qualitative and policy work before peer review.
  - Realism: Guaranteed as preprint; this is a natural fit for an education research paper and establishes priority before journal submission.

## After required revisions
- **In Silico**
  - Fit: The stated target venue. As an overlay journal for preprint review, In Silico is ideally suited to this work: it reviews qualitative research where claims can be checked against the manuscript and methods are transparent. The required revisions (codebook, protocol deposition, reframing causal claims, positioning against Luo 2025 and syllabus-analysis studies) directly address In Silico's reproducibility and clarity standards. The paper's reflexive thematic analysis and ethics are already at 5/5 confidence.
  - Realism: Very likely once revisions are incorporated. The editor's 'minor' verdict with committed revisions translates to conditional acceptance. In Silico explicitly accepts methods-transparent qualitative work and does not require raw transcripts where re-identification risk and consent conditions justify non-deposition (provided codebook and protocol are public). The core finding—that instructors diagnose learning harms but predominantly enact assessment-integrity policies, reporting substantial second-order burden—is sound and will survive the required reframing intact.
- **ACM Transactions on Computing Education (TOCE)**
  - Fit: Flagship peer-reviewed venue for CS education research. Publishes qualitative work (interviews, ethnography, design studies) and values implementation-level insights on instructor decision-making and policy design. The learning-harms vs. assessment-harms distinction and the empirical puzzle of why instructors choose policing over guiding are exactly the kind of nuanced practitioner problems TOCE covers. After revision to state the clear distinction from concurrent syllabus-text analyses and survey-based trust studies, the paper is well-positioned.
  - Realism: Realistic after revision (score ~4.2/5 would likely clear minor-to-accept threshold). TOCE values methodological transparency and would require the codebook and protocol deposition, which the required revisions include. The paper's strength in data analysis (5/5) and ethics (5/5) are TOCE benchmarks.
- **Computers & Education**
  - Fit: Established, cross-disciplinary education research journal that regularly publishes qualitative studies on teaching innovation and instructor decision-making. The paper's focus on how instructors navigate competing pressures (learning vs. assessment integrity) when designing policies fits well. Accepts self-reported instructor outcomes studies when clearly framed as such.
  - Realism: Realistic after revision. The reframed abstract (explicit attribution to instructor observation, removal of directional causal language) aligns with this venue's standards for qualitative education work. Codebook and protocol deposition would be expected but not unusual for qualitative work here. Positioning against Luo (2025, trust erosion from lack of transparency) and Ali et al. (2025, syllabus policy content) as distinct from this study's enforcement/reasoning layer strengthens differentiation.

## Alternative outlets
- **IEEE Transactions on Education**
  - Fit: Mid-tier education venue with explicit focus on computing and STEM teaching. Accepts qualitative research on instructor practice and curriculum design. Less stringent reproducibility requirements than top-tier CS education venues, making it accessible if TOCE or In Silico desk-reject, while still maintaining peer review.
  - Realism: Moderately likely even with minor polish only (current score 4.17/5 would likely pass). This is a realistic fallback if the target venues ask for deeper revisions or if the authors need faster publication.
- **Educational Technology Research & Development**
  - Fit: Interdisciplinary venue for research on teaching and learning with technology, which encompasses AI policies and their pedagogical design. Publishes design-oriented and practitioner-focused qualitative work. Would view the learning-oriented vs. assessment-oriented policy contrast as a contribution to instructional design theory.
  - Realism: Moderately likely after revision. Less specialized in CS than TOCE, but broader in scope and receptive to cross-disciplinary submissions about technology policy in education. Would accept the non-deposition of transcripts with explicit justification.
- **ACM Conference workshop (ITiCSE, SIGCSE)**
  - Fit: The ACM computing education conferences host workshops and education talks specifically for policy, pedagogy, and practitioner-focused research. The paper's framing of instructor dilemmas ('to police or to guide') and the concrete policy recommendations resonate with the practitioner audience. Conference papers do not require full reproducibility artifacts in the way journals do.
  - Realism: Very likely. Workshop/talk tracks at ITiCSE and SIGCSE actively seek qualitative interview studies on instructor practice, and the paper is ready to present now. Conference dissemination is a lower-friction alternative if journal peer review stalls, and the work would be well-received by the target audience (CS instructors, CS education researchers).

## Notes
The manuscript is realistically publishable after the required minor revisions, which are all documentable changes to text and supplementary materials—no new data collection needed. The core contribution (instructor policy design and implementation patterns, second-order costs of policing approaches) is sound and appropriate for CS education and education technology venues. The main barrier to as-is submission is the overclaimed causal framing in the abstract and title; once reframed to attribute observations to instructor report (which the body text already does consistently), the work is competitive at mid-tier peer-reviewed venues. In Silico as the stated target is a strong fit post-revision, given its explicit welcome of interpretive research with transparent methods and public reviews. Preprint deposition (arXiv, EdArXiv) is zero-friction and recommended as a staging step regardless of peer-reviewed venue choice.
