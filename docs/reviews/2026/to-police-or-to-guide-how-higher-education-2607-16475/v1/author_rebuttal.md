## Concessions
- **clarity** — Abstract does not state the central learning-harms vs assessment-harms distinction that structures the paper.
  - Proposed change: Rewrite the abstract to explicitly name the two harm types, state that assessment-oriented (paper exams, grading-weight) policies predominate over learning-oriented ones in our sample, and give the precise fraction (9/13) rather than 'most'.
- **clarity** — Key terms ('AI-proof', 'second-order costs') are used without formal definition, and quantifiers like 'most' and 'surprisingly common' are imprecise.
  - Proposed change: Add explicit one-sentence definitions at first use of 'AI-proof' (Sec 1) and 'second-order costs' (Sec 4.2), and replace vague quantifiers throughout with counts out of 13 (e.g., '9 of 13 instructors adopted some form of paper exam').
- **rigor** — Abstract, title, and Section 5.2 use causal/directional language ('worsening relationships', 'hinder student learning', 'reshaping relationships') unsupported by the observational, self-report design.
  - Proposed change: Revise the abstract, introduction, and Section 5.2 to consistently frame all outcome claims as instructor-perceived (e.g., 'instructors report experiencing burden and describe more adversarial dynamics' rather than 'policies worsen relationships'), and reframe learning-oriented recommendations explicitly as exploratory suggestions warranting empirical validation, matching the hedging already used in the body text and in the Limitations section.
- **methodology** — Claims about paper exams hurting learning, or reduced office hours reflecting AI use, are treated as findings without ruling out alternative explanations.
  - Proposed change: Add a paragraph to Section 4.1.3/4.2.2 explicitly noting alternative explanations (e.g., scheduling changes, general help-seeking trends) considered by us and acknowledging these observations are instructor-attributed rather than independently verified causal claims.
- **reproducibility** — No codebook, full interview protocol, or transcript deposition statement provided, preventing verification of the thematic analysis.
  - Proposed change: Add a supplementary appendix containing the full interview protocol and the final codebook (code names, definitions, and example quotes mapped to themes); add a Data Availability statement clarifying that transcripts cannot be deposited due to consent/re-identification risk given the small population of US CS instructors, but that de-identified coded excerpts are available upon request under IRB-approved conditions.
- **literature** — Citations to Baldeo (2026) and Shen & Tamkin (2026) may be broader (executive function, general skill formation) than the specific programming-skill claims attributed to them.
  - Proposed change: Re-check both sources against the specific claims in Section 2.1; if the papers do not specifically address programming/debugging skills, either narrow the claim to match the source's actual scope or replace/supplement with a more directly relevant citation.
- **literature** — Missing citations for restorative justice, test anxiety, and self-determination theory that ground concepts discussed in findings/discussion.
  - Proposed change: Add citations to restorative-justice-in-academic-integrity literature in Section 4.4.1, test-anxiety/assessment-validity literature in Section 4.2.2, and Deci & Ryan's self-determination theory in Section 4.3.3.
- **novelty** — The paper's positioning against concurrent work (Luo 2025, Ali et al. 2025, Tom Tong et al. 2025) is unclear, making the novelty claim overstated.
  - Proposed change: Add a paragraph in Section 2.2 explicitly contrasting our enforcement/implementation-level interview data against Luo's (2025) survey-based trust findings and Ali et al./Tom Tong et al.'s syllabus-text analyses, specifying that our contribution is the enforcement-and-reasoning layer these studies do not capture.
- **ethics** — IRB committee name and protocol number are not disclosed.
  - Proposed change: Add the IRB committee name and protocol/exemption number to Section 3.1, and add one sentence on data storage/retention practices for recordings and transcripts.

## Disagreements
- **methodology** — The study's design cannot support any of its claims about policy effects because it lacks control groups or outcome measurement, undermining the paper's contribution.
  - Response: We agree the design cannot support causal claims and are revising the abstract/discussion accordingly (see concessions). However, the core descriptive finding—that instructors who diagnose learning harms nonetheless overwhelmingly enact assessment-integrity policies, and report substantial burden doing so—is fully supported by the interview data at the level the study claims: instructor perception and stated reasoning. This is not a causal claim about student outcomes; it is a claim about instructor cognition and behavior, which is precisely what semi-structured interviews are suited to establish.
  - Manuscript reference: Section 4.2, opening: 'Even though instructors identified more ways AI might harm student learning than their assessments, because the assessments are more directly under their control, AI policies shared by instructors usually focused on assessments.'
- **novelty** — The distinction between learning harms and assessment harms is not a novel framework but a relabeling of well-established concerns (cognitive offloading, assessment integrity) already documented in prior literature.
  - Response: The novelty is not in identifying that cognitive offloading or assessment-integrity concerns exist—those are indeed well documented—but in showing empirically that these are two *distinct, separately motivating* pressures on the same instructor, and that instructors' policy responses track the assessment-harm dimension almost exclusively despite naming the learning-harm dimension as the deeper problem. That mismatch, and the finding that instructors describe it as an uncomfortable tradeoff rather than a solved problem, is the analytic contribution, not a repackaging of the underlying harms.
  - Manuscript reference: Section 4.1.4: 'In aggregate, instructors perceive challenges from AI from two dimensions... The AI policy items they adopt can thus be sorted based on whether the policy seeks to influence learning or assessment.' combined with Section 4.2's demonstration that nearly all concrete policy artifacts (paper exams, grading-weight rules, uncovered-code detection) map onto the assessment dimension.
- **reproducibility** — Without a codebook and deposited transcripts, the learning/assessment-harm framework cannot be distinguished from a framework imposed by researchers already primed to find it.
  - Response: We will release the protocol and codebook (see concession above), which addresses verifiability of the coding structure. But the suggestion that the theme itself may be an artifact of researcher priors is addressed by our reflexive methodology section, which documents that the theme emerged iteratively across three researchers with differing prior stances toward AI (daily users to non-users), converging independently on the assessment/learning distinction from separately taken notes before group discussion.
  - Manuscript reference: Section 3.3: 'We met over several meetings to compare our notes and discuss our interpretations. During these meetings, we shared our initial codes... and theorized overarching patterns of meaning... All three researchers have recent programming teaching experience... The researchers have varying degrees of skill and trust in using GenAI tools, ranging from daily use to non-use due to ethical concerns.'

## Load-bearing critiques
- Reproducibility's demand for deposited raw transcripts may be irreconcilable with participant re-identification risk given the small, describable population of US CS instructors (Sec 3.2); if the venue requires full transcript deposition as a strict condition rather than accepting a justified non-deposition with codebook/protocol release, this could not be resolved this cycle without recontacting all 13 participants for expanded consent.
