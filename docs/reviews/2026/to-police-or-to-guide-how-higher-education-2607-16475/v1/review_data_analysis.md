This is a qualitative interview study using reflexive thematic analysis. It contains no quantitative hypothesis tests, statistical comparisons, p-values, confidence intervals, effect sizes, sample-size calculations, or statistical claims to evaluate.

## Summary
This manuscript reports a qualitative study of 13 semi-structured interviews with CS instructors, analyzed using reflexive thematic analysis to identify patterns in how instructors design and implement AI policies. The work is explicitly framed as exploratory and interpretivist, with the authors stating that 'findings from the analysis are not meant to be representative of a broader population' and that they document their analysis process 'in lieu of reporting inter-rater reliability.' There are no quantitative analyses, statistical tests, or numerical claims requiring statistical validation. The paper's contribution lies in qualitative insight into instructor reasoning and policy design, not in statistical inference. As such, this manuscript falls outside the scope of statistical and data-analysis review.

## Strengths
- The authors transparently acknowledge the qualitative, non-representative nature of their findings and the interpretivist methodology, avoiding false claims of generalizability.
- The reflexive thematic analysis methodology is clearly described, including the multi-researcher coding process and iterative refinement of themes.
- The authors explicitly disclose their positionality (teaching experience, varying AI tool use, motivation for the research), which is appropriate for reflexive qualitative work.
