# Statistics & Data-Analysis Reviewer

## Summary
This is a qualitative interview study with no quantitative hypothesis tests, statistical models, or numerical comparisons. The manuscript employs reflexive thematic analysis on 13 semi-structured interviews, a methodologically sound approach for exploratory research in education. There are no p-values, confidence intervals, effect sizes, sample-size calculations, or statistical claims to evaluate. The authors explicitly acknowledge the interpretivist, non-representative nature of their findings and state that the work is exploratory rather than confirmatory. From a statistics and data-analysis perspective, there are no HARD or SOFT issues to flag: the manuscript makes no quantitative claims that require statistical validation, and the qualitative methodology is transparently reported.

## Strengths
- The authors explicitly state their reflexive thematic analysis approach and acknowledge that findings are exploratory and not meant to be representative of a broader population (Section 3.3).
- The analysis process is documented in detail: the three researchers' coding, iterative refinement of themes, and collaborative interpretation are described, replacing inter-rater reliability metrics with reflexive transparency as appropriate for interpretivist methodology (Section 3.3).
- The authors acknowledge their own positionality and potential biases (varying experience with GenAI, prior teaching experience), which is best practice for qualitative research (Section 3.3).
- No quantitative claims are made without support; the manuscript does not report p-values, effect sizes, or statistical tests where none were conducted.
- Sample size (n=13) is appropriate for semi-structured interview research and is not misrepresented as sufficient for statistical generalization.
