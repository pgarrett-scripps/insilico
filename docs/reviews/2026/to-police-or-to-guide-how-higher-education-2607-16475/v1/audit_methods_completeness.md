# Methods Completeness & Reagent Traceability Auditor

## Summary
This is a qualitative interview study of 13 US higher education CS instructors examining their design and implementation of generative AI policies. The manuscript reports semi-structured interviews analyzed via reflexive thematic analysis. The study received IRB exemption and compensated participants ($50). The primary methodological categories triggered are human subjects (IRB approval, informed consent, participant demographics) and computational/analysis methods (thematic analysis procedure, coding, reflexivity documentation). The manuscript provides IRB approval status and interview compensation but lacks explicit informed-consent documentation, detailed participant demographics beyond teaching context, and complete transparency on the reflexive analysis process (e.g., inter-rater reliability alternatives, code saturation criteria, or analysis software). Data and code availability are not addressed. The analysis relies on reflexive thematic analysis with acknowledged researcher positionality, but key procedural details for reproducibility are sparse.

**HARD gaps (blocking): 2** · SOFT gaps: 3 · unverifiable: 2

## HARD gaps — reproduction blockers
- **[Human subjects / qualitative research] Informed consent documentation and process** — The manuscript states IRB exemption was granted and participants were compensated ($50), but does not explicitly state that informed consent was obtained or describe the consent process. Section 3.1 mentions compensation and remote Zoom interviews but does not address consent documentation.
- **[Human subjects / qualitative research] Participant demographics (age, gender, race, career stage, institution type)** — Section 3.2 states: 'we opted to take extra care in protecting the participants' identities and not report their demographic information.' Only teaching context (institution type, courses taught, class size) is reported in Table 2. No demographic data on instructors themselves (age, gender, race, years of experience, etc.) are provided.

## Unverifiable (raise as questions)
- **[Qualitative analysis methods / thematic analysis] Coding procedure and inter-rater reliability or reflexive alternative** — Section 3.3 describes the process: 'three researchers began by closely reading through each transcript and taking notes' and 'met over several meetings to compare our notes and discuss our interpretations.' Initial codes and thematic codes are listed as examples ('time and human cost of detecting AI use', 'focus on debugging skills', etc.). However, the manuscript states: 'As part of the reflexive methodology, we the researchers want to acknowledge our positionality' and 'the findings from the analysis are not meant to be representative of a broader population.' The authors explicitly reject inter-rater reliability metrics: 'we will document our analysis process in detail in lieu of reporting inter-rater reliability.' No quantitative measure of agreement, saturation criteria, or formal reliability alternative (e.g., triangulation matrix, audit trail, or consensus-building procedure) is documented. The reflexive stance is declared but the specific procedural safeguards are not detailed.
- **[Qualitative analysis methods / thematic analysis] Reflexive analysis procedure and how researcher subjectivity was managed** — Section 3.3 states: 'reflexive thematic analysis is an interpretivist method that acknowledges how the meaning of interviewees' words is constructed by the researchers' and 'researchers should recognize their active and reflexive role in analyzing data, instead of obscuring their subjectivity with more positivist metrics, such as computing inter-rater reliability.' The authors acknowledge reflexivity and positionality but do not detail specific procedures (e.g., memo-writing, code-checking against raw quotes, peer debriefing beyond the three authors, or member checking with participants) used to manage or document reflexive interpretation.

## SOFT gaps — recommended
- **[Qualitative analysis methods / thematic analysis] Data saturation or stopping criterion** — The manuscript does not state whether data saturation was reached, how saturation was assessed, or what criterion determined that 13 interviews were sufficient. No stopping rule or saturation check is mentioned.
- **[Qualitative analysis methods / thematic analysis] Software or tool used for analysis (if any)** — The manuscript does not mention whether qualitative analysis software (e.g., NVivo, Atlas.ti, MAXQDA, Dedoose) was used or whether coding was done manually. No analysis tool is named.
- **[Qualitative analysis methods / thematic analysis] Data availability and code/transcript availability** — No data-availability statement is provided. The manuscript does not state whether interview transcripts, coded data, or analysis materials are available, deposited in a repository, or available upon request. Section 3.2 notes that 'we opted to take extra care in protecting the participants' identities,' which may justify anonymization, but no statement on conditional availability (e.g., 'available upon request with IRB approval') is made.

## Documented (for the record)
- **[Human subjects / qualitative research] IRB approval and exemption status** — Section 3.1: 'The interview study was reviewed and granted exemption by the IRB.'
- **[Human subjects / qualitative research] Participant inclusion/exclusion criteria** — Section 3.2: 'we recruited instructors from US institutions only' and 'instructors who were actively teaching computer science courses in higher education institutions.' Recruitment was via 'purposive sampling' and 'snowball sampling.'
- **[Human subjects / qualitative research] Sample size (n) with clear definition of what n represents** — Section 3.1 and throughout: 'we conducted 13 semi-structured interviews with instructors' and Table 2 lists 13 interviewees (I1–I13). n=13 represents individual instructors interviewed.
- **[Qualitative analysis methods / thematic analysis] Analysis method named and described** — Section 3.3: 'we applied reflexive thematic analysis to identify patterns' following 'the six phases of thematic analysis' by Braun and Clarke (citations 14, 13, 15). The method is explicitly named and referenced.
- **[Qualitative analysis methods / thematic analysis] Interview protocol availability** — Section 3.1, Table 1: 'Sample questions from the interview protocol' are provided, showing four sections (Teaching Context, AI Policy on Paper, AI Policy Enforcement, AI Policy Design) with example questions. The full protocol is not reproduced, but representative items are shown.
- **[Qualitative analysis methods / thematic analysis] Researcher positionality and potential bias** — Section 3.3: 'As part of the reflexive methodology, we the researchers want to acknowledge our positionality. All three researchers have recent programming teaching experience, with two of the three having taught computer science prior to the advent of GenAI. The researchers have varying degrees of skill and trust in using GenAI tools, ranging from daily use to non-use due to ethical concerns. All three researchers are motivated by interest in AI policies and their effects.'
- **[Qualitative analysis methods / thematic analysis] Limitations of qualitative scope and generalizability** — Section 5.3: 'While we strived to recruit instructors that teach in a wide range of learning environments, in an effort to define the scope of this study, we recruited instructors from US institutions only.' Also: 'this interview study focuses on undergraduate CS education, and thus any interpretations of the data is limited to that context.' And: 'the answers to these questions are solely self-reported accounts by the instructors, without another source of empirical data to validate them.'
- **[Protocol provenance] Reflexive thematic analysis method — resolvability of cited protocol** — The method is attributed to Braun and Clarke (2006, 2019, 2023; citations 14, 13, 15). These are published, citable sources describing the six-phase thematic analysis procedure. The citations are resolvable and the method is standard in qualitative research. The manuscript follows the stated procedure (reading, coding, meeting to discuss, refining themes, extracting quotes).
- **[Protocol provenance] Interview recruitment and sampling procedure — completeness** — Section 3.1: 'Most interviewees were recruited by purposive sampling, with some instructors recruited by snowball sampling as we encouraged interviewees to forward the recruitment message to others who are qualified and interested in participating.' The procedure is described, though recruitment materials and eligibility criteria are not fully detailed.
