# Methods Completeness & Reagent Traceability Auditor

## Summary
This is a qualitative interview study of 13 CS instructors' AI policies. The manuscript reports reflexive thematic analysis of semi-structured interviews. Applicable categories are Human Subjects (IRB approval, informed consent, participant demographics) and Computational/ML (if any custom analysis code was used). The study provides IRB exemption status, interview protocol structure, and participant teaching contexts, but lacks some standard reporting details: no explicit informed-consent documentation, no participant demographic breakdown (intentionally withheld for anonymity), no data-availability or code-availability statement, and no explicit statement on whether custom analysis code exists or is available. The analysis method is described qualitatively rather than with computational parameters.

## Categories checked
- Human subjects/qualitative research
- Protocol provenance

**HARD gaps (blocking): 3** · SOFT gaps: 3 · unverifiable: 1

## HARD gaps — reproduction blockers
- **[Human subjects/qualitative research] Informed consent documentation and process** — The manuscript states IRB exemption was granted and that interviewees were compensated ($50), but does not explicitly state that informed consent was obtained or describe the consent process. For human subjects research, even exempt studies typically require consent unless waived; the exemption status does not confirm consent was sought or documented.
- **[Human subjects/qualitative research] Inclusion/exclusion criteria for participant selection** — Section 3.1 states 'Most interviewees were recruited by purposive sampling, with some instructors recruited by snowball sampling' but does not specify explicit inclusion/exclusion criteria (e.g., 'currently teaching CS courses', 'US-based', 'undergraduate level', 'within last X years'). The criteria are implied but not formally stated.
- **[Protocol provenance] Data availability statement (interview transcripts or anonymized data)** — The manuscript contains no data-availability statement. It does not indicate whether interview transcripts, anonymized data, or coded themes are available, deposited in a repository, or available upon request. This is standard for qualitative research reporting.

## Unverifiable (raise as questions)
- **[Computational/ML] Custom analysis code availability (if thematic coding was computational)** — Section 3.3 describes the analysis as qualitative thematic coding performed by three researchers through iterative discussion and refinement. It is unclear whether custom code (e.g., for coding, theme extraction, or visualization) was written or used. No code-availability statement is provided. The analysis appears manual/interpretive rather than computational, but this is not explicitly stated.

## SOFT gaps — recommended
- **[Human subjects/qualitative research] Participant demographic information (age, gender, race, career stage, institution type breakdown)** — Section 3.2 states: 'Since the population of higher education CS instructors in the US is relatively small, we opted to take extra care in protecting the participants' identities and not report their demographic information.' The authors intentionally withheld demographics for anonymity. Table 2 provides teaching context (institution type, courses, class size) but not personal demographics.
- **[Human subjects/qualitative research] Sample size justification or power calculation** — The manuscript reports n=13 interviews but provides no justification for this sample size, no power calculation, and no saturation analysis. Section 3.3 acknowledges the findings are exploratory and not meant to be representative, but does not explain why 13 was chosen.
- **[Protocol provenance] Inter-rater reliability or coding agreement metrics** — Section 3.3 explicitly states: 'As such, we will document our analysis process in detail in lieu of reporting inter-rater reliability.' The authors chose not to report inter-rater reliability, citing the reflexive methodology. This is a deliberate choice, not an omission, but is worth noting for transparency.

## Documented (for the record)
- **[Human subjects/qualitative research] IRB approval status and protocol number** — Section 3.1: 'The interview study was reviewed and granted exemption by the IRB.'
- **[Protocol provenance] Reflexive thematic analysis procedure — reference to Braun & Clarke method** — Section 3.3 cites Braun and Clarke [14, 15] and describes the six-phase approach: 'Following the six phases of thematic analysis [14], the three researchers began by closely reading through each transcript and taking notes...' The method is delegated to [14] (Braun & Clarke 2006) and [15] (Braun & Clarke 2023), both resolvable published sources.
- **[Protocol provenance] Interview protocol — availability and full specification** — Section 3.1 describes the protocol structure in four sections (teaching context, AI policy on paper, enforcement, design) and Table 1 provides example questions. The full protocol is not reproduced in the manuscript, but the structure and sample questions are documented. No statement is made about protocol availability in a repository or appendix.
- **[Protocol provenance] Researcher positionality and potential bias disclosure** — Section 3.3 includes a reflexivity statement: 'As part of the reflexive methodology, we the researchers want to acknowledge our positionality. All three researchers have recent programming teaching experience, with two of the three having taught computer science prior to the advent of GenAI. The researchers have varying degrees of skill and trust in using GenAI tools, ranging from daily use to non-use due to ethical concerns.'
