# Reproducibility Reviewer

## Summary
This qualitative interview study lacks the data and procedural transparency necessary for independent reproduction or verification. While the research questions and thematic analysis approach are sound, the manuscript provides no access to the interview transcripts, no detailed coding scheme, no analysis software specifications, and no clear mapping between raw data and reported findings. The reflexive thematic analysis method is described in general terms but without the granular procedural detail needed to rerun the analysis. For a study making claims about instructor beliefs and policies based on 13 interviews, the inability to inspect the primary data or verify the coding decisions is a fundamental reproducibility failure. The authors acknowledge their positionality but do not deposit the materials needed for independent verification or replication.

## Strengths
- The manuscript clearly states the reflexive thematic analysis method and cites Braun & Clarke (2006, 2023), providing a methodological anchor.
- The authors transparently acknowledge their positionality and potential biases in the analysis, noting varying degrees of AI tool familiarity and prior teaching experience.
- Interview protocol structure is outlined with example questions (Table 1), showing the four-section design.
- Participant teaching contexts are documented in Table 2, providing some transparency about the sample composition.

## Weaknesses
- No interview transcripts, audio recordings, or transcript excerpts beyond short quotes are deposited or made available; the manuscript states data will be protected for participant anonymity but provides no path to access de-identified transcripts or a data repository with access conditions.
- The coding scheme and thematic codebook are not provided; the manuscript describes initial codes ('time and human cost of detecting AI use', 'focus on debugging skills') and thematic codes ('second-order harms from assessments', 'fraying student-instructor relationships') but does not deposit the full codebook, code definitions, or code-to-quote mappings needed to verify the analysis.
- No software or tool version is specified for the thematic analysis; it is unclear whether NVivo, Atlas.ti, or manual coding was used, and no analysis scripts or configuration files are provided.
- The three-researcher coding and refinement process is described narratively ('we met over several meetings to compare notes') but without documenting inter-rater agreement, disagreement resolution procedures, or the sequence of coding iterations; no audit trail is provided.
- No random seed, reproducibility statement, or version control information is given for the analysis workflow.
- The manuscript explicitly states 'the findings from the analysis are not meant to be representative of a broader population' and avoids reporting inter-rater reliability, citing reflexive methodology, but this does not eliminate the need to deposit the raw data and coding decisions so readers can assess the analysis independently.
- No supplementary materials, appendices, or data repository links are mentioned in the manuscript; the statement 'we took extra care in protecting the participants' identities and not report their demographic information' does not explain why de-identified transcripts cannot be deposited.
- The mapping between interview sections (Table 1) and the reported findings is implicit; it is unclear which specific interview questions generated which themes, making it difficult to trace claims back to source data.

## Questions
- Will the de-identified interview transcripts be deposited in a public repository (e.g., OSF, Zenodo, institutional repository) with a DOI and access conditions clearly stated, or are they available only on request from the authors?
- Can the authors provide the full codebook used in the thematic analysis, including code definitions, hierarchies, and examples of coded segments from the transcripts?
- What software tool was used for the coding and thematic analysis (e.g., NVivo, Atlas.ti, manual spreadsheet)? If custom scripts were used, will they be deposited with version information?
- How were disagreements between the three coders resolved during the refinement meetings? Was a consensus procedure documented, and if so, can it be provided as supplementary material?
- Can the authors provide a table or appendix mapping each reported theme to the interview questions that generated it, and the number of participants who discussed each theme?
- The manuscript states 'we will document our analysis process in detail in lieu of reporting inter-rater reliability' — can this detailed documentation (e.g., an audit trail of coding decisions, meeting notes, or iterative refinements) be deposited as supplementary material?
- Are there any intermediate analysis artifacts (e.g., initial code lists, theme development notes, or code frequency tables) that could be deposited to support reproducibility?
